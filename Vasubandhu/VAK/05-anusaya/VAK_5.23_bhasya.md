# VAK_5.23 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā

> rāgapratighamānaiḥ syād atītapratyupasthitaiḥ |
> yatrotpannā 'prahīṇās te tasmin vastuni saṃyutaḥ || 5.23 ||

**Best current translation:**  
One is connected with a given object by past or presently arisen attachment, aversion, and conceit when those afflictions have arisen with respect to that object and have not been abandoned.

## Literal Prose Translation

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

In summary, these afflictions are of two kinds.

Attachment, aversion, and conceit are afflictions having a particular determination (`svalakṣaṇa-kleśa`). View, doubt, and ignorance are afflictions having a general determination (`sāmānya-kleśa`).

First, concerning the former:

> One is connected by past or presently arisen attachment, aversion, and conceit with that object regarding which they have arisen and have not been abandoned.

Past and present attachment, aversion, and conceit: with whatever object they have arisen, if they have not been abandoned, one is connected with that object through them.

For these, because they are `svalakṣaṇa-kleśas`, do not necessarily arise for every person with respect to every object.

---

## Course of the Argument

The Bhāṣya introduces a new twofold classification before explaining the verse:

```text
kleśa
    ↓
    two kinds

svalakṣaṇa-kleśa
    rāga
    pratigha
    māna

sāmānya-kleśa
    dṛṣṭi
    vicikitsā
    avidyā
```

This distinction supplies the reason for the exact form of VAK 5.23.

The three `svalakṣaṇa-kleśas` are not universally related to every object. They arise selectively:

```text
this rāga
    toward this vastu

this pratigha
    toward this vastu

this māna
    with respect to this vastu
```

Therefore the relation of bondage must be indexed to the object with respect to which the affliction actually arose.

```text
utpanna with respect to Vastu X
    +
aprahīṇa
    ↓
saṃyukta with Vastu X
```

The temporal qualification is also precise. The Bhāṣya glosses the kārikā's `atīta-pratyupasthita` with:

```text
atīta
pratyutpanna
```

Thus the contrast here is past versus present, not merely latent versus manifest.

A past affliction can still ground present connectedness to its former object so long as it remains unabandoned.

## Voice Identification

The entire passage is straightforwardly explanatory:

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

No opponent intervenes in this unit. The Bhāṣya's principal contribution is the explicit classification into `svalakṣaṇa-kleśa` and `sāmānya-kleśa`.

---

## New Technical Terms

- **svalakṣaṇa-kleśa** — particularized affliction; an affliction whose occurrence is indexed to a determinate object and is not necessary for every person with respect to every object
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only classification made explicit before 5.23
  - **function:** explains the object-specific rule governing rāga, pratigha, and māna

- **sāmānya-kleśa** — general affliction
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only classification paired with `svalakṣaṇa-kleśa`
  - **function:** names the contrasting class consisting of dṛṣṭi, vicikitsā, and avidyā; its consequences are developed in the following verse

- **pratyutpanna** — presently existing / present
  - **status:** Bhāṣya gloss of the kārikā's `pratyupasthita`
  - **function:** fixes the temporal meaning of the verse

- **saṃyukta** — connected with, bound to
  - **status:** technical relational use
  - **function:** describes the person's relation to a particular `vastu` through an unabandoned affliction

## Doctrinal Determination

The decisive Bhāṣya result is that the three afflictions named in the verse are **particular afflictions**.

```text
rāga / pratigha / māna
    ≠ universally active toward every object
```

Instead:

```text
particular person
    + particular affliction
    + particular vastu
    + actual arising
    + non-abandonment
    = particular bondage relation
```

This clarifies `saṃyuta`. The verse is not merely saying that the continuum possesses an affliction. It says that the person is connected **with that object** through the affliction.

The Bhāṣya therefore sharpens our first-pass conclusion that bondage is indexed. It now gives the doctrinal reason:

```text
indexed bondage
    because
svalakṣaṇa-kleśa
```

The passage also distinguishes chronological cessation from abandonment:

```text
past affliction
    may no longer be present as an event
    yet
if aprahīṇa
    it still determines connectedness
```

Abandonment, not mere passage into the past, terminates the relevant bond.

## Effect on the Kārikā Translation

**Clarification required.**

The first-pass translation is substantially correct, but the Bhāṣya makes two points explicit:

1. `pratyupasthita` is explained as `pratyutpanna`, so the intended contrast is **past / present**.
2. `tasmin vastuni saṃyutaḥ` is grounded specifically in the `svalakṣaṇa` character of these afflictions.

A tighter canonical rendering is:

> Through past or present attachment, aversion, and conceit, one is connected with that particular object regarding which those afflictions have arisen, so long as they have not been abandoned.

## Logical Determination

The Bhāṣya introduces a distinction between **general** and **particular** afflictive determination.

```text
svalakṣaṇa-kleśa
    object-indexed
    contingent occurrence

sāmānya-kleśa
    general scope
    developed in 5.24
```

For 5.23, the relation has the form:

```text
Person P
Affliction A
Object V

A ∈ {rāga, pratigha, māna}
A arose regarding V
A is not abandoned
    ↓
P is connected with V through A
```

The essential point is that a universal predicate cannot be inferred from the mere existence of the affliction-class:

```text
Rāga exists in the continuum
    does not imply
rāga toward every vastu
```

The Bhāṣya explicitly denies such universalization:

```text
na sarvasya
na avaśyaṃ
na sarvatra
```

not for everyone,
not necessarily,
not everywhere / with respect to every object.

This threefold negation is the logical signature of `svalakṣaṇa-kleśa` in the present passage.


## Bhāṣya Pass Result

VAK 5.23 begins a new relational analysis by dividing afflictions into particular and general forms:

> **Attachment, aversion, and conceit are `svalakṣaṇa-kleśas`. Because they do not necessarily arise for every person with respect to every object, bondage through them is object-specific. A person is connected with precisely that object regarding which the affliction actually arose and remains unabandoned. The affliction may be past in time and still sustain this connection; only abandonment removes the relation.**

The contrast with `sāmānya-kleśa` is opened here and becomes the governing issue of VAK 5.24.
