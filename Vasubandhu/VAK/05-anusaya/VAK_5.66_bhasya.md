# VAK 5.66 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā Anchor

**VAK 5.66**

> **anāgamya-phalaṃ sarvā dhyānānāṃ pañca vāthavā /**  
> **aṣṭau sāmantakasyaikā maulārūpya-trayasya ca // 5.66 //**

**Working translation:**  
All [nine parijñās] are fruits of the anāgamya. Of the [fundamental] dhyānas there are five—or, on another view, eight. One is the fruit of the [first formless] preliminary concentration, and one is the fruit of the three fundamental formless attainments.

See: [VAK_5.66.md](./VAK_5.66.md)

## Voice

**Voice:** Vasubandhu reporting and distinguishing school positions.

- **Vaibhāṣika position:** fundamental dhyānas have five parijñās as fruits.
- **Bhadanta Ghoṣaka:** fundamental dhyānas have eight.
- The prose then states the assignments of anāgamya, dhyānāntara, the preliminary concentration of the sphere of infinite space, and the three fundamental formless attainments.

## Translation and Argument

The question is no longer **what the nine parijñās are**. VAK 5.64–5.65 established that. The present question is:

> From which meditative bases are these completed abandonments obtained as fruits?

The Bhāṣya distributes the nine parijñās according to the samādhi-basis capable of supporting the corresponding path.

### 1. Anāgamya: all nine

The Kārikā begins:

> **anāgamyaphalaṃ sarvā** — all [nine] are fruits of the anāgamya.

Thus the preliminary concentration before the first fundamental dhyāna is capable of supporting paths whose fruits comprise the entire ninefold set of parijñās.

This is a statement of **path-capacity**, not an identification of anāgamya with any parijñā.

```text
anāgamya
    → capable of yielding all nine parijñās
```

### 2. Fundamental dhyānas: five according to the Vaibhāṣikas

The Bhāṣya is explicit:

> **vaibhāṣikamatena mauladhyānaphalaṃ pañca parijñā yā rūpārūpyāvacarakleśaprahāṇasvabhāvāḥ** [323|15-323|16]

According to the Vaibhāṣikas, the fundamental dhyānas have as their fruits **five parijñās whose nature consists in abandonment of afflictions belonging to the form and formless domains**.

The desire-domain abandonments are excluded from this fivefold count because:

> **kāmāvacarakleśaprahāṇasyānāgamyaphalatvāt** [323|16]

—abandonment of desire-domain afflictions is a fruit of the anāgamya.

The five are therefore the higher-domain completions among the nine:

```text
higher-domain duḥkha+samudaya seeing-abandonment
higher-domain nirodha seeing-abandonment
higher-domain mārga seeing-abandonment
rūparāga-kṣaya
sarva-saṃyojana-paryādāna
```

### 3. Bhadanta Ghoṣaka: eight

The alternative is explicitly attributed:

> **bhadantaghoṣakasya matenāṣṭau** [323|16]

For Bhadanta Ghoṣaka, the fundamental dhyānas have **eight** parijñās as fruits.

The reason is:

> **sa hi vītarāgasyāpi kāmāvacarāṇāṃ darśanaheyānāṃ prahāṇaṃ darśanamārgaphalamicchanti** [323|17]

He accepts that even for one already free from desire-domain passion, abandonment of the desire-domain afflictions abandoned by seeing is a fruit of the path of seeing.

The Bhāṣya grounds this in:

> **anāsravavisaṃyogaprāptilābhāt** [323|18]

—because there is acquisition of an undefiled attainment of disconnection.

This adds the three desire-domain **darśana-heya** parijñās to the Vaibhāṣika five:

```text
5 + 3 = 8
```

But one parijñā still remains excluded:

> **avarabhāgīyaprahāṇaparijñā tvanāgamyaphalameva** [323|18]

The parijñā of abandonment of the lower-part fetters is **exclusively a fruit of anāgamya**.

Thus Ghoṣaka's eightfold count does not collapse the distinction between anāgamya and the fundamental dhyānas.

### 4. Dhyānāntara follows the dhyānas

The Bhāṣya adds:

> **dhyānāntaraṃ dhyānavaddraṣṭavyam** [323|19]

The intermediate dhyāna is to be understood **like the dhyānas**.

This answers one open question from the Kārikā pass: its parijñā-capacity follows the same rule as the fundamental dhyānas under the relevant doctrinal position.

### 5. Preliminary concentration of ākāśānantyāyatana: one

For the formless attainments:

> **ākāśānantyāyatanasāmantakasyaikā rūparāgakṣayaparijñā phalam** [323|20-323|21]

The preliminary concentration of the sphere of infinite space has exactly one parijñā as fruit:

```text
rūparāga-kṣaya-parijñā
    destruction of attachment to form
```

This is the boundary-path by which form-domain attachment is abandoned.

### 6. Three fundamental formless attainments: one

Finally:

> **maulānāṃ ca trayāṇāmārūpyāṇāmekaiva sarvasaṃyojanaparyādānaparijñā phalam** [324|02]

For the three fundamental formless attainments, there is only one parijñā as fruit:

```text
sarva-saṃyojana-paryādāna-parijñā
    complete exhaustion of all fetters
```

The Bhāṣya does not here assign three different parijñās to the three fundamental formless attainments. They share the same final parijñā-type as their possible fruit.

## New Technical Terms

### anāgamya

- **status:** previously encountered in the path/concentration system
- **function here:** meditative basis whose path can yield all nine parijñās

The present passage defines it functionally through its range of path-fruits.

### maula-dhyāna

- **status:** previously encountered
- **function:** fundamental dhyāna, contrasted with a preliminary concentration

### vītarāga

- **status:** previously encountered
- **function:** one already free from passion with respect to a lower domain

In Ghoṣaka's argument this prior freedom does not prevent a fresh **anāsrava-visaṃyoga-prāpti** from being acquired through the path of seeing.

### anāsrava-visaṃyoga-prāpti

- **status:** important Bhāṣya formulation connecting directly to 5.63
- **function:** undefiled acquisition of disconnection

This phrase explains why an abandonment already achieved in one sense can still receive a new path-based acquisition of disconnection.

### dhyānāntara

- **status:** previously encountered
- **function:** intermediate dhyāna; explicitly treated here like the fundamental dhyānas for parijñā-fruit classification

### ākāśānantyāyatana-sāmantaka

- **status:** technical concentration term
- **function:** preliminary concentration immediately preceding the sphere of infinite space; basis for the rūparāga-kṣaya-parijñā

## Structural Determination

The verse adds a new relation to the ninefold parijñā system:

```text
Parijñā
    ≠
Meditative basis
```

Instead:

```text
Meditative basis
    supports a path
        whose fruit is
            a determinate parijñā
```

The distribution is:

```text
anāgamya
    → 9

fundamental dhyānas
    → 5  [Vaibhāṣika]
    → 8  [Bhadanta Ghoṣaka]

dhyānāntara
    → treated like the dhyānas

ākāśānantyāyatana-sāmantaka
    → 1: rūparāga-kṣaya

three fundamental ārūpyas
    → 1: sarva-saṃyojana-paryādāna
```

The disagreement concerns **attribution of fruits to meditative bases**, not the existence or number of the nine parijñās themselves.

## Relation to VAK 5.63

Ghoṣaka's explanation is especially important in light of 5.63.

VAK 5.63 distinguished:

```text
one destruction of a kleśa
    from
repeated acquisition of disconnection
```

VAK 5.66 supplies a concrete application:

```text
prior freedom from desire-domain passion
    does not prevent
fresh undefiled visaṃyoga-prāpti
    through darśanamārga
```

Thus the eightfold Ghoṣaka count is intelligible only if:

```text
kṣaya
    ≠
visaṃyoga-prāpti-lābha
```

This is a strong internal confirmation of the distinction established in 5.63.

## Effect on Kārikā Translation

**Clarification only.**

The first-pass translation is substantially correct, but the Bhāṣya fixes the referents:

- `pañca` = the five parijñās whose nature is abandonment of form- and formless-domain afflictions, according to the Vaibhāṣikas.
- `aṣṭau` = Bhadanta Ghoṣaka's count, adding the three desire-domain seeing-abandonment parijñās.
- `sāmantakasyaikā` = specifically the preliminary concentration of the sphere of infinite space, yielding rūparāga-kṣaya-parijñā.
- `maulārūpya-trayasya` = the three fundamental formless attainments, each having the final sarva-saṃyojana-paryādāna-parijñā as fruit.

## Logical Determination

The central distinction is:

```text
attainment-content
    ≠
attainment-basis
```

and:

```text
higher meditative level
    ≠
necessarily wider range of parijñā-fruits
```

The widest range belongs to anāgamya, while increasingly determinate higher bases have narrower relevant fruit-sets.

The doctrinal dispute also gives a clean provenance rule:

```text
same parijñā ontology
    + different theory of path-basis attribution
    → different fruit count
```

No contradiction in the ninefold enumeration is required.

## Organon Contact Point

Minimal contact point only:

```text
A determination of achieved freedom
must be distinguished from
its operative basis of production.
```

The passage also confirms the importance of keeping **destruction**, **acquisition of disconnection**, and **basis of renewed acquisition** separate.

No broader Organon synthesis should be attempted before the planned clarifying pass.

## Bhāṣya Questions Resolved

1. **Why all nine for anāgamya?** The Bhāṣya states the assignment but does not here offer a separate abstract explanation beyond the path-fruit distribution.
2. **Which five for the dhyānas?** The five whose nature is abandonment of form- and formless-domain afflictions.
3. **Why eight for Ghoṣaka?** He counts the three desire-domain seeing-abandonments as fruits of darśanamārga even for a vītarāga, because an undefiled acquisition of disconnection is newly obtained.
4. **Why not nine for Ghoṣaka?** The lower-part-fetter abandonment parijñā remains exclusively anāgamya-phala.
5. **Dhyānāntara?** Treat it like the dhyānas.
6. **Which sāmantaka?** The preliminary concentration of ākāśānantyāyatana.
7. **What does it yield?** Rūparāga-kṣaya-parijñā.
8. **What do the three fundamental ārūpyas yield?** The single final sarva-saṃyojana-paryādāna-parijñā.
