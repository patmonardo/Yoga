## Literal Prose Translation

“This is called distancing. How many kinds of distancing are there? Distance is said to be fourfold.

Distance through difference of characteristic is like that among the great elements: even though they arise together, they are distant because their characteristics differ.

Distance through opposition is like misconduct in relation to discipline.

Distance through spatial separation is like places widely separated from one another, as the eastern and western oceans.

Temporal distance is such that the past and future are called distant.

... It would be more appropriate to say this: the future is distant from a dharma’s own characteristic because it has not yet attained it, and the past because it has fallen away from it.”

## Argument Reconstruction

### 1. Why discuss `dūratā` here?

The preceding verse distinguished the **dūrībhāva-pratipakṣa**, the counteragent that further distances what has already been abandoned. Vasubandhu therefore asks what “distance” itself can mean.

The answer is not one generic relation but four:

```text
1. vailakṣaṇya-dūratā
   distance by difference of characteristic

2. vipakṣa-dūratā
   distance by opposition

3. deśa-viccheda-dūratā
   distance by spatial separation

4. kāla-dūratā
   distance by temporal separation
```

### 2. The four examples

The Bhāṣya fixes the examples that were uncertain in the Kārikā pass:

```text
vailakṣaṇya
    mahābhūtas
    even when co-arisen, they remain distant through unlike characteristics

vipakṣatva
    śīla / dauḥśīlya
    discipline and misconduct are contraries

deśa-viccheda
    eastern / western ocean
    distance through spatial separation

kāla
    past / future
    temporal remoteness
```

### 3. Temporal distance is the difficult case

The Bhāṣya does not merely state that past and future are far from the present. It tests several proposed explanations.

#### Proposal: temporal paths are mutually distinct

> adhvanānātvena taddūraṃ na cirabhūtabhāvitvena

Past and future would be distant because they belong to different temporal courses, not because a long duration separates them.

**Objection:** by that reasoning even the present would be distant.

#### Proposal: distance through non-functioning

> akāritrāt tarhi taddūram

Perhaps past and future are distant because they lack present causal activity.

**Objection:** then how would the unconditioned be established as near?

#### Proposal: past and future are separated by the present

Past and future might be distant from one another because the present intervenes, while the present is near to both.

**Objection:** this makes past and future both near as well, since each is adjacent to the present.

### 4. Vasubandhu’s preferred resolution

He concludes:

```text
evaṃ tu yuktaṃ syāt
```

“It would be appropriate in this way.”

The relation is to the dharma’s own characteristic:

```text
future
    distant because
    its own characteristic has not yet been attained

past
    distant because
    it has fallen away from that characteristic
```

Thus temporal remoteness is not finally explained by clock-time, mere succession, or spatial analogy.

## Voice Identification

**Voice: Vasubandhu** — introduces the fourfold division and gives the four examples.

**Voice: Objector / dialectical testing** — the objections to successive accounts of temporal remoteness are embedded in the rapid question-answer sequence.

**Voice: Vasubandhu** — `evaṃ tu yuktaṃ syāt` marks the preferred final formulation.

## Doctrinal Determination

The verse does not reduce `dūrībhāva` to spatial remoteness. The Bhāṣya explicitly distinguishes four heterogeneous relations.

```text
non-identity of characteristic
    ≠
contrariety
    ≠
spatial interval
    ≠
temporal remoteness
```

The first two are especially important for the doctrine of counteragents:

```text
vipakṣa
    is not merely something different
    but something opposed
```

Thus a counteragent can be “far” from an affliction not because it exists elsewhere, but because it stands in a contrary determination.

The temporal analysis is subtler. Vasubandhu refuses to define past and future merely by quantitative temporal distance. Their remoteness is finally indexed to the relation of a dharma to its own characteristic:

```text
future
    not yet reached

past
    fallen away
```

## Effect on Kārikā Translation

**Translation should be clarified.**

The first-pass phrase “the two pairs involving element and conduct, and region and temporal course” was too compressed.

Preferred translation:

> **Remoteness is fourfold: through difference of characteristic, through opposition, through spatial separation, and through time—as with the great elements, discipline and misconduct, distant regions, and the past and future.**

The Bhāṣya establishes the examples explicitly.

## Logical Determination

The verse is a classification of a relation according to the **ground of separation**.

```text
Distance(x,y)
    may be grounded in

CharacteristicDifference(x,y)
Opposition(x,y)
SpatialSeparation(x,y)
TemporalStatus(x,y)
```

The crucial logical result is:

```text
difference
    ≠
opposition
```

The four great elements can be co-present and still distant through difference of mark. Discipline and misconduct are distant in a stronger sense: they stand as contraries.

The temporal dialectic adds a second result:

```text
temporal remoteness
    is not adequately explained by
mere elapsed duration
or mere non-presence
```

It is relationally determined through a dharma’s status with respect to its own characteristic.

## Organon Contact Point

For now the contact point should remain minimal:

```text
“Distance” is relation-indexed.
```

A single predicate of remoteness can arise from radically different grounds:

```text
qualitative difference
contrariety
place
time
```

The later clarifying pass should therefore resist collapsing every form of “otherness” into one relation.
