## Literal Prose Translation

**Voice: Vasubandhu / inherited Abhidharma exposition**

These eleven anuśayas have been stated to be universal within a homogeneous domain.

> Of these, nine have higher domains as cognitive support, excluding the two views.

Excluding satkāyadṛṣṭi and antagrāhadṛṣṭi, the other nine anuśayas are universal with respect to a different domain. Sometimes they take one different domain as cognitive support, sometimes two.

**Voice: Objector**

When someone situated in the desire domain produces, with respect to Brahmā, the view that he is a being and the view that he is permanent, how can satkāyadṛṣṭi and antagrāhadṛṣṭi not have a different domain as their cognitive support?

**Voice: Abhidharmikas**

Because Brahmā is not apprehended as self or belonging to self; and antagrāhadṛṣṭi arises from such self-appropriation.

**Voice: Objector**

Then what kind of view is this?

**Voice: Abhidharmikas**

It is not a dṛṣṭi; rather, it is false knowledge (`mithyājñāna`).

**Voice: Vasubandhu**

But how is it established that another cognition having that as its cognitive support is a dṛṣṭi, while this one is not a dṛṣṭi? The doctrine must be demonstrated.

Are only the anuśayas themselves universal? No:

> Whatever dharmas arise together with these are also universal, except acquisition.

Whatever other dharmas arise together with universal anuśayas are also universal. Acquisitions, however, are not so, because they have many kinds of result.

For this reason a fourfold analysis can be made between what is a universal anuśaya and what is a cause by the universal-cause relation. Future universal anuśayas furnish the first case; past and present co-arisen dharmas furnish the second.

## Course of the Argument

VAK 5.13 first corrects the possible misunderstanding that `sarvatraga` is confined to one's own domain.

The eleven universal anuśayas from 5.12 divide as follows:

```text
11 sarvatraga anuśayas

2 excluded from upward/different-domain object-range:
    satkāyadṛṣṭi
    antagrāhadṛṣṭi

9 remaining:
    can take a different or higher domain as ālambana
```

The Bhāṣya is explicit:

```text
anye navānuśayāḥ
    visabhāgadhātu-sarvatragāḥ
```

The other nine are universal with respect to a heterogeneous domain.

This is a genuine correction to the first Kārikā pass, which had read the first pāda in the opposite direction.

---

## Why the Two Views Are Exceptional

The inherited explanation is relational rather than merely cosmological.

`satkāyadṛṣṭi` requires apprehension under:

```text
ātman
or
ātmīya
```

self or belonging-to-self.

`antagrāhadṛṣṭi` in turn depends upon that appropriation, because its eternalist or annihilationist extreme concerns what has first been established under the self-relation.

Hence the Abhidharmika position:

```text
no self/mine appropriation of the higher-domain object
    ↓
no satkāyadṛṣṭi there
    ↓
no antagrāhadṛṣṭi generated from it
```

---

## Vasubandhu's Critical Intervention

The Brahmā example forces a problem.

A person in the desire domain may cognize Brahmā as:

```text
a being
and
permanent
```

The Abhidharmikas refuse to classify this as the two exceptional views. They call it `mithyājñāna`, false knowledge, rather than `dṛṣṭi`.

Vasubandhu does not simply accept the distinction. He asks, in effect:

```text
Why is one cognition with that ālambana called dṛṣṭi,
while this cognition is denied that status?
```

His conclusion is methodological:

```text
siddhāntas tu pramāṇayitavyaḥ
    the doctrinal thesis must be demonstrated
```

This is an important Bhāṣya moment. The inherited taxonomy is not protected from demand for proof merely because it is traditional.

---

## Co-Arisen Universality

The second half asks whether only the anuśayas themselves are `sarvatraga`.

The answer is no.

```text
sarvatraga anuśaya
    + sahabhu dharmas
        → those sahabhu dharmas are also sarvatraga
```

Thus universality characterizes the afflicted event-complex, not merely one isolated factor.

But one item is excluded:

```text
prāpti
    acquisition
```

The Bhāṣya explains the exclusion with:

```text
anekaphalatvāt
    because of having many results / heterogeneous fruition
```

So functional co-arising transmits the universal classification, whereas acquisition does not simply inherit it.

---

## Sarvatraga Anuśaya vs Sarvatraga-Hetu

The prose immediately distinguishes two relations that might otherwise be conflated:

```text
being a sarvatraga anuśaya

vs

being a cause through the sarvatraga-hetu relation
```

The Bhāṣya therefore introduces a `catuṣkoṭika`, a four-corner analysis.

The first two corners are stated here:

```text
future universal anuśayas
    sarvatraga anuśaya
    but not functioning as sarvatraga-hetu

past/present co-arisen dharmas
    can function in the universal causal relation
    without themselves being anuśayas
```

The classification of a dharma and its causal function are therefore distinct axes.

## Voice Identification

- **Voice: Vasubandhu / exposition** — initial elevenfold and ninefold classification.
- **Voice: Objector** — Brahmā counterexample and follow-up question.
- **Voice: Abhidharmikas** — denial that the Brahmā cognition is dṛṣṭi; classification as `mithyājñāna`.
- **Voice: Vasubandhu** — explicit demand that the doctrinal distinction be demonstrated.

This is one of the clearer local examples where Vasubandhu distances himself from an inherited Abhidharma assertion.

---

## New Technical Terms

- **visabhāgadhātu** — a different or heterogeneous domain
  - **status:** technically decisive in the Bhāṣya
  - **function:** specifies the upward/cross-domain cognitive range of nine universal anuśayas

- **ūrdhvālambana** — having a higher domain as cognitive support
  - **status:** Kārikā term clarified by prose
  - **function:** marks cross-domain object-range

- **mithyājñāna** — false knowledge
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only distinction here
  - **function:** Abhidharmika category used to avoid classifying the Brahmā cognition as one of the two exceptional dṛṣṭis

- **siddhānta** — established doctrine / doctrinal thesis
  - **status:** Bhāṣya argumentative vocabulary
  - **function:** object of Vasubandhu's demand for demonstration

- **pramāṇayitavya** — to be demonstrated or established by a means of knowing
  - **status:** Bhāṣya argumentative vocabulary
  - **function:** methodological demand placed upon the inherited thesis

- **sahabhu** — co-arisen dharma
  - **status:** Kārikā term technically explained
  - **function:** dharmas sharing the universal status through co-arising

- **anekaphalatva** — having many or heterogeneous results
  - **status:** Bhāṣya explanation
  - **function:** reason `prāpti` does not inherit sarvatraga status

- **sarvatragahetu** — universal cause relation
  - **status:** Bhāṣya technical distinction
  - **function:** separates affliction-type from causal function

- **catuṣkoṭika** — four-cornered analysis
  - **status:** Bhāṣya logical procedure
  - **function:** tests overlap and non-identity between two classifications

## Doctrinal Determination

VAK 5.13 gives `sarvatraga` a more precise structure than the first pass could recover:

```text
universality has at least three dimensions

1. same-domain range
2. different/higher-domain object-range
3. shared status of the co-arisen complex
```

But each dimension is constrained.

```text
satkāyadṛṣṭi + antagrāhadṛṣṭi
    excluded from different-domain range

prāpti
    excluded from co-arisen inheritance
```

Most importantly, Vasubandhu exposes a weakness in the inherited explanation concerning the two exceptional views and demands proof rather than mere doctrinal assertion.

---

## Effect on the Kārikā Translation

**Translation should be revised.**

The first-pass translation reversed the force of the first pāda.

It had effectively read:

> those excluding the two views do not have higher levels as objects.

The Bhāṣya shows the opposite:

> **Nine of these, excluding the two views, can take higher/different domains as cognitive support.**

A corrected translation is:

> Of these universal anuśayas, nine—excluding satkāyadṛṣṭi and antagrāhadṛṣṭi—can take higher domains as their cognitive support. Whatever dharmas arise together with these universal anuśayas are themselves universal, except acquisition.

---

## Logical Determination

The verse distinguishes extension from unrestricted universality.

```text
sarvatraga
    ≠ without boundaries
```

Rather, universality is generated through determinate relations:

```text
same-domain reach
cross-domain ālambana
co-arisen participation
causal function
```

These relations do not coincide automatically.

The `catuṣkoṭika` makes that explicit by refusing the inference:

```text
sarvatraga anuśaya
    =
sarvatraga-hetu
```

This is a strong instance of Abhidharma logical discipline: classification by what something is must be distinguished from classification by the causal relation in which it functions.

---

## Light Organon Contact Point

The strongest contact point is methodological rather than architectural.

Vasubandhu's challenge can be stated generally:

```text
a classification distinction
cannot be protected merely by naming it;
its differentiating rule must be demonstrable
```

And the catuṣkoṭika supplies a second principle:

```text
entity type
    and
functional relation
must not be conflated
```

Both principles can later be valuable for a Rational Kośa formalization, but the primary result here remains textual and doctrinal.

---

## Bhāṣya Pass Result

VAK 5.13 materially corrects the Kārikā pass:

> **Of the eleven universal anuśayas, nine can take a higher or different domain as cognitive support; satkāyadṛṣṭi and antagrāhadṛṣṭi are excluded. The Abhidharmikas explain the exclusion through the self/mine structure of the first and the dependence of the second upon it, but Vasubandhu explicitly demands demonstration of their further claim that an analogous cognition of Brahmā is merely false knowledge rather than dṛṣṭi. Co-arisen dharmas share the universal status, except acquisition, and universal affliction must be distinguished from the universal-cause relation.**
