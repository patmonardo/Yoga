# VAK 5.37 — Bhāṣya

## Kārikā Anchor

**VAK 5.37**

> tathaughayogā dṛṣṭīnāṃ pṛthagbhāvas tu pāṭavāt |
> nāsraveṣv asahāyānāṃ na kilāsyānukūlatā || 5.37 ||

Best current translation:

> Likewise for the floods and yokes; views, however, are set apart because of their sharpness. But among the influxes they are not separately established, since when unaided they are not suited to the function of seeping/settling.

## Translation

### Literal

Likewise are the floods and the yokes. But the views have separate status because of their sharpness.

The desire-influx itself is the desire-flood and the desire-yoke. Likewise the existence-influx itself is the existence-flood and existence-yoke, except for the views. These, it is said, are established separately among the floods and yokes because of their sharpness.

But among the influxes they are not [established separately], because when unaided they are not suited to its [specific] function.

The derivation of *āsrava* as “that which makes [beings/dharmas] settle or remain” will be explained later. Views alone, because of their sharpness, are not suited to this settling function. Therefore they are not separately established among the influxes; they are included there in mixture with the others.

Thus the desire-flood consists of twenty-nine real items: fifteen instances of craving, hostility, and conceit; four doubts; and ten envelopments. The existence-flood consists of twenty-eight real items: twenty instances of craving and conceit, and eight doubts. The view-flood consists of thirty-six real items. The ignorance-flood consists of fifteen real items. The yokes are to be understood exactly like the floods.

### Philosophical

The same afflictive inventory is repartitioned according to the operation being analyzed. As influxes, views are mixed into the domain-based currents because their own sharp, penetrating character does not independently perform the slow “seeping/settling” function associated with an āsrava. As floods and yokes, however, that very sharpness becomes decisive: views deserve a separate class because they overwhelm and fasten the continuum with exceptional force.

The classification therefore changes not because the underlying dharmas have changed, but because the operative question has changed.

## Course of Argument

### 1. The same basic material is reused

**Voice: Vaibhāṣika position / expository voice**

The Bhāṣya begins from the āsrava classes of 5.35–5.36 and says:

```text
kāmāsrava
    → kāmaugha
    → kāmayoga

bhavāsrava
    → bhavaugha
    → bhavayoga
```

But there is one major alteration: **dṛṣṭi is extracted and given an independent class** in the flood and yoke systems.

### 2. Why views are separated

**Voice: expository Vaibhāṣika position**

The reason is `paṭutva` / `pāṭava`: sharpness, acuteness, penetrating force.

```text
dṛṣṭi
    unusually sharp
    → distinctive power as flood
    → distinctive power as yoke
```

This confirms the first-pass insight that the classification is functional. The same views that are not a separate āsrava become a separate ogha and yoga because the relevant operation is different.

### 3. Why views are not separately counted as an āsrava

The compressed pāda `nāsraveṣv asahāyānāṃ na kilāsyānukūlatā` is explained explicitly:

> `na ca kila kevalā dṛṣṭya āsyānukūlāḥ paṭutvāt`

Views **by themselves** (`kevalāḥ`, clarifying `asahāyānām`) are not suited to the `āsya`-function because of their sharpness.

The Bhāṣya promises the etymological explanation later:

> `āsayantīty āsravāṇāṃ nirvacanaṃ paścād vakṣyate`

The key contrast is therefore:

```text
sharp / penetrating
    → suitable for flooding and yoking

slowly seeping / settling / sustaining
    → defining tendency of āsrava
```

Because views alone do not fit the latter function, they are not a separate influx-class. They are `miśrīkṛtya sthāpitāḥ`, placed there mixed together with the other afflictions.

### 4. Exact flood counts

The Bhāṣya then gives the complete fourfold ogha inventory.

#### kāmaugha — 29 dravyas

```text
rāga + pratigha + māna = 15
vicikitsā = 4
paryavasthāna = 10
------------------------
total = 29
```

Views and ignorance have been removed to their own flood classes.

#### bhavaugha — 28 dravyas

```text
rāga + māna = 20
vicikitsā = 8
----------------
total = 28
```

Again, views and ignorance are separated.

#### dṛṣṭyogha — 36 dravyas

All thirty-six views across the three domains constitute the view-flood.

#### avidyaugha — 15 dravyas

The fifteen ignorance-types across the three domains constitute the ignorance-flood.

### 5. Yokes reproduce the same partition

The Bhāṣya closes:

> `oghavad yogā veditavyāḥ`

The yokes are to be understood exactly like the floods.

Thus the same numerical partition applies:

```text
kāmayoga = 29
bhavayoga = 28
dṛṣṭiyoga = 36
avidyāyoga = 15
```

## Voice Map

- **Voice: Vaibhāṣika position / expository voice** — classification of āsrava, ogha, and yoga and their numerical extensions.
- **Voice: Vasubandhu** — no explicit polemical rejection here; the passage presents the received classification and its rationale.
- **Voice: uncertain** — the repeated `kila` marks received doctrinal explanation, so the degree of Vasubandhu's personal endorsement should not be overstated.

## New Technical Terms

- **ogha** — flood; an afflictive grouping according to the power of overwhelming/carrying away.
  - **status:** newly prominent in this block.
  - **function:** functional reclassification of anuśayas.

- **yoga** — yoke/bond; an afflictive grouping according to the power of fastening/binding.
  - **status:** newly prominent in this block.
  - **function:** functional reclassification of the same afflictive material.

- **paṭutva / pāṭava** — sharpness, acuteness, penetrating force.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya explanation of why views form independent flood/yoke classes.
  - **function:** classificatory ground.

- **asahāya / kevala** — unaided, alone, by itself.
  - **status:** resolves the compressed Kārikā wording.
  - **function:** specifies that views considered independently fail to perform the defining āsrava-function.

- **āsyānukūla** — suited to the “settling/seeping” operation associated with the derivation of `āsrava`.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only explanatory vocabulary here.
  - **function:** criterion for independent influx-status.

- **miśrīkṛtya sthāpita** — established in mixture, included together with others rather than separately.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya explanatory phrase.
  - **function:** explains how views remain within āsrava despite lacking separate āsrava status.

## Reflective Vocabulary

### Same extension, different functional partition

The passage gives a particularly clear example of one determinate field receiving different classifications according to different powers.

```text
same afflictive dharmas
    ↓
āsrava standpoint: sustaining / seeping current
ogha standpoint: overwhelming flood
yoga standpoint: fastening bond
```

No new substances are introduced. What changes is the **relation under which the same determinations are grasped**.

### Sharpness is not universally advantageous to classification

The same property, `paṭutva`, produces opposite classificatory consequences:

```text
paṭutva
    → separate dṛṣṭyogha
    → separate dṛṣṭiyoga

but

paṭutva
    → no separate dṛṣṭyāsrava
```

This is important. A property does not determine classification in isolation; its significance depends on the operative universal under consideration.

## Doctrinal Determination

The four flood classes are:

```text
kāmaugha      29
bhavaugha     28
dṛṣṭyogha     36
avidyaugha    15
```

The four yokes have the same extension.

Views are separately classified among floods and yokes because their sharpness gives them an independent overwhelming and binding power. They are not separately classified among influxes because, considered by themselves, their sharpness makes them unsuitable to the gradual settling/seeping function implied by `āsrava`.

Thus:

```text
independent functional role in one taxonomy
    ≠
independent functional role in another taxonomy
```

## Effect on Kārikā Translation

**Translation should be revised / clarified.**

The first-pass treatment correctly recognized the functional reclassification, but the Bhāṣya settles the difficult second half more precisely:

- `asahāyānām` = **when alone / unaided**, clarified by `kevalā dṛṣṭayaḥ`.
- `asya` refers to the relevant **āsrava-function**, not broadly to “support.”
- `anukūlatā` is suitability to the `āsya` or settling/seeping function indicated by the forthcoming etymology of āsrava.

A better philosophical translation is:

> Likewise for the floods and yokes; views, however, are set apart because of their sharpness. But among the influxes they are not separately established, since by themselves they are not suited to the settling/seeping function of an influx.

## Logical Determination

This verse establishes a strong rule:

```text
DharmaIdentity
    ≠
FunctionalRole
```

and more specifically:

```text
property P
    has classificatory force
    only relative to operation O
```

The same `paṭutva` can therefore justify both separation and non-separation depending upon the target operation.

This is a determinate, relation-sensitive taxonomy rather than a flat ontology.

## Organon Contact Point

This is directly useful for the project's relation architecture.

A single determination can occupy multiple functional roles without being duplicated ontologically:

```text
View
    sustainsStream only derivatively / in mixture
    overwhelmsStream independently
    bindsStream independently
```

So relation-types must be first-class and cannot be inferred merely from node identity or class membership.

The deeper principle is:

> **One determination can have different explanatory powers under different relations.**

This should be kept distinct from saying that one thing “has many arbitrary labels.” The Bhāṣya supplies a ground for each partition.
