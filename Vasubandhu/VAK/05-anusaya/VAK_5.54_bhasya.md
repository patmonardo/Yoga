# VAK 5.54 Bhāṣya

## Kārikā Anchor

**VAK 5.54**

```text
samānasiddhā dṛggheyā manovijñānabhūmikāḥ /
upakleśāḥ svatantrāś ca ṣaḍvijñānāśrayāḥ pare // 5.54 //
```

**Working translation:** Those to be abandoned by seeing, together with the two cultivation-abandonable cases indicated by the transmitted phrase, belong to the plane of manovijñāna. Independent upakleśas likewise are mental-only; the others have the six consciousness-groups as their basis.

See: [VAK_5.54.md](./VAK_5.54.md)

## Source Caution

The transmitted e-text reads **samānasiddhā** in the Kārikā and **saha mānasiddhābhyāṃ** in the prose. The prose clearly indicates two additional bhāvanā-prahātavya cases accompanying the darśana-prahātavya group, but the exact transmitted compound is textually opaque in the present non-critical source. It should not be silently normalized here.

## Translation

The question is now asked: among these anuśayas, how many belong to the mental plane, and how many belong to the six consciousness-groups? In brief:

All those to be abandoned by seeing are mental-plane determinations. Together with them, the text includes two cultivation-abandonable cases designated by the transmitted phrase `mānasiddhābhyām`; these too belong wholly to the mental plane.

Likewise, whatever upakleśas are independent are to be understood as belonging only to the mental plane, even though they are themselves abandoned through cultivation.

The remaining kleśas and upakleśas belong to the six consciousness-groups. Which are these remaining ones? The cultivation-abandonable rāga, pratigha, and avidyā, together with the upakleśas associated with them: āhrīkya, anapatrāpya, styāna, auddhatya, and those already taught among the kleśa-mahābhūmikas.

## Course of Argument

### 1. A new classificatory question

The preceding block classified kleśas and upakleśas by domain, ethical quality, derivation, and mode of abandonment. VAK 5.54 changes axis again:

```text
Which afflictions are manobhūmika?
Which are ṣaḍvijñānakāyika?
```

This is not another statement about origin. It concerns the **cognitive field in which the affliction can occur**.

### 2. All darśana-prahātavya afflictions are mental-only

**Voice: Vasubandhu / Abhidharma exposition**

The prose is categorical:

```text
darśanaprahātavyāḥ sarve manobhūmikāḥ
```

All afflictions abandoned by seeing belong to the mental plane.

This is doctrinally intelligible because the seeing-abandonable afflictions involve discriminative, conceptual, or doctrinal determinations rather than merely sensory contact.

The point should nevertheless be stated narrowly:

```text
darśana-prahātavya
    → manobhūmika
```

The Bhāṣya does not here offer a philosophical theory of judgment; it gives a taxonomic distribution.

### 3. Two additional cultivation-abandonable cases are also mental-only

The prose adds:

```text
saha mānasiddhābhyāṃ bhāvanāheyābhyām api
```

The exact lexical form naming these two is unclear in the transmitted e-text. What is secure is:

```text
two bhāvanā-heya cases
    +
all darśana-heya cases
        ↓
manobhūmika only
```

The file therefore preserves the uncertainty rather than importing a normalization from memory.

### 4. Independent upakleśas are likewise mental-only

The Bhāṣya next states:

```text
ye ca kecid upakleśāḥ svatantrāḥ
...
manobhūmikā eva draṣṭavyāḥ
```

Whatever upakleśas are **svatantra** are to be regarded as mental-plane only, even though they are abandoned by cultivation.

This is important because it shows that:

```text
bhāvanā-heya
    does not entail
six-consciousness support
```

Mode of abandonment and cognitive support are independent classificatory axes.

### 5. The remaining kleśas and upakleśas are six-consciousness based

The text then asks what the `pare`, the remaining cases, are.

It explicitly names:

```text
bhāvanā-prahātavya rāga
bhāvanā-prahātavya pratigha
bhāvanā-prahātavya avidyā
```

plus the upakleśas associated with them:

```text
āhrīkya
anapatrāpya
styāna
auddhatya
```

and the factors previously included among the **kleśa-mahābhūmikas**.

These are:

```text
ṣaḍvijñānabhūmika
```

They can arise on the basis of the six consciousness-groups rather than being restricted to manovijñāna.

## New Technical Terms

- **manobhūmika / manovijñānabhūmika** — belonging to the mental plane / plane of mental consciousness.
  - **status:** known vocabulary, newly used here as a classificatory axis for affliction.
  - **function:** cognitive-support classification.

- **ṣaḍvijñānakāyika / ṣaḍvijñānabhūmika** — belonging to the six consciousness-groups.
  - **status:** new local classificatory expression.
  - **function:** distinguishes afflictions capable of occurring with sensory as well as mental consciousness.

- **svatantra upakleśa** — independent upakleśa.
  - **status:** previously introduced; here assigned a definite cognitive range.
  - **function:** mental-only derivative affliction despite being bhāvanā-heya.

- **kleśa-mahābhūmika** — a factor belonging to the set universally associated with afflicted mind.
  - **status:** established earlier in Chapter 2.
  - **function:** identifies further six-consciousness-supported upakleśas.

## Doctrinal Determination

VAK 5.54 establishes another independent coordinate in the affliction map:

```text
cognitive support
```

The principal distinction is:

```text
Mental-only
    all darśana-prahātavya afflictions
    two additional bhāvanā-heya cases named unclearly in the transmitted text
    independent upakleśas

Six-consciousness based
    remaining kleśas and upakleśas
    including bhāvanā-heya rāga, pratigha, avidyā
```

The crucial control is that **support is not origin**.

```text
an affliction may arise with visual consciousness
without visible form being its genetic root
```

Likewise, **support is not abandonment-mode**:

```text
some bhāvanā-heya factors are mental-only
others are six-consciousness based
```

This confirms the increasingly multi-axial character of the chapter.

## Effect on Kārikā Translation

**Translation should be revised / source caution required.**

The first-pass translation treated `samānasiddhā` too confidently as a general phrase meaning “similarly established.” The Bhāṣya prose instead indicates a specific pair of additional cultivation-abandonable cases, but the transmitted e-text does not securely preserve their names in a form that should be silently normalized.

The safe translation is therefore deliberately restrained:

> Those to be abandoned by seeing, together with the two cultivation-abandonable cases indicated in the transmitted text, belong to the plane of manovijñāna. Independent upakleśas likewise are mental-only; the others are based in the six consciousness-groups.

## Logical Determination

VAK 5.54 demonstrates that one determination can carry several independent predicates:

```text
Affliction(x)
    hasAbandonmentMode(x)
    hasDomainRange(x)
    hasEthicalStatus(x)
    hasCognitiveSupport(x)
```

None of these relations can simply be derived from the others.

For example:

```text
bhāvanā-heya(x)
    ↛ ṣaḍvijñāna-supported(x)
```

because some cultivation-abandonable upakleśas are explicitly mental-only.

Likewise:

```text
manobhūmika(x)
    does not itself state
origin(x)
```

The verse therefore continues the chapter's general lesson:

```text
one dharma
    can require
several independent relational determinations
for complete specification
```

## Organon Contact Point

Keep the Organon contact minimal at this stage.

The useful result is simply:

```text
classification by cognitive support
    ≠

    ≠

```

This should be retained for the later clarifying pass, where the chapter can be reconstructed as a relational matrix before any synthetic Organon commentary is attempted.
