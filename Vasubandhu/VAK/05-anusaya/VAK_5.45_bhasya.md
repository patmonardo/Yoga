## Kārikā Anchor

**VAK 5.45**

```text

auddhatyamānamohāś ca vidvaśād bandhanatrayam // 5.45 //
```

**Current first-pass translation:** The higher-group fetters are precisely fivefold: the two attachments arising with respect to form and the formless, and the triad of bonds—restlessness, conceit, and delusion.

**Kārikā analysis:** [VAK_5.45.md](./VAK_5.45.md)

> **Bhāṣya control:** the first-pass translation must be revised. The Bhāṣya closes the five higher fetters after `auddhatya, māna, avidyā`; `bandhanatrayam` then begins a new classification, whose three members are rāga, dveṣa, and moha.

## Source Caution


but the prose immediately explains the triad through the **three feelings** (`trivedanā...`). The transmitted form is therefore textually suspect. It should not be silently emended in the repository without checking a critical or printed Sanskrit edition.

For this Bhāṣya pass we preserve the transmitted Kārikā reading while recording the semantic control supplied by the prose:

```text
bandhana-traya
    is determined by its relation to
three vedanās
```

## Translation

Just as the Blessed One taught five fetters belonging to the lower division, so again there are precisely five belonging to the higher division.

How so? There are the two attachments arising in relation to form and the formless, together with restlessness, conceit, and ignorance. These are the five higher fetters: attachment to form, attachment to the formless, restlessness, conceit, and ignorance. Because these have not been abandoned, one does not pass beyond the higher domains.

This concludes the discussion of fetters.

What are the bonds? There are three bonds: all desire is a bond, all hostility is a bond, and all delusion is a bond.

Why did the Blessed One teach precisely these three as bonds?

The bonds operate in dependence upon the three kinds of feeling. With respect to pleasant feeling, desire lies latent through object and association; with respect to painful feeling, hostility; with respect to neither-painful-nor-pleasant feeling, delusion, while desire and hostility do not operate there in the same way. Or the restriction may be understood with respect to an object belonging to one's own continuum.

The anuśayas have already been explained. The secondary afflictions are now to be stated. All kleśas are also upakleśas insofar as they afflict the mind.

## Course of Argument

**Voice: Vasubandhu / reported scriptural-Abhidharma classification**

### 1. Completion of the higher-fetter classification

The first half of the verse completes the opposition begun in VAK 5.43:

```text
five lower fetters
    ↕
five higher fetters
```

The higher five are explicitly enumerated as:

```text
rūparāga
ārūpyarāga
auddhatya
māna
avidyā
```

Their common function is equally explicit:

```text
eṣām aprahāṇena
    because these are not abandoned

ūrdhvadhātu-anatikrama
    there is no passing beyond the higher domains
```

Thus `ūrdhvabhāgīya` is not merely a descriptive label for refined mental states. These are the fetters whose non-abandonment retains the stream within the higher domains.

The Bhāṣya then marks a hard boundary:

```text
samāptaḥ saṃyojanaprasaṅgaḥ
    the discussion of fetters is complete
```

This sentence governs the syntax of the second half of the Kārikā.

### 2. `bandhanatrayam` begins a new taxonomy

The Bhāṣya asks:

```text
bandhanāni katamāni?
    what are the bonds?
```

and answers:

```text
rāga
    all desire is a bond

dveṣa
    all hostility is a bond

moha
    all delusion is a bond
```

This is the decisive correction to the first-pass analysis.

The Kārikā file had construed:

```text
auddhatya + māna + moha
    = bandhana-traya
```

But the Bhāṣya explicitly separates the classifications:

```text
auddhatya + māna + avidyā
    complete the five higher saṃyojanas

rāga + dveṣa + moha
    constitute the three bandhanas
```

The verse therefore crosses a classificatory boundary internally.

### 3. Why exactly three bonds?

The question is not why these are afflictions; that is already known. It is why these three, specifically, receive the designation `bandhana`.

The answer relates them to the three kinds of feeling:

```text
pleasant feeling
    → rāga lies latent

painful feeling
    → dveṣa lies latent

neither-painful-nor-pleasant feeling
    → moha lies latent
```

For pleasant feeling, Vasubandhu specifies the two modes already familiar from the anuśaya analysis:

```text
ālambana
    through taking the feeling as object

saṃprayoga
    through association with it
```

The resulting bond-triad is therefore not an arbitrary list. It maps the afflictive field onto the complete threefold field of feeling.

### 4. Transition onward

The prose closes this unit rapidly:

```text
anuśayāḥ pūrvam evoktāḥ
    the anuśayas have already been explained
```

and then opens the next category:

```text
upakleśāḥ vaktavyāḥ
    the secondary afflictions must now be stated
```

Thus VAK 5.45 is structurally a hinge:

```text
saṃyojana
    completed
        ↓
bandhana
    defined
        ↓
anuśaya
    already treated
        ↓
upakleśa
    next topic
```

## New Technical Terms

- **ūrdhvadhātu-anatikrama** — failure to pass beyond the higher domains.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya explanatory compound.
  - **function:** gives the functional criterion of the five higher fetters.

- **samāptaḥ saṃyojanaprasaṅgaḥ** — “the discussion of fetters is complete.”
  - **status:** Bhāṣya discourse marker.
  - **function:** establishes the classificatory boundary inside VAK 5.45.

- **bandhana** — bond; here a new classification of rāga, dveṣa, and moha.
  - **status:** Kārikā category made explicit by Bhāṣya.
  - **function:** taxonomic reclassification of affliction.

- **trivedanā-vaśa-vartin** — operating under / in dependence upon the three feelings; the e-text is imperfect but this is the clear force of the prose.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya explanatory vocabulary; transmitted text uncertain.
  - **function:** ground for the threefold bond classification.

- **svāsāṃtānika-ālambana** — an object belonging to one's own continuum.
  - **status:** Bhāṣya-only qualification in this passage.
  - **function:** alternative explanation of the restriction (`niyama`).

## Doctrinal Determination

VAK 5.45 contains two different systems of classification.

First:

```text
saṃyojana
    five higher fetters
    classified by retention in higher domains
```

Second:

```text
bandhana
    rāga / dveṣa / moha
    classified through the threefold field of feeling
```

The same broad afflictive material is therefore reorganized according to different relations:

```text
realm-retention
    versus
feeling-affliction relation
```

This reinforces the lesson of VAK 5.41: classifications in the Kośa are relation-indexed. They are not merely alternative names for an invariant extensional list.

The three bonds achieve a special comprehensiveness because the three vedanās exhaust the basic hedonic field:

```text
sukha
    pleasant

duḥkha
    painful

aduḥkhāsukha
    neither-painful-nor-pleasant
```

Correspondingly:

```text
rāga
    appropriation of the pleasant

dveṣa
    rejection of the painful

moha
    non-discrimination in the neutral field
```

The last formulation is an interpretive compression of the Bhāṣya's mapping, not a separate doctrinal assertion.

## Effect on the Kārikā Translation

**Translation should be revised.**

The first-pass translation incorrectly made restlessness, conceit, and delusion the `bandhana-traya`.

Preferred provisional translation, preserving the textual uncertainty of `vidvaśād`:

> The higher-part fetters likewise are precisely five: the two attachments born of the form and formless domains, together with restlessness, conceit, and ignorance. [Then, according to the relation governed by feeling,] there is a triad of bonds.

The Bhāṣya requires us to understand the second statement as opening a new classification:

```text
three bonds
    rāga
    dveṣa
    moha
```

A final reconstruction of the corrupt/suspect `vidvaśād` should wait for direct comparison with the printed Sanskrit witnesses.

## Logical Determination

The strongest logical feature of this passage is the **internal change of classificatory principle**.

The first half asks:

```text
What prevents transcendence of the higher domains?
```

and yields:

```text
five higher fetters
```

The second half asks:

```text
What three afflictions bind in relation to the complete field of feeling?
```

and yields:

```text
three bonds
```

Formally:

```text
same afflictive field
    + different relation
        → different valid partition
```

The verse boundary does not coincide with the classificatory boundary. The Bhāṣya is required to disclose that structure.

The feeling relation is particularly clean:

```text
VedanāField = {pleasant, painful, neutral}

BondField = {rāga, dveṣa, moha}
```

with a determinate correspondence between the two.

This is not identity but mediation:

```text
feeling-type
    conditions the mode in which affliction binds
```

## Organon Contact Point

The passage supplies a strong rule for the Rational Kośa architecture:

```text
Do not infer taxonomy from adjacency in a sentence or verse.
Infer taxonomy from the explicit relation that generates membership.
```

VAK 5.45 is almost a perfect test case. Surface syntax tempts us to group:

```text
auddhatya + māna + moha
```

under `bandhana-traya`.

The reflective prose interrupts that reading and regenerates the structure correctly:

```text
HigherFetter(x)
    iff x participates in retention within the higher domains

Bond(x)
    iff x belongs to the rāga/dveṣa/moha triad mapped across feeling
```

This is exactly why the Bhāṣya pass cannot be reduced to annotation. It changes the articulation of the determinate proposition by exposing a boundary hidden in the compressed Kārikā.
