# The DharmaCakra of Mathematics

Math is a Dharma. A result belongs to Mathematics only when its form, permitted operations, proof, range, and Principle can be exhibited together. A solved problem is one turn of this wheel. The DharmaCakra presents the power by which such turns are possible and by which their limits become intelligible.

## Work backward from a result

Consider the Pythagorean relation for a right triangle:

```text
a² + b² = c²
```

Its proof is already more than a numerical output. It presupposes a right angle, lengths measured under one Euclidean metric, a construction of squares, and operations preserving equality. Alter the metric or remove the right-angle condition and the same displayed formula no longer states the same theorem. The result therefore sends us backward:

One proof arranges four congruent right triangles inside a square of side *a + b*, leaving a central square of side *c*. Equality of areas gives

```text
(a + b)² = 4(ab/2) + c²
a² + 2ab + b² = 2ab + c²
a² + b² = c²
```

The arrangement, congruence, area measure, and algebraic cancellation each have to be licensed. The calculation displays a conclusion; the proof shows why this construction licenses it.

```text
result
  ← proof preserving equality
  ← permitted constructions and transformations
  ← measurement and orthogonality in a specified Form Base
  ← Principles that determine what counts as length, equality, and proof
```

The backward movement is not a story about which step the mathematician happened to think of first. It asks what the result must presuppose to be valid. A proof earns its necessity by making those presuppositions available for examination and by showing how the conclusion follows from them.

## Form Base as Faculty

The **Form Base** is where a mathematical determination can be constructed and encountered. In geometry, drawn figures, coordinates, and diagrams belong here as formed presentations. Their exact mathematical standing comes from the rules under which they are constructed and compared. A diagram can suggest a theorem; only a warranted transformation makes the theorem hold.

Form Base is **Faculty** in the project's operative sense when it governs the production and comparison of these forms. It is not a warehouse of finished shapes. Nor is it a factory that can only stamp out instances. A Faculty can make a form available, test its admissible transformations, and recognize the limit of the present construction. The slip from “Faculty” to “Factory” is revealing: production is real, but production becomes mathematical only when its governing power is known.

The Kośa gives the needed distinction. *Rūpa-skandha* is Form Base; *indriya* names Faculty through governing efficacy. Form Base includes faculties among its determinations, but the two Sanskrit terms are not identical. “Form Base as Faculty” names our systematic question: how does formed presentation become an effective condition for exact construction and knowledge?

## The threefold determination

The Kośa's **Base : Essence : Principle** gives Mathematics a way to show its own range.

| Determination | Mathematical work |
|---|---|
| **Base** | Gather the constructed figures, symbols, magnitudes, and operations that actually occur. |
| **Essence** | Determine the relations under which a construction can be accessed, compared, measured, or transformed. |
| **Principle** | State and justify the kind of object and the rules that make those relations necessary. |

These moments reciprocally determine one another. A principle without an applicable construction cannot ground this proof; a construction without a principle cannot claim necessity. The proof returns from their relation to the result and can then be repeated, criticized, or generalized.

For the right-triangle example, the Base includes the constructed triangle and squares; the Essence is the measurable relation among their sides and areas; the Principle fixes the Euclidean conditions under which the relation is invariant. Replacing the metric changes the principial setting and demands a new result. This is a change of mathematical Dharma, not an exception hidden beneath the old theorem.

## The wheel turns through contradiction and limit

Mathematical advance is not an accumulation of answers. A method encounters a limit: a proof fails, an operation ceases to preserve an invariant, or two admissible descriptions disagree. The limit must be determined. The framework can then be corrected, extended, or replaced while carrying forward what still holds.

The wheel therefore needs five explicit records for every serious result:

1. **Principles:** what objects and relations are admitted;
2. **Faculty:** what constructions and operations are permitted;
3. **Proof:** why each transformation preserves the claim;
4. **Limit:** where the result ceases to hold and why;
5. **Return:** which principles the result confirms, revises, or forces us to distinguish.

This is how Mathematics becomes a DharmaCakra rather than a catalogue of solved exercises. The result returns to its ground. The ground becomes more determinate through the result. What can be taught is the complete movement, not the final line alone.

## Mathematics in our fourfold system

The Kośa is our discipline of exact classification, conditions, faculties, and consequences. Yoga asks how consciousness performs and knows those determinations. Sāṃkhya articulates the formal object and its powers; the Gītā will test the place of mathematical work within action and its ultimate orientation. Their relation does not make a theorem depend on devotion for its validity. It makes the mathematical act visible within a larger Science of Principles.

This essay is an opening construction, not a claim that Mathematics has been solved. The next work is to take a particular mathematical development and trace its complete wheel, including a genuine limit and a principled successor. Only then will the DharmaCakra of Mathematics be presented rather than announced.
