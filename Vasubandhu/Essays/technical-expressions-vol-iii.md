# Volume III: Technical Expressions for the Subjective Science of Dharma

## Scope

The Organon's Subjective Wheel is `[[Anuśaya:Mārga] [Jñāna:Samāpatti]]`.
The project reads this as the *liṅgamātra:aliṅga* side of the guṇa division:
the work moves through what is marked and its unmarked limit toward the
emergence of Meaning in a System. This is the organizing determination for
Volume III, not a translation of a single Kośa kārikā.

Each expression below has three distinct jobs: its Sanskrit use in the
passage, its English in a continuous translation, and its Organon function.
Revise an entry when the kārikā and Bhāṣya establish a more exact relation.

## Opening expressions

| Expression | Translation control | Organon determination |
|---|---|---|
| *kāmadhātu / kāmāvacara / kāmaja* | **Desire-realm / belonging to the desire-realm / arising in the desire-realm**. Use “desire-realm” consistently in Chapter 5 translations, Bhāṣya studies, and these expression notes. | Keep the realm qualification visible when counting anuśayas, assigning their objects, or marking dispassion. |
| *anuśaya* | Retain **anuśaya**. In 5.02, the favored Sautrāntika account calls a dormant affliction anuśaya: its seed condition is a capacity of the continuum, not another entity. The Vaibhāṣikas identify anuśaya with the affliction itself. | The first inquiry on the subjective side: what lets impure action remain reproductively effective; preserve the opposed positions when reporting the Bhāṣya. |
| *kleśa* | **Affliction**. In 5.01, *kleśaḥ pravartamānaḥ* is an affliction in operation; 5.02 distinguishes its dormant and manifest conditions in the Sautrāntika account. | Track the affliction's operation and capacity without turning the capacity into an independent substance. |
| *paryavasthāna* | **Manifest affliction** or **afflictive possession** as the passage requires. In 5.02 the awakened *kleśa* bears this name. | Distinguish present operation from the continuum's capacity to produce another affliction. |
| *bījabhāva* | **Seed condition**: in 5.02, the capacity of an existing continuum to produce affliction again. | A causal capacity, not a separate hidden item. |
| *bhava* | **Existence** or **conditioned existence** in 5.01, where karma brings it into manifestation. | The existence whose continuing production calls for the subjective inquiry. |
| *karma* | **Action**; in 5.01 the Bhāṣya speaks of its accumulation under anuśaya. | The objective wheel's accomplished account enters the inquiry into its afflictive ground. |
| *mārga* | **Path**, specified by the passage. | The corresponding path-side determination of the anuśaya inquiry; its operations must be established by the Mārga passages. |
| *jñāna* | **Knowing**, distinct from *vijñāna* as Cognition and *prajñā* as discriminative comprehension. | The knowing-side determination of the Subjective Wheel, to be developed from its passages. |
| *samāpatti* | **Attainment** or **meditative attainment** as context determines. Distinguish it from *samāpattirāga*, attachment to attainment. | The corresponding attainment-side determination, to be developed from its passages. |
| *samāpattirāga* | **Attachment to samāpatti**. The Bhāṣya names it as the prevalent form of *bhavarāga* in the two higher domains; it is not samāpatti itself. | Inward refinement can retain appropriation of one's conditioned mode of existence. |
| *bhavarāga* | **Attachment to existence** in the form and formless domains. The Bhāṣya specifies *ātmabhāva*, one's constituted mode of existence, as what is savored through samāpatti with its support. | A distinct form of attachment after sensual attachment has been left behind. |
| *rūpadhātu / ārūpyadhātu* | **Form domain / formless domain** in 5.02. Here *dhātu* marks the two domains of *bhavarāga*. | This local domain sense does not replace the controlled Principle rendering in the eighteenfold cognitive matrix. |
| *saṃprayukta / viprayukta* | **Associated / dissociated**. In 5.02 the terms frame the dispute over whether anuśaya is a factor associated with consciousness or a separate dissociated item. | Preserve the debate's alternatives; the favored account does not posit an additional item to classify either way. |
| *dṛṣṭi* | **View** in the afflictive classification. In 5.03 the one view among six anuśayas is divided into five forms, giving ten basic anuśayas. | Meaning emerges through the form and position of each view in the system, rather than from an undifferentiated label of error. |
| *satkāyadṛṣṭi / mithyādṛṣṭi / antagrahadṛṣṭi* | **Satkāya-view / false view / extreme-grasping view**. The 5.03 kārikā names these three with a shared *dṛṣṭi* ending; their full definitions await later units. | Keep the species distinct while their domain and abandonment relations are established. |
| *dṛṣṭiparāmarśa / śīlavrataparāmarśa* | **Adherence to views / adherence to discipline and observance**. The 5.03 kārikā names the pair with a dual *parāmarśau*. | Distinguish grasping a view from grasping a practice as authoritative. |
| *dṛṣṭisvabhāva* | **Having the nature of view**. In the 5.03 Bhāṣya, this predicate identifies five of the ten basic anuśayas. | A classifying predicate, not a definition of all ten anuśayas as views. |
| *darśanaprahātavya / bhāvanāprahātavya* | **To be abandoned through seeing / through cultivation**. The 5.03 Bhāṣya introduces these modes without yet deriving each case. | Keep the path operation distinct from the type and domain of the anuśaya. |
| *dṛṣṭi / darśana* | **Afflicted view / seeing**. In 5.04, the former names excluded views and the latter the seeing of suffering, origination, cessation, or Path through which an anuśaya is abandoned. | Their shared verbal root does not erase the difference between the afflictive stance and truth-disclosing operation. |
| *darśanamātra* | **Mere seeing**. The 5.04 Bhāṣya explains the thirty-two desire-realm cases as abandoned through seeing the truths themselves. | This is the stated mode of removal for this unit; the cultivation mode begins in the next verse. |
| *prahīyante / prahāṇa* | **Are abandoned / abandonment**. In 5.04 the passive verb correlates each count with a particular truth-seeing. | Preserve the specified operation and its classified object rather than treating abandonment as an undifferentiated negation. |
| *bhāvanāheya / mārgābhyāsa* | **To be abandoned through cultivation / practice of the Path**. In 5.05, attachment, resistance, conceit, and ignorance form the cultivation-abandoned desire-realm group after the truths have been seen. | Preserve the temporal and operative difference between seeing a truth and cultivating the Path. |
| *apratigha* | **Without resistance**. In 5.05, each higher-domain classification is obtained from the thirty-six desire-realm cases by removing the five modes of *pratigha*. | The two thirty-onefold totals are constrained transformations of one classification, not new lists made independently. |
| *ālambana* in 5.05 | **Cognitive condition** or **support**. The Bhāṣya assigns a seeing-abandoned anuśaya by the class to which its *ālambana* belongs; the remainder are cultivation-abandoned. | Keep this conditioning relation distinct from Object; it is the operative criterion of the removal classification. |
| *ākāra / prakāra / dhātubheda* | **Aspect / mode / domain distinction** in the 5.05 Bhāṣya's final explanation of the ninety-eightfold reckoning. | These are coordinates of the classification, not three additional anuśayas. |
| *bhavāgra* | **Summit of existence**. In 5.06 it is the level at which the relevant *kṣānti*-counteracted anuśayas are abandoned only through seeing. | A level within conditioned existence, not a name for liberation. |
| *kṣānti / kṣāntivadhya* | Retain **kṣānti** as a technical Path term; *kṣāntivadhya* means counteracted by it. The 5.06 Bhāṣya specifies *anvayajñānakṣānti* at the summit and the applicable *dharma-* or *anvayajñānakṣānti* on other levels. | The counteragent class must remain distinct from the mode of abandonment it enables. |
| *akṣāntivadhya / jñānavadhya* | **Not counteracted by kṣānti / counteracted by knowledge**. The 5.06 Bhāṣya identifies the former class with the latter; both practitioner groups abandon it through cultivation. | Do not treat “not by kṣānti” as absence of a counteragent. |
| *ārya / pṛthagjana / bāhyaka* | **Noble practitioner / ordinary person / outsider**. In 5.06 the first two receive distinct removal routes; *bāhyaka* occurs in a separate attributed dispute. | Practitioner status changes the applicable operation, but an ordinary person is not automatically an outsider. |
| *satkāya / satkāyadṛṣṭi* | **Five aggregates of appropriation / view of self or mine concerning them** in the 5.07 Bhāṣya. Its explanation of *sat* and *kāya* resists taking that complex as permanent or compact. | Keep the afflictive appropriation of aggregates distinct from every positive project use of Self. |
| *antagrāhadṛṣṭi* | **Extreme-grasping view**: the thing already construed as self is regarded as permanent or annihilated. | The extreme depends on the preceding self-construal; the two views are distinct operations. |
| *mithyādṛṣṭi* | **False view** in its specific 5.07 sense: saying “it does not exist” of a truth such as suffering that obtains. | Its distinctive operation is cancellation of an obtaining determination. |
| *dṛṣṭiparāmarśa* in 5.07 | **Taking the inferior as supreme**. The Bhāṣya identifies the inferior here with what has outflows (*sāsrava*). | Preserve the ranking error rather than reducing this view to opinion in general. |
| *śīlavrataparāmarśa* in 5.07 | **Taking a non-cause as cause or a non-path as Path**. The Bhāṣya's religious examples are its attributed doctrinal judgments. | Diagnose a false means–end relation without inserting those examples into the kārikā translation as universal project conclusions. |
| *apavādikā / samāropikāḥ* | **Canceling / superimposing**. The 5.07 Bhāṣya assigns the former especially to false view and the latter to the other views. | Two different failures of determination: denial of what obtains and imposition of what does not. |
| *nityātma-viparyāsa* | **Inversion concerning permanence and self**. In 5.08, the supposed cause is first grasped as permanent, one, self, and agent. | Identify the prior misconstrual from which the causal fixation arises. |
| *kāraṇābhiniveśa* | **Fixation on a cause**. In 5.08, adherence to Īśvara or a similar supposed cause is generated by that inversion. | Trace the derivative fixation to its ground; preserve the difference between inquiry into causes and adherence to a falsely constituted cause. |
| *duḥkha-dṛg-gheya* | **Abandoned through seeing suffering**. Seeing suffering removes the permanence and self graspings that generate the fixation. | The mode of correction follows the fixation's generative ground. |
| *parīkṣya* in 5.08 | **To be examined**. The Bhāṣya ends its wider discussion of abandonment classes with *tasmāt parīkṣya eṣo 'rthaḥ*. | Preserve the source's reservation about extending this explanation to every case. |
| *viparyāsa-catuṣka / dṛṣṭi-traya* | **Four inversions / three views**. In 5.09, eternalist extreme-view yields permanence-inversion, view-adherence yields pleasure- and purity-inversions, and satkāya-view yields self-inversion. | Keep the four contents distinct from their three generating views. |
| *viparītataḥ / nitīraṇāt / samāropāt* | **Through contrariness / decisive determination / superimposition**. The three 5.09 ablatives delimit proper inversion together. | A positive misdetermination of a cognitive support; mere negation or passing error does not meet this definition. |
| *saṃjñā-citte / tad-vaśāt* | **Recognition and consciousness / through its power**. In 5.09, these associated factors are called inverted through the governing view-inversion. | Preserve the primary view and derivative recognition and citta without collapsing their functions. |
| *māna / pravṛttibheda* in 5.10 | **Conceit / difference in operation**. The Bhāṣya defines *māna* as *cittasya unnati*, elevation of citta, and distinguishes seven forms by how it operates. | Keep the comparative and appropriative forms distinct while tracing their common operation. |
| *navavidhāḥ tribhyaḥ* | **Nine modes from three**. The Bhāṣya derives the nine comparative modes from *māna*, *atimāna*, and *ūnamāna*; its compact compound requires separation at that point. | The nine modes refine the sevenfold account rather than adding nine independent conceits. |
| *dṛg-bhāvanā-kṣayāḥ* | **Exhausted through seeing or cultivation**. In 5.10, these modes of removal distribute across determinate occurrences of the seven conceits. | Do not require both operations jointly of every occurrence. |
| *vadhādi-paryavasthāna / samudācāra* | **Afflictive occupation leading to killing and similar acts / active manifestation**. The 5.10 Bhāṣya says the deliberate occupation is cultivation-abandonable and distinguishes what remains un-abandoned from what presently manifests. | Track classification, susceptibility, and present occurrence separately. |
| *vibhavecchā / vibhavatṛṣṇā* | **Wish / thirst for nonexistence**. In 5.11, the Bhāṣya construes its object as impermanence in the three domains. | Keep this afflicted wish distinct from release and from the particular thirst for existence also included by the preceding *tathā*. |
| *vidhādayaḥ / asmitā* in 5.11 | **The series beginning with conceit-modes / I-am conceit**. The Bhāṣya extends the series through thirst for nonexistence and reads *asmitā* here as *asmimāna*. | Preserve the verse's series and the commentary's local interpretation without collapsing every use of *asmitā* into one term. |
| *dṛṣṭi-puṣṭatva / bhagnapṛṣṭhatva* | **Being nourished by views / having its back broken**. In 5.11, seeing removes views on which certain cultivation-abandonable formations depend for manifestation. | Separate a formation's abandonment class from its ability to arise after its support is broken. |
| *kaukṛtyaṃ ... aśubham* | **Unwholesome remorse**. The 5.11 Bhāṣya treats it as cultivation-abandonable yet absent in the noble practitioner because it arises from doubt. | Keep this separate dependency from the verse's view-nourished series. |
| *sarvatraga / sabhāgadhātu* | **Universally ranging / corresponding domain**. In 5.12, eleven anuśayas range through the whole of their own domain; the Bhāṣya does not make them unrestricted across every domain. | Represent scope together with its domain boundary. |
| *duḥkha-hetu-dṛg-gheya* | **Abandoned through seeing suffering and origination**. The 5.12 Bhāṣya resolves *hetu* here as *samudaya* and counts seven views, two doubts, and two truth-specific ignorance classes. | Keep the abandonment class distinct from the question of object-range. |
| *vimati / vicikitsā* | **Doubt**. The verse says *vimati*; the Bhāṣya names *vicikitsā* and counts the suffering- and origination-related cases separately. | Preserve the two truth-specific instances within the elevenfold count. |
| *saṃprayuktā avidyā / āveṇikī avidyā* | **Associated ignorance / independent ignorance**. In 5.12, the verse distinguishes ignorance associated with the views from independent ignorance. | Distinguish mode of association from the two truth-specific ignorance classes counted by the Bhāṣya. |
| *svalakṣaṇa-kleśa / sāmānya-kleśa* | **Particular-characteristic / general-characteristic affliction**. The Vaibhāṣika reply in 5.12 excludes attachment and conceit from the universal class on this ground. | Wide recurrence or association with a universal view does not by itself confer general-characteristic scope. |
| *ūrdhvālambana / visabhāgadhātu* | **Having a higher domain as cognitive support / a different domain**. In 5.13, nine of the eleven universal anuśayas can take one or two higher domains as supports. | Keep a directed cross-domain support relation distinct from the corresponding-domain range of 5.12. |
| *dṛṣṭi-dvaya-vivarjita* | **Excluding two views**. The 5.13 Bhāṣya identifies satkāya-view and extreme-grasping view; the Brahmā counterexample tests the stated reason for their exclusion. | Preserve the attributed classification and its explicit challenge. |
| *sahabhu / prāpti-varjya* | **Co-arisen / excluding acquisitions**. In 5.13, dharmas co-arising with universal anuśayas share universal status, while acquisitions are excluded because of their heterogeneous results. | Distinguish co-arisen participation from acquisition. |
| *sarvatraga-hetu* | **Universal-cause relation**. The 5.13 Bhāṣya opens a four-corner comparison with universal anuśaya status and states only its first two corners. | Keep class membership and causal function separate without supplying unstated corners as source prose. |
| *mithyādṛg-vimatī / tābhyām* | **False view and doubt / with those two**. The opening dual of 5.14 supplies the referents for the ignorance associated with them. | Preserve the pair before distributing it across cessation and Path. |
| *yuktā avidyā / kevalā avidyā* | **Associated ignorance / independent ignorance**. The 5.14 Bhāṣya counts ignorance as one anuśaya species in each truth-specific class despite naming both modes; its sentence has damaged punctuation. | Keep the two modes visible without inflating the sixfold count or claiming the damaged syntax is settled. |
| *anāsrava-gocara / anāsrava-ālambana* | **Ranging over an uncontaminated field / having an uncontaminated cognitive support**. In 5.14, six afflicted anuśayas take cessation or Path as their support. | Type the status of the support separately from the afflictive cognition that takes it. |
| *sāsrava-ālambana* in 5.14 | **Having a support with outflows**. The Bhāṣya assigns this status to the complement of the six among the ninety-eight anuśayas. | Preserve the support-based partition without turning it into a claim that the six themselves lack affliction. |
| *svabhūmy-uparama / svabhūmi-nirodha* | **Cessation of one's own level**. In 5.15, cessation-supporting afflictions take only the cessation of their own level as support. | Index the end to the determinate level whose process ceases. |
| *ṣaḍbhūmi dharmajñāna-pakṣa / navabhūmika anvayajñāna-pakṣa* | **Six-level dharmajñāna side / nine-level anvayajñāna side**. The Bhāṣya distributes the verse's two path counts between desire-realm and higher-domain path-supporting afflictions. | Preserve the two path ranges and their domain assignments pending fuller treatment of the knowledge terms. |
| *anyo 'nya-hetuka / pratipakṣa* | **One path causing another / counteragent**. The 5.15 Bhāṣya grants causal relations among paths while restricting admissible supports by counteragent function. | Causal connection alone does not make every path an object for every path-related affliction. |
| *gocara / viṣaya / ālambana* in 5.15 | **Range / determinate object / cognitive support**. The verse and Bhāṣya use all three in the local object-range argument. | Preserve their distinct syntactic roles while relating them in this passage. |
| *rāga / varjyatva / kuśaladharmacchanda* | **Attachment / being to be relinquished / wholesome aspiration toward a wholesome dharma**. In 5.16, attachment is excluded from uncontaminated supports because it must be relinquished; the Bhāṣya distinguishes it from wholesome aspiration. | A positive orientation to the Path is not classified as attachment merely by its positive direction. |
| *dveṣa / anapakārataḥ* | **Aversion / because there is no injury**. The 5.16 Bhāṣya requires an injurious support for aversion; cessation and Path do not provide one. | State the object-condition of the afflictive relation before assigning it. |
| *parāmarśau* | **The two adherences**. In 5.16, the dual covers discipline-and-observance adherence and view-adherence; actual purity excludes the former's false purity-grasp, and genuine supremacy excludes the latter's elevation of the inferior. | Keep the two erroneous relations separate even where the verse groups them. |
| *śānta / śuddha / agra* | **Peaceful / pure / supreme**. The 5.16 Bhāṣya distributes these actual characters of cessation and Path across conceit and the two adherences. | The truth of the object-character matters to whether an afflictive relation can obtain. |
| *anuśerate / ālambanataḥ* | **Adhere / through cognitive support**. In 5.17, the verse states the general range rule for anuśaya's adherence by taking a support. | Keep object-support adherence distinct from adherence through association, which the Bhāṣya's opening question also names. |
| *sarvatraga / sakalā svabhūmi* in 5.17 | **Universally ranging / entire own level**. The Bhāṣya specifies all five abandonment classes within that level. | Universality has a determinate level and internal fivefold scope. |
| *asarvaga / svanikāya* | **Non-universal / own abandonment class**. In 5.17, such anuśayas take only their own truth- or cultivation-specific class as support within their level. | Preserve a positive, restricted range rather than treating non-universal as without a range. |
| *utsarga / apavāda* in 5.17 | **General rule / exception**. The Bhāṣya explicitly marks the verse's scope statements as the rule before 5.18's qualification. | Do not promote the general formulation into an exceptionless definition. |
| *anāsrava-ūrdhva-viṣaya* | **Having uncontaminated or higher objects**. In 5.18, the initial *na* excludes object-based adherence in those objects while allowing that they can be cognized. | Distinguish reference to an object from afflictive footing in it. |
| *asvīkāra / vipakṣa* | **Non-appropriation / counteragent**. Uncontaminated dharmas and higher levels are not appropriated by self-view and thirst and oppose the relevant afflictions. | State why an object can be taken without becoming a field where the affliction settles. |
| *pratiṣṭhā / ānuguṇya* | **Footing / congeniality**. The 5.18 Bhāṣya uses lack of footing in its main explanation; unnamed others explain non-adherence through lack of congeniality. | Keep the attributed alternative distinct from the running explanation. |
| *saṃprayogataḥ anuśayana* | **Adherence through conjunction**. In 5.18, an anuśaya adheres in the dharma with which it is associated while it remains unabandoned. | Type conjunction separately from object-reference and object-based adherence. |
| *avyākṛta / aśubha / akuśala* | **Ethically indeterminate / unwholesome**. In 5.19, the higher-domain anuśayas are indeterminate; the desire-realm exceptions are satkāya-view, extreme-grasping view, and ignorance associated with them, while the remaining cases are unwholesome. | Keep ethical status distinct from whether an anuśaya is afflicted. |
| *sahābhyāṃ mohaḥ* | **Ignorance associated with those two**. The instrumental dual in 5.19 points to satkāya-view and extreme-grasping view. | Restrict the indeterminate ignorance to the stated conjunction rather than all desire-realm ignorance. |
| *duḥkha-vipāka / paravyābādha* | **Painful maturation / harm directed toward others**. The 5.19 Bhāṣya appeals to their absence in explaining higher-domain indeterminacy. | Distinguish affliction from the cause and result characteristic of strict unwholesomeness. |
| *sahajā / vikalpitā satkāyadṛṣṭi* | **Innate / conceptually constructed satkāya-view**. Earlier teachers call the latter unwholesome while the innate form is indeterminate; the Bhāṣya's objection and reply remain compressed. | Preserve the attributed qualification without silently replacing the verse's general classification. |
| *akuśala-mūla / avyākṛta-mūla* | **Root of the unwholesome / root of the ethically indeterminate**. In 5.20, root-status adds a productive function to ethical status. The running verse repeats *akuśala-mūlāni* in the third pāda, while the Bhāṣya prose explicitly introduces the indeterminate triad. | Keep the adopted textual correction and the witness reading visible while distinguishing status from generative role. |
| *rāga-pratigha-mūḍhayaḥ* | **Attachment, hostility, and delusion**. The 5.20 Bhāṣya restates the unwholesome roots as *lobha*, *dveṣa*, and *moha*, excluding ignorance conjoined with the two indeterminate views of 5.19. | Restrict the root triad to its unwholesome instances. |
| *tṛṣṇā / avidyā / mati* in 5.20 | **Craving / ignorance / discriminative cognition** in their indeterminate modes. The Bhāṣya glosses *mati* as *prajñā* and attributes even maturation-born cases to the Kāśmīra teachers. | Do not infer liberative function from the name of a cognitive capacity alone. |
| *vipākaja* in 5.20 | **Born of maturation**. The Kāśmīra position includes even indeterminate *prajñā* of this kind among indeterminate roots. | Preserve the attribution and distinguish being a result from functioning as a root. |
| *dvaidhordhva-vṛtti* | **Divided and upward movement**. In 5.21, the Bhāṣya distributes these grounds to doubt and conceit respectively when defending their exclusion from the Kāśmīra root list. | Keep two reasons and their two subjects distinct within the compressed verse compound. |
| *sthira / adho-vṛtti* | **Stable / downward-moving**. The 5.21 Bhāṣya uses these for its ordinary root analogy; nearby transmitted wording remains difficult. | Treat the analogy as a local criterion of roothood without building a certain philology from damaged prose. |
| *bāhyakāḥ / catvāri avyākṛta-mūlāni* | **Outside authorities / four indeterminate roots**. The rival 5.21 account names craving, view, conceit, and ignorance; it replaces *mati/prajñā* from the Kāśmīra triad. | Preserve the competing lists and avoid assigning the unnamed outside authorities a more specific school. |
| *dhyāyitṛtva / avidyā-vaśa* | **Meditator types / under the power of ignorance**. The rival account infers its roots from meditators dominated by craving, view, or conceit, all governed by ignorance. | Distinguish a generated practitioner type from the common condition that governs it. |
| *avyākṛta-dharma / avyākṛta-praśna* | **Ethically indeterminate dharma / question set aside**. The 5.22 Bhāṣya explicitly separates the earlier ethical use from the classification of a question. | Shared wording does not make ethical status and answer-mode the same determination. |
| *ekāṃśato vyākaraṇa / vibhajya vyākaraṇa* | **Categorical answer / answer after division**. In 5.22, death illustrates the first and renewed birth the second; the Bhāṣya discusses alternative assignments under changed wording. | Select the answer form from the determinate scope of the question. |
| *paripṛcchya vyākaraṇa / sthāpanīya praśna* | **Answer after questioning in return / question to be set aside**. Relative superiority lacks a specified comparator; the being-apart-from-aggregates question presupposes an unestablished substantial subject. | Distinguish eliciting a missing relation from setting aside a defective presupposition. |
| *maraṇa / utpatti / viśiṣṭa / ātma-anyatā* | **Death / rebirth / relative superiority / otherness of self**. The final 5.22 compound lists the four illustrations in the order of the answer modes. | Keep the ordered examples tied to their Bhāṣya-explicated questions rather than treating the list as four unrelated topics. |
| *svalakṣaṇa-kleśa / sāmānya-kleśa* in 5.23 | **Particular-character / general-character affliction**. The Bhāṣya places attachment, aversion, and conceit in the first class and view, doubt, and ignorance in the second; 5.23 states the first class's object-indexed rule. | Preserve the different scopes and leave the second class's full rule to 5.24. |
| *atīta / pratyupasthita / pratyutpanna* | **Past / presently arisen / present**. In 5.23, the Bhāṣya glosses the verse's *pratyupasthita* with *pratyutpanna*. | Temporal pastness and present occurrence are distinct from abandonment status. |
| *utpanna / aprahīṇa* | **Arisen / unabandoned**. The 5.23 bond requires actual arising with respect to the particular object and persists while the affliction remains unabandoned. | Do not infer abandonment from an occurrence's passage into the past. |
| *vastu / saṃyuta* | **Determinate object / bound or connected**. The relative-correlative construction in 5.23 fixes the very object regarding which the affliction arose. | Keep the person's object-indexed bond distinct from *saṃprayukta*, association of factors. |

| *prahāṇa-bhāvanā-rāmatā / bhava-kāma-rāga-vaimukhya* | **Delight in cultivating abandonment / turning away from craving for existence and sensual desire**. The Bhāṣya names the fourth lineage and gives the reason it too has non-greed as its nature. | Delight in practice is determined by its direction away from craving; the following verse may develop its further role. |
| *tribhir vṛttiḥ / caturthena karma / karmānta* in 6.08 | **Livelihood by the three / work by the fourth / work to be done**. The three contentment lineages provide a bounded livelihood; delight in abandonment and cultivation gives the practitioner a task. | Maintenance sustains the practitioner for a determinate work, so the four lineages form a practical three-plus-one structure. |
| *tṛṣṇotpāda-vipakṣataḥ / cīvara / piṇḍapāta / śayyāsana / bhava-vibhava* | **Counterforces to the production of thirst / robes / alms-food / lodging / existence and non-existence**. The cited sūtra supplies four occasions for thirst, each met by a lineage. | Preserve the counterforce's target instead of treating thirst as a single undifferentiated field. |
| *mamakāra-vastu / ahaṃkāra-vastu / ātmabhāva* | **Basis of mine-making / basis of I-making / one's own existence**. Robes and similar supports supply the first basis; one's own existence supplies the second. | Appropriating supports as “mine” and taking existence as “I” are distinct relations. Here *vastu* is a basis, not the Organon's technical Object. |
| *tat-kāla-śānti / atyanta-śānti / ubhaya-icchā* | **Present pacification / final pacification / both desires**. The first three lineages presently pacify desire toward what is made “mine”; the fourth finally pacifies both mine-making and I-making desire. | Keep the time and extent of pacification attached to their proper lineage functions; present simplicity does not itself complete the work. |
| *avatāra / aśubha / ānāpānasmṛti / yathākramam* in 6.09 | **Entry / contemplation of the unattractive / recollection of breathing / in order**. The Bhāṣya assigns the first practice to desire-dominant character and the second to discursive-thought-dominant character. | Entry into cultivation selects a counter-operation by diagnosed predominance; a future Feature Grammar can preserve the paired distribution. |
| *rāgacarita / vitarkacarita / avicitrālambana* | **Desire-dominant character / discursive-thought-dominant character / non-variegated cognitive Condition**. One attributed explanation gives the simple Condition of breathing as a reason it cuts discursive thought; a second explanation concerns outward orientation. | Preserve the two attributed explanations and type the Condition separately from the practitioner character it serves. |
| *varṇa-rāga / saṃsthāna-rāga / sparśa-rāga / upacāra-rāga* | **Desire for color / configuration / touch / bodily bearing**. The prose assigns distinct unattractive presentations to these four forms of bodily desire. | Each counter-presentation answers the feature through which attraction operates; the diagnostic map is more precise than a generic contemplation label. |
| *śaṃkalā / asthisaṃkalā / sarva-rāgin* | **Skeletal framework / bone framework / all desire-dominant practitioners**. The separate kārikā has *śaṃkalā*, while the running witness reads *śaṅkalā* and later has a damaged *saṃkaklāyām*; the skeleton is recommended in common because the four attractive features are absent. | A single support can cover several diagnosed subtypes without erasing their earlier differentiation. |
| *adhimukti-prādeśika-manaskāra / viṣkambhaṇa / kleśa-prahāṇa* | **Resolutive attention to a limited field / suppression / definitive abandonment of affliction**. The Bhāṣya attributes only suppression to unattractive contemplation under these conditions. | Keep actual preparatory efficacy distinct from the Path-capacity required for definitive abandonment. |
| *ādikarmika / kṛtajaya / kṛtaparijaya / atikrāntamanaskāra* in 6.10 | **Beginner / one who has gained mastery / one who has gained familiarity / one whose attention has passed beyond**. The commentary names three stages; this verse describes the first two, while the third begins in 6.11. | Stage is determined by demonstrated operation over the contemplative representation, not by an unqualified label of attainment. |
| *asthi-vistāra / ā-samudra / adhimokṣa-abhivardhana* | **Expansion of the skeleton / as far as the ocean / increase of determinate resolution**. The beginner extends the imagined skeletal field through widening surroundings for this stated purpose. | A Feature Grammar can record scope and purpose on the reflective surface; the expanded field is a practiced determination, not a cosmological claim. |
| *saṃkṣepa / citta-saṃkṣepa* | **Contraction / gathering of consciousness**. The beginner contracts the ocean-wide field back to their own skeleton; the next stage gathers it further. | Preserve scope contraction and its intended effect as different relations; *citta* retains its controlled rendering, consciousness. |
| *pādāsthna / kapālārdha-tyāga / tyāga* | **From the foot bones / relinquishment until half the skull / setting aside**. The first form is difficult, and the running verse has *kapālārtha*; the prose specifies removal from the feet and attention to the remaining half-skull. | Mastery includes ordered release of the counter-representation itself. Preserve the witness variants while using the prose to establish the operation. |
| *atikrānta-manaskāra / bhrūmadhye citta-dhāraṇa* in 6.11 | **Attention passed beyond / holding consciousness between the eyebrows**. After releasing the last half-skull, the practitioner sustains *citta* between the brows; the stage-name is explicitly scoped to contemplation of the unattractive. | Freedom from this constructed representation is a local mastery determination, not passage beyond consciousness or cognition generally. |
| *ālambana-parīttatā / vaśitā-parīttatā / catuṣkoṭika* | **Limited Condition extent / limitation in mastery / fourfold analysis**. The Bhāṣya crosses the narrow or broad cognitive Condition with mastered or unmastered attention. | Model Condition extent and mastery as independent features on a reflective surface; neither feature entails the other. |
| *alobha / daśabhū / kāma-dṛśya-ālamba / nṛja* | **Non-greed / ten grounds / visible form of the desire-realm as cognitive Condition / human-arisen**. These answer the Bhāṣya's four questions about nature, grounds, Condition, and production locus in order. | A practice is determined by a bundle of distinct predicates; its shared name does not settle their values. |
| *sāsrava / adhimukti-manaskāra / vairāgya-lābhikī / prāyogikī* | **With influx / resolutive attention / gained through detachment / developed through application**. | *ānāpānasmṛti / prajñā / smṛti-balādhāna-vṛtti* in 6.12 | **Recollection of breathing / Prajñā / functioning through recollection's power**. | *vāyur-gocarā / vāyv-ālambana / kāmāśrayā* | **Wind as cognitive field / wind as cognitive Condition / desire-realm basis**. | *ānāpānau / yataḥ kāyaḥ / yadbhūmikaḥ kāyaḥ tadbhūmikau* in 6.13 | **Inhalation and exhalation / whatever ground the body has / those two have that ground**. | *kāya-citta-viśeṣa-saṃniśrita / śuṣira / saṃmukhībhūta* | **Dependent on a particular body and consciousness configuration / open or porous / present**. Here *citta* is consciousness. |
| *sattvākhya / anupāttaka / indriya-vinirbhāgitva* | **Counted within a sentient being / unappropriated / separable from the faculties**. | *naiḥṣyandika / aupacayika / vipākaja* | **Homogeneous-flow product / accumulation product / maturation-born product**. | *nādhareṇa manasā lakṣyete / svabhūmi / uparibhūmi* | **Not discerned by lower-ground mind / own ground / higher ground**. Preserve the unresolved final witness clause rather than deriving a broader rule from it. |

| *smṛtyupasthāna-bhāvanā / vipaśyanā-saṃpādana / kuryāt* | **Cultivation of the establishments of recollection / completion of vipaśyanā / should undertake**.
