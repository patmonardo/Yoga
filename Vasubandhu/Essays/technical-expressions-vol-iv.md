# Volume IV: Technical Expressions for Jñāna and Samāpatti

## Scope

Volume IV begins the Jñāna–Samāpatti movement of the Organon's Subjective
Wheel, `[[Anuśaya:Mārga] [Jñāna:Samāpatti]]`. [Volume III](technical-expressions-vol-iii.md)
develops Anuśaya–Mārga. [Volumes I](technical-expressions-vol-i.md) and
[II](technical-expressions-vol-ii.md) continue to govern the established
Base–Essence–Principle and Basis–Support–Condition distinctions.

This opening index comes from a scan of the existing Chapter 7 and 8 study
headings and selected technical-vocabulary sections. The local Sanskrit
witness was checked at the chapter openings: 7.01 `[391|02–391|15]` and
8.01 `[432|05–433|03]`. The later loci
below are leads for sequential review, not a completed source audit. Each
expression has three jobs: establish its Sanskrit force in the passage,
control its English in continuous translation, and state its Organon
determination separately. The third column names a question where the
reading has not yet established an answer.

## Jñāna: opening expressions

| Expression | Translation control | Organon determination |
|---|---|---|
| *jñāna* in 7.01–7.02 | **Knowledge** in the opening classification; **Knowing** remains available where a later passage requires the act or accomplished function. Distinguish *vijñāna* (Cognition) and *prajñā* (discriminative comprehension). | Test the conditions under which a discriminative capacity receives knowledge-status; do not infer liberating completion or correctness from that status alone. |
| *kṣānti* in 7.01 | **Acceptance**. The uncontaminated acceptances are excluded from knowledge while the relevant doubt remains unabandoned; the Bhāṣya still calls them views. | Preserve acceptance's Path function and its difference from accomplished knowledge when the classifications intersect. |
| *dṛk / dṛśaḥ / dṛṣṭi* in 7.01 | **View**. The verse uses root-noun forms; the Bhāṣya uses *dṛṣṭi*. The six worldly views include five afflicted views and worldly right view. | View and knowledge overlap without becoming synonyms. Follow the source's class and status qualifications at each occurrence. |
| *dhī / prajñā* in 7.01 | **Discriminative comprehension** is the inherited control for *prajñā*; compare the kārikā's *dhī* and the Bhāṣya's *prajñā* before fixing each English phrase. | Determine how the classified capacity relates to knowledge and view without collapsing it into Cognition (*vijñāna*). |
| *kṣayajñāna / anutpādajñāna* in 7.01, 7.04 | **Knowledge of exhaustion / knowledge of non-arising** as provisional names. The opening excludes both from view; their positive determinations await their later loci. | Test their terminal function through the chapter's own definitions, not through the names alone. |
| *sāsrava / anāsrava* in 7.02 | **With influx / without influx**, or **contaminated / uncontaminated** where the classification requires it. These qualify knowledge-status, not every item in a knower's continuum. | Keep purity-status distinct from the kind and object-range of knowledge. |
| *saṃvṛtijñāna / dharmajñāna / anvayajñāna* in 7.02–7.03 | **Conventional knowledge / Dharma-knowledge / anvaya-knowledge** provisionally. The latter two receive different truth and domain ranges in 7.03; the English force of *anvaya* remains under review. | Derive each range from the passage before placing the forms in the tenfold classification. |
| *viṣaya / gocara / ālambana* in 7.03 | **Condition / range / Support** as the inherited controls permit. The local study's “object” and “object-support” wording needs review against the Sanskrit construction. | Do not promote the three words to one undifferentiated Object or to a three-level ontology. |
| *ākāra* in 7.10 | **Aspect**. The study distributes truth-aspects among knowledges; its counts require a separate source check. | Keep the aspect of apprehension distinct from a knowledge's status and range. |
| *abhijñā* in 7.42 | **Higher knowledge** provisionally; the Bhāṣya relates it to direct cognitive realization. | Test each member's function before reading the six as a single kind of power. |

## Samāpatti: opening expressions

| Expression | Translation control | Organon determination |
|---|---|---|
| *dhyāna / upapattidhyāna / samāpattidhyāna* in 8.01 | Retain **dhyāna** where the technical class is at issue; distinguish corresponding **rebirth** from **meditative attainment**. | The twofold classification does not identify the attainment with birth in its corresponding domain. |
| *samāpatti* in 8.01 | **Attainment** or **meditative attainment** as context requires. The local definition concerns attainment-dhyāna. | Develop the attainment-side determination from the passage; do not transfer properties of *samāpattirāga* (attachment to attainment) to attainment itself. |
| *śubhaikāgrya / kuśalacittaikāgratā / samādhi* in 8.01 | **Wholesome one-pointedness of Consciousness** is the prose expansion of the verse's definition. Retain **samādhi** until its relation to one-pointedness and a distinct mental factor is settled. | Test the unity of the collected Consciousnesses through the Bhāṣya's dispute; one Support alone does not decide the ontological question. |
| *ekālambanatā* in 8.01 | **Having one Support**. The Bhāṣya offers this as an explanation of one-pointedness, then tests its sufficiency. | Keep Support (*ālambana*) distinct from Basis (*āśraya*) and Condition (*viṣaya*). |
| *citta / caitasika dharmāntara* in 8.01 | **Consciousness / distinct mental factor**. The Bhāṣya asks whether samādhi is the one-pointed Consciousnesses themselves or a distinct associated factor. | Preserve the two positions and the ensuing argument; do not make either alternative a translation of the kārikā. |
| *pañca-skandha / sānuga* in 8.01 | **Five Bases / with accompaniment**. The accompanied dhyāna has five-Base constitution; the verse does not list five meditation factors. | Distinguish the defining one-pointedness from the constitution of its accompanied attainment. |
| *vitarka / vicāra* in 8.02 | Retain the technical pair pending the verse's analysis of initial and sustained examination. | Follow their distribution across dhyānas without turning both into a generic “thought.” |
| *maula / śuddha / anāsrava* in 8.05 | **Principal / pure / without influx** as distinct classification terms. The study's eight principal attainments and the summit exception are scan leads. | Keep attainment level and purity-status separate; “pure” does not silently mean “without influx.” |
| *śūnyatā / ānimitta / apraṇihita* in 8.24 | **Emptiness / signless / non-directed** as names of samādhis; review each through its stated truth-aspects and Bhāṣya explanation. | Do not read the names as unrestricted metaphysical definitions before their objects and aspects are established. |
| *aśubhā / śubha* in 8.32 | **Unattractive / beautiful or pure** in the release analysis; *śubha* may mean **wholesome** in another construction. | Let the local contrast select the sense; do not infer ethical status from the unattractive/beautiful pair. |
