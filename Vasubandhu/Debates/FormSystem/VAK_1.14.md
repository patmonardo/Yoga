# VAK_1.14 — The Same Ten, Feeling, and Reflection

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

> इन्द्रियार्थास्त एवेष्टा दशायतनधातवः ।
>
> वेदनानुभवः संज्ञा निमित्तोद्ग्रहणात्मिका ॥ १.१४ ॥

## 4. Grammar

**The same ten under two arrangements**

*Indriyārthāḥ* names the plural subject. *Te eva* fixes its reference:
these very faculties and meanings. *Iṣṭāḥ* supplies the predication
“are accepted,” with the copula understood. The Bhāṣya distributes
*daśa* across the two members of *āyatanadhātavaḥ*:

```text
āyatanavyavasthāyām  → daśa āyatanāni  → ten Essences
dhātuvyavasthāyām    → ta eva daśa dhātavaḥ → the same ten Principles
```

This is ten in each arrangement. The five Faculties and five meanings
belong to the Form Base already established; *avijñapti* is not among
these ten. Its Dharma Essence and Dharma Principle placements are
specified in 1.15.

**Feeling and reflection**

*Vedanā anubhavaḥ* is a nominal definition: feeling is undergoing.
The subject and predicate nouns have different grammatical genders;
both stand in the nominative. The Bhāṣya resolves undergoing as pleasant, painful, or neither
painful nor pleasant.

*Saṃjñā nimittodgrahaṇātmikā* is an adjectival predication.
The feminine adjective agrees with *saṃjñā* and specifies its nature
through the taking up of marks. The Bhāṣya supplies marks such as blue and yellow, long and short,
woman and man, *mitramitra*, and pleasure and pain. The adopted study
translation renders this operation as Reflection.

## 5. Translation

### Literal Translation

These very Faculties and meanings are accepted as the ten Essences and
the same ten Principles. Feeling is undergoing. Reflection has the
taking up of marks as its nature.

### Bhāṣya-informed study translation

The five Faculties and the five meanings already stated are accepted as
the ten Essences and the same ten Principles. *Avijñapti* is not among
these ten. Feeling is undergoing: pleasant, painful, or neither.
Reflection has the taking up of marks as its nature. The Bhāṣya divides
Feeling into six groups from eye-contact through mind-contact, and
Reflection into six groups on the same pattern.

## 6. Systematic Placement

The same five Faculties and five meanings already included in the Form
Base are now placed as ten Essences and the same ten Principles. This
verse establishes ten of the twelve Essences and ten of the eighteen
Principles; it does not enumerate the complete census. Feeling and
Reflection are then defined as distinct Bases, before the Formations
Base is treated in 1.15.

## 9. Interpretive Note

*Te eva*, “these very ones,” is the hinge: the same five Faculties and
five meanings are carried into two distinct arrangements. The ten Essences
and ten Principles are not separate inventories of new contents. The
definitions of Feeling and Reflection open further Base-level
determinations; the detailed Essence and Principle placements continue
in the following verses.
