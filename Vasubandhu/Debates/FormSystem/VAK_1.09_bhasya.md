# VAK_1.09 Bhāṣya — The Form Base and Its Support Relations

## 1. Kārikā Anchor

> rūpaṃ pañcendriyāṇy arthāḥ pañcāvijñaptir eva ca /
>
> tadvijñānāśrayā rūpaprasādāś cakṣurādayaḥ // 1.09 //

### Close translation

> Form is the five faculties, the five meanings, and *avijñapti* also.
> The Form clarities, beginning with the eye, are supports of the
> corresponding cognitions.

### Bhāṣya-informed translation

> The Form Base consists of five faculties—eye, ear, nose, tongue, and
> body—the five respective meanings or conditions (*viṣaya*) of those
> faculties, and *avijñapti*. The meanings are visible form, sound, odor,
> taste, and the tangible. The faculties are Form clarities and support
> the cognitions corresponding to those meanings. *Avijñapti* is included
> but not defined here. The five meanings are named and remain to be
> explained.

Span: Pradhan `[005|21]`–`[006|07]`. The five Bases beginning with Form
have already been stated; this unit defines Form Base and leads into the
further explanation of the five meanings.

## 2. Continuous Sanskrit Witness

> pañca rūpādayaḥ skandhā ity uktam /
> tatra rūpaṃ pañcendriyāṇy arthāḥ pañcāvijñaptir eva ca /
> pañcendriyāṇi cakṣuḥśrotraghrāṇajihvākāyendriyāṇi /
> pañcārthās teṣām eva cakṣurādīnām indriyāṇāṃ yathāsvaṃ ye pañca viṣayāḥ rūpaśabdagandharasaspraṣṭavyākhyāḥ /
> avijñaptiś ceti / etāvān rūpaskandhaḥ /
> tatra ya ete pañca rūpādayo 'rthā uktāḥ /
> tadvijñānāśrayā rūpaprasādāś cakṣurādayaḥ /
> rūpaśabdagandharasaspraṣṭavyavijñānānām āśrayabhūtā ye pañca rūpātmakāḥ prasādās te yathākramaṃ cakṣuḥśrotraghrāṇajihvākāyā veditavyāḥ /
> yathoktaṃ bhagavatā “cakṣur bhikṣo ādhyātmikam āyatanaṃ catvāri mahābhūtāny upādāya rūpaprasāda” iti vistaraḥ /
> yāny etāni cakṣurādīny uktāni tadvijñānāśrayā rūpaprasādāś cakṣurādayaḥ /
> cakṣurvijñānādyāśrayā ity arthaḥ /
> evaṃ kṛtvā prakaraṇagrantho 'py anuvṛtto bhavati /
> “cakṣuḥ katamat / cakṣurvijñānāśrayo rūpaprasāda” iti vistaraḥ /
> nirdiṣṭāni pañcendriyāṇi / arthāḥ pañca nirdeśyāḥ /

The witness's word division is retained. The unit concludes by saying that
the five faculties have been indicated and the five meanings remain to be
indicated.

## 3. Continuous Translation

> It has been stated that the five Bases begin with Form. Among them, Form
> consists of five faculties, five meanings, and *avijñapti* also.
>
> The five faculties are the faculties of eye, ear, nose, tongue, and body.
> The five meanings are the five respective conditions (*viṣaya*) of those
> very faculties, each its own: visible form, sound, odor, taste, and the
> tangible.
>
> And *avijñapti*. This is the whole of the Form Base.
>
> There, the five meanings beginning with visible form have been stated.
> The eye and the remaining faculties are Form clarities, supports of
> the corresponding cognitions. The five clarities, whose nature is Form,
> that support the cognitions of visible form, sound, odor, taste, and the
> tangible, are to be known in order as eye, ear, nose, tongue, and body.
>
> As the Blessed One said: “The eye, monk, is an internal Essence
> (*āyatana*), a Form clarity depending on the four Great Elements,”
> and the rest.
>
> Those designated beginning with the eye are Form clarities and
> supports of their corresponding cognitions. The meaning is: supports of
> eye-cognition and the rest. So understood, the Prakaraṇa is also in
> agreement: “What is the eye? A Form clarity that is the support of
> eye-cognition,” and the rest.
>
> The five faculties have been indicated. The five meanings are to be
> indicated.

Here *upādāya* expresses dependence on the four Great Elements. It is not
the appropriation relation discussed in 1.08.

## 4. Movement of the Commentary

```text
five Bases beginning with Form already stated
    → Form Base defined
enumeration
    → five faculties, five meanings, avijñapti
faculties specified
    → Form clarities
    → supports of corresponding cognitions
scripture
    → eye as internal Essence, dependent on the four Great Elements
Prakaraṇa
    → agrees with the support-reading
close
    → faculties indicated; meanings remain to be indicated
```

No opponent or school is named. The Bhāṣya cites the Blessed One's
scriptural statement and the Prakaraṇa.

## 5. Eleven Members of the Form Base

`Etāvān rūpaskandhaḥ`, “this is the whole of the Form Base,” closes the
enumeration:

```text
five faculties
+ five meanings
+ one avijñapti
= eleven Form-Base members
```

The singular *avijñaptiḥ* contributes one member. The cognitions supported
by the faculties occur in the second line's relational determination;
they are not additional members of the Form Base.

The meanings are each respective to a faculty. The Bhāṣya calls them
*viṣaya* and identifies them as visible form, sound, odor, taste, and the
tangible. This is the specification of the five meanings, not an extra
five-member category.

## 6. Clarity and Support

The five faculties are *rūpaprasāda*, Form clarities. The Bhāṣya
connects each clarity to the cognition corresponding to its condition:

| faculty | condition / meaning | supported cognition |
|---|---|---|
| eye | visible form | eye-cognition |
| ear | sound | ear-cognition |
| nose | odor | nose-cognition |
| tongue | taste | tongue-cognition |
| body | tangible | body-cognition |

The cited scripture calls the eye an internal Essence (*āyatana*), a
Form clarity dependent on the four Great Elements. The Prakaraṇa reading
makes explicit that the eye is support of eye-cognition; the Bhāṣya
says the treatise agrees when read this way. Both statements preserve
support as the relation. Neither adds a member to the Form Base.

## 7. Decisions for the Kārikā

1. Form Base comprises five faculties, five meanings, and *avijñapti*.
2. The meanings are respective conditions of the faculties.
3. The faculties are Form clarities and supports of corresponding
   cognitions.
4. The supported cognitions are not additional members of the Form Base.
5. The scripture names the eye as an internal Essence and a clarity of
   Form dependent on the four Great Elements.
6. *Upādāya* here means dependence, not appropriation.
7. The five meanings are named; their further explanation remains ahead.

## 8. Interpretation

This is the first detailed specification of the Form Base within the Base
system. The count is exact: five faculties, five meanings, and one
*avijñapti*. The second line then crosses from what is included in the Base
to the sensory cognitions supported by its faculties. Those cognitions are
essential to the relation, but they do not enter the eleven-member count.

The Kośa explicitly articulates the Dharma System through the Defined
Concept `<Base, Essence, Principle>`. This passage enacts that system: it
specifies Form Base, cites the eye's Essence classification, and states the
support relation to Discriminative Cognition. Kant's principles disclose
their systematic unity.

The distinctions matter: a faculty is not an anatomical organ substituted
for *indriya*; a meaning is not a second inventory; and a supported
cognition is not another Form-Base member. The Form Base is one subsystem
of the larger Dharma System, not the whole system.

The Kośa explicitly presents *rūpaprasāda* (“Form clarities”) as the
transition from the sensory manifold returned by the faculties to the
clarity Saṃjñā presents through Manas, yielding True Meanings. This carries
the analysis beyond Form Base into Dharma Base. The same *avijñapti* is
classified in both; Vijñāna bears a *prati* relation to it and governs Manas.
Vijñāna joins and governs Perception and Conception: their unity is
Inconceivable as a homogeneous operation, while its Idea is disclosed in
Vijñāna-skandha.
