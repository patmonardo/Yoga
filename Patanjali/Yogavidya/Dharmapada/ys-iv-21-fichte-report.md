# Another Citta Cannot Complete the Relation

## A report on Yoga Sūtra IV.21, Vyāsa, and Fichte

## The Sūtra

`cittāntara-dṛśye buddhi-buddher atiprasaṅgaḥ smṛti-saṅkaraś ca`

### Simple translation

> If one Citta were seen by another Citta, cognition of cognition would
> regress without end, and memory would become confused.

`Cittāntara` is another Citta. `Dṛśya` is what is seen. `Buddhi-buddhi` is
cognition of cognition. `Atiprasaṅga` is an excessive consequence—here an
infinite regress. `Smṛti-saṅkara` is mixture or confusion of memory.

The proposal is initially plausible:

```text
Citta 1 knows the Thing

Citta 2 knows Citta 1
```

But what knows Citta 2?

```text
Citta 3 knows Citta 2
Citta 4 knows Citta 3
...
```

Adding another Citta never completes the Essential Relation. It only moves
the problem to the next member of the series.

## Why this follows IV.20

IV.20 showed that Citta cannot completely determine both itself and its
object in the same moment. IV.21 tests the most obvious repair: let a
subsequent Citta know the preceding one.

```text
IV.20
one Citta cannot occupy both relational positions at once
                          ↓
proposed solution
another Citta observes the first
                          ↓
IV.21
the observer itself requires another observer
```

The series of Cittas is real as conditioned succession. What fails is the
attempt to derive the unity of knowing from one more conditioned member of
that succession.

## Vyāsa's Bhāṣya

Vyāsa formulates the proposal in specifically serial terms: perhaps a Citta
that has ceased is grasped by another Citta arising immediately after it.

He asks:

> If Citta is apprehended by another Citta, by what is the cognition of that
> Citta apprehended?

If it is apprehended by another, and that by another, the regress has no
terminus.

The problem also infects memory. Each cognition of a cognition would generate
its own experience and therefore its own memory:

```text
experience of object                 memory of object
experience of Citta 1                memory of Citta 1
experience of Citta 2 knowing 1      memory of Citta 2 knowing 1
...
```

The resulting memories would mix together, and the determination of one
coherent memory would become impossible.

Vyāsa uses this argument polemically against Buddhist momentarists and
concludes that Puruṣa is the owner and experiencer of Citta. We can preserve
the regress argument without importing Puruṣa as an external substance.

Its strict result is:

> The unity that knows a succession of Cittas cannot be supplied by another
> conditioned Citta within that succession.

## Puruṣa as Spectral Tattva

Puruṣa is a Tattva and therefore a determinate Spectral Tattva. It names the
invariant Aspect of Light within the Essential Relation:

```text
Puruṣa                           Citta

invariant knownness              changing cognitive determination
one spectral pole                the complementary spectral pole
```

Puruṣa does not end the regress by becoming a final super-Citta. That would
only place an exceptionally powerful observer at the end of the same series.
It ends the regress because invariant knownness is not another changing
member requiring subsequent cognition.

The changeless flame does not become one more twist produced by the wind. It
is the stable spectral determination through which the twisting is manifest
as twisting.

This is a formal perfection, not yet spiritual omnipotence.

## Fichte's Bhāṣya

Fichte resumes from the hiatus discovered in IV.20. Pure reason is enclosed
within itself in unchangeable oneness. Yet its persistence is not dead: it is
eternally genesis.

```text
unchangeable oneness
          =
eternally living genesis
```

Existing knowing does not appear externally as another generated item. Its
inward awareness declares an original principle and what follows from that
principle in a one-sided, non-reciprocal order.

This matters because an original principle cannot wait for a later cognition
to validate it. If each principle were grounded by a subsequent knowing, the
Fichtean version of the Citta-regress would begin.

## Reason and understanding

Fichte then makes a decisive discrimination. In the preceding proof we did
not become pure reason itself. We gained an insight into reason by
reconstructing its non-appearing original genesis.

He calls this reconstruction **understanding**:

```text
Reason
non-appearing original genesis
              ↓ reconstructed as determinate terms
Understanding
clarification of that genesis
```

Understanding is necessary, but it must not claim to be absolute. If it did,
each reconstruction would require another reconstruction to certify it:

```text
understanding of reason
          ↓
understanding of that understanding
          ↓
further understanding
          ↓
regress
```

Reason breaks this regress by negating understanding's claim to be the
absolute source. But reason cannot become intelligible for us without the
reconstruction performed by understanding.

Fichte therefore gives a reciprocal epistemic dependence together with a
non-reciprocal order of principle:

> There is no insight into reason without provisionally treating
> understanding as absolute; there is no insight into understanding except
> through its negation as absolute by reason.

## The higher insight

The solution is not a third understanding watching the first two. It is the
higher insight that comprehends reason and understanding together:

```text
understanding of reason
             \       /
              \     /
          higher insight
              /     \
             /       \
reason of understanding
```

From this standpoint, Fichte says:

> We are the understanding of reason and the reason of understanding, and
> thus both in oneness.

The two remain distinguished:

- understanding reconstructs Appearance;
- reason negates the reconstruction's claim to absolute independence.

Their unity is not another member in an endless series. It is insight into
the relation and order of both.

## Yoga and Fichte

| Yoga IV.21 | Fichte |
|---|---|
| one Citta cannot be completed by another Citta | one understanding cannot be grounded by another reconstruction |
| cognition of cognition produces regress | understanding treated as absolute reproduces the explanatory demand |
| memory loses its unity | reconstructed contents lack a final unifying standpoint |
| Puruṣa supplies invariant knownness | pure reason negates understanding as absolute |
| Puruṣa is not another Citta | higher insight is not another subordinate reconstruction |

Fichte gives us the logical form needed to interpret Yoga without turning
Puruṣa into a supernatural observer:

> The regress ends through insight into the unity and order of the relation,
> not through the addition of one more observer.

## Memory and Eka Citta

The memory argument reconnects Appearance with the Eka Citta of Reflection.

Many Cittas can occur serially. Their memories need not belong to separate
little owners. But the series requires a synthetic unity through which one
experience, its cognition, and its recollection are determinately connected.

```text
Citta-event       →       Saṃskāra       →       Smṛti
      \                                             /
       \___________________________________________/
                    Eka Citta
```

Eka Citta is not an extra observer inserted after the sequence. It is the
unity under which the sequence can count as one knowable and memorable
history.

Thus IV.21 confirms the earlier Kantian principle:

> The `I think` must be able to accompany the many Cittas; it cannot be one
> more Citta among them.

## Practical Reason and the Kośa

The Kośa's serial analysis does not require a separate metaphysical owner for
every cognitive moment. But it does require exact causal and mnemonic
relations among the moments.

Practical responsibility would collapse if every act, awareness of the act,
and memory belonged to an unrelated observer:

```text
one Citta acts
another knows the act
another remembers the knowing

Who learns? Who changes? Who undertakes practice?
```

The Immeasurables depend upon continuity without substantial ownership.
Repeated acts of Maitrī or Karuṇā form Saṃskāras, alter prospective Vāsanās,
and become a transformed practical disposition. Eka Citta gives this process
synthetic unity without becoming an immortal empirical personality.

## Completion of Appearance

The ten Sūtras of Appearance now form one sequence:

1. **IV.12:** Dharmas have distinct temporal paths.
2. **IV.13:** their manifest and subtle forms are Guṇa-nature.
3. **IV.14:** unity of transformation constitutes the Thing.
4. **IV.15:** the Thing and differing Cittas have distinct paths.
5. **IV.16:** the Thing does not depend upon one Citta.
6. **IV.17:** the Thing is known when it colors Citta.
7. **IV.18:** Citta-vṛttis are always known through invariant knowing.
8. **IV.19:** Citta as seen is not self-illuminating.
9. **IV.20:** Citta cannot determine itself and its object at once.
10. **IV.21:** another Citta produces regress and confusion of memory.

Appearance therefore ends with a demonstrated inadequacy:

```text
the objective Thing alone          cannot explain knowing

the changing Citta alone           cannot illuminate itself

another Citta                      produces infinite regress

external Puruṣa                    freezes the relation into dualism
```

What is required is not another term but comprehension of the whole
Essential Relation.

Fichte reaches the same threshold. Reason and understanding are distinct but
can be known only in their higher unity. He ends the section by saying that
only one more principle is required for the matter to be completely
explained.

That principle belongs to **Actuality**.

## Provisional result

IV.21 states:

> If one Citta were seen by another Citta, cognition of cognition would
> regress without end, and memory would become confused.

The regress cannot be stopped by a final super-Citta. Puruṣa functions as the
invariant Spectral Tattva, while Eka Citta supplies the synthetic unity of the
serial Cittas.

Fichte supplies the corresponding logical resolution: understanding must
reconstruct reason, while reason negates understanding's claim to be
absolute; the higher insight comprehends both in one ordered relation.

Appearance has now reached its limit. Tomorrow, Actuality can begin by asking
how the inner and outer, seeing and seen, invariant Light and changing Citta,
are one without collapsing their determinate difference.
