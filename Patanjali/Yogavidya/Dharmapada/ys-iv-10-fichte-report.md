# The Beginningless Wish to Continue

## A report on Yoga Sutra IV.10 and Fichte

## The Sutra

`tasam anaditvam ca-asisa nityatvat`

### Plain translation

> These Vāsanās are beginningless because the wish to continue is permanent.

`Tāsām` refers back to the Vāsanās. `Anāditva` denies an assignable first
beginning. `Āśis` is a wish, desire, aspiration, or benediction. In this
context it is the elementary wish for continued being: may I continue; may I
not cease. `Nityatva` names its persistence.

The Sutra does not require a first Vāsanā:

```text
wish to continue
      |
      v
Vāsanā -> manifestation -> action -> Saṃskāra
   ^                                  |
   |__________________________________|
```

The circle is beginningless because every manifest tendency presupposes an
earlier disposition, while the wish to continue sustains the reproduction of
the whole series.

## Fichte's Bhasya

Fichte analyzes a strange demand: seeing seeks a new genesis while already
possessing the content it seeks. Reflection does not acquire an entirely new
object. It demands that the same content be generated again in another
qualitative form—as seen, related, and grounded.

The "should" is the principle of this renewed genesis:

```text
already determinate seeing
          |
          | should become genetically explicit
          v
the same content in a new form of seeing
```

This should persists wherever reflection occurs. It is not first produced by
one particular reflection; every search for a ground already enacts it. In
that precise sense it has no discoverable beginning within appearance.

| Yoga IV.10 | Fichte |
|---|---|
| beginningless Vāsanās | no first factical act explains the entire series |
| persistent wish | the should as the enduring demand for renewed genesis |
| continuation of the series | seeing reproduces its content in another form |
| latent tendency | idealism persisting in form after its content is overcome |

## The danger in the `should`

IV.7 showed the should as free self-determination. IV.10 reveals its ambiguous
side. The demand that a conditioned identity continue reproducing itself can
also be the formal engine of an endless Vāsanā-series within Citta.

```text
free should       the act determines itself from within

binding wish      the act must keep reproducing its own conditioned form
```

These are not yet two unrelated principles. They are two possible readings of
the same formal self-continuation. Freedom requires that the should become
transparent to its ground. Otherwise it persists as the unquestioned wish to
continue being this constructed I.

Fichte therefore turns from idealism to realism. The We can reconstruct, but
it cannot create primordial reason. After every construction is abstracted,
pure light remains by itself. The error is to transfer the freedom of our act
of abstraction to the arising of reason and imagine that the I produced what
appears when the I withdraws.

## Citta-Dharma

IV.10 defines the deepest reproductive tendency operating within Citta. A
Vāsanā is not merely retained and manifested under congruent conditions; it
also carries the wish for the continuation of its determinate identity.

> Vāsanā is beginningless because each conditioned Citta can inherit and renew
> the wish that its determinate pattern should continue in another Citta.

This is the threshold of IV.11. If one searches for a first historical Vāsanā,
the search never ends. Cessation must instead concern the present structure by
which the Vāsanā-series is collected and supported. Nothing here entails the
cessation of Eka Citta.

Fichte expresses the threshold starkly: philosophy becomes possible only when
the reconstructing I "perishes" and pure reason comes to manifestation. This
does not mean the destruction of knowing or of Eka Citta. It means the removal
of the constructed I's claim to be the source and owner of reason.

## Provisional result

The wish to continue explains why the Vāsanā-series has no first internal
starting point. But beginningless does not mean unconditioned. A process may
lack a first temporal member and still cease when the conditions collecting
and supporting it are absent. IV.11 identifies those conditions while leaving
Eka Citta unharmed.
