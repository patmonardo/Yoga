# The Occasion Is Only Shine

## Yoga Sutra IV.3 with Hegel

`nimittam aprayojakam prakrtinam varana-bhedas tu tata ksetrikavat`

> The occasion does not impel nature's powers; it only breaks their barrier,
> as when a farmer opens a channel.

Fichte placed productive power within being and reduced the philosophical We
to an occasion of insight. Hegel gives the external occasion its logical name:
**shine**.

Shine appears immediate, as though it possessed an independent being. In
truth, it exists only through its relation to essence. It is non-self-
subsistent immediacy: a determination that shows essence but does not ground
itself.

This is exactly the limited office of `nimitta`:

```text
nimitta appears as cause
        |
        | reflection removes false independence
        v
nimitta is only the occasion in which nature's power shines
```

The barrier is the apparent separation between the external condition and the
immanent power. Breaking it does not erase appearance. It recognizes that the
appearance is essence's own shining rather than a second power acting upon
essence from outside.

Hegel also sharpens the criticism of idealism. Moving the cause from a thing-
in-itself into a subjective I does not solve the problem if the content still
confronts the I as immediately given. Externality has merely been relocated.

The Hegelian Varttika is:

> The occasion is not productive because it has no self-subsistence apart from
> the power that appears through it. Once its false independence is removed,
> it is recognized as the shine of nature's own activity.

The farmer does not create the flow. More strongly, the farmer's effective
act belongs within the manifestation of the same process whose barrier is
opened.
