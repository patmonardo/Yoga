# Memory, Samskara, and Condition

## Yoga Sutra IV.9 with Hegel

`jati-desa-kala-vyavahitanam api-anantaryam smrti-samskarayo eka-rupatvat`

> Even when separated by kind, place, and time, there is continuity because
> memory and Samskara have one form.

Fichte found one formal should persisting across changing contents. Hegel
adds that formal identity alone cannot explain actual continuity. Ground
requires **condition**.

Condition first appears as an immediate manifold existence: a kind, place,
time, body, object, or circumstance. It seems external to the grounded
process. Yet it is a condition only through its reference to another, and the
ground becomes actual only through this immediate field.

Smrti and Samskara display the same double relation:

```text
Samskara     retained ground-form
Smrti        that form reactivated under present conditions
```

Their `eka-rupatva` preserves intelligible identity. Their difference of
status—latent and recollected—requires determinate conditions. Separation by
kind, place, and time changes the immediate field without breaking the form
capable of renewed manifestation.

The one form is therefore neither a metaphysical object traveling unchanged
through time nor an empty resemblance imposed afterward. It is a reflected
determination whose reappearance depends upon conditions while preserving its
content through them.

The Hegelian Varttika is:

> Memory continues Samskara because one reflected form mediates itself through
> different conditions. The intervals are real at the level of immediate
> existence but do not break the genetic identity of the determination.

This gives Citta a conditioned history without converting Eka Citta into an
eternal substance behind the series.
