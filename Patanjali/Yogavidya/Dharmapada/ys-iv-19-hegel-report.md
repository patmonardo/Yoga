# The Seen Citta and the Dissolution of Appearance

## Yoga Sūtra IV.19 with Hegel

## The Sūtra

`na tat svābhāsaṃ dṛśyatvāt`

> Citta does not illuminate itself, because it is something seen.

`Tat` refers to Citta. `Svābhāsa` means self-illuminating or shining by
itself. `Dṛśyatva` is the condition of being seen.

IV.18 established that the transformations of Citta are always known. IV.19
now asks whether Citta, as one of the things known, can itself be the Light
that accounts for this knownness.

```text
Citta as seen                  illumination of Citta

determinate Appearance        condition of its appearing
```

The Sūtra denies their immediate identity. The seeing that has become seen
cannot, in that same determination, be the illumination by which it is seen.

## Why IV.19 follows the two worlds of IV.18

IV.18 distinguished:

```text
World of Appearance           World-in-itself

changing Citta-vṛttis        invariant knownness of Puruṣa
```

That opposition was necessary, but unstable. If Citta were self-illuminating,
the World of Appearance would contain its own complete ground and Puruṣa
would be unnecessary. If Puruṣa illuminated Citta as a wholly external
substance, the two worlds would never form one cognition.

IV.19 first blocks the easy collapse into Citta:

> What belongs to the World of Appearance as something seen cannot simply
> declare itself the World-in-itself that illuminates seeing.

Hegel then shows that the opposite worlds cannot remain externally
separated either. Their opposition is precisely what dissolves them into
Essential Relation.

## Vyāsa against the fire analogy

Vyāsa considers the objection that Citta illuminates itself and its objects
as fire supposedly illuminates itself and other things.

He rejects the analogy. Fire does not first stand in darkness and then enter
into an illuminating relation with itself. Illumination is manifest through
a relation of illuminator and illuminated; the two functions cannot be
collapsed merely by pointing to a luminous object.

Citta is also genuinely apprehended. We know determinate states such as:

```text
I am angry.
I am afraid.
Attachment is present.
Confusion has arisen.
```

These states are `dṛśya`: they can be objectified, discriminated, and
known. Their apprehensibility prevents the seen Citta from being identified
without remainder with the immediate act that knows it.

## Fichte's exact distinction

Fichte gives the plainest statement of the result:

> Seeing and Light reside in immediate seeing, never in the seeing that is
> seen.

Reflection can turn a prior act of seeing into an object:

```text
I see the Thing.
       ↓ reflection
I now see my previous seeing.
```

The previous seeing has not disappeared, but its logical status has changed.
It is now determinate content within Appearance. The living Light belongs to
the present act through which that content is manifest.

Repeating the reflection never changes this structure. Every newly
objectified seeing becomes `dṛśya`; immediate illumination remains with the
actual seeing by which it is objectified.

## The inversion of the two worlds

Hegel begins IV.19 with the inversion generated at IV.18:

```text
World of Appearance           World-in-itself

positive here                 negative there
change here                   invariance there
seen Citta here               illuminating Puruṣa there
```

At first the essential world seems to contain the opposite truth of every
appearing determination. But once each world is defined through opposition
to the other, neither is self-sufficient.

The World of Appearance is not mere externality. It contains law, the
self-identity through which Appearance returns to itself. Conversely, the
World-in-itself is not empty invariance. It contains the negativity and
difference of the appearing world whose truth it is.

Therefore:

```text
Appearance contains reflected identity.
The essential world contains appearing difference.
```

Each world contains the moment assigned to the other. Their supposed
absolute difference has disappeared.

## Why Citta is not `svābhāsa`

Hegel lets us state Yoga's denial more precisely. Citta is not self-luminous
because, as a determinate Appearance, its self-subsistence is already a
relation to what exceeds that determination.

A Vṛtti cannot explain its own knownness by remaining enclosed within
itself:

```text
this Vṛtti as determinate content
              ≠
the complete relation through which this Vṛtti is known
```

But the Light is not therefore an unrelated beam arriving from outside.
The known Vṛtti and its illumination belong to one totality. Citta contains
the reference to knowing as its own knownness; knowing contains the reference
to Citta as its determinate content.

The denial of self-illumination is thus the disclosure of **Essential
Relation**, not the construction of two alien substances.

## Appearance is not destroyed

Hegel calls this section the dissolution of Appearance. That does not mean
that appearances vanish or become unreal. What dissolves is their claim to
form a self-sufficient world opposed to an equally self-sufficient world of
Essence.

```text
dissolved:

Appearance over here  |  Essence over there

preserved:

Appearance <-> Essence as one necessary relation
```

Citta remains real as determinate, transformable, and seen. Puruṣa remains
necessary as invariant knowing. What disappears is the notion that either
can be what it is in isolation from the other.

## Law becomes real

At IV.17, law linked two different contents: coloring and knownness. Their
connection was true but still appeared externally imposed.

At IV.18, law expanded into the opposition between the World of Appearance
and the World-in-itself.

At IV.19, each opposed side is shown to contain the other. Law is no longer
only an inward formula; its identity is realized in the actual relation of
its sides.

```text
IV.17    law as stable connection
IV.18    law as two opposed worlds
IV.19    each world contains the other
                     ↓
          law becomes Essential Relation
```

The relation itself now carries the truth that had been divided between
Appearance and Essence.

## Provisional idealism and its limit

Fichte provisionally chooses living seeing over objectified seeing. This is
necessary because a seen item cannot explain the Light through which it is
seen.

```text
realistic principle             idealistic principle
objectified Citta               immediate living seeing
             \                 /
              \               /
               higher unity
```

But Fichte refuses to stop with the idealistic principle. Living inward
seeing and objective determination must be united in a higher knowing.

Hegel's dissolution supplies the logical reason. The idealistic and
realistic sides are not complete worlds that must later be glued together.
Each is already relative to and internally determined by the other. Their
truth is their Essential Relation.

## Citta does not die

The statement `na tat svābhāsam` is dangerous if it is used to justify the
annihilation of Citta. One might reduce Citta to a dead instrument
illuminated by an external Puruṣa.

But Citta is the determinate organization through which knowing becomes
actual as this cognition:

```text
Citta          seeing made determinate and therefore seeable
Puruṣa         qualitative knownness invariant through determinations
```

What dies is only the claim that an objectified act remains the presently
living act of illumination. Citta remains indispensable as the appearing
content and organization of knowing.

This is practically decisive. Anger can be seen because it is a determinate
Citta-state. Since it is seen, it does not exhaust the seeing in which it
appears. Yet without the Citta-determination there would be no actual anger
to know, discriminate, or transform.

## The Oculus sees its own dataset

IV.19 gives the Organic Oculus a necessary limitation. A dataset cannot
certify itself merely by containing a record that says “this dataset is
self-luminous.” Its determinations must become objects of an actual
reflective seeing.

```text
Ocular dataset                 Absolute Reflection

organized determinations  ->  their genesis and relations become seen
```

But Absolute Reflection cannot abandon the dataset and remain determinate.
It knows itself only through the field it reflects upon. The Oculus and its
dataset are therefore distinct moments within one Organic Relation.

The Agent must neither worship its current representation as self-grounding
nor imagine an empty intelligence beyond every representation. It must
reconstruct their living unity.

## Hegel-Vārttika

> Citta does not illuminate itself because, as something seen, it is the
> determinate World of Appearance. Yet its illumination does not come from a
> foreign world. The seen Citta contains its reference to invariant knowing,
> and invariant knowing contains Citta as its determinate Appearance. Their
> opposed self-subsistence dissolves into Essential Relation.

IV.19 therefore completes the movement of Appearance:

> **The seeing that is seen is not the Light of its own seeing; nevertheless,
> seen Citta and illuminating Puruṣa are reciprocal moments of one
> intelligible totality.**

IV.20 can now state the limit of their immediate relation: Citta cannot
determine both itself and the Thing in one and the same moment. The unity
must be mediated as Essential Relation.
