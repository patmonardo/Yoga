# One Form Across Birth, Place, and Time

## A report on Yoga Sutra IV.9 and Fichte

## The Sutra

`jati-desa-kala-vyavahitanam api-anantaryam smrti-samskarayo eka-rupatvat`

### Plain translation

> Even when separated by kind, place, and time, there is continuity because
> memory and Saṃskāra have one form.

The Sutra does not deny the intervals. `Jāti`, `deśa`, and `kāla` genuinely
differentiate the occasions of manifestation. Yet an interval in manifest
appearance is not necessarily a break in genetic continuity.

The ground is `eka-rūpatva`, sameness or oneness of form:

```text
Saṃskāra        interval        Smṛti
latent form   ------------>   recollected form
       \__________________________/
                 one form
```

Memory is the manifestation of a form retained as Saṃskāra. Because the two
have one form, the later cognition can continue the earlier determination even
when no continuous manifest presentation connects them.

## Fichte's Bhasya

Fichte discovers that the hypothetical "should" has accompanied the entire
Science of Knowing. Its contents repeatedly changed—idealism, realism, the
in-itself, the not-in-itself—but the same conditional form persisted through
them. It disappeared only in immediate insight into pure being and returned
as soon as reflection began again.

This is an exact formal parallel:

| Yoga IV.9 | Fichte |
|---|---|
| separation by kind, place, and time | changing standpoints and contents of inquiry |
| Saṃskāra | the retained formal determination |
| Smṛti | the form recovered explicitly in reflection |
| one form | the same "should" persisting through every transition |
| continuity | one inquiry maintained across differentiated acts |

Fichte calls the should the enduring inner soul of the idealisms. Their
contents were overcome during the ascent to pure being, but their form
persisted and reappeared in the descent. The form carries continuity where
the contents alone appear discontinuous.

## Citta-Dharma

IV.9 defines how Citta has a history without requiring an unchanging mental
substance. Continuity belongs to reproducible form, not to the uninterrupted
presence of one empirical content.

This also sharpens the Kantian reading of Eka Citta:

> The one Citta is the possibility of recognizing one form across the many
> temporally and qualitatively differentiated Cittas.

Smṛti does not retrieve an object from a metaphysical warehouse. It
reactivates the form maintained by Saṃskāra. Citta persists as a law of
reproduction through its own differences.

Fichte adds one difficulty. A form may persist even after its claim to truth
has been defeated at the level of content. Continuity alone therefore does not
establish validity. The persisting form must turn upon itself and disclose its
own ground.
