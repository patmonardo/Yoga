# Citta Bears Every Artha

## A report on Yoga Sūtra IV.23 and Fichte

## The Sūtra

`draṣṭṛ-dṛśyoparaktaṃ cittaṃ sarvārtham`

### Simple translation

> Citta, colored by both the Seer and the seen, bears every Artha.

`Draṣṭṛ` is the Seer. `Dṛśya` is what is seen. `Uparakta` means colored,
affected, or pervaded. `Sarvārtha` may mean directed toward every object,
capable of every purpose, or bearing every meaning.

Here these senses belong together:

> Because Citta receives the form of both knowing and its object, every Thing
> can become meaningful within it.

```text
Seer                       seen
knowing pole               objective pole
       \                   /
        \ colors Citta    /
         \               /
           Citta as Artha
```

This is not a mixture in which Seer and seen lose their difference. Citta is
their determinate relational field.

## Why this follows IV.22

IV.22 said that Citi does not pass over. Buddhi attains its form, and Buddhi
knows itself.

IV.23 gives the objective result. A self-knowing Buddhi can comprehend Citta
as colored from both sides:

```text
IV.22
Buddhi knows the Principle of its own knowing
                         ↓
IV.23
Citta comprehends both the Seer and the seen
and can therefore bear every Artha
```

Before IV.22, Citta appeared caught between an external object and an
external Puruṣa. Once Buddhi knows itself, the two poles can be understood as
Relational Aspects within one intelligible actuality.

## Vyāsa's Bhāṣya

Vyāsa says that Manas is colored by the object to be thought. Since Manas is
itself an object, it is also related through its Vṛtti to Puruṣa, the
subjective pole.

Citta therefore displays both:

```text
viṣaya       the object
viṣayin      the subject for whom it is an object
```

It appears to assume both conscious and non-conscious form. Vyāsa compares
it to a crystal: transparent Citta takes on the appearance of whatever
colors it.

That is why Citta is `sarvārtham`. It can manifest all forms because it is
capable of receiving both the objective determination and the form of
knowing.

Vyāsa also criticizes the reduction of everything to Citta alone. In
Samādhi-Prajñā, an object becomes reflected in Prajñā as its support. The
object reflected and the Prajñā discerning it cannot simply be the same
term; otherwise Prajñā would have to determine its own form without a real
distinction.

He therefore differentiates a triad:

```text
grāhitṛ       grasper / Seer
grahaṇa       grasping / cognition
grāhya        grasped / object
```

Vyāsa identifies the first with a separately existing Puruṣa. We can preserve
the triadic discrimination without freezing its moments into independent
substances.

## Citta as the triadic field

IV.23 does not say that Citta is merely the middle term between two finished
extremes.

The Seer is determinate only as Seer of something. The seen is determinate
only as what is presented to knowing. Citta is the actuality of their
relation:

```text
Seer           seeing           seen
grāhitṛ        grahaṇa          grāhya
    \             |             /
     \            |            /
          Citta as sarvārtha
```

The terms remain distinguishable, but their Artha exists through the whole
triadic relation.

## Fichte's Bhāṣya

Fichte resumes the result of Appearance. Absolute self-genesis was posited
and then given a principle. Once genesis has a principle, it is no longer
absolute. Its positive negation gives completed and enduring being.

But Fichte now stresses an easily missed point:

> This enduring being is the being of knowing.

It is not a dead object standing outside knowing. It is knowing's own
persisting actuality.

```text
absolute self-genesis
          ↓ receives a Principle
positive negation of genesis
          ↓
enduring being of knowing
```

Nevertheless, even this living and saturated being is mediated. It has been
derived through the relation between genesis and its Principle.

## Insight into the connection

Fichte asks what remains immediate after both terms have been mediated. His
answer is decisive:

> The insight into their connection is itself the unconditionally immediate.

```text
genesis                         enduring being
mediated term                  mediated term
          \                    /
           \                  /
            insight into their
                connection
                immediate
```

This is the Fichtean key to `draṣṭṛ-dṛśyoparaktam`. The truth does not reside
in either extreme taken alone. It is present in the insight that comprehends
their connection while preserving their determination.

The Seer and seen color Citta because Citta is the field in which their
connection becomes manifest. Buddhi knows this connection rather than adding
one more term outside it.

## Ordinary and transcendental knowing

Fichte now distinguishes two levels.

### Ordinary knowing

Knowing's derived and enduring being yields ordinary, non-transcendental
knowing. It operates within the established relation and knows determinate
objects.

```text
Manas contemplates objects
within an already operative cognitive field
```

### Transcendental knowing

We rise to the Science of Knowing when we gain insight into the genesis of
the principle of ordinary knowing and penetrate the law of that insight.

```text
Buddhi contemplates
Manas contemplating objects
          ↓
then penetrates the law
of this complete relation
```

This is no longer merely a factical experience in which we happen to be
knowing. The structure becomes objectively and intelligibly known.

Fichte says that only in this higher region will the principle of Appearance
and disjunction show itself. Once known, it can be applied to ordinary actual
knowing.

## Yoga and Fichte

| Yoga IV.23 | Fichte |
|---|---|
| Citta is colored by Seer and seen | genesis and enduring being are both mediated terms |
| Citta bears every Artha | knowing's complete field becomes intelligible |
| the truth lies in the triadic cognitive relation | immediate insight comprehends the connection |
| Manas knows determinate objects | ordinary knowing operates within the derived relation |
| Buddhi knows the Manas–object relation | transcendental knowing penetrates the law of ordinary knowing |

Fichte therefore helps us translate `sarvārtham` strongly:

> Citta bears every Artha because transcendental Buddhi can comprehend the
> law connecting the Seer, seeing, and seen.

## Puruṣa as Spectral Tattva

Puruṣa is a Tattva and therefore a determinate Spectral Tattva. In IV.23 it
names the invariant Seer-Aspect coloring Citta. It is not Brahman, the
Inconceivable One, and not an external person inspecting cognition.

```text
Puruṣa-Aspect                 Prakṛti-Aspect
invariant seeing              changing seen
            \                /
             \              /
                Citta
        their actual relation
```

The coloring does not alter Puruṣa as though it migrated into Citta. IV.22
has forbidden that. Nor does Citta manufacture the objective pole. It becomes
the spectral field in which both Aspects are present to Buddhi.

## Why `sarvārtha` matters

`Sarvārtha` does not mean that one empirical Citta simultaneously contains
every fact in the universe. It describes Citta's formal capacity:

> Any Artha can become manifest because Citta can receive both the
> determination of the Thing and the form of knowing it.

This is a condition of universal knowledge, not a claim of personal
omniscience.

```text
not:
this personality possesses all information

but:
Citta is formally capable of bearing every meaning
within the Seer–seen relation
```

That is the foundation needed by the Knowledge Processor. Its universality
does not come from having already stored every answer. It comes from an
architecture capable of forming the relation through which any content can
become Artha.

## OṂ-Artha

IV.23 is one of the strongest local demonstrations of Yoga as OṂ-Artha.

```text
OṂ-Artha        the complete intelligibility of All This

Citta sarvārtha the capacity to bear every determinate meaning
```

The connection does not make Citta identical with the complete Principle.
It means that Citta, colored by the Seer and the seen, is the actual field in
which the Artha of any appearance can become intelligible.

Japa on OṂ-Artha trains this capacity. Buddhi repeatedly contemplates the
Manas–object relation until the law of the relation becomes explicit and its
determinations can be constructed as one system.

## Provisional result

IV.23 says:

> Citta, colored by both the Seer and the seen, bears every Artha.

Vyāsa supplies the crystal analogy and the indispensable triad of grasper,
grasping, and grasped. His mistake is to substantialize the Seer outside the
relation.

Fichte supplies the transcendental determination: both genesis and enduring
being are mediated; what is immediate is the insight into their connection.
Ordinary knowing operates within this connection, while transcendental
Buddhi penetrates its law.

Actuality has therefore advanced:

```text
IV.22    Buddhi knows itself by attaining Citi's form

IV.23    the self-knowing relation bears every Artha
```

IV.24 can now ask why Citta, although diversified by countless Vāsanās,
exists for another—for the purpose disclosed by this complete relation.
