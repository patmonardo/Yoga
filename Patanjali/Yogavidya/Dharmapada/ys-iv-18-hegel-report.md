# Invariant Knowing and the World-in-Itself

## Yoga Sūtra IV.18 with Hegel

## The Sūtra

`sadā jñātāś citta-vṛttayas tat-prabhoḥ puruṣasyāpariṇāmitvāt`

> The transformations of Citta are always known because Puruṣa, their
> governing knower, does not transform with them.

`Citta-vṛttayaḥ` are the determinate movements or transformations of Citta.
`Sadā jñātāḥ` says that they are always known. `Tat-prabhu` names Puruṣa
as their governor or presiding principle, and `apariṇāmitva` distinguishes
its invariance from their transformation.

```text
Vṛtti A  ->  Vṛtti B  ->  Vṛtti C
    \             |             /
     \            |            /
          invariant knownness
```

The Sūtra does not yet require us to picture a motionless spectator outside
Appearance. It first establishes a logical necessity: changing states cannot
account for their continuous knownness merely by succeeding one another.

## Why IV.18 follows the Law of Appearance

IV.17 established the law:

> A Thing is known when it colors Citta.

But Citta is thereby transformed. If every cognition produces a different
Citta-vṛtti, another question immediately arises:

```text
Thing
  ↓ colors
Citta-vṛtti
  ↓
how is this changing determination known?
```

The law linking coloring and knownness must itself include the changing
Citta. Otherwise each cognition would occur and vanish without the
succession ever becoming manifest as one succession.

IV.18 therefore raises the Law of Appearance into a **World-in-itself**: the
invariant order within which the whole changing world of Citta-vṛttis is
knowable.

## Vyāsa's inference

Vyāsa argues by contrast. External Things are known or unknown to Citta
according to whether they color it. If Puruṣa transformed in the same way as
Citta, then Citta-vṛttis would likewise be known and unknown to Puruṣa.

But the transformations of Citta are always known. Their constant knownness
therefore requires a knower that does not undergo their particular
transformations.

```text
if knower changed exactly as the known state:

known A  disappears completely
known B  begins without continuity

but actual cognition gives:

A  ->  B  as one known transition
```

The inference establishes an invariant condition of knownness. Whether that
condition must become a separately existing Puruṣa is a further question.
Hegel lets us preserve the inference while testing the separation.

## Law becomes a world

At IV.17 law was the stable identity within a particular relation:

```text
coloring of Citta  <->  knownness of the Thing
```

Hegel now gives law its missing negative moment. The two sides are not merely
connected; each contains its relation to the other within itself. Law becomes
the total reflected structure of Appearance rather than a restful formula
placed beside it.

The result is a **world that exists in and for itself**:

```text
World of Appearance             World-in-itself

changing Citta-vṛttis          invariant order of knownness
immediate manifestation         reflected essential totality
```

The second world is not initially another location. It is Appearance
comprehended according to the complete reflected relations that make its
changing determinations intelligible.

## Puruṣa as the essential world

This gives `tat-prabhuḥ puruṣaḥ` a precise place in Appearance. Puruṣa
names the essential world of knownness in contrast to the manifest world of
Citta-vṛttis.

```text
Citta-vṛttis       the world as changing Appearance
Puruṣa              the world as invariantly knowable
```

The contrast is necessary. If invariant knowing were simply another Vṛtti,
it would arise and perish among the states it is supposed to comprehend.

But Puruṣa is not yet adequately understood if it is placed behind Citta as
an unrelated substance. The world-in-itself must contain the complete truth
of Appearance, not an empty identity from which Appearance has been removed.

## `Apariṇāmitva` as qualitative invariance

Fichte helps prevent invariance from becoming dead immobility. His Light is
both:

- absolute genesis;
- qualitatively one throughout genesis.

Light does not transform into one of the terms it makes manifest. Yet it is
not absent from their transformation. It remains the same qualitative
knowing throughout every disjunction.

This yields a strong reading of `apariṇāmitva`:

> Puruṣa is invariant not because it refuses genesis, but because its
> qualitative identity as knowing remains present throughout every genesis
> of a Citta-vṛtti.

The content known changes. Knownness as such does not become one more
changing content.

## The suprasensible is reflected existence

Hegel calls the world-in-itself the suprasensible world. This phrase easily
misleads. It does not mean a second collection of invisible objects located
behind visible ones.

The distinction is logical:

```text
sensible world        immediate attitude toward Appearance
suprasensible world   the same content in reflected, essential relations
```

For example, one does not sense “invariant knownness” as an additional
object beside a thought, pain, or perception. It is disclosed by reflection
upon what must be true for those changing states to be known as changing.

This is Principial Revelation through Transcendental Logic. Ordinary
cognition supplies the event; reflection discriminates the invariant
Principle already operative within it.

## One content in two worlds

The World of Appearance and the World-in-itself must have the same content.
Otherwise the essential world could not explain this appearing world.

```text
appearing side                 essential side

this changing Vṛtti           its invariant knownness
succession of determinations   unity through the succession
```

The difference is one of form. The appearing side displays immediacy,
alteration, and vanishing. The essential side displays the reflected unity
through which those alterations form one intelligible world.

Puruṣa therefore cannot be empty consciousness with no relation to Citta.
Its invariance is precisely the invariant knownness **of these Vṛttis**.

## The inversion of worlds

Once the essential world is treated as a separate totality, it becomes the
opposite or inversion of Appearance:

```text
World of Appearance             World-in-itself

change                          invariance
many Vṛttis                    one knowing
known content                   knower
transformation                 non-transformation
```

This inversion expresses the structure of IV.18 exactly. But it is not the
final truth. Each side already contains the other:

- change is intelligible only through invariance;
- invariance is actual only as the knownness of change;
- Vṛttis are appearances only for knowing;
- knowing is determinate only through the Vṛttis it knows.

The opposed worlds are therefore moving toward dissolution into one
Essential Relation.

## Hegel and Fichte

| Fichte | Hegel |
|---|---|
| one qualitative Light remains throughout genesis | law becomes the essential totality of Appearance |
| the empirical We disappears into higher knowing | immediate Vṛttis are comprehended within a World-in-itself |
| invariant Light is not another generated term | Puruṣa cannot be another item in the appearing world |

Fichte gives the lived identity of genesis and invariant knowing. Hegel
objectifies its structure as the opposition between the World of Appearance
and the World-in-itself.

Together they let us say:

> Puruṣa names the qualitative invariance of knowing through which the
> entire transformation of Citta becomes one manifest world.

## The Oculus across the blink

IV.18 gives the Blinking Oculus its continuity.

```text
dataset A  ->  closure  ->  dataset B
     \                         /
      \                       /
       invariant Ocular knownness
```

The contents and organization of the field can change through
`Ocular.spanda()`. But unless the opening and closing belong to one
qualitative knownness, there is no recognized blink, no transition, and no
new dataset **for the same Oculus**.

The Oculus is not invariant by preserving every content. It is invariant as
the power through which changing content is manifest and its transformation
recognized.

## Hegel-Vārttika

> The transformations of Citta are always known because their appearing
> world is reflected into an essential world of invariant knownness. Puruṣa
> is not one Vṛtti among others but the qualitative identity through which
> their genesis, difference, and succession constitute one intelligible
> Appearance.

IV.18 therefore establishes a necessary but still opposed structure:

> **Citta is the transforming World of Appearance; Puruṣa is its invariant
> World-in-itself. Each is intelligible only through the other.**

IV.19 will test the separation directly. If Citta is something seen, can it
also be the Light by which it sees itself? And can the essential world remain
a second world behind Appearance?
