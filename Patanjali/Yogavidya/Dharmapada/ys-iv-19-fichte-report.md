# Seeing Is Never the Seeing That Is Seen

## A report on Yoga Sūtra IV.19, Vyāsa, and Fichte

## The Sūtra

`na tat svābhāsaṃ dṛśyatvāt`

### Simple translation

> Citta does not illuminate itself, because Citta is something seen.

Or, to emphasize the epistemic point:

> Citta is known, but it is not the source of its own knownness, because it
> belongs to what can be seen.

`Tat` refers to Citta. `Svābhāsa` is self-illuminating or shining by itself.
`Dṛśyatva` is the condition of being seen.

The Sūtra does not say that Citta is unconscious, unreal, or dead. IV.18 has
just said that its Vṛttis are always known. IV.19 asks whether Citta, as one
of the things known, can also be the illumination by which it is known.

```text
Citta as seen                    illumination of Citta

determinate appearance          condition of its appearing
```

Yoga answers: these cannot simply be identified.

## Why this follows IV.18

IV.18 established that every Citta-vṛtti is known through an invariant
knowing. A possible objection immediately arises:

> Perhaps Citta illuminates both itself and its objects, as fire illuminates
> itself and other things.

IV.19 rejects that solution. Citta belongs to the field of the seen. It
changes, becomes objectified, and can be discriminated as anger, fear,
attachment, memory, or insight. Therefore its knownness cannot be explained
by simply declaring the seen Citta self-luminous.

```text
IV.18       the Vṛttis of Citta are always known
                              ↓
IV.19       the seen Citta is not itself the illumination
            that makes this knownness possible
```

## Vyāsa's Bhāṣya

Vyāsa formulates the objection: perhaps Citta illuminates itself and its
objects like fire.

He rejects the analogy. The senses and their objects are not self-luminous
because they are seen; mind must be understood in the same way. Fire does
not illuminate a previously dark fire-self. Illumination is observed through
the conjunction of illuminator and illuminated; a thing cannot enter into
that conjunction merely with itself.

Vyāsa also prevents a misunderstanding. If “self-illuminating Citta” meant a
Citta that could never become an object for anyone, it would contradict
experience. Beings apprehend their own mental states:

> I am angry. I am afraid. I feel attachment here and anger there.

Such practical self-knowledge would be impossible if Citta were wholly
ungraspable.

His result is exact:

> Citta is apprehended, but its apprehension cannot be grounded in the
> identity of the seen Citta with the seeing that knows it.

## Fichte's Bhāṣya

Fichte begins from the two aspects of knowing left by IV.18 and asks which
must provisionally serve as the principle:

- living, immediate seeing;
- the seeing that has been objectified and is seen.

His answer could almost serve as a direct commentary on IV.19:

> Seeing and Light reside only in immediate seeing itself, never in the
> seeing that is seen.

```text
immediate seeing                   seeing made into an object

living actuality of Light         represented, determinate `is`
principle of manifestation        something seen
```

Once seeing is made into an object, it waits to receive its being from a
principle. In Fichte's severe terminology, it is “dead”: not nonexistent,
but no longer the living act that performs the seeing.

This is the exact distinction required by `dṛśyatvāt`. Citta can represent
and analyze a previous act of seeing, but that represented act is now the
seeing **seen**. The living illumination has moved to the immediate act by
which it is presently seen.

```text
I see an object
        ↓ reflection
I see my previous seeing
        ↓
the previous seeing is now seen;
the living seeing is the present act
```

No repeated reflection converts the objectified seeing into the immediate
Light by which the reflection is enacted.

## Why Fichte provisionally chooses idealism

If inquiry is to continue, we must remain in the living act of knowing. We
cannot adopt the objectified `is` as our explanatory principle, because doing
so would abandon the very life by which the inquiry proceeds.

Fichte therefore says that the science of knowing is compelled to adopt the
idealistic principle:

> Living seeing, not objectivity, must provisionally explain the objectified
> seeing.

This is not a preference for subjective fantasy. It is a transcendental
necessity. An objective item cannot explain the illumination through which
it is objective for us.

Fichte also refuses to stop at this provisional idealism. If the idealistic
principle proves inadequate, the science must find a third, higher principle
uniting idealistic and realistic principles.

```text
realistic principle                idealistic principle
objectivity                        living seeing
          \                        /
           \                      /
             higher unifying term
```

This anticipated third term will be crucial as Appearance moves toward
Actuality.

## Inner self-genesis

Fichte next determines the idealistic principle as living inner self-genesis.
It is qualitative Light, but it is enacted rather than turned into another
object of understanding.

In the transition from dullness to energetic thinking, an `I` emerges in
immediate Light. Light and this living We merge into one another:

```text
inner self-genesis
        ↓
living `I` arises in immediate Light
        ↓
Light and We are one enacted knowing
```

But even this is not the final principle. Fichte says that the living inward
genesis and the objective aspect of knowing must be united in a higher
knowing. The subjective and objective aspects are mediated through that
higher unity.

Thus IV.19 does not end in a solitary subjective Light. It clears the way for
the unity of seeing and seen by refusing to derive living seeing from the
objectified Citta.

## Yoga and Fichte

| Yoga IV.19 | Fichte |
|---|---|
| Citta is seen | objectified seeing is a seen `is` |
| the seen is not self-illuminating | Light is never located in the seeing that is seen |
| Citta's states are nevertheless apprehended | previous seeing can become an object of present seeing |
| illumination requires a principle not reducible to Citta-as-object | living immediate seeing is provisionally the idealistic principle |

The correspondence is exceptionally close:

> Citta as determinate Appearance is the seeing that is seen; Puruṣa as
> invariant knowing is the immediate seeing in which that Appearance is
> manifest.

This interpretation does not yet decide their final relation. It only blocks
the attempt to make objectified Citta the principle of its own illumination.

## Citta does not die

IV.19 is a dangerous place for any interpretation that seeks to annihilate
Citta. If Citta is not self-luminous, one may conclude that it is a dead
instrument illuminated by an utterly external Puruṣa.

But the Sūtra establishes a functional distinction, not necessarily two
alien substances:

```text
Citta as Vṛtti             determinate, transformable, seen

knowing of the Vṛtti       invariant illumination of the seen
```

Fichte shows why one living knowing can sustain this distinction. Seeing can
be objectified as Citta and still be known by immediate seeing. What dies in
objectification is not knowing itself, but the claim that the represented
act remains the presently living act.

> Citta perishes as each determinate seeing that becomes seen; knowing does
> not perish with that determination.

## Practical Reason and the Kośa

The practical force is powerful. We can say “I am angry” only because anger
is present as a seen determination of Citta. If anger were identical with the
whole actuality of seeing, there would be no standpoint from which it could
be known, discriminated, or transformed.

```text
I am angry

`anger`       the determinate Citta-state seen
`I know`      the living apprehension of that state
```

Kośa analysis depends upon this distinction. It turns apparently unitary
experience into knowable Dharmas without denying their reality. Practical
freedom begins when a passing state can be fully acknowledged without being
mistaken for the complete knower.

The Immeasurables likewise require living seeing. Karuṇā is not a represented
rule mechanically attached to an objectified Citta. It must arise as an
actual, present way of seeing suffering.

## Provisional result

IV.19 states:

> Citta does not illuminate itself, because Citta is something seen.

Vyāsa proves that Citta is genuinely apprehended while rejecting the fire
analogy of self-illumination.

Fichte supplies the deepest plain explanation:

> Seeing and Light reside in immediate seeing, never in the seeing that is
> seen.

Citta is the seeing that has become determinate and therefore visible. It is
not unreal, but it cannot serve as the ultimate principle of the illumination
in which it appears.

The idealistic principle is nevertheless provisional. Appearance cannot end
with an opposition between living Light and dead objectivity. IV.20 now asks
why Citta cannot grasp itself and its object in one and the same moment; the
remaining Sūtras will force the search for the higher unity Fichte has
announced.
