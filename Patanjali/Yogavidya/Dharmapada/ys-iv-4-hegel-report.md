# Constructed Cittas as Reflection

## Yoga Sutra IV.4 with Hegel

`nirmana-cittani-asmita-matra`

> Constructed Cittas arise from mere I-am-ness.

Fichte distinguished being's real self-construction from the ideal image
constructed by consciousness. Hegel refuses to leave these as two independent
processes. Their truth is **reflection**: a movement that differentiates
itself while remaining within itself.

Reflection has no inert substrate beneath it. Hegel describes it as a movement
from nothing to nothing and thereby back to itself. Each determination is
posited, negated, and returned into the movement that generated it.

This gives `asmita-matra` a precise office. Bare I-am-ness is not a little
substance manufacturing mental creatures. It is minimal self-reference—the
movement through which a determinate Citta is posited as mine and taken back
into one reflective activity.

```text
Asmitā       self-reference
    |
    +-> Citta 1  posited determination
    +-> Citta 2  negation and renewal
    +-> Citta 3  return within one reflective movement
```

The many constructed Cittas are the determinate moments of reflection, not
separate fictional minds. Their construction is real as positedness, but no
single moment is self-subsistent.

The Hegelian Varttika is:

> I-am-ness constructs Citta by reflecting determination into self-reference.
> The constructed Citta is neither an independent substance nor an illusion;
> it is the shine in which reflection gives itself determinate form.

Hegel thereby transforms the Fichtean question. We need not choose between a
real construction and an ideal copy. The ideal construction is a genuine
moment of being's reflection, provided it relinquishes its claim to stand
alone.
