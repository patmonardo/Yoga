# The Interruptions Are Relinquished like the Kleśas

## A report on Yoga Sūtra IV.28 and Fichte

## The Sūtra

`hānam eṣāṃ kleśavad uktam`

### Simple translation

> Their relinquishment is accomplished as already explained for the
> Kleśas.

The terms are concise:

- `hānam`: relinquishment, removal, or abandonment;
- `eṣām`: of these—the other Pratyayas arising in the openings;
- `kleśavat`: as with the Kleśas;
- `uktam`: it has already been explained.

Yoga does not introduce a new mechanism. The interruptions of Viveka and the
Kleśas have the same basic structure and therefore require the same kind of
knowledge.

```text
Kleśa                         interrupting Pratyaya

conditions cognition         arises from Saṃskāra
while hiding its genesis     in an opening of Viveka
             \              /
              \            /
          relinquished through
       insight into its Principle
```

## Why contingency can rule

IV.26 gave Citta an immanent inclination toward Viveka. IV.27 showed that
older Saṃskāras can still produce other cognitions in the openings of that
movement.

The resulting contingency has a precise form:

```text
pāramārthika Principle
is known but not yet continuously actual
                    ↓
an opening remains
                    ↓
a retained formation supplies the determination
                    ↓
the contingent Pratyaya is accepted as immediate truth
```

Contingency “rules” not because the pāramārthika has become false, but
because its actuality has not yet comprehended every condition of Citta.

The interruption reveals the exact point where necessity remains abstract.

## Why these Pratyayas are like Kleśas

A Kleśa is not merely an unpleasant feeling. It is a formative contradiction
that governs cognition and action while presenting its conditioned result as
immediate reality.

The interrupting Pratyaya does the same thing:

```text
Saṃskāra supplies an inherited determination
                    ↓
Citta holds the determination to be true
                    ↓
its conditioned genesis disappears
                    ↓
the result appears immediate and self-grounding
```

This is why the alternative cognition can interrupt Viveka even after its
truth has been understood. Its power lies in the established Being of its
Saṃskāra, not in the strength of its argument.

## `Hāna` is not Citta-cide

The Sūtra says the Pratyayas are to be relinquished. It does not say that the
faculty producing cognition must be destroyed.

```text
not:

Pratyaya arises ──► suppress Citta


but:

Pratyaya arises
      ↓
trace it to its Saṃskāra
      ↓
comprehend the contradiction sustaining it
      ↓
withdraw the holding that gives it governing force
```

What is abandoned is the Pratyaya's claim to determine truth independently
of the pāramārthika Principle.

The content may remain available as memory, representation, or practical
information. It ceases to govern Citta as an unquestioned Dharma.

## The earlier Yoga method

Yoga has already supplied two complementary operations for the Kleśas:

```text
subtle formation
returned toward its originating ground

active Vṛtti
made transparent through meditation
```

Applied here, the method becomes:

```text
other Pratyaya
      ↓ discrimination
its active form becomes visible
      ↓ return to origin
its supporting Saṃskāra is comprehended
      ↓
the formation loses its independent necessity
```

The aim is not blankness. It is complete genetic intelligibility.

## Prajñā masters Saṃskāra through Saṃskāra

Because Prajñā is itself Saṃskāra, the process is not a battle between pure
Knowing and formation as such.

```text
ordinary Saṃskāra
retains the result while concealing its genesis

Prajñā-Saṃskāra
retains insight into the genesis and its Principle
```

Prajñā relinquishes the interrupting formation by comprehending it. Its own
Sattvic durability allows Viveka to resume after the contingent Pratyaya has
arisen.

The victory is not that Citta has ceased to contain history. Its history has
ceased to dictate truth without being known.

## Fichte's Bhāṣya: the methodological maze

Fichte uses the brief corresponding passage to remind his listeners of the
extremely penetrating methodological remarks made at the turning points of
the Science. They must keep these remarks present if they are to find their
way through the maze ahead.

This is not an incidental classroom warning. Method is the practical form of
Prajñā-Saṃskāra.

```text
isolated philosophical result
can be memorized and misapplied

method retained through the whole movement
allows the genesis of each result to be reconstructed
```

Without living possession of the method, an earlier form of cognition can
reappear and occupy a gap in the argument. One may repeat a correct
proposition while understanding it through the very factical assumptions
that the proposition was meant to overcome.

## Method as the relinquishment of contingency

Fichte has insisted upon several methodological rules:

- never isolate a determination from the whole context that generates it;
- distinguish factical discovery from genetic comprehension;
- do not allow the factical `we` to become the hidden ground of truth;
- turn from the form of insight to its content so that the content explains
  the form;
- let the unresolved problem arise through the movement itself rather than
  inserting an arbitrary solution.

These rules perform the work of `hāna`.

```text
contingent philosophical assumption
                 ↓ tested by method
its hidden genesis becomes explicit
                 ↓
its claim to govern the Science is relinquished
```

The method does not guarantee that no other cognition will ever arise. It
ensures that an arising cognition can be returned to its ground and denied
false necessity.

## Absolute Modality

IV.28 gives the relation between contingency and necessity:

```text
contingency
another Pratyaya arises from a retained Saṃskāra

necessity
the Pratyaya is comprehended through the same genetic law as the Kleśas

actual freedom
its contingent arising no longer grants it authority over truth
```

Absolute necessity does not mean that contingency never appears. It means
that contingency is unable to constitute an independent ground.

This is a Middle Way between two errors:

```text
deny contingency entirely        surrender truth to contingency
“nothing can arise”               “whatever arises must rule”
                 \               /
                  \             /
              comprehend its genesis
              and relinquish its claim
```

## Yoga and Fichte

| Yoga IV.28 | Fichte |
|---|---|
| interrupting Pratyayas are treated like Kleśas | apparent novelties must be navigated through the already established method |
| each interruption is traced to Saṃskāra | each determination is reconstructed from its genetic context |
| `hāna` withdraws its governing force | factical assumptions lose authority when their genesis is exposed |
| Citta is not destroyed | the living art of Knowing is preserved |
| Viveka becomes increasingly continuous | method carries one through the speculative maze |

## Provisional result

IV.28 says:

> An alternative cognition arising from Saṃskāra is relinquished in the same
> way as a Kleśa: its concealed genesis is made intelligible, and its claim
> to govern Citta is withdrawn.

Fichte's terse instruction is exact. At this stage, progress depends upon
retaining the complete method of the Science. Otherwise a previously
overcome factical assumption can return inside a new philosophical form.

The transition through Absolute Modality now reads:

```text
IV.26    necessity becomes Citta's inclination

IV.27    contingency appears in the openings of that inclination

IV.28    contingency is returned to its ground and loses
         the authority of independent necessity
```

IV.29 can therefore ask what becomes possible when even the highest result
of discrimination is no longer appropriated.
