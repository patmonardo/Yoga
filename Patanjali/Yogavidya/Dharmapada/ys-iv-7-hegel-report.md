# Uncolored Karma and Absolute Ground

## Yoga Sutra IV.7 with Hegel

`karma-asukla-akrsnam yogina trividham itaresam`

> The yogin's karma is neither white nor black; that of others is threefold.

Fichte read the yogin's act through the pure should: activity determined from
within rather than by an awaited empirical result. Hegel asks what makes this
inner determination concrete rather than merely formal. His answer is
**ground**.

In absolute ground, ground and grounded are not two independent things. The
ground makes itself into the grounded, and the grounded is the ground in
posited form. Form and essence are moments of one mediation.

White and black karma remain determinations within opposition. Each action is
grounded through the result by which it returns to and further determines the
agent. The yogin's act is neither because its ground is not one side of this
opposition.

```text
ordinary karma   act -> colored result -> conditioned agent

yogin's karma    ground expresses itself in the act
                 without manufacturing a colored identity
```

Neither-white-nor-black is therefore not absence of determination. It is the
unity of ground and grounded in which the act's truth is immanent to its form.

Hegel's Varttika corrects a possible weakness in the Fichtean should:

> Free action cannot rest only upon a formal inward command. Its form must be
> the self-expression of its essential ground.

The yogin's karma remains effective action, but it does not return as a
separate positive or negative determination deposited upon Citta.
