# One Thing and the Different Paths of Citta

## Yoga Sūtra IV.15 with Hegel

## The Sūtra

`vastu-sāmye citta-bhedāt tayor vibhaktaḥ panthāḥ`

> Although the Thing is the same, because Cittas differ, the paths of the
> Thing and its cognition are distinct.

`Vastu-sāmya` holds the Thing in identity. `Citta-bheda` introduces a real
difference among the cognitions through which it appears. `Tayoḥ` then
distinguishes the two sides, the Thing and its cognition, and `vibhaktaḥ
panthāḥ` assigns them different paths.

```text
                   one Thing
                       |
          ---------------------------
          |            |            |
       Citta A       Citta B       Citta C
          |            |            |
     appearance A  appearance B  appearance C
```

The Sūtra does not deny that Citta conditions Appearance. It denies that the
different conditioning of Cittas divides the Thing into private objects.

## Why IV.15 follows IV.14

IV.14 first secured `vastu-tattva`:

> The truth of the Thing consists in the unity of its transformation.

Only after the Thing has been established as one transformation can Yoga
introduce different Cittas without falling into private idealism.

```text
IV.14                              IV.15

one Guṇa-transformation              different Cittas enter
constitutes the Thing        ->       different relations with it
```

The order matters. If Citta-difference came first, the common Thing could be
dismissed as a construction projected by several cognizers. IV.15 instead
asks how one objective transformation can become differently manifest along
different cognitive paths.

## Vyāsa's objective safeguard

Vyāsa identifies the two paths as those of `vastu` and `jñāna`, the Thing
and its cognition. One Thing can become the support of many Cittas, while
their cognitions differ according to dharma, adharma, avidyā, attachment,
and right seeing.

His decisive question is:

> By which Citta was the common Thing constructed?

No one Citta can account for the object's presence to the others. Nor can a
private construction in one Citta explain why other Cittas encounter the
same resistant and corrigible object. The Thing is therefore
`svapratiṣṭha`, established in itself.

This does not make cognition irrelevant. It establishes the distinction
needed to investigate cognition without dissolving its object.

## From property to matter

Hegel begins IV.15 with a difficulty produced by the preceding analysis.
IV.14 showed that a Thing is not an empty substrate behind its properties.
Its properties are the determinate ways it expresses itself in relation.

But once property is recognized as the Thing's real subsistence, it appears
to become independent of this particular Thing. Hegel calls this reflected,
self-continuous property **matter** or **stuff**.

For example, color is first treated as a property of a particular object.
Reflection then considers coloring matter as something that can continue
through many objects. The determination is no longer enclosed within one
Thing:

```text
property of this Thing
          ↓ reflection into itself
self-continuous matter
          ↓
present across different Things
```

Hegel is not recommending obsolete physical theories of caloric or luminous
fluid. He is exposing a logical movement: a determination first dependent
upon a relation claims self-subsistence and continuity across the particular
relations in which it appears.

## Two forms of self-subsistence

The Thing now divides into two moments:

```text
positive self-subsistence       negative self-subsistence

matter continuous with itself  this Thing as a determinate one
across different Things         distinguished from other Things
```

The matter does not belong exclusively to this Thing; it continues into
others. The “this” gathers matters into one determinate configuration but
does not create their content.

This gives Hegel an exact form for the two paths of IV.15:

- the **path of the Thing** preserves the objective continuity of its
  determinations;
- the **path of Citta** gives those determinations a particular reflected
  configuration in cognition.

The same objective content can therefore continue through different
Citta-configurations without being reducible to any one of them.

## `Vastu-sāmya`: continuity through difference

The sameness of the Thing cannot mean featureless identity. IV.14 already
showed that the Thing is one transformation expressed through properties.
Its sameness in IV.15 is the continuity of that transformation across
different cognitive relations.

Consider one object encountered by several Cittas:

```text
objective Guṇa-transformation
               |
       -------------------
       |        |        |
   desired   feared   neutrally known
```

The desire, fear, and neutral cognition are not properties stored inside the
Thing as three finished contents. They arise through different Citta-paths.
Yet the common objective determination is not invented by those responses.
It continues through them and permits their comparison and correction.

`Vastu-sāmya` therefore means identity robust enough to appear differently.
If every difference of appearance produced a different object, there would
be no Thing whose path could be distinguished from cognition.

## `Citta-bheda`: determinate configuration

Each Citta is a concrete “this”: a determinate unity of Saṃskāra, Vāsanā,
attention, affect, and prior knowledge. These factors configure how the
objective determination becomes manifest.

```text
same Vastu
    |
    +-- attachment-conditioned Citta  -> desirable Appearance
    +-- aversion-conditioned Citta    -> threatening Appearance
    +-- confused Citta                -> distorted Appearance
    +-- disciplined Citta             -> adequate Appearance
```

The difference belongs to cognition's path. But it is not arbitrary. A
Citta can disclose only what arises through its actual relation to the
Thing, and the Thing's objective path constrains that relation.

Thus Citta neither passively copies a finished object nor freely creates its
world.

## The danger of the mere “also”

Hegel shows that the Thing, when treated as a collection of self-subsistent
matters, becomes a mere “also”:

```text
this amount of matter A
also this amount of matter B
also this amount of matter C
                    = this Thing
```

Its unity is then only quantitative and external. The matters remain
indifferent to the bond that collects them.

This is also a danger for Citta-analysis. We could describe a cognition as:

```text
sensation + memory + affect + concept + attention
```

But an inventory does not explain why these factors form **this one
cognition of this one Thing**. It gives an aggregate, not the genesis of
Appearance.

The distinction of paths therefore cannot be the last word. We must preserve
the Thing and Citta as distinct without reducing their encounter to an
external addition of independent components.

## Hegel and Fichte

Fichte says that genesis and seeing converge in an actual Appearance. Hegel
now supplies the objective difference within that convergence.

| Fichte | Hegel |
|---|---|
| seeing is the living genesis of determinate Appearance | a determination continues objectively across different configurations |
| Appearance is not a passive copy | property becomes self-continuous matter rather than a private image |
| one Light remains present through differentiated acts | the “this” and its matters form distinct moments of concrete existence |

Together they yield a balanced result:

> The Thing is not produced by a private Citta, but its determinate
> Appearance exists only through the living cognitive path in which the
> Thing becomes manifest.

## Objective correction

The distinction of paths makes error and correction intelligible.

If each Citta produced its own object, one appearance could never be judged
more adequate than another. There would be only unrelated private worlds.
Because the Thing maintains its own path, Citta can discover that its
conditioned appearance fails to express the objective determination
adequately.

```text
Citta's appearance
        ↓ tested through renewed relation
objective path of the Thing
        ↓
correction of cognition
```

Mastery of Citta therefore does not mean mastering the Thing by projection.
It means freeing the cognitive path from conditions that prevent the Thing's
own transformation from appearing adequately.

## Hegel-Vārttika

> The Thing remains the same because its determinate content continues
> through the different relations in which it appears. Each Citta gathers
> that content into a particular cognitive configuration, but this “this” of
> cognition neither creates nor exhausts the Thing. The objective continuity
> of the Thing and the conditioned unity of cognition therefore have
> distinct paths.

IV.15 gives Appearance its necessary duality:

> **One self-continuous Vastu becomes manifest through many determinate
> Cittas, while the paths of objective transformation and cognitive
> configuration remain distinct.**

But Hegel has also exposed the instability of this distinction. If Thing and
Citta remain only externally connected, their unity becomes a mere “also.”
IV.16 must therefore test whether the Thing depends upon any one Citta and
what remains when that particular cognitive relation is absent.
