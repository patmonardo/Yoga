# The One Guṇa-Nature of Manifestation

## A report on Yoga Sūtra IV.13 and Fichte

## The Sūtra

`te vyakta-sūkṣmā guṇātmānaḥ`

### Simple translation

> Those Dharmas, whether manifest or subtle, have the Guṇas as their nature.

`Te`, “those,” refers to the Dharmas whose temporal paths were distinguished
in IV.12. `Vyakta` is manifest, explicit, or developed. `Sūkṣma` is subtle,
latent, or not yet manifest. `Guṇātman` says that their nature or self is the
Guṇas.

The force of the Sūtra lies in that final compound:

> Manifest and subtle Dharmas are not two orders of being. Both are
> Guṇa-nature.

Their difference is real, but it is a difference within one qualitative
nature.

```text
subtle Dharma                         manifest Dharma
not explicitly displayed             explicitly displayed
          \                            /
           \______ Guṇātman _________/
             one Guṇa-nature
```

## Why this follows IV.12

IV.12 said that past and future exist according to their own forms because
the Dharmas differ along their temporal paths.

IV.13 now prevents those different paths from breaking reality into separate
worlds. A Dharma that is subtle and a Dharma that is manifest have the same
kind of nature: both are determinations of the Guṇas.

The future plant is not already manifest as a miniature plant hidden inside
the seed. But neither is it sheer nothing. Its determination is subtle within
the same Guṇa-process that becomes manifest in the developed plant.

```text
IV.12        difference of temporal path
                         ↓
IV.13        one Guṇa-nature across the difference
```

The Thing itself has not yet emerged. IV.13 establishes the qualitative
field of its determinations. IV.14 will show how their transformation becomes
one Thing.

## Fichte's Bhāṣya

Fichte begins from a decisive change in the status of knowing. Previously a
free `We` adopted a premise and followed the manifest relation between that
premise and its consequence. Now absolute manifestness reaches the premise
itself. The premise no longer rests upon our decision to posit it; it posits
itself.

```text
secondary knowing

We posit a premise  →  a consequence becomes manifest


the new region

manifestness reaches the premise itself
                    →  the premise posits itself
```

This annuls the freedom of the presupposing `We`. Knowing is encountered as
immediately self-grounding and from itself.

Yet the self-positing premise is still a **premise**. As such, it posits a
consequence and a context. It therefore supplies the principle of
Appearance, but it is not yet the purely self-contained Absolute:

```text
self-positing premise
          ↓
      consequence
          ↓
        context

= the form of Appearance
```

Appearance begins when absolute manifestness enters a relation of premise
and consequence.

## The Light is an absolute `from`

Fichte then releases the propositional form and retains its content:

> The Light is an absolute `from`.

Light is the essential, qualitative, and material oneness of knowing. It
cannot be captured as another known object. Whenever knowing is made an
object, its living actuality lies in the knowing by which that object is
known. Repeating the reflection does not alter this:

```text
knowing represented as an object
              ↑
the living knowing that represents it
              ↑
any further act of reflection
```

The qualitative oneness of Light must be immediately enacted. But this Light
is not a dead, self-enclosed `is`. It is intrinsically an absolute `from`—an
original genesis.

The two determinations are inseparable:

```text
qualitatively one Light = absolute genesis
```

Light is not first complete and then made productive. Its changeless
qualitative oneness consists in permeating the `from`.

## Disjunction in the `from`

Every `from` contains disjunction. But it does not begin with terms that were
already different independently of their genesis.

```text
not:

finished term a + finished term b + an external connection


but:

             absolute “from”
               /        \
              a          b
```

The terms are produced and distinguished as terms only through the `from`.
Because the one Light is identical with the `from`, the same qualitative
Light spreads across both terms without losing its oneness.

Fichte's claim is therefore stronger than the generic assertion that
different things participate in one consciousness:

> The qualitative oneness of Light and the genesis of its disjoined terms
> are one act.

The Light neither stands behind the terms as a hidden substrate nor observes
their distinction from outside. It is livingly present throughout the
disjunction that produces them.

## Yoga and Fichte

| Yoga IV.13 | Fichte |
|---|---|
| `vyakta-sūkṣma`, manifest and subtle | terms differentiated within the `from` |
| the terms differ in mode | disjunction really produces distinct terms |
| `guṇātman`, one qualitative Guṇa-nature | one qualitative Light permeates the disjunction |
| subtle and manifest remain within one Nature | the terms never become independent of their genesis in Light |

The correspondence is not an identity between Guṇas and Light. Fichte's
specialized object of study is Light—the living actuality of knowing. Yoga's
claim has a wider natural scope. With the single word `guṇātman`, Yoga says
that all manifest and subtle Dharmas have one triadically differentiated
nature.

Fichte makes one structure within this claim luminous: qualitative oneness
does not exclude disjunction. It is the oneness that remains itself in the
genesis of distinct terms.

## Appearance, but not yet the Thing

At IV.13 we have:

```text
one qualitative nature
          ↓
manifest / subtle difference
```

This is enough for Appearance to begin, but it is not enough to constitute a
Thing. We have differentiated terms within one qualitative field; we have
not yet shown that their transformation is one.

That further step belongs to IV.14:

```text
IV.13                               IV.14

one Guṇa-nature across              unity of transformation
manifest/subtle difference    →     constitutes the Thing
```

Fichte makes the same transition possible. At IV.13 the one Light permeates
the original `from` and its terms. In IV.14 that structure will be animated
as a comprehended transformation whose subordinate divisions remain within
one genesis.

## Citta-Dharma

The manifest content of a Citta and the subtle determination capable of
becoming manifest are not alien kinds of entity. Both belong to the same
Guṇa-nature.

```text
subtle Saṃskāra / Vāsanā
             ↓ manifestation
manifest Citta-determination
```

But IV.13 is not reducible to this psychological example. `Guṇātman` applies
to the Dharmas as such. It establishes the common qualitative nature within
which both Citta and its appearing object can be differentiated.

This is Yoga's power. Fichte gives a concentrated science of how the one
Light remains qualitatively identical through disjunction. Yoga gives the
simple universal determination: manifest and subtle Dharmas alike are
Guṇa-nature.

## Provisional result

IV.12 gave the Dharmas distinct temporal paths. IV.13 establishes that their
manifest and subtle forms nevertheless possess one qualitative nature.

Fichte's contribution is the heart of his science of knowing:

> The one Light is an absolute `from`. Its disjoined terms arise only through
> its genesis, and its unchanged qualitative oneness permeates them both.

Yoga states the corresponding natural determination with even greater
economy:

> `te vyakta-sūkṣmā guṇātmānaḥ` — manifest or subtle, those Dharmas are
> Guṇa-nature.

The qualitative field of Appearance is now present. The Thing will emerge
only when IV.14 determines this differentiated field as one transformation.
