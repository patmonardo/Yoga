# Complete Attention and the Range of Mastery

## A report on Yoga Sūtra I.40 and Fichte

## The Sūtra

`paramāṇu-parama-mahattvānto 'sya vaśīkāraḥ`

### Relatively necessary ordinary translation

> One's mastery extends from the smallest atom to the greatest magnitude.

This translation gives access to the words:

- `paramāṇu`: the smallest atom or minimal determination;
- `parama-mahattva`: the greatest magnitude or maximal whole;
- `antaḥ`: extending to the limits;
- `asya`: of this practitioner or Citta;
- `vaśīkāraḥ`: mastery, command, or complete availability.

But the ordinary translation does not yet give the Sūtra's philosophical
meaning. Fichte does:

> Mastery is complete attention capable of constructing and holding any
> determination, from the minimal point to the maximal whole, until Truth
> generates its own insight.

## Why Prajñāpāda begins here

Dharmapāda completed the Sphere of Essence. Citi-Śakti became fully actual
through the Science of Citta.

Prajñāpāda now returns to the logically prior Sphere of Being:

```text
minimal determination                     maximal whole
paramāṇu                                   parama-mahattva
       \                                   /
        complete range of immediate Being
```

Before Reflection, Appearance, and Actuality can be reconstructed, the
Scientist of Knowing must be able to hold a determination simply and
completely.

I.40 specifies that capacity.

## `Vaśīkāra` is not domination

Mastery does not primarily mean exercising occult control over tiny and
enormous objects.

It means that the scale or complexity of a determination does not dislodge
Knowing from complete attention.

```text
minimal content
one exact distinction
        ↓ held completely

maximal content
the articulated whole
        ↓ held completely

same mastery
undivided attention adequate to the determination
```

The object is “under command” because Citta neither scatters across its
parts nor substitutes fantasy for its actual construction.

## Fichte's sole serious prerequisite

Fichte says that the sole serious prerequisite for the Science of Knowing is
experience in fundamental scientific inquiry.

He does not require this because the Science presupposes a store of technical
information. Particular information is neither presupposed nor accepted
without qualification.

Scientific inquiry matters because it awakens and exercises:

> full, complete attention.

```text
not primarily:
possession of scientific facts

but:
the trained power to remain wholly within
one exact construction
```

This makes I.40 the entrance requirement for Scientists-of-Knowing in
training.

## Complete attention

Fichte defines complete attention as the Spirit throwing all its power into
the present object, placing itself there, and becoming completely absorbed.

```text
Spirit
   ↓ places its whole power in
the present construction
   ↓
no room remains for an alien thought or fantasy
```

Complete attention does not stare passively at an object. It inhabits and
sustains the construction through which the determination can become
manifest.

This is the first practical form of Samāpatti.

## Complete attention has no degrees

Fichte makes an important distinction:

```text
scattered attention
can be weaker or stronger, better or worse
and therefore has degrees

complete attention
fills the Spirit completely
and therefore has no degrees
```

Complete and incomplete attention are not merely different quantities of
the same state. They are logically contradictory forms.

```text
complete
nothing alien occupies the construction

incomplete
the construction shares Citta with fugitive contents
```

This gives `paramāṇu-parama-mahattva` a deeper meaning. The mastery is not
measured by how much mental force is applied. The same indivisible attention
is present at both extremes.

## The smallest and greatest

The two limits can be read scientifically:

### `Paramāṇu`

The minimal determinateness that can be constructed and discriminated:

```text
one point
one distinction
one exact qualitative determination
```

### `Parama-mahattva`

The maximal whole within which every subordinate determination must be held
in its relation:

```text
the complete theorem
the whole Science
the encyclopedic Principle
```

Mastery is the ability to move across this range without losing either the
precision of the smallest term or the unity of the greatest whole.

## The training procedure

Fichte gives a precise three-stage exercise:

```text
1. Construct a specific Concept internally.

2. Hold the complete construction together.

3. Insight springs up by itself like lightning.
```

The first two are the work of trained attention. The third is not an act
manufactured by the empirical mind.

This is Samāpatti as a reproducible Science rather than an accidental flash.

## Truth creates itself

Fichte's decisive statement is:

> We do not create Truth; Truth creates itself by its own Power wherever the
> conditions of its creation are present.

```text
Scientist constructs and sustains the conditions
                         ↓
Truth produces its own manifestness
                         ↓
insight appears at the same rate in Knowing as such
```

The Scientist does not invent the result. Nor does an external Being insert
it into Citta. The construction becomes the field of Truth's own
self-production.

This is the Prajñā form of `vaśīkāra`.

## Fast and slow minds are irrelevant

Fichte says the Science is addressed neither to quick minds nor slow minds,
but to mind as such—provided it can attend.

```text
speed of associative Manas
does not produce the insight

complete construction held in Buddhi
provides the conditions in which Truth manifests
```

Once the construction is complete, the empirical speed of thought has no
role in the lightning-flash of insight.

This is important for trainees. Difficulty does not prove lack of innate
genius. It usually identifies a construction that has not yet been held in
complete clarity.

## Failure is diagnostic

If manifestness does not arise, Fichte does not conclude that Truth is
unavailable. The construction faded or distraction intervened.

```text
no insight
    ↓ inspect
Was the Concept constructed exactly?
Was the whole construction sustained?
Did an alien determination enter?
```

This makes the method testable. Failure points back to the conditions of
attention rather than inviting invention of a substitute conclusion.

## The return of illusion

Fichte then explains why a previously exposed illusion can return.

Suppose one has seen that in the one self-same Knowing, the division into an
independent subject and object arises only within changeable Appearance.

The illusion may recur. But Fichte reverses the usual explanation:

> The insight does not leave because the illusion enters; the illusion
> enters because the insight has left.

```text
complete insight sustained
no independent place exists for the old illusion

insight no longer completely inhabited
an opening appears
the old subject-object construction returns
```

This anticipates IV.27, where other Pratyayas arise in the openings of
Viveka from Saṃskāras.

## To see is to become the insight

For Fichte, genuine insight is not a proposition possessed by an empirical
subject.

```text
not:

I stand outside and remember that the theorem is true


but:

I dissolve into, root myself in, and become the insight
```

If the old illusion repeatedly governs, one entered only halfway and never
became fully established in the insight.

This is Samāpatti: Citta takes the form of the known Principle so completely
that no foreign self-position remains beside it.

## Obstacles to complete attention

Fichte collects the obstacles under deficient love of Science.

### Pride or self-valuation

The empirical self resents a Truth it did not already discover. It attends
not to understand but to catch the Science in an error.

```text
divided purpose
understand the construction + defend self-valuation
                         ↓
complete attention becomes impossible
```

### Self-enjoyment

The empirical self enjoys the free play of its faculties and calls invention
“thinking for oneself.” It resists a Science that places thought under the
rule of one self-sustaining Truth.

### Trained memory

The merely empirical mind wants fixed results and externally repeatable
formulas. When the same content is genetically rearranged under new symbols,
it believes the Science has contradicted itself.

```text
external schematic coherence
same words and stored results

inner genetic coherence
the same Principle reappears through transformed determinations
```

### Weak love of Science

Anyone who seeks something besides Science through Science divides
attention. Fichte says even moral improvement, though beautiful, is too
external if made the governing purpose of Pure Science.

## Science must be loved for itself

Fichte calls Science the sweet expression of the Absolute. Like the
Absolute, it must be loved for itself.

This does not make practice irrelevant. It establishes the correct order:

```text
first
Truth known and loved for itself

then
purification and right activity arise immanently
from living Knowledge
```

This repeats the Dharmapāda result: saintliness is not an external reward or
purpose imposed upon Knowing. It is the practical actuality of Knowledge.

## Prayer and the Science of Knowing

It is delightful that Fichte mentions a general day of prayer at the opening
of this lecture. But the lecture itself distinguishes orientation from the
work of Knowing.

Prayer may gather Citta and turn it toward the Absolute. Complete attention
must still construct and sustain the conditions in which Truth manifests
itself.

```text
prayer
orientation and recollection
        ↓
complete attention
scientific construction
        ↓
self-producing insight
```

Neither substitutes for the other.

## Prajñā and Samāpatti

Prajñā is not a decorative insight added to Citta. It is the Being of Knowing
established through complete attention.

```text
construction sustained
        ↓
Truth creates manifestness
        ↓
Citta assumes the form of the insight
        ↓
Prajñā-Saṃskāra
the insight becomes an enduring determination of Knowing
```

I.40 gives the mastery required for the Samāpatti sequence beginning at
I.41.

## From Being to Essence

Because we studied Dharmapāda first, the deeper importance of I.40 is now
visible.

```text
Prajñāpāda / Sphere of Being
construct and hold the determination
from minimal point to maximal whole
                    ↓
Dharmapāda / Sphere of Essence
reconstruct its Reflection, Appearance,
Actuality, and Necessity
```

Being supplies the content that Essence reflects. Essence supplies the
Science through which immediate Being is known in its complete ground.

Together they form our Abhidharma.

## Instructions for Scientists-of-Knowing in training

1. Construct the stated determination; do not substitute an association.
2. Hold all of its moments together without allowing one to fade.
3. Do not invent the expected conclusion.
4. Wait within the complete construction for manifestness to produce itself.
5. If insight does not arise, inspect the construction and attention.
6. If an old illusion returns, restore the insight rather than fighting the
   illusion as an independent power.
7. Distinguish genetic coherence from memorized verbal consistency.
8. Do not convert insight into private self-valuation.
9. Love the Science for its own Truth.
10. Practice until mastery extends from the smallest determination to the
    systematic whole.

## Decisive points

1. `Vaśīkāra` is command of complete attention, not domination of objects.
2. `Paramāṇu` and `parama-mahattva` mark the full range from minimal
   determination to maximal whole.
3. Complete attention has no degrees; scattered attention does.
4. The Scientist constructs and sustains the conditions but does not invent
   Truth.
5. Truth creates its own insight wherever its conditions are complete.
6. Mental speed is irrelevant once the construction is held.
7. An illusion returns because insight has ceased to be fully inhabited.
8. Genuine insight is something Knowing becomes, not merely possesses.
9. Pride, self-enjoyment, trained memory, and subordinate motives divide
   attention.
10. I.40 specifies the capacity required for Samāpatti and the entire Science
    of Prajñā.

## Provisional result

Yoga I.40 says:

> Mastery extends from the smallest atom to the greatest magnitude.

Fichte gives the meaning:

> The Scientist of Knowing can construct and sustain any determination, from
> the minimal point to the maximal systematic whole, with complete undivided
> attention. When the construction is complete, Truth produces its own
> manifestness.

```text
paramāṇu
minimal exact determination
        ↓ held through complete attention
parama-mahattva
maximal articulated whole
        ↓
vaśīkāra
the complete range is available to Knowing
```

I.41 can now present Samāpatti itself: when Vṛtti has become transparent,
Citta takes the form of grasper, grasping, and grasped like a clear jewel.
