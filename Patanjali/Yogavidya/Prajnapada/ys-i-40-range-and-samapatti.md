# I.40: Range of Mastery and the Kośa Samāpatti Machine

## A Yogavidya essay preparing I.41–51

### The textual hinge

Yoga Sūtra I.34–39 repeatedly offers another support with *vā*. I.40
changes the form of the account:

> परमाणु परममहत्त्वान्तोऽस्य वशीकारः ॥ १.४०॥

> *paramāṇu parama-mahattvānto 'sya vaśīkāraḥ*

The sūtra no longer offers a further object or exercise. It states the
range of *vaśīkāra*, from smallest atom to greatest magnitude. I.41 then
names *samāpatti* and describes consciousness as a clear jewel in relation
to grasper, grasping, and grasped. The sequence gives I.40 a precise
role: mastery of range prepares the explicit description of attainment.
See the [paired I.40 study](../../01-prajna/YS-I.40.md) and the
[current I.41 report](../../../../Docs/Prajnapada/ys-i-41-fichte-report.md).

### What the Kośa lets us inspect

[VAK 8.01 and its Bhāṣya](../../../../Vasubandhu/VAK/08-samapatti/VAK_8.01_bhasya.md)
define *samāpatti* as *śubhaikāgrya*, wholesome one-pointedness. The
Bhāṣya calls this *kuśala-citta-ekāgratā*, asks what one-pointedness is,
and answers *ekālambanatā*: having one object-support. It then asks
whether this is merely a property of mind or a distinct *samādhi* factor.
With its attendant factors included, the attainment is analyzed through
the five aggregates. The passage is a machine for analysis because it
separates three questions that are easily fused:

| Question | Kośa determination |
|---|---|
| What is the mark of attained *samāpatti*? | Wholesome one-pointedness. |
| What makes that one-pointedness determinate? | One object-support. |
| What accompanies the attainment? | A field of factors described through the five aggregates. |

Yoga I.40 asks a different question: over what range does mastery extend?
The Kośa does not explain *parama-mahattva* or turn Yoga's scale into its
own list of aggregates. It does, however, keep our Organon reading from
treating complete attention as an empty power. Attention is exercised
with a support and a functioning field.

### The small and the great

The Kośa can speak of a *paramāṇu* as a material unit, as in its
[Dhātu discussion at VAK 1.20](../../../../Vasubandhu/VAK/01-dhatu/VAK_1.20_bhasya.md).
Yoga's word retains that ordinary lower-limit force. The
[Fichte report on I.40](../../../../Docs/Prajnapada/ys-i-40-fichte-report.md)
then makes a further Organon determination: minimal exact content and
maximal articulated whole must both be available to complete attention.
That philosophical expansion belongs to Yogavidya. It should be tested
against the Yoga sequence rather than silently replacing the Sanskrit
atom with an abstract principle.

The resulting question is practical and scientific: can consciousness
hold a very small determination without losing its relation to the whole,
and can it hold a large whole without blurring its determinate parts?
This is how *vaśīkāra* becomes a capacity relevant to *samāpatti*. I.40
states the range; I.41 will show the relation in which that capacity is
exercised.

### Forward to the jewel

I.41 gives three poles: *grahītṛ*, *grahaṇa*, and *grāhya*. Our Organon
reading works through them as Principle Base, Essence Base, and Form Base
within one disciplined rational process. The mapping is a project
determination, not a translation of the three Sanskrit nouns. The Kośa's
one object-support and accompanying factors help ask whether this one
process can keep all its determinations present without scattering or
flattening them.

This essay establishes the first question for the I.40–51 sequence. The
next essays should follow I.41's jewel and I.42–44's *vitarka* and
*vicāra*, then the later *prajñā* and *saṃskāra* determinations. Each
topic can use the Kośa's analysis, the Yoga sūtra's own order, and the
existing reports as distinct sources for a future Vārttika.
