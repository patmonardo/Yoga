# YS I.2 — Why Yoga Is the Science of Consciousness

> योगश्चित्तवृत्तिनिरोधः ॥ १.२॥
>
> *yogaś cittavṛttinirodhaḥ*

> **Yoga is the Science of Consciousness.**

This translation reads I.2 as the opening definition of a metaphysical science. The sūtra's compound gives that science its problem: *citta*, its *vṛttis*, and their *nirodha*. The English word “science” renders the ordered knowledge undertaken by *yogānuśāsanam* in I.1 and defined here. It names the whole undertaking.

## Citta : vṛtti

*Citta* is consciousness. In our Yoga–Kośa system it occupies the *dhātu* position: the principial field in which determinate conscious life becomes possible. *Vṛtti* is the turning of consciousness into a determinate operation. In the Kośa register, this is the *caitta* side of the relation: consciousness is never understood by severing its field from its actual operations.

The colon in **citta : vṛtti** marks reciprocal determination. A movement is a movement *of consciousness*; consciousness becomes knowable through the ways it moves, apprehends, remembers, mistakes, and knows. Neither term is an isolated item to which the other is later attached. The science must articulate the relation, including the kinds of movement and the conditions under which they occur.

I.5 immediately prevents a blanket condemnation of the *vṛttis*: they are *kliṣṭa* and *akliṣṭa*, afflicted and unafflicted. I.6–11 then name their kinds. If every movement were simply an error to be erased, the distinction between afflicted and unafflicted movements would lose its work. The truth of consciousness must therefore be sought through the determination of its movements, not through their indiscriminate dismissal.

## Contradiction and the end of a vṛtti

A saint may encounter a contradictory movement, judge it false, and bring it to an end. Our metaphysical work cannot end with that judgment. It must determine what made the movement appear true, how it contradicts the truth of consciousness, and what is preserved when its false form is sublated. Yoga and Kośa undertake this work as systems: each determination must answer to the others.

The contradiction is internal to the relation **citta : vṛtti**. Consciousness appears in a movement; under the power of an afflicted seed, that movement can claim independence and obscure the consciousness it expresses. Simply cancelling the movement leaves its ground unexplained. Sublation removes the false claim and lets the relation become intelligible.

## Nirodha as sublation

We render *nirodha* as **sublation**. The problem is not that consciousness has determinate movements. The problem is that their afflicted form can conceal their truth. I.4 names the seer's taking the form of the movements in the other condition; I.3 names abiding in the seer's own form. Read together, these sūtras ask how a relation of misidentification gives way to a truthful relation.

The later sūtra III.50 names *doṣa-bīja-kṣaya*, the destruction of the seeds of defect. That phrase gives our account of sublation a precise target. The seeds that reproduce distortion must lose their power; the truth of **citta : vṛtti** can then shine. Sublation removes the obstructing determination and releases the relation it had concealed. It neither destroys consciousness nor makes every movement equally false.

This also gives *nirodha* a scientific burden. We must determine which movements are afflicted, how their seeds persist, what counteracts them, and what changes when the obstruction is removed. The answer requires the sūtra sequence, not a single instruction to become still.

## Why the translation names a science

“Yoga is the Science of Consciousness” states the whole relation disclosed by the sūtra. Yoga knows consciousness in and through its determinate movements and the sublation of their afflicted form. The Kośa teaches us to demand a complete discrimination of dharmas; Yoga gives that demand a field in consciousness and its operations. The resulting science is capable of being taught, tested, and written for others.

The short I.2 translation is therefore a thesis for Prajñā-pāda. Its proof must be earned through I.3–18, through the account of *samāpatti*, and through the later analysis of the *doṣa-bīja*. The present essay states the structure to test as those passages are read.
