# Degrees of Intensity According to the Kośa

## A Kośa companion essay for Yoga Sūtra I.22

I.21 said that samādhi and its fruit are near for those of intense *saṃvega*. I.22 adds *mṛdu-madhya-adhimātratvāt tato 'pi viśeṣaḥ*: mild, middle, and very intense degrees differentiate even that nearness. The Yoga Bhāṣya makes the nested grading explicit: mild-intense, middle-intense, and very-intense *saṃvega* correspond to near, nearer, and nearest attainment. It also places the strongest case with a very strong means. Thus “near” has a comparative structure within the path already laid out in I.20–21. [Yoga I.22 with Bhāṣya](https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/yoga-sutra-with-bhasya-sanskrit/d/doc1216819.html).

## Three degrees, then nine

The Kośa contains an unusually precise formal parallel. VAK 6.33 first says that defects (*doṣas*) in each level have nine grades, and so do their counteractive qualities or paths (*guṇas*). It then specifies the construction: mild, middle, and intense are the three basic grades; each is again divided into mild, middle, and intense. The result is nine determinate grades, from mild-mild to intense-intense. This is a method for finer discrimination inside a familiar threefold vocabulary. [Kośabhāṣya, VAK 6.33, source p. 355](../../../Vasubandhu/Sources/kosabhasya.txt).

The Kośa applies the ninefold scheme to the ordered abandonment of afflictions. It does not simply match equally named strengths against each other: the mild-mild path counteracts the intense-intense affliction, and the sequence proceeds toward subtler defects. The Bhāṣya compares cleaning coarse dirt from cloth before fine dirt. This is important for reading the formal parallel responsibly. Yoga I.22 differentiates practitioners' urgency and proximity to samādhi; Kośa VAK 6.33 differentiates afflictions and counteractive path moments. The same recursive grading does different analytic work in the two sources. [Kośabhāṣya, VAK 6.33, source p. 355](../../../Vasubandhu/Sources/kosabhasya.txt).

## Cultivation and speed

The Kośa has two further comparators that bring its grading closer to Yoga's practical question. In describing a cultivated meditative attainment, it says one proceeds from mild to middle and from middle to intense. Degree here is something practice can develop, not a fixed character label. In its path analysis, sharper faculties are associated with quicker penetration, while weaker faculties with slower penetration. These passages do not say that Kośa *tīkṣṇa-indriya* is Yoga *tīvra-saṃvega*, but they show how strength of a developed capacity affects the tempo of realization. [Kośabhāṣya, source p. 420](../../../Vasubandhu/Sources/kosabhasya.txt); [Kośabhāṣya, VAK 6.66, source p. 382](../../../Vasubandhu/Sources/kosabhasya.txt).

The jewel of I.22 is therefore methodological and practical. A path cannot be adequately described by the bare presence of faith, effort, recollection, samādhi, and wisdom, or by the single label “intense.” The factors have degrees; their cultivation changes those degrees; and degree changes the nearness of a specified result. Yoga states that result as samādhi and its fruit. The Kośa's nine grades and its accounts of cultivation and speed make the principle more exact while preserving its different theory of afflictions and path moments.
