# Firm-Grounded Practice According to the Kośa

## A Kośa companion essay for Yoga Sūtra I.14

I.13 defined *abhyāsa* as effort toward *sthiti*. I.14 now specifies the conditions under which that practice is *dṛḍhabhūmi*, firmly grounded: long duration (*dīrghakāla*), lack of interruption (*nairantarya*), and earnest regard (*satkāra*) in its cultivation (*āsevita*). The three qualify the same practice. The received Yoga Bhāṣya expands *satkāra* through *tapas*, *brahmacarya*, *vidyā*, and *śraddhā*. It supplies a practical test for the result: the practice is not quickly overcome by *vyutthāna-saṃskāra*. This is a claim about the firmness of practice amid contrary formations, not simply about the length of an exercise. [Yoga I.14 with Bhāṣya](https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/yoga-sutra-with-bhasya-sanskrit/d/doc1216811.html).

## Vigor has a determinate quality

The Kośa defines *vīrya* as *cetaso 'bhyutsāhaḥ*, the mind's energetic engagement. It lists *vīrya* and *śraddhā* among the factors present in wholesome consciousness. Yoga's Bhāṣya named *vīrya* in I.13 when explaining effort and *śraddhā* in I.14 when explaining respectful cultivation. The recurrence does not make the two classifications identical. It does, however, give a Kośa test for the word “practice”: sheer repetition cannot tell us whether the operative mental condition has the quality that sustains the path. [Kośabhāṣya, source pp. 55–56](../../../Vasubandhu/Sources/kosabhasya.txt).

## Cultivation and continuance

The Kośa distinguishes initial seeing (*darśanamārga*) from the path of cultivation (*bhāvanāmārga*). In its discussion of the transition, it associates the latter with practising what has been seen (*yathādṛṣṭābhyasana*). This is not a definition of Yoga *abhyāsa*, but it shows why the repeated work after an initial determination cannot be dismissed as mere duplication. Its continuity has a role in a path. [Kośabhāṣya, source p. 353](../../../Vasubandhu/Sources/kosabhasya.txt).

The strongest Kośa comparison comes in a debate about *prāpti*, the alleged possession of a dharma. Against the claim that abandonment needs such a separately real possession, the Bhāṣya appeals to a difference in the basis (*āśraya*): through the power of the paths of seeing and cultivation it has been transformed so that afflictions to be abandoned can no longer sprout there. The analogy is a seed scorched by fire. This passage describes a durable alteration of conditions, not merely a string of successful moments. [Kośabhāṣya, source p. 63](../../../Vasubandhu/Sources/kosabhasya.txt).

Yoga I.14 does **not** assert that every firm practice has reached that Kośa result. Its Bhāṣya says that emergence-formations do not *quickly* overwhelm it; the Kośa passage describes an inability of particular abandoned afflictions to sprout again. The difference of force matters. Read together, the texts pose a precise question about durability: how does sustained, uninterrupted, rightly regarded cultivation change what can overcome the practice? Yoga answers here with *dṛḍhabhūmi* and the test of *vyutthāna-saṃskāra*. The Kośa offers a further analysis of vigor, cultivation, and transformed causal support, while keeping its own path and result distinct.
