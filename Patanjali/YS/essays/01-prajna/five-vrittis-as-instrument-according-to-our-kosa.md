# The Five Vṛttis as an Instrument: Our Kośa Reading

## A companion essay for Yoga Sūtra I.6–11

I.6 names *pramāṇa, viparyaya, vikalpa, nidrā,* and *smṛti*. I.7–11 then gives each its own criterion. This is more than an inventory of mental contents. The five specify ways consciousness can determine, misdetermine, construct, suspend an ordinary object-field, and retain what has been experienced. I.5 has already said that movements may be afflicted or unafflicted, so their type alone does not assign a moral grade. I.12 will ask how their *nirodha* is cultivated. [Yoga I.5–12](../../../Sources/Yoga.txt).

The Kośa provides the operative frame. VAK 2.23 says *citta* and associated factors (*caittas*) necessarily arise together. VAK 2.34 says their association involves common support, object, and aspect. In our register, *citta* is consciousness as the unified field; *vijñāna* is differentiated cognition of an object; *prajñā* is discriminative comprehension. The Kośa's own VAK 2.34 can use *citta, manas,* and *vijñāna* as names of one mental occurrence, so this differentiation of roles is **our systematic use**, not a lexical rule imposed on every Kośa passage. The Yoga five are not five individual Kośa *caittas*. [Kośabhāṣya, VAK 1.16 and 2.23, 2.34](../../../../Vasubandhu/Sources/kosabhasya.txt).

## What each operation tests

| Yoga movement | Sūtra's criterion | Kośa question for our instrument |
|---|---|---|
| *Pramāṇa* | Perception, inference, testimony (I.7) | What exactly warrants the claim about the object? |
| *Viparyaya* | Cognition established in an alien form (I.8) | Has a mistaken form become a view governing associated cognition? |
| *Vikalpa* | Word-led and without a corresponding *vastu* (I.9) | Does object-support for cognition establish the being named by the word? |
| *Nidrā* | Movement supported by an absence-presentation (I.10) | Is the condition dull contraction, lucid gatheredness, or another kind of interruption? |
| *Smṛti* | Non-loss of an experienced object (I.11) | What operation retains access, and what formed capacity enables its return? |

These questions arise from distinct Kośa arguments. Its challenge *pramāṇābhāvāt* demands evidence for a separately real entity; its four reversals show how false predication can become a governing view; its three *vikalpas* prevent us from calling all differentiation false; its account of *sālambana* distinguishes cognitive support from a thing posited by words; its analyses of *styāna–middha* and *samāpatti* distinguish kinds of reduced object activity; its definition of *smṛti* as non-loss of *ālambana* and its memory-capacity analogy explain retention without a warehouse of unchanged images. [Kośabhāṣya, source pp. 22–23, 54, 68–70, 76–77, 278–79, 283–84, 432–33](../../../../Vasubandhu/Sources/kosabhasya.txt).

## A usable sequence

Our inquiry can run through the five functions as a disciplined cycle. A proposed determination seeks warrant; an alien form is detected and corrected; verbal construction is tested for its corresponding being; an absence-supported interval interrupts the previous selection; memory returns an experienced object for renewed determination. This is **our use of the set as an instrument**, not a claim that I.6 orders every actual episode in a compulsory temporal sequence. The sūtras give five kinds, and their definitions give the checks that make such a cycle possible.

The image-to-object problem of [I.9](vikalpa-according-to-kosa.md) shows why this matters for our Principle : Rule work. An image or word can support cognition without yet establishing the being it proposes. A memory can preserve access while also carrying a prior mistake. *Pramāṇa* tests the present claim; *prajñā* asks how the determinations hang together and what must be relinquished. In this sense the sequence programs the instrument for knowing dharmas, while I.12 asks what restraint of its movements accomplishes. The project synthesis remains answerable to each sūtra's exact criterion and each Kośa passage's own question.
