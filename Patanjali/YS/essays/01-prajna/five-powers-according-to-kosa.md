# The Five Powers in Yoga and the Kośa

## A Kośa companion essay for Yoga Sūtra I.20

I.20 gives the means-based path for “the others”: *śraddhā, vīrya, smṛti, samādhi, prajñā*. These are conditions preceding the other samādhi of I.18, not a miscellaneous list of admirable qualities. The Yoga Bhāṣya unfolds the sequence as a succession of enabling acts. Confidence clears and steadies the mind; a seeker of discernment develops energy; recollection becomes present; consciousness becomes unconfused in samādhi; then discriminative *prajñā* knows the object as it is. Repeated cultivation of that discernment and dispassion toward its objects lead toward *asaṃprajñāta* samādhi. [Yoga I.20 with Bhāṣya](https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/yoga-sutra-with-bhasya-sanskrit/d/doc1216817.html).

The Kośa contains the **same five names together**. Its Indriyanirdeśa lists *śraddhā, vīrya, smṛti, samādhi,* and *prajñā* as five faculties (*indriyas*). It explains their governing force: they hold afflictions at bay and bring forth the path (*kleśāś ca viṣkabhyante mārgaś cāvāhyate*). That makes them operative powers, not a catalogue of sentiments. Some instances are with outflows and bear karmic result; their outflow-free instances do not. The five therefore also require discrimination by level and path-context. [Kośabhāṣya, VAK 2.4 and 2.10, source pp. 39–40, 46](../../../../Vasubandhu/Sources/kosabhasya.txt).

VAK 6.68–69 gives a second, deeper account. In reducing the many awakening factors to their underlying constituents, it counts the five again as both *indriyas* and powers (*balas*). Wisdom, energy, and samādhi each also underlie other path factors; the Bhāṣya warns that naming a predominant constituent does not exclude the other qualities involved in practice. This prevents a mechanical reading in which one power simply replaces the previous one. [Kośabhāṣya, VAK 6.68–69, source pp. 383–84](../../../../Vasubandhu/Sources/kosabhasya.txt).

The exact recurrence of the five is the jewel. The Kośa gives a theory of why each is a faculty with power over affliction and path. Yoga's Bhāṣya gives a temporal and epistemic account of how they lead to discriminative knowing and beyond it, with dispassion. They can be read together as two specifications of an instrument of practice. Their *prajñā* and their ultimate path-objects should still be determined from each source rather than presumed identical.
