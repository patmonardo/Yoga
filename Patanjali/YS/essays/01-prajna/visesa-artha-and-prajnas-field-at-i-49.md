# Viśeṣa-Artha: Prajñā's Distinct Field

## A Samāpatti Essay on Yoga Sūtra I.49

The subject of I.49 is the *ṛtambharā prajñā* just named. It has an *anya-viṣaya*, a different field, from the prajñās of hearing and inference, **because of its *viśeṣa-artha***. The sūtra thus asks for a difference in what knowing discloses. It does not ask us to dismiss testimony or inference, which have their own uses. [YS I.49](../../01-prajna/YS-I.49.md)

In I.42–43, *artha* belonged to the impure, Objective Artha of the Form : Essence field. A named object and its cognition could be held, tested, and clarified. That work was necessary. I.49 asks how a truth-bearing Prajñā has a distinct *artha* when the Principle governing that field is made explicit. Calling both simply “object” would erase the transition; calling I.49's *artha* pure by fiat would leave the transition unproved. The distinction has to be achieved through the preceding samāpattis.

Our **Principle Base** reading takes *viśeṣa-artha* as the determinate content of a Principle known in its relation to Form and Essence. Testimony can name that relation, and inference can establish consequences from premises. Prajñā must see the principle through which these determinations belong together. The [Fichte report on I.49](../../../../Docs/Prajnapada/ys-i-49-fichte-report.md) develops the genetic version of this claim; this Bhāṣya essay retains the sūtra's simpler test: distinct field, distinctive *artha*.

The [Kośa at VAK 1.02](../../../../Vasubandhu/VAK/01-dhatu/VAK_1.02_bhasya.md) distinguishes prajñā cultivated toward ultimate Abhidharma from stainless prajñā with its attendants. That comparison prevents us from treating every occurrence of the word *prajñā* as one undifferentiated achievement. Yoga I.49 supplies its own criterion for the present one. The parallel is illuminating without making the two classifications identical.

This distinct knowing will have a consequence within the practitioner. I.50 says that a *saṃskāra* is born from it. The Principle's truth and the formation produced by knowing it must then be distinguished.
