# Savitarkā and Nirvitarkā: From Dharma-skandha to Principle

## A Bhāṣya essay on Yoga Sūtra I.42–43

I.41 established the clear jewel in relation to grasper, grasping, and
grasped. I.42–43 give its first definite work. In *savitarkā
samāpatti*, word, *artha*, and knowledge remain intermixed. In
*nirvitarkā*, memory is purified, the attainment appears as though
empty of its own form, and *artha* alone shines. The second sūtra
does not cancel the first; it discloses what the first has not yet
distinguished. [YS I.42](../../01-prajna/YS-I.42.md) ·
[YS I.43](../../01-prajna/YS-I.43.md)

### Savitarkā: the impure Dharma-skandha at work

We call the I.42 process **Dharma-skandha** in our architectural sense:
an impure Dharma is worked through its gathered, named, and known
determinations. This is not a claim that Patañjali uses the compound,
or that the Kośa's *dharmaskandha* always means this process. The
project's [Dharma-skandha study](../../../../Vasubandhu/Essays/dharma-skandha.md)
groups *vedanā*, *saṃjñā*, and *saṃskāra* as an ordinary-knowledge view
while keeping *vijñāna* a distinct cognition member. I.42 lets us
watch such an instrument work upon a particular *artha*.

Throughout this essay, *artha* in the *saṃvṛti* field is **impure
Objective Artha**, which implicates an object. “Object” names that implication;
it does not replace the determination *artha* is doing. This differs
from the question opened by OṂ's *artha* at I.28: Subjective Artha,
ultimately Artha as Absolute Attribute. Even when
I.43 says *artha-mātra*, the shine belongs initially to this impure
artha. Its relation to *paramārtha* must be established through the
Principle, not smuggled in through an English gloss.

The **Form Base** presents a determinate content. An uttered word or
visible sign may itself be a form, while the impure *artha* it
indicates must be investigated in its own right. Recognition and
retained names operate here; they can make the familiar name seem
identical with the present *artha*. This is the *saṅkīrṇa* of I.42:
an actual unity whose moments have not yet been given their proper
distinction. It is genuine samāpatti because the jewel holds the
mixture clearly enough for inquiry.

The Form : Essence opposition is already implicit: what is presented
can resist what our designation makes of it. But *savitarkā* works
primarily on the **Form** side of that opposition. *Vicāra* at I.44
will take up **Essence** as Reflection in its own right. We call the
impure operative field *saṃvṛti*; its impurity does not mean that
nothing true happens there.

The Kośa gives a basic account of associated operation. Its
[VAK 1.32 Bhāṣya](../../../../Vasubandhu/VAK/01-dhatu/VAK_1.32_bhasya.md)
distinguishes the modes in which *vitarka* and *vicāra* accompany
cognition-domains. Its [VAK 8.01 Bhāṣya](../../../../Vasubandhu/VAK/08-samapatti/VAK_8.01_bhasya.md)
calls *samāpatti* wholesome one-pointedness on one object-support,
with accompanying factors in the full attainment. Yoga contributes
the sharper determination of I.42: *śabda*, *artha*, and *jñāna*
intermixed in this very samāpatti.

### Nirvitarkā: vijñāna and access to Principle

I.43 does not ask us to destroy the instrument. It names
*smṛti-pariśuddhi*: memory is purified so that a retained word or
familiar form no longer dictates the present impure *artha*. The phrase
*svarūpa-śūnyā iva* says **as though** empty of its own form. The
qualification preserves the knowing activity while denying its
representative form independent authority. Then *artha-mātra*
shines as impure *artha*, now released from that dictation. This lets
the Principle of the Form determination become accessible through the
impure field's clarification.

In our account **vijñāna** is the differentiated cognition through
which that access occurs. It apprehends a determinate impure *artha*
and its implicated object; it is not automatically pure wisdom.
Kośa [VAK 1.31](../../../../Vasubandhu/VAK/01-dhatu/VAK_1.31_bhasya.md)
is useful here: the mind, dharma, and mental-cognition domains may be
with or without outflows. Their type alone does not settle their
purity. The question for I.43 is whether cognition, freed from the
memory-led mixture, can disclose the Principle of this Form
determination.

The [I.41 essay](samapatti-and-the-three-bases-at-i-41.md) poses the
larger *paramārtha* question, and Kośa
[VAK 1.02](../../../../Vasubandhu/VAK/01-dhatu/VAK_1.02_bhasya.md)
anchors the possibility of an outflow-free five-aggregate complex in
stainless *prajñā* with its attendants. I.43 has a more exact task:
*nirvitarkā* finds a Principle within Form work by releasing impure
*artha* from the authority of remembered appearance. It does not
thereby complete *paramārtha* or the Science. I.44 opens the distinct
work of Essence Base Reflection.

Thus the two modes are one inquiry at two degrees of explicitness:
**savitarkā** holds the impure Dharma's Form determination in its
mixture; **nirvitarkā** lets *vijñāna* find the Principle of
that Form determination. The jewel does not leave the domain behind.
I.44 now takes the inquiry into Essence Base through *vicāra*.
