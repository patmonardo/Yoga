# Samāpatti and the Three Bases at Yoga Sūtra I.41

## A Bhāṣya essay on the opening of the absolute idea

I.40 gives *vaśīkāra* its range, from the smallest to the greatest. I.41
gives that mastery an inner form. When the movements of consciousness
have become attenuated, consciousness resembles a clear jewel: it rests
in and takes the coloring of *grahītṛ*, *grahaṇa*, or *grāhya*—grasper,
grasping, or grasped. Patañjali calls this *samāpatti*. These are three
stations in one act of knowing. The jewel can turn toward each without
losing the relation among them. [YS I.41](../../01-prajna/YS-I.41.md)

Our Kośa architecture lets us investigate that relation. In this
project, *skandha* is **Base**, *āyatana* **Essence**, and *dhātu*
**Principle**. One dharma can be considered through all three without
being multiplied into three dharmas. The Kośa cross-classifies the same
material: the cognition aggregate, for example, becomes mind-āyatana
and seven mind and cognition dhātus. VAK 1.18 grounds inclusion in a
dharma's own nature. Our reading of this cross-classification as a
Form–Essence–Principle architecture is the work of this essay.
[VAK 1.16 Bhāṣya](../../../../Vasubandhu/VAK/01-dhatu/VAK_1.16_bhasya.md) ·
[VAK 1.18](../../../../Vasubandhu/VAK/01-dhatu/VAK_1.18.md)

| Station in I.41 | First architectural question | Work of the jewel |
|---|---|---|
| Grasped, *grāhya* | **Form Base:** what determination is presented? | Hold its formed content exactly. |
| Grasping, *grahaṇa* | **Essence Base:** how is it disclosed? | Show the mediation through which it can be known. |
| Grasper, *grahītṛ* | **Principle Base:** what makes this one intelligible act? | Disclose the governing determination of the relation. |

These are questions, not dictionary equivalents for the Sanskrit nouns.
Each station can itself be examined through all three Bases. The grasped
has an Essence and a Principle; the grasper must become a determinate
presentation. The table is an entrance into their reciprocal movement.

### Form Base: the presented determination

The grasped is where inquiry may begin. A form is given with enough
determinacy to hold attention. Yet presenting it does not settle what
it is. In our Kośa reading, Form Base is the constituted field in which
a dharma can be encountered; it is not inert matter. The Kośa counts
five faculties, five corresponding objects, and *avijñapti* in
*rūpa-skandha*. The last is especially useful: *avijñapti* retains its
Form Base membership while entering dharmāyatana and dharmadhātu. A
single determination thus calls for more than one view.

[VAK 1.15](../../../../Vasubandhu/VAK/01-dhatu/VAK_1.15.md)

The jewel first lets this formed content appear without substitution.
Its next question is how such appearing occurs. That question moves us
to Essence.

### Essence Base: disclosure through relation

*Grahaṇa* brings the act of grasping into view. A presented form is
selected, held, and disclosed through an act; the act gains its own
determination through what it grasps. Essence Base names this reflective
mediation. The Kośa gives a basic analysis of the attained field: VAK
8.01 calls *samāpatti* wholesome one-pointedness, explains it through
one object-support, and includes accompanying factors in the fuller
attainment. Yoga I.41 then lets consciousness rest upon grasping itself.
Its interest is the intelligibility of the relation, not merely the
persistence of attention. [VAK 8.01 Bhāṣya](../../../../Vasubandhu/VAK/08-samapatti/VAK_8.01_bhasya.md)

Essence discloses a determinate object through relation. Yet a relation
can connect terms while leaving its own unity unexplained. That presses
the inquiry toward Principle.

### Principle Base: the relation comprehends itself

*Grahītṛ* turns inquiry toward the one for whom and through whom this
knowing is actual. Our Principle Base asks which determination makes
the whole act intelligible. A Principle is not another item beside the
grasped. It comes into view when formed content and its mediation are
comprehended together. The Kośa's *dhātu* classification models
determinate inclusion for our project; Yoga's jewel gives this inquiry
its transparent cognitive form.

The jewel is clear because it can show each term without letting one
conceal the others. The grasper becomes knowable through grasping what
is grasped; grasping has its sense through both poles; the grasped is
known through this complete act. Their distinction persists in lucid
unity. Thus Form, Essence, and Principle are not three consecutive
objects of attention. They are three determinations of one intelligible
process.

### The two truths within the jewel

This architecture lets us pose the two truths as a relation inside
*samāpatti*. **Principle Base** is, ideally, the implicit pure Skandha
system: *paramārtha* as the unity from which a determination can be
known in its truth. **Form Base : Essence Base** is the differentiated
field of opposed impure Skandha determinations: *saṃvṛti* as presented
form and its reflective disclosure. The Principle becomes explicit as
this field is reconstructed through knowing. Its determinate domain
must be shown, not merely asserted over the opposition.

The question of *artha* entered earlier, at I.28, as the *artha* of
OṂ in the Īśvara practice. Our reading does not classify that opening
as Objective Artha. It raises Subjective Artha and, ultimately, Artha
as Absolute Attribute. I.42–43 will first specify **Objective Artha**:
the impure *artha* implicated in the Form : Essence field. The two
truths become a question of how that field is related to the pure
Principle, rather than two interchangeable meanings of a word.

The Kośa gives the pure side a strong anchor. VAK 1.02 calls ultimate
Abhidharma stainless *prajñā* with its attendants and explains it as
an outflow-free five-aggregate complex. It also distinguishes the
defiled cultivation directed toward that attainment. Our identification
of the former with implicit Principle Base and of the latter's field
with the Form : Essence opposition is the proposed Theory of Knowing,
not a two-column formula stated by the Kośa.
[VAK 1.02 Bhāṣya](../../../../Vasubandhu/VAK/01-dhatu/VAK_1.02_bhasya.md)

The phrase *tat-stha-tad-añjanatā* gives this theory its Yogic action.
We read *tat-stha* as abiding in **that** Principle and *tad-añjanatā*
as taking **that** coloring in the derived domain. The second moment
comes from the first and reconstructs the Principle's determinate
domain in appearance. Grammatically *tat* and *tad* are forms of the
same demonstrative; the sūtra does not assign them two separately
named objects. The philosophical distinction is made through its two
linked operations: abiding and being colored. Here Fichte's Theory of
Knowing comes into view as a science of how the Principle becomes
explicit through the reconstruction of what it governs.

### A beginning whose proof lies ahead

This is the **first part of the absolute idea** in our Yoga reading:
the relation of knowing begins to become transparent to itself.
Fichte's [I.41 report](../../../../Docs/Prajnapada/ys-i-41-fichte-report.md)
develops the passage from factical construction to insight into its
law. At this Bhāṣya level, we can say: the jewel presents Form,
discloses Essence, and inquires into Principle in one disciplined
movement. I.41 gives that movement its architecture; it does not yet
give every determination of its result.

I.42 begins with word, object, and cognition still intermixed in
*savitarkā samāpatti*. That is the first definite material on which
this architecture works. The movement through I.51 will show how far
the lucidity announced at I.41 can go.
