# Yoga Sūtra I.1–12: The Science of Consciousness

I.1 opens an ordered instruction. I.2 states its governing determination: **Yoga is the Science of Consciousness**. Its *citta-vṛtti-nirodha* is read here as the sublation of the movements of consciousness. I.3–4 give the first contrast to be explained: the seer's own-form abiding and its conformity to a movement's form. I.5–11 distinguish the movements and their conditions; I.12 names practice and dispassion as the joint means of their sublation. ## Determinations to carry forward

The five kinds are operations of a single instrument, not five individual Kośa *caittas*. I.5's afflicted/unafflicted distinction crosses all five kinds. A valid cognition is still a *vṛtti*; its validity does not make it the whole Science, and its sublation does not retroactively make its content false. [Five-vṛtti essay](01-prajna/five-vrittis-as-instrument-according-to-our-kosa.md)

I.6 enumerates the kinds but does not prescribe that every cognitive event passes through them in order. Our use of the list as a cycle of testing is a project construction. Each sūtra's own criterion remains decisive: warrant, alien form, absent *vastu*, absence-supported movement, or non-loss. The essays test those criteria through Kośa analyses of cognition, factors, conditions, and retention.

The Kośa's two technical *nirodhas* distinguish discriminative disjunction from non-arising through missing conditions. They sharpen questions about knowledge and causal support; they do not define Yoga's sublation or divide between *abhyāsa* and *vairāgya*. The [Nirodha essay](01-prajna/nirodha-according-to-kosa.md) now begins from I.2 and I.12 rather than from a received Yoga Bhāṣya account.

I.13–16 will determine practice and dispassion further. I.43 and I.50–51 will test memory, formation, and sublation at the Samāpatti level. Those later arguments should develop from this opening Science without replacing its first distinctions.
