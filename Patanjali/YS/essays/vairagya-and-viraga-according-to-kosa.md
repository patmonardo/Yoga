# Vairāgya and Virāga According to the Kośa

## A Kośa companion essay for Yoga Sūtra I.15

The sūtra defines *vairāgya* as *vaśīkārasaṃjñā* in one free from thirst for *dṛṣṭa* and *ānuśravika* objects: objects encountered and objects known through teaching. The Yoga Bhāṣya gives examples of ordinary enjoyments, then of heaven, a bodiless condition, and *prakṛtilayatva* as scripturally known attainments. It says that even in contact with divine and nondivine objects, consciousness that sees their defects has, through the force of *prasaṃkhyāna*, a mastery free of driven appropriation. The received explanation therefore makes discriminative seeing part of this grade of dispassion. It does not present simple non-contact as sufficient. [Yoga I.15 with Bhāṣya](https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/yoga-sutra-with-bhasya-sanskrit/d/doc1216812.html).

## The Kośa: attachment can move inward

The Kośa distinguishes sensual attachment (*kāmarāga*) from attachment to existence in the form and formless realms (*bhavarāga*). In explaining the latter it says that those concerned are often attached to meditative attainment (*samāpattirāga*), an attachment turned inward. Its point is diagnostic: freedom from coarse sensual objects does not by itself show freedom from attachment to a subtler state. This gives I.15's reach beyond immediately experienced objects a precise comparator. Yoga's “heard-about objects” and Kośa *bhavarāga* are not identical classifications, but both block a premature claim that relinquishing ordinary pleasure has exhausted thirst. [Kośabhāṣya, source p. 279](../../../Vasubandhu/Sources/kosabhasya.txt).

## The Kośa: the level and path of dispassion matter

In VAK 6.45 the Kośa distinguishes worldly and supramundane paths to *vairāgya*. From the highest level of existence (*bhavāgra*), dispassion requires a supramundane path; for lower levels, the text allows either a worldly or a supramundane path. The Bhāṣya's reason is technical: a path belonging to the very level permeated by an affliction cannot serve as its counteragent. Thus “dispassion” is not an undifferentiated accomplishment; one must ask from which level, toward what, and by which path. This is a Kośa distinction, not a hidden list inside I.15. [Kośabhāṣya, source p. 366](../../../Vasubandhu/Sources/kosabhasya.txt).

VAK 6.78 gives a still sharper formulation: *virāga* is the exhaustion of *rāga*; *virāgadhātu* is the abandonment of attachment. It distinguishes that from *prahāṇadhātu*, abandonment of other afflictions, and *nirodhadhātu*. The three names belong to its account of unconditioned liberation. Yoga I.15, by contrast, defines *vaśīkārasaṃjñā* through absence of thirst toward two classes of object. We can compare the exclusion of attachment without identifying the Yoga condition with the Kośa's unconditioned element. [Kośabhāṣya, source p. 389](../../../Vasubandhu/Sources/kosabhasya.txt).

## A Sāṃkhya pressure point

Sāṃkhya Kārikā 23 names *dharma*, *jñāna*, *virāga*, and *aiśvarya* as sattvic forms of Buddhi. That gives a textual basis for treating *virāga* as an intelligible determination of Buddhi rather than only a rule against sense pleasure. The Kārikā names four forms; it does not itself call *virāga* and *aiśvarya* two species of *prajñā*. That further ordering is a philosophical proposal to develop separately. [Sāṃkhya Kārikā 23](../../../Reference/Samkhya.txt).

Kārikā 45 says *vairāgyāt prakṛtilayaḥ*: from dispassion, dissolution into Prakṛti. The Yoga Bhāṣya on I.15 places attainment of *prakṛtilayatva* among the heard-about objects toward which thirst is to cease. This is a genuine tension between two textual deployments of the term, not a reason to erase either passage. The Kośa's warning about attachment to meditative states helps formulate the question: when does an attainment become another possible object of attachment? It does not by itself settle what Sāṃkhya's *prakṛtilaya* or Yoga's final liberation is. [Sāṃkhya Kārikā 45](../../../Reference/Samkhya.txt); [Yoga I.15 with Bhāṣya](https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/yoga-sutra-with-bhasya-sanskrit/d/doc1216812.html).

[I.16](higher-vairagya-according-to-kosa.md) names a higher *vairāgya* through *puruṣakhyāti* and lack of thirst for the *guṇas*. Its Bhāṣya explicitly calls that grade the culmination of knowledge. For I.15, the Kośa-first result remains exact: dispassion requires specification of its object, level, path, and the possibility of subtler attachment. The Yoga Bhāṣya adds that the present mastery arises through discriminative seeing even in contact with objects.
