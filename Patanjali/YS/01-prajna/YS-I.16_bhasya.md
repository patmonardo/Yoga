# YS I.16 — Our Bhāṣya

## 1. Sūtra Anchor

> तत्परं पुरुषख्यातेर्गुणवैतृष्ण्यम्॥ १.१६॥

> *tat paraṃ puruṣakhyāter guṇavaitṛṣṇyam* — That higher dispassion is freedom from thirst for the *guṇas* through discernment of *puruṣa*.

## 2. Commentary

The word *param* distinguishes this dispassion from I.15's mastery. Its object is no longer only what has been experienced or promised; thirst falls away with respect to the *guṇas* in their manifest and unmanifest forms. The sūtra gives the ground: *puruṣakhyāti*. The received Yoga Bhāṣya explains that practice of seeing *puruṣa* clarifies Buddhi. It explicitly calls this higher *vairāgya* the culmination of knowledge and says *kaivalya* follows inseparably from it.

The Kośa makes the difference between levels of release exact. It detects attachment even to meditative attainment (*bhavarāga*), requires a supramundane path for dispassion from the summit of conditioned existence, and names the exhaustion of attachment *virāgadhātu*. Its discriminative cessation is attained through a special *prajñā* concerning the truths. These passages show why freedom from coarse objects is not yet final freedom from attachment. They do not make Kośa's supramundane path into Yoga's *puruṣakhyāti*. The [companion essay](../essays/higher-vairagya-according-to-kosa.md) develops the comparison.

## 3. Project Determination

The strongest source-grounded conclusion is that higher *vairāgya* is inseparable from knowing in the Yoga Bhāṣya. The Sāṃkhya Kārikā's placement of *virāga* among Buddhi's sattvic forms reinforces the importance of this connection, while the Kośa supplies a distinct account of discriminative release from attachment. A further identification of these as one Science of Absolute Knowing belongs to the separate Yogavidya layer.

## 4. Review Status

Kośa-first pass. I.15–16 now establish two grades of *vairāgya* in the Yoga Bhāṣya. Continue with I.17 only after reviewing how *puruṣakhyāti*, Buddhi, and Kośa *prajñā* have been kept distinct.
