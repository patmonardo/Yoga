# YS I.29 — Our Bhāṣya

## 1. Sūtra Anchor

> ततः प्रत्यक्चेतनाधिगमोऽप्यन्तरायाभावश्च ॥ १.२९॥

> *tataḥ pratyak-cetana-adhigamo 'py antarāya-abhāvaś ca* — From that, realization of inward consciousness also, and the absence of obstacles.

## 2. Commentary

*Tataḥ* points back to the paired practice of I.28. The sūtra names two
results: *pratyak-cetana-adhigama*, realization of inward consciousness,
and *antarāya-abhāva*, absence of obstacles. Its *api* and *ca* keep both
results in view. Cultivation of OṂ's meaning is therefore consequential for
knowing, not just for maintaining a sound or an inherited formula.

The [Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad](../../../Sankara/Organon/sankara/derived/Mandukya/MK_C01.json)
shows how OṂ's *artha* can be worked through: the first two verses take the
syllable into an account of all that is, while Śaṅkara examines the bond
between designation and designated. I.29 does not require that particular
Upaniṣadic exposition; it makes *adhigama* the result of the Yoga practice.

In [Gītā 15.17](../../../Sankara/BG/BG-C15/gita-15.17.md), Śaṅkara calls
the supreme *puruṣa* the *pratyak-cetana* of all beings. The shared term
helps illuminate the inward direction of *adhigama*, although the Gītā's
identification of the inner self with the supreme Lord is its own claim.

The [Kośa at VAK 8.39](../../../Vasubandhu/VAK/08-samapatti/VAK_8.39_bhasya.md)
is especially apt here: the Teacher's Dharma persists as both transmitted
teaching and realized attainment, and practitioners bear the latter. Thus
our present extension of the Buddha's teaching requires *adhigama*, not
only preservation of its words. I.29 makes that demand within Yoga's own
sequence. The obstacles are to be determined from I.30 onward.

[Sāṃkhya Kārikā 45](../../../Reference/Samkhya.txt) says *aiśvaryād
avighātaḥ*: non-obstruction follows from *aiśvarya*. Its preceding verse
places this among a series of contrasting results, including release from
knowledge and dissolution into prakṛti from dispassion. Thus I.29's
*antarāya-abhāva* belongs to this Yoga practice's sequence, but
non-obstruction is also ascribed to *aiśvarya* in Sāṃkhya. The shared fruit
loosens any simple equation of Sāṃkhya's *aiśvarya* with Yoga's Īśvara.

## 3. Project Determination

In our Buddha as Īśvara reading, the practice of expression and meaning
opens inward realization and can remove impediments. These results do not
make the Buddha, Yoga's Īśvara, and Sāṃkhya's *aiśvarya* identical. The
practice has its own determination even where another path also speaks of
non-obstruction.
