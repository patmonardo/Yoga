# YS I.15 — Our Bhāṣya

## 1. Sūtra Anchor

> दृष्टानुश्रविकविषयवितृष्णस्य वशीकारसंज्ञा वैराग्यम्॥ १.१५॥

> *dṛṣṭānuśravikaviṣayavitṛṣṇasya vaśīkārasaṃjñā vairāgyam* — Dispassion is the state called mastery in one free from thirst for experienced and traditionally taught objects.

## 2. Commentary

The second means of I.12 is now defined. Dispassion concerns not only objects already enjoyed but also objects and attainments promised by teaching. The Yoga Bhāṣya says that discriminative examination and seeing the defects of objects leave consciousness without thirst even when those objects are encountered. This is *vaśīkārasaṃjñā*, the state called mastery. The force of the Bhāṣya is cognitive as well as practical; I.15 does not reduce dispassion to avoiding contact.

The Kośa exposes a danger of narrower detachment. Its account of *bhavarāga* says attachment can turn inward toward meditative attainment after desire for ordinary pleasures has fallen away. It also distinguishes worldly from supramundane dispassion by the level from which one becomes free. Its *virāgadhātu* names the exhaustion of *rāga* within an account of unconditioned liberation. These distinctions clarify why a claim of “no desire” needs its object and range specified. They do not make Yoga's *vaśīkārasaṃjñā* identical with the Kośa's *virāgadhātu*. The [companion essay](../essays/vairagya-and-viraga-according-to-kosa.md) follows the passages.

## 3. Project Determination

Sāṃkhya Kārikā 23 explicitly places *virāga* with *dharma*, *jñāna*, and *aiśvarya* among the sattvic forms of Buddhi. That source gives reason to investigate dispassion as an aspect of discernment and capacity, not merely as inhibited appetite. Kārikā 45's *vairāgyāt prakṛtilayaḥ* and the Yoga Bhāṣya's inclusion of *prakṛtilayatva* among the heard-about objects of dispassion create a further interpretive problem. The Kośa-first essay records the problem without deciding it by importing a later system.

## 4. Review Status

Kośa-first pass. Read I.16 for the higher grade of *vairāgya* before identifying its full scope. The thesis that *vairāgya* and *aiśvarya* are two species of a larger *prajñā*, or that their outcome is *brahman-nirvāṇa*, belongs to a later systematic argument; it is not stated in I.15 or the cited Kārikās.
