# YS I.37 — Consciousness Directed to the Dispassionate

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

> वीतरागविषयं वा चित्तम्॥ १.३७॥

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

> vītarāga-viṣayaṃ vā cittam // 1.37 //

## 3. Lexical Analysis

| Pada | Form | Force |
|---|---|---|
| *vīta-rāga* | compound member | one from whom attachment has departed; dispassionate |
| *-viṣayam* | neuter nominative singular compound ending | having this as its object-field |
| *vā* | alternative particle | or |
| *cittam* | neuter nominative singular | consciousness |

## 4. Grammar

*Vītarāga-viṣayam* qualifies *cittam*: consciousness has the dispassionate
as its object-field. The compound does not say whether this is a person,
a state of consciousness, or both. *Vā* adds another alternative in the
sequence of supports for steadiness.

## 5. Translation

### Literal translation

> Or, consciousness whose object is the dispassionate.

## 6. Logical Determination

The series moves from breath and object-bearing activity through luminosity
to a field marked by freedom from *rāga*. The sūtra directs consciousness
to that field without specifying a named exemplar or equating this
practice with the final *samāpatti* account.

## 7. Interpretive Note

[Gītā 4.10](../../../Sankara/BG/BG-C04/gita-4.10.md) speaks of people
freed from attachment, fear, and anger, taking refuge in the Lord;
Śaṅkara reads their purification through knowledge. The [Kośa at VAK
6.30](../../../Vasubandhu/VAK/06-marga/VAK_6.30_bhasya.md) uses
*vītarāga* for a practitioner free from attachment to a specified realm.
These passages make dispassion a real path determination, while I.37 leaves
its contemplated *viṣaya* open.
