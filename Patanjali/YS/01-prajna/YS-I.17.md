# YS I.17 — Saṃprajñāta Samādhi

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

> वितर्कविचारानन्दास्मितारूपानुगमात्सम्प्रज्ञातः ॥ १.१७॥

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

> vitarka-vicāra-ānanda-asmitā-rūpa-anugamāt saṃprajñātaḥ // 1.17 //

## 3. Lexical Analysis

| Pada | Form | Force |
|---|---|---|
| *vitarka* | compound member | gross engagement with an object-support, as the Bhāṣya explains |
| *vicāra* | compound member | subtler engagement |
| *ānanda* | compound member | delight or gladness |
| *asmitā* | compound member | the sense of “I am”; here explained by the Bhāṣya as unitary awareness |
| *rūpa-anugamāt* | ablative singular of a compound ending in *anugama* | because of accompaniment by these forms |
| *saṃprajñātaḥ* | masculine nominative singular | the cognitively determinate [samādhi] |

## 4. Grammar

The four initial nouns qualify *rūpa*, and the compound culminates in the causal ablative *anugamāt*. *Saṃprajñātaḥ* agrees with an understood masculine *samādhiḥ*, supplied explicitly by the Bhāṣya. The sūtra's compound names accompanying forms; the Bhāṣya determines their graded arrangement. The local source also records parenthetical variant readings beginning *smitā-* after the numbered sūtra; they are not included in the main-text anchor above.

## 5. Translation

### Literal translation

> The [samādhi] accompanied by the forms of *vitarka*, *vicāra*, *ānanda*, and *asmitā* is *saṃprajñāta*.

## 6. Logical Determination

The received Bhāṣya describes four increasingly spare forms: *savitarka* accompanies all four terms; *savicāra* lacks *vitarka*; *sānanda* lacks *vicāra* as well; and the fourth has *asmitā* alone. It says all four are *sālambana*, supported by an object. Thus the sequence is a specification of object-supported samādhi, not simply a list of four interchangeable mental states.

## 7. Interpretive Note

The [Kośa companion essay](../essays/samprajnata-and-kosa-dhyana.md) compares the gross/subtle distinction and the changing factors of meditative attainment. The Kośa's four *dhyānas* have their own factor lists; their number does not make them identical to the four forms set out by the Yoga Bhāṣya.
