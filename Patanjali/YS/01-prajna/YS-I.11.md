# YS I.11 — Smṛti, the Non-Loss of an Experienced Object

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

> अनुभूतविषयासम्प्रमोषः स्मृतिः ॥ १.११॥

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

> anubhūtaviṣayāsaṃpramoṣaḥ smṛtiḥ // 1.11 //

## 3. Lexical Analysis

| Pada | Form | Force |
|---|---|---|
| *anubhūta* | compound member, past passive participle | experienced |
| *viṣaya* | compound member | object or field of cognition |
| *asaṃpramoṣaḥ* | nominative masculine singular | non-loss, not letting slip away |
| *smṛtiḥ* | nominative feminine singular | memory, recollection |

## 4. Grammar

The compound *anubhūta-viṣaya-asaṃpramoṣaḥ* is the masculine predicate noun defining feminine *smṛtiḥ*. The gender difference belongs to the two nouns in an equational sentence. *Anubhūta* qualifies the *viṣaya*: what is not lost is the object **as experienced**, not an object named without any prior encounter. The sūtra does not state the causal mechanism of retention.

## 5. Translation

### Literal translation

> Memory is the non-loss of an experienced object.

## 6. Logical Determination

I.11 completes the fivefold list of I.6. Its definition concerns continuity across experience: the *viṣaya* can cease to be presently given without being lost to cognition. The sūtra does not guarantee that the earlier experience was valid; memory can retain what arose through other kinds of *vṛtti* as well.

## 7. Interpretive Note

The [companion Kośa essay](../essays/smrti-and-samskara-according-to-kosa.md) compares this non-loss of an experienced *viṣaya* with the Kośa's non-loss of an *ālambana*. It then distinguishes the recollective operation *smṛti* from a memory-producing capacity and examines the Yoga Bhāṣya's explicit *saṃskāra* account. Later I.43 and I.50 will test how retained form is purified and how insight forms a new capacity.
