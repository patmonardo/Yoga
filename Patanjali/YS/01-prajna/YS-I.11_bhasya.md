# YS I.11 — Our Bhāṣya

## 1. Sūtra Anchor

> अनुभूतविषयासम्प्रमोषः स्मृतिः ॥ १.११॥

> *anubhūtaviṣayāsaṃpramoṣaḥ smṛtiḥ* — Memory is the non-loss of an experienced object.

## 2. Commentary

The fifth movement completes I.6's enumeration. What has been experienced can cease to be present without being lost. Yoga names that non-loss *smṛti*. The received Yoga Bhāṣya asks whether the object or the prior cognition is remembered and explains that the earlier cognition leaves a *saṃskāra* capable of producing memory with aspects of both the grasped and the grasping.

The Kośa defines *smṛti* as *ālambanāsaṃpramoṣaḥ*, non-loss of the object-support. Its account of recollective *vikalpa* identifies memory as a mental operation; elsewhere a memory-producing capacity illustrates how an earlier cognition can remain causally effective without being a second remembered object. The [companion essay](../essays/smrti-and-samskara-according-to-kosa.md) keeps the Kośa's contextual arguments and Yoga's Bhāṣya account distinct.

## 3. Project Determination

Memory preserves a way of reaching an object through consciousness. Because the retained form includes how the object was taken, memory can support recognition or impose an old determination on what now appears. Its purification at I.43 will not mean erasing the past; it will mean making the retained form transparent to the present *artha*. A new *saṃskāra* at I.50 will mean that insight has become an operative capacity rather than merely a proposition recalled.
