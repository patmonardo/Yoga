# YS I.28 — Our Bhāṣya

## 1. Sūtra Anchor

> तज्जपस्तदर्थभावनम्॥ १.२८॥

> *taj-japas tad-artha-bhāvanam* — Repetition of that, and cultivation of its meaning.

## 2. Commentary

The designation of I.27 now becomes a practice. *Japa* repeats the
*praṇava*; *bhāvanā* cultivates its *artha*. Repetition of OṂ is familiar.
The less obvious work is sustained attention to what OṂ means. Mere repeated
sound has not yet performed that work.

[Gītā 8.13](../../../Sankara/BG/BG-C08/gita-8.13.md) joins utterance of OṂ
with remembrance of the Lord. Śaṅkara explicitly calls OṂ a designation
and the Lord its meaning. His explanation makes the relation of repetition
and meaning unusually clear. The Gītā verse concerns departure from the
body; I.28 states the practice without that restriction.

The [Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad 1–2](../../../Sankara/Organon/sankara/derived/Mandukya/MK_C01.json)
provides a practice in attending to that meaning: it expounds OṂ through all
that is and through what exceeds the three times. Śaṅkara's comment on its
second verse considers the designation and the designated together. Reading
and returning to these determinations is itself a form of *artha-bhāvanā*;
recitation and inquiry can therefore work together.

The [Kośa Bhāṣya at VAK 8.39](../../../Vasubandhu/VAK/08-samapatti/VAK_8.39_bhasya.md)
distinguishes the Teacher's transmitted Dharma (*āgama*) from its realized
mode (*adhigama*). Preservers and speakers bear the former; practitioners
bear the latter. This precisely clarifies how we may become present
extensions of the Buddha's teaching. On our Buddha as Īśvara reading,
*tad-artha-bhāvanam* can help move an inherited expression toward lived
realization. The Kośa does not identify that expression with OṂ.

## 3. Project Determination

Repetition maintains a determinate address to Īśvara; cultivation of meaning
tests whether that address becomes knowledge and practice. The present
extension of the Buddha's teaching lies in the latter work, while the sūtra
holds sound and meaning together.
