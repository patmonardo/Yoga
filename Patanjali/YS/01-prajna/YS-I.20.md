# YS I.20 — The Means-Based Route

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

> श्रद्धावीर्यस्मृतिसमाधिप्रज्ञापूर्वक इतरेषाम्॥ १.२०॥

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

> śraddhā-vīrya-smṛti-samādhi-prajñā-pūrvaka itareṣām // 1.20 //

## 3. Lexical Analysis

| Pada | Form | Force |
|---|---|---|
| *śraddhā* | compound member | confidence or trust |
| *vīrya* | compound member | energy or exertion |
| *smṛti* | compound member | recollection or mindfulness |
| *samādhi* | compound member | collectedness |
| *prajñā* | compound member | discerning wisdom |
| *pūrvakaḥ* | masculine nominative singular, sandhi *-pūrvaka itareṣām* | preceded by these |
| *itareṣām* | genitive plural | for the others |

## 4. Grammar

The five members govern *-pūrvakaḥ*, whose masculine singular refers to the understood *anyaḥ samādhiḥ* of I.18. *Itareṣām* contrasts these practitioners with the two groups in I.19. The sūtra names conditions but does not itself spell out their causal order; the received Bhāṣya does.

## 5. Translation

### Literal translation

> For the others, [the other samādhi] is preceded by confidence, energy, recollection, collectedness, and discerning wisdom.

## 6. Logical Determination

The Bhāṣya calls this *upāya-pratyaya*, conditioned by means. It unfolds a sequence: confidence gives rise to energy, energy to recollection, recollection steadies consciousness in samādhi, and collected consciousness yields discriminative *prajñā* that knows its object as it is. Practice of that discernment and dispassion toward its objects lead to *asaṃprajñāta* samādhi. Thus *prajñā* is an operative condition of the path, and dispassion remains necessary.

## 7. Interpretive Note

The [Kośa essay](../essays/five-powers-according-to-kosa.md) investigates the unusually exact shared fivefold set. The Kośa calls these five *indriyas* and *balas*, but Yoga's Bhāṣya gives them its own order and destination.
