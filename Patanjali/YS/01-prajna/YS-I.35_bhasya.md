# YS I.35 — Our Bhāṣya

## 1. Sūtra Anchor

> विषयवती वा प्रवृत्तिरुत्पन्ना मनसः स्थितिनिबन्धिनी ॥ १.३५॥

> *viṣayavatī vā pravṛttir utpannā manasaḥ sthiti-nibandhinī* — Or, an arisen activity possessing an object-field secures steadiness of the mind.

## 2. Commentary

The recurring *vā* keeps the paths of practice open. I.35 names an arisen
*pravṛtti* that is *viṣayavatī*, furnished with a determinate object-field.
Such an activity can secure *manasaḥ sthiti*, the mind's steadiness. The
sūtra therefore gives content a positive role. It does not say that
steadiness requires the mind to become empty of every object.

The [Kośa Bhāṣya at VAK 8.01](../../../Vasubandhu/VAK/08-samapatti/VAK_8.01_bhasya.md)
defines *ekāgratā* through *ekālambanatā*: one object-support. That
comparison helps us read steadiness as a determinate relation, not mere
absence of movement. Yet the Kośa is discussing *samāpatti* in its own
technical setting, and I.35 has not itself supplied that name or a
particular object.

The movement toward Yoga's [I.41
*samāpatti*](../../../Docs/Prajnapada/ys-i-41-fichte-report.md) is visible
without being complete. There consciousness is described in relation to
the grasper, grasping, and grasped. Here the sūtra establishes the simpler
point: an object-bearing activity can help mind remain steady enough for
further investigation.

## 3. Project Determination

For our Science of Consciousness, a stable object-field is a support for
knowing. It gives practice something determinate to hold and to examine.
In our Organon reading of I.41, grasper, grasping, and grasped can be worked
through as Principle Base, Essence Base, and Form Base. They are held
together in one disciplined rational process; this mapping is our
architecture, not an assertion made by I.35. The greater disclosure of
*samāpatti* must still be read from I.41 onward.
