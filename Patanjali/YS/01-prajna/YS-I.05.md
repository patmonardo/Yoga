# YS I.5 — The Fivefold Movements

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

> वृत्तयः पञ्चतय्यः क्लिष्टाऽक्लिष्टाः ॥ १.५॥

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

> vṛttayaḥ pañcatayyaḥ kliṣṭākliṣṭāḥ // 1.5 //

## 3. Lexical Analysis

| Pada | Form | Force |
|---|---|---|
| *vṛttayaḥ* | nominative feminine plural | movements of consciousness |
| *pañcatayyaḥ* | nominative feminine plural | fivefold, of five kinds |
| *kliṣṭāḥ* | nominative feminine plural | afflicted |
| *akliṣṭāḥ* | nominative feminine plural | unafflicted |

## 4. Grammar

The four forms agree with *vṛttayaḥ*. The source writes *kliṣṭā'kliṣṭāḥ* with avagraha marking the elided initial *a-* of *akliṣṭāḥ*. The sūtra gives two distinct cuts: five kinds of movement, and afflicted or unafflicted condition. I.6 names the five kinds.

## 5. Translation

### Literal translation

> The movements are fivefold, afflicted and unafflicted.

## 6. Logical Determination

The fivefold division concerns **kind**; the afflicted/unafflicted division concerns **condition**. Each kind must be examined under that second distinction. The verse neither identifies one kind with affliction nor supplies the five names before I.6.

## 7. Interpretive Note

I.5 gives Yoga's compact classification of the inner instrument's operations. Kośa's analysis of *citta* and associated *caittas* will help determine how a movement arises and what makes it afflicted. The classification prepares the Īśvara-centered inquiry; it does not complete it.
