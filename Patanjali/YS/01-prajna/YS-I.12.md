# YS I.12 — Nirodha

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

> अभ्यासवैराग्याभ्यां तन्निरोधः ॥ १.१२॥

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

> abhyāsavairāgyābhyāṃ tannirodhaḥ // 1.12 //

## 3. Lexical Analysis

| Pada | Form | Force |
|---|---|---|
| *abhyāsa* | compound member | repeated practice |
| *vairāgya* | compound member | dispassion, freedom from attachment |
| *-ābhyām* | instrumental dual ending | by the two together |
| *tat* | demonstrative, assimilated before *nirodhaḥ* | of those movements just discussed |
| *nirodhaḥ* | nominative masculine singular | restraint, cessation |

## 4. Grammar

The instrumental dual *abhyāsa-vairāgyābhyām* names two means. In *tan-nirodhaḥ*, *tat* looks back to the *vṛttis* of I.5–11, while *nirodhaḥ* is the sentence's nominative head. The sūtra gives the means of their restraint; it does not yet define either means. I.13–16 will do that.

## 5. Translation

### Literal translation

> Their restraint is by practice and dispassion.

## 6. Logical Determination

I.12 returns from the five named movements to I.2's *nirodha*. The dual instrumental makes practice and dispassion jointly operative. The received Bhāṣya describes consciousness as a river flowing toward either discernment and liberation or non-discernment and worldly continuation: dispassion blocks the current toward objects, while practice of discerning vision opens the current toward discernment. That explanation gives the pairing a direction without turning the two into successive steps.

## 7. Interpretive Note

The [companion essay](../essays/nirodha-according-to-kosa.md) compares the Kośa's discriminative cessation and cessation through missing conditions with Yoga's *nirodha*. The comparison does not equate the Kośa's two unconditioned dharmas with Yoga's restraint of the movements.
