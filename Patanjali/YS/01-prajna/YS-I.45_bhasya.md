# YS I.45 — Our Bhāṣya

## 1. Sūtra Anchor

> सूक्ष्मविषयत्वं चालिङ्गपर्यवसानम्॥ १.४५॥

> *sūkṣma-viṣayatvaṃ cāliṅga-paryavasānam* — Subtle objectivity terminates at *aliṅga*.

## 2. Commentary

I.44 turned samāpatti toward subtle *viṣaya*. I.45 gives that inquiry
an end: *aliṅga*, what has no further manifest mark. The end is
significant for Vicāra. Reflection follows a determination through
its relations and marks; it must then discover the limit of treating
those marks as independently sufficient explanations.

The fourfold division in [II.19](../../Sources/Yoga.txt) makes
the movement readable: *viśeṣa*, *aviśeṣa*, *liṅgamātra*, *aliṅga*.
They move from specified product through subtler generative condition
to mark as such and the unmarked root. The local
[Sāṃkhya Kārikā 3](../../../Reference/Samkhya.txt) distinguishes
root-prakṛti from the productive-and-produced series beginning with
*mahat*. This supports the difference between the unmarked terminus
and marked effects without reducing Yoga's four terms to a list of
Sāṃkhya entities. They are four divisions of the guṇas within the
seen. *Aliṅga* is thus not a name for the whole of Reality or a
simple synonym for the pure Principle.

At the basic Kośa level, [VAK 1.32 Bhāṣya](../../../Vasubandhu/VAK/01-dhatu/VAK_1.32_bhasya.md)
helps distinguish the cognitive factors *vitarka* and *vicāra*.
Yoga's own I.44–45 determines what the subtle reflective inquiry
reaches. The Kośa comparison does not supply Yoga's four *guṇa-parvans*.

## 3. Project Determination

Our Central Mark System treats *liṅga* as a determinate sign through
which Reflection can trace a relation. *Aliṅga* is the limit at
which that procedure cannot simply add one more mark. The Dharma
Base is active through its Dharma Essence and Dharma Principle:
formed determinations are reflected into their conditions, and the
Principle of that reflection must be found rather than presumed.
This is a first pass on the four divisions; their full reconstruction
belongs with the later study of II.19.
