# YS I.31 — Our Bhāṣya

## 1. Sūtra Anchor

> दुःखदौर्मनस्याङ्गमेजयत्वश्वासप्रश्वासा विक्षेपसहभुवः ॥ १.३१॥

> *duḥkha-daurmanasya-aṅgamejayatva-śvāsa-praśvāsā vikṣepa-sahabhuvaḥ* — Pain, mental distress, trembling of the limbs, and breathing disturbances accompany dispersion.

## 2. Commentary

I.30 named nine obstacles and gathered them as *citta-vikṣepa*. I.31 now
describes their felt and bodily company. *Duḥkha* and *daurmanasya* should
not be collapsed: pain and dejection need not be the same occurrence.
*Aṅgamejayatva* names disturbance of the limbs; *śvāsa-praśvāsa* brings
breathing into view. The key word is *sahabhuvaḥ*: these arise with
dispersion. This is an account of accompaniment, not a claim that all four
must be present in every practitioner.

The [Kośa at VAK 2.09](../../../Vasubandhu/VAK/02-indriya/VAK_2.09_bhasya.md)
distinguishes painful feeling from *daurmanasya*, the mental displeasure
faculty. Its analysis can sharpen our reading of the first two Yoga terms
without importing its whole faculty taxonomy into the sūtra. Śaṅkara's
[Gītā 6.2 comment](../../../Sankara/BG/BG-C06/gita-6.02.md) says that
intention fixed on action's fruit causes *citta-vikṣepa*. That shows one
way dispersion is generated, while I.31 describes how it is lived.

The next movement matters. I.32 answers dispersion through practice of one
principle; I.33 then introduces the four relational cultivations. The
[Kośa's immeasurables at VAK 8.29–30](../../../Vasubandhu/VAK/08-samapatti/VAK_8.29.md)
will be especially useful there. We can follow that sequence toward the
explicit account of *samāpatti* at I.41 without forcing the later result
into this diagnosis.

## 3. Project Determination

The Science of Consciousness attends to the full condition of dispersion:
feeling, bodily steadiness, and breath as well as the objects and judgments
named among the obstacles. The work toward *samāpatti* begins with this
concrete field of practice.
