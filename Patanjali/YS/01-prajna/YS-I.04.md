# YS I.4 — Conformity to the Movements

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

> वृत्तिसारूप्यमितरत्र ॥ १.४॥

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

> vṛttisārūpyam itaratra // 1.4 //

## 3. Lexical Analysis

| Pada | Form | Force |
|---|---|---|
| *vṛtti* | compound member | movement of consciousness |
| *sārūpyam* | neuter singular noun | conformity of form, taking the same form |
| *itaratra* | indeclinable | elsewhere; in the other condition |

## 4. Grammar

The compound *vṛtti-sārūpyam* is the nominal predicate. *Itaratra* contrasts with *tadā* in I.3. The seer remains the implicit subject of the contrast; I.4 does not repeat *draṣṭuḥ*.

## 5. Translation

### Literal translation

> In the other condition, conformity to the movements.

### Connected translation

> Otherwise, the seer appears in conformity with the movements of consciousness.

## 6. Logical Determination

I.3 gives abiding in the seer's own form; I.4 gives conformity to the form of the *vṛttis* in the other condition. The contrast does not require the seer to become a movement. It asks how the movement's determinate form can be taken as the seer's form. I.5 will distinguish afflicted and unafflicted movements.

## 7. Interpretive Note

Kośa VAK 2.23 and 2.34 show that a cognition appears with associated factors sharing its support, object, and aspect. This gives *vṛtti-sārūpya* a concrete comparison: determinate cognition has a form through which something appears. Yoga's claim concerns the seer's conformity to that form; the Kośa does not thereby supply a *puruṣa* or settle the seer's status.
