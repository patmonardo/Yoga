# YS I.38 — Our Bhāṣya

## 1. Sūtra Anchor

> स्वप्ननिद्राज्ञानालम्बनं वा ॥ १.३८॥

> *svapna-nidrā-jñāna-ālambanaṃ vā* — Or, [consciousness] supported by knowledge of dream and sleep.

## 2. Commentary

The *vā* continues the alternatives. I.37 offered a dispassionate object;
I.38 names a support involving *svapna*, *nidrā*, and *jñāna*. The
compound is compressed, but its inclusion of *jñāna* matters. The practice
uses knowledge connected with dream and sleep; the sūtra does not praise
ordinary drowsiness or treat every dream image as authoritative.

The [Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad 4–5](../../../Sankara/Organon/sankara/derived/Mandukya/MK_C01.json)
distinguishes the dream condition as inward knowing and deep sleep as a
different condition in which no dream is seen. Its exposition gives our
Organon Yoga package a disciplined way to study what knowledge of these
states could mean. The Māṇḍūkya is not a gloss on Yoga I.38; it is a
second text whose determinations can be brought into the inquiry.

The [Kośa Bhāṣya at VAK 5.47](../../../Vasubandhu/VAK/05-anusaya/VAK_5.47_bhasya.md)
includes *middha*, sleepiness, among the enveloping afflictions when it is
afflicted. This is a different classification from Yoga's dream-and-sleep
knowledge. The difference is useful: a condition of dullness cannot simply
be substituted for the cognitive support I.38 names.

As we approach I.41, the question is how consciousness takes even dream
and sleep as determinate fields of inquiry. Our Principle Base, Essence
Base, and Form Base account belongs to that later *samāpatti* analysis; it
must be developed through the actual distinctions these states disclose.

## 3. Project Determination

Dream and sleep can be studied as modes in which consciousness and its
appearances are differentiated. Their knowledge, rather than their mere
occurrence, is the support named here. The fuller Organon treatment will
follow the Māṇḍūkya's own sequence before using it to interpret Yoga.
