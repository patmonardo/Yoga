# YS I.9 — Word-Led Vikalpa

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

> शब्दज्ञानानुपाती वस्तुशून्यो विकल्पः ॥ १.९॥

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

> śabdajñānānupātī vastuśūnyo vikalpaḥ // 1.9 //

## 3. Lexical Analysis

| Pada | Form | Force |
|---|---|---|
| *śabdajñānānupātī* | nominative masculine singular adjective; *śabda-jñāna-anupātin* | following verbal cognition |
| *vastuśūnyaḥ* | nominative masculine singular adjective, with *-o* before *v-* | empty of a corresponding thing |
| *vikalpaḥ* | nominative masculine singular | conceptual construction, differentiation |

## 4. Grammar

The two adjectives agree with *vikalpaḥ*. *Śabdajñāna* can be read as cognition arising through words; *anupātin* marks what follows or accompanies it. *Vastuśūnyaḥ* specifies the absence of a corresponding thing, not the absence of the verbal cognition itself. The sūtra is a nominal definition: it supplies no separate finite verb.

## 5. Translation

### Literal translation

> Vikalpa follows verbal cognition and is empty of a corresponding thing.

## 6. Logical Determination

I.7 named valid cognition; I.8 defined mistaken cognition by its false fit to an object. I.9 marks another movement: a word-led content is articulated while the corresponding *vastu* is absent. This is why its difference from *viparyaya* matters. The mere presence of words does not establish the thing the words appear to present.

## 7. Interpretive Note

The [Kośa essay](../essays/01-prajna/vikalpa-according-to-kosa.md) distinguishes intrinsic, examining, and recollective *vikalpa*. That wider Kośa range makes Yoga's word-led, *vastuśūnya* case more specific. Kośa *sālambana* says cognition takes an object-support; it does not establish the corresponding being that a word proposes. In our project register, Principle and Rule determine when an image becomes a workable object-model. That reconstruction tests the word's claim to *vastu*; it is not a hidden translation of I.9.
