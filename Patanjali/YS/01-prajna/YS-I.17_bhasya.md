# YS I.17 — Our Bhāṣya

## 1. Sūtra Anchor

> वितर्कविचारानन्दास्मितारूपानुगमात्सम्प्रज्ञातः ॥ १.१७॥

> *vitarka-vicāra-ānanda-asmitā-rūpa-anugamāt saṃprajñātaḥ* — The [samādhi] accompanied by these four forms is *saṃprajñāta*.

## 2. Commentary

After practice and dispassion, the sūtra names the determinate samādhi to be explained. The received Yoga Bhāṣya calls *vitarka* gross engagement of consciousness with an object-support and *vicāra* its subtler engagement; it glosses *ānanda* as delight and *asmitā* as unitary awareness. Its fourfold sequence removes one preceding feature at each step until *asmitā* alone remains. Every member is still *sālambana*. This last statement prevents the progressively subtler condition from being mistaken for the unsupported samādhi discussed next.

The Kośa provides a precise comparison for the first distinction. It defines *vitarka* by the relative grossness of mind and *vicāra* by its subtlety. In VAK 8.1, meditative attainment is wholesome one-pointedness of mind with a single object-support; in 8.7–8, the factors of the four *dhyānas* change in specified ways. The comparison illuminates how one meditative field can be differentiated by the presence and absence of factors. It does not identify Yoga's *ānanda* with Kośa *prīti* or *sukha*, or Yoga's *asmitā* with any Kośa factor. The [companion essay](../essays/samprajnata-and-kosa-dhyana.md) sets out the passages.

## 3. Project Determination

The source-grounded determination is a graded analysis of object-supported samādhi. The Yoga Bhāṣya supplies the progression, while the Kośa gives a distinct, comparably exact analysis of mental texture, object-support, and meditative factors. Their lists should be read together as a productive comparison, not as one inherited classification.
