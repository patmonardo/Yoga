# YS I.37 — Our Bhāṣya

## 1. Sūtra Anchor

> वीतरागविषयं वा चित्तम्॥ १.३७॥

> *vītarāga-viṣayaṃ vā cittam* — Or, consciousness whose object is the dispassionate.

## 2. Commentary

The *vā* continues the open series. I.37 gives consciousness an object-field
defined by freedom from *rāga*. Attending to a dispassionate one or to a
dispassionate mode of consciousness can steady the practitioner by making
freedom from attachment present as something intelligible. The sūtra itself
does not name an individual or prescribe a technique for this attention.

[Gītā 4.10](../../../Sankara/BG/BG-C04/gita-4.10.md) describes many who
are free from attachment, fear, and anger, devoted to the Lord and purified
by the austerity of knowledge. Śaṅkara interprets this as a movement toward
the Lord's state through knowledge. This gives a concrete example of
*vītarāga* without making the Gītā's full account the meaning of I.37.

The [Kośa Bhāṣya at VAK 6.30](../../../Vasubandhu/VAK/06-marga/VAK_6.30_bhasya.md)
calls a practitioner *vītarāga* with respect to the desire realm after the
relevant grade of affliction has been abandoned. This is more specific than
a vague mood of detachment. The Yoga sūtra does not specify that realm or
the same path stage, so the comparison clarifies the term without fixing
its whole doctrinal setting.

Our Buddha as Īśvara reading may take the Buddha as a compelling exemplar
of the dispassionate. That is one way to use the open *viṣaya* in practice;
I.37 neither requires nor excludes a particular exemplar. The focus here
is on the steadiness of consciousness directed toward realized freedom,
while I.41 will give the fuller account of *samāpatti*.

## 3. Project Determination

The dispassionate becomes an object of study because the practitioner can
grasp the principle of freedom through its realized expression. This is
part of our movement from a determinate Form Base through Essence toward
Principle, held within one rational process rather than a detached image
of an admirable person.
