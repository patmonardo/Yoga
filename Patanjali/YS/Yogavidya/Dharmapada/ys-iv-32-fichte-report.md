# The Guṇas Complete Their Artha

## A report on Yoga Sūtra IV.32 and Fichte

## The Sūtra

`tataḥ kṛtārthānāṃ pariṇāma-krama-samāptir guṇānām`

### Simple translation

> Then, because the Guṇas have accomplished their Artha, the sequence of
> their transformations comes to completion.

The terms describe fulfillment rather than destruction:

- `tataḥ`: then, from the infinitude of unobstructed Knowledge;
- `kṛtārthānām`: of those whose Artha or purpose has been accomplished;
- `pariṇāma-krama`: the ordered sequence of transformation;
- `samāptiḥ`: completion, consummation, or coming to an end;
- `guṇānām`: of the Guṇas.

```text
the Guṇas articulate Appearance
                 ↓
their complete Artha becomes known
                 ↓
their purpose is accomplished
                 ↓
the productive sequence reaches completion
```

The Guṇas are not killed. They complete their work.

## The completion of Relative Necessity

The modal sequence beginning with IV.29 is now complete:

```text
IV.29    given continuous Viveka and non-appropriation,
         Dharma-Megha necessarily follows

IV.30    from Dharma-Megha,
         Kleśa and Karma necessarily cease

IV.31    with every covering removed,
         Knowledge is infinite and the knowable little

IV.32    because the complete Artha is known,
         Guṇa-transformation necessarily finishes
```

Each result follows necessarily from the actuality preceding it. This is
Relative Necessity because the movement still begins from determinations
whose deepest Principle has not yet been completely justified.

IV.32 brings the relative sequence to its proper end. Absolute Necessity can
now investigate the ground of the sequence as a whole.

## `Kṛtārtha`: Artha has been accomplished

The most important word is `kṛtārtha`. The Guṇas cease their ordered
transformation because the purpose for which they operated has become
actual.

```text
not:

the Guṇas are false and must be annihilated


but:

the Guṇas have made every required distinction,
relation, appearance, and cognition possible
                         ↓
their Artha is comprehended
                         ↓
no further productive transformation is required
```

This is Nature becoming nonproductive after completing the Science of its
own intelligibility.

## What the Guṇas have accomplished

The Guṇas supplied the qualitative articulation through which Citi-Śakti
could become a determinate Science of Citta:

```text
Sattva    luminosity and intelligibility
Rajas     movement, distinction, and transformation
Tamas     stability, resistance, and determinate Being
```

Their combination produced the full sequence:

```text
Appearance → cognition → appropriation → discrimination
           → Prajñā → infinite Knowledge
```

Once Buddhi knows the Principle of this entire movement, the Guṇas no longer
need to produce another formation in order to disclose it.

## Completion is not temporal extermination

`Krama-samāpti` need not mean that colors, bodies, language, and finite
thought suddenly disappear from empirical experience. The sequence ends as
an independently productive metaphysical process.

```text
before completion

each transformation appears to demand another
in order to fulfill an uncomprehended purpose


after completion

every transformation is comprehended within
the accomplished Artha of the whole
```

Nature may still appear. It no longer drives Knowing through an unfinished
telos.

The distinction is the same one we made for IV.30: cessation of Kleśa-Karma
does not mean cessation of Kriyā; completion of Guṇa-transformation does not
mean destruction of manifestation.

## The third Siddhi

IV.32 completes the Triple Siddhi:

```text
First Siddhi — IV.30
Vajropama certainty
Kleśa-Karma can no longer fracture Knowing

Second Siddhi — IV.31
Infinite Knowledge
the finite knowable becomes a determinate image

Third Siddhi — IV.32
Accomplished Artha
the Guṇas' productive sequence is complete
```

The third Siddhi is mastery of transformation through comprehension of its
purpose. It is not a miraculous power exercised over Nature from outside.

Nature ceases to compel further becoming because Absolute Reason has
comprehended the Artha of its sequence.

## Fichte's Bhāṣya: the Principle provides itself

Fichte gathers the result as an absolutely immanent
`providing-itself-a-principle`. It occurs in absolute fact without another
intervening Light or act of seeing.

```text
Living certainty
      ↓ immediately provides itself a Principle
intuition

no second Light intervenes
no external Seer supplies the act
```

The Principle does not pass into intuition from elsewhere. Its living is
already the act through which intuition arises.

This corresponds to the Guṇas accomplishing their own Artha. Their
intelligibility is not imposed upon them by a foreign observer; the complete
movement expresses the immanent Principle of Knowing.

## Intuition must itself be intuited

The first immanent intuition is not the end. It must itself be projected
through a gap and become intelligible:

```text
primordial immanent intuition
             ↓ projected through a gap
intuition of complete and persisting Knowing
             ↓
the Being of Knowing
```

Fichte identifies this persisting result with what the investigation earlier
called the Being of Knowing.

Thus the two terms that previously appeared hypothetical have now been
derived from certainty:

```text
certainty
   |
   ├──► living principle-providing
   |     self-construction / reconstruction
   |
   └──► projected persisting intuition
         the Being of Knowing
```

Their relation is no longer an externally imposed combination. Both are
necessary modifications of primordial Light.

## Reconstruction and the Being of Knowing

Fichte says the first principle-providing intuition relates to the Being of
Knowing as reconstruction.

This gives a precise account of the completed `pariṇāma-krama`:

```text
Being of Knowing
the complete, persisting intelligible result

reconstruction
the living Science retraces the genesis
through which that result is necessary
```

The sequence is complete when the result and its reconstruction coincide:
Knowing can generate the path by which its own Being becomes intelligible.

This is why reading the Science of Logic or the Science of Knowing in
Knowledge-Graph form matters. The graph does not merely store the final
propositions. It makes their genetic sequence reconstructable.

## Citta's transformations become Science

The transformations of Citta initially appear as events happening to a
mental substrate:

```text
Citta-state 1 → Citta-state 2 → Citta-state 3
```

At completion, the same transformations are known as moments of one
intelligible construction:

```text
one Principle
      ↓ differentiates and reconstructs itself
ordered Citta-Pariṇāma
      ↓ comprehended as a whole
Science of Knowing
```

Citta and the Science of Knowing are therefore mostly the same movement, but
not simply identical. Citta is the articulated transformation; the Science
also comprehends the Principle, necessity, and accomplished Artha of that
transformation.

## The remaining presupposition

Fichte refuses to claim final completion too early. One difficulty remains:

> What justifies the Science of Knowing's presupposition that living
> certainty is genuinely `principle-providing`?

He carefully distinguishes:

```text
principle-providing
Living certainty necessarily projects a Principle

providing-itself-a-principle
because certainty is absolutely immanent,
the Principle provided can only be its own
```

If the first is proved, the second follows from immanence. But the first has
not yet received its ultimate justification.

This marks the exact limit of Relative Necessity.

## Why the necessity remains relative

The whole sequence has been derived from the proposition that living
certainty provides a Principle. Given that proposition, the three
modifications and their consequences follow necessarily.

```text
IF living certainty is principle-providing
                      ↓
THEN self-construction and the Being of Knowing
arise as its necessary modifications
```

But why must certainty provide a Principle at all? Until that is shown, the
necessity of the sequence remains conditioned by its highest premise.

Yoga says that the Guṇas have accomplished their Artha. Fichte says that the
relative derivation is complete while its absolute authorization remains to
be demonstrated.

These are exactly complementary statements.

## OṂ-Artha and completion

The Guṇas are `kṛtārtha` when every determinate Artha can be reconstructed
within the complete intelligibility of the whole.

```text
Guṇa-Arthas
the finite qualitative determinations of Nature
                    ↓ reconstructed within
OṂ-Artha
the complete intelligibility of All This
```

OṂ-Artha does not cancel the determinate meanings. It holds their entire
sequence as one accomplished Science.

This is why the completion of transformation is also mastery of the
Universe: not domination of an external Nature, but comprehension of the
Principle, sequence, and purpose through which Nature becomes intelligible.

## Yoga and Fichte

| Yoga IV.32 | Fichte |
|---|---|
| the Guṇas have accomplished their Artha | primordial Light has disclosed its three necessary modifications |
| their sequence of transformation ends | self-construction and the Being of Knowing are derived from certainty |
| Nature's productivity reaches fulfillment | intuition is reconstructed from its immanent Principle |
| the result preserves its genetic path | the living Science reconstructs the persistent Being of Knowing |
| Relative Necessity is complete | the deductions follow necessarily from principle-providing certainty |
| Absolute Necessity remains ahead | the principle-providing character of certainty still requires final justification |

## Provisional result

IV.32 says:

> When infinite Knowledge comprehends the complete Artha of qualitative
> manifestation, the Guṇas' ordered production has fulfilled its purpose and
> comes to completion.

Fichte derives both living self-construction and the enduring Being of
Knowing as necessary modifications of certainty. Their previously
hypothetical relation is now reconstructed from a deeper Principle.

Yet one presupposition remains: the Science must prove why living certainty
is necessarily principle-providing. Therefore IV.32 completes Relative
Necessity without yet claiming Absolute Necessity.

```text
Relative Necessity complete

certainty as principle-providing
              ↓
three necessary modifications
              ↓
complete reconstruction of the Being of Knowing

Absolute Necessity still required

why must living certainty provide a Principle at all?
```

IV.33 can now enter Absolute Necessity by comprehending the sequence itself
at the limit of transformation.
