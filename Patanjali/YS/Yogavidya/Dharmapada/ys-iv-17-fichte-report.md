# When the Thing Colors Citta

## A report on Yoga Sūtra IV.17, Vyāsa, and Fichte

## The Sūtra

`tad-uparāgāpekṣitvāc cittasya vastu jñātājñātam`

### Simple translation

> A Thing is known or unknown according to whether it colors Citta.

More explicitly:

> Because Citta depends upon being affected by the Thing, the Thing is either
> known or unknown.

`Tad` refers to the Thing. `Uparāga` is coloring, tinging, affecting, or
entering into close relation. `Apekṣitva` is dependence upon or requirement
of that condition. `Jñāta-ajñāta` distinguishes the known from the unknown.

The Thing does not become existent when it colors Citta. IV.16 has already
denied that dependence. It becomes **known** to this Citta.

```text
Thing exists, but does not color Citta  →  unknown to this Citta

Thing colors Citta                      →  known to this Citta
```

IV.17 therefore identifies the condition of Appearance for a particular
Citta.

## From Thing and World to Appearance in Citta

IV.13–16 established the objective field:

```text
IV.13    manifest and subtle Dharmas are Guṇa-nature
IV.14    one transformation constitutes the Thing
IV.15    the Thing and its cognition have distinct paths
IV.16    the Thing does not depend upon one Citta
```

We are now past the initial constitution and defense of `Vastu`. A world of
independently determinate Things has been secured.

IV.17 turns to the positive relation through which one of those Things
appears in Citta:

```text
World of Things
       |
       | selective relation
       v
coloring of Citta
       |
       v
this Thing is known
```

Not everything that is actual is presently known. Knowledge requires a
specific relation between the Thing and Citta.

## Vyāsa's Bhāṣya

Vyāsa compares objects to magnets and Citta to iron. A magnet affects iron
when the two enter into an appropriate relation. Similarly, an object enters
into relation with Citta and colors it.

```text
magnet          relation          iron

Thing           uparāga           Citta
```

The object by which Citta is colored is known. Other objects, which do not
color it, remain unknown.

Vyāsa draws an important conclusion: Citta is `pariṇāmin`, transformable. It
has the forms “this is known” and “this is unknown” because it changes
according to its relations with objects.

This is not the private creation of IV.16. The magnet is not produced by the
iron it affects. The analogy instead joins objectivity with cognitive
transformation:

> The independently existing Thing becomes known by producing a corresponding
> determination in Citta.

## What `coloring` means

Coloring must be distinguished from three weaker notions.

It is not bare physical proximity. Many objects may be near us without being
known.

It is not a passive photograph deposited in Citta. Citta undergoes a
determinate transformation.

It is not necessarily emotional contamination. Attachment and aversion can
color Citta, but `uparāga` here names the more fundamental condition under
which any object becomes cognitively manifest.

```text
uparāga

Thing and Citta enter a determinate relation
                         ↓
Citta takes on a form adequate to presenting the Thing
                         ↓
the Thing is known
```

The quality of that coloring remains open. As IV.15 showed, it may become
pleasant, painful, confused, or equanimous according to Citta's condition.

## Fichte's Bhāṣya

Fichte returns to his central proposition:

> Light permeates the `from` in the qualitative oneness of its essence.

The exercises in energetic reflection given at IV.15–16 were intuitive means
of displaying this penetration. They allowed us to see energy, genesis, and
manifestness open together.

But Fichte now says the result does not depend upon those particular facts or
examples. It is necessary a priori:

> If Light is to be, it must permeate the `from`.

Light cannot remain on one side while genesis occurs on the other. A `from`
that was not permeated by Light would not appear and could not be known. A
Light that did not permeate genesis would be an empty qualitative point with
no manifestation.

```text
Light without the “from”       no determinate Appearance

“from” without Light           no seeing of the genesis

Light permeating the “from”    genesis is manifest as knowing
```

This is Fichte's specialized version of the relation Yoga calls `uparāga`.
Yoga speaks of the Thing coloring Citta. Fichte speaks of Light penetrating
genesis so that the genesis is itself manifest.

## Do not search for knowing outside knowing

Fichte then states the methodological consequence. We prove the essential
character of knowing through our capacity actually to see it:

> We ourselves are knowing; because we can know in this way and now actually
> know in this way, knowing is constituted in this way.

To demand that knowing be demonstrated from a standpoint outside knowing is
to make the subject inaccessible in principle. Every proposed external proof
would itself have to be known and would therefore return us to knowing.

```text
attempt to prove knowing from outside
                   ↓
the alleged external proof must be known
                   ↓
we are already within knowing
```

Fichte's proof is therefore performative rather than observational. Its
object must become manifest in the disciplined act of knowing itself.

## Attention as a condition

Fichte emphasizes that this insight requires sharp thinking, strong
attention, and preparation. Something can be maximally clear in itself and
still remain concealed from a Citta that does not fulfil the conditions for
seeing it.

This aligns exactly with IV.17:

```text
clarity of the Thing or insight     ≠     its being known by this Citta

knowledge requires the relation in which Citta is affected by it
```

The unknown is therefore not necessarily obscure in itself. It may be
unknown because Citta has not entered the required relation, lacks the
relevant capacity, or refuses the discipline of attention.

## Yoga and Fichte

| Yoga IV.17 | Fichte |
|---|---|
| the Thing colors Citta | Light permeates the `from` |
| known and unknown depend upon this relation | genesis is manifest only through the penetration of Light |
| Citta transforms according to the object | knowing appears together with its genesis |
| cognition requires a condition of Citta | insight requires energetic reflection and strong attention |

The directions differ. Yoga begins from the Thing and asks how it becomes
known in Citta. Fichte begins from Light and asks how genesis becomes
manifest within knowing.

Their convergence is the principle of Appearance:

> Neither an isolated Thing nor an empty knowing is an Appearance. Appearance
> is the determinate penetration through which the Thing is present in
> knowing.

## Practical Reason and the Kośa

IV.17 makes the practical dimension explicit. Citta is not equally open to
every Thing. Its prior conditioning governs what can color it and how.

The Kośa provides a detailed analysis of those conditions: attention,
contact, feeling, recognition, volition, Saṃskāra, and the accompanying
mental factors. Its practical disciplines do not merely add moral opinions
after cognition. They transform the conditions of manifestation itself.

For example:

```text
another being's suffering
          |
          +-- Citta closed by indifference  → remains practically unknown
          |
          +-- Citta colored by aversion     → appears as a threat or burden
          |
          +-- Citta trained in Karuṇā       → appears as a call for response
```

The suffering is not manufactured by these Cittas. But what it becomes for
knowledge and action depends upon the coloring of Citta.

This is why the Immeasurables are modes of Practical Reason. They educate
`uparāga`: they train how All This enters and determines Citta.

## Citta-mastery

Citta-mastery cannot mean preventing all coloring. A Citta wholly incapable
of relation would know no Thing at all.

Mastery instead concerns whether Citta can receive the object's
determination without compulsively overlaying it with inherited Vāsanā:

```text
unmastered coloring

Thing + attachment/aversion → distorted practical appearance


mastered coloring

Thing adequately determines Citta → knowledge and fitting action
```

The practical goal is not colorlessness as blank isolation. It is adequate
coloring: Citta taking the form required for the Thing to be known without
confusing its own conditioning with the Thing's path.

## Provisional result

IV.13–16 established the World of Appearance and the independence of the
Thing from any one Citta. IV.17 now states the condition under which a Thing
within that world becomes known:

> A Thing is known or unknown according to whether it colors Citta.

Vyāsa explains this as the relation of magnet and iron and concludes that
Citta is transformable.

Fichte explains the corresponding structure within knowing: Light must
permeate genesis, and this penetration must be realized through knowing's own
disciplined activity rather than observed from outside.

The result advances both theory and practice:

> Objectivity establishes that the Thing is there; disciplined `uparāga`
> determines whether and how its reality becomes knowledge for Citta.

IV.18 must now ask what remains constant while these Citta-transformations
alternate between the known and unknown.
