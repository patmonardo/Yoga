# Dual Coloring as the Absolute Attribute

## A report on Yoga Sūtra IV.23 with Hegel and Fichte

## The Sūtra

`draṣṭṛ-dṛśyoparaktaṃ cittaṃ sarvārtham`

### Simple translation

> Citta, colored by both the Seer and the seen, bears every Artha.

The Sūtra contains one Citta and two colorings:

```text
draṣṭṛ                       dṛśya
Seer                         seen
reflecting/thinking form     objective form
       \                     /
        \ colors one Citta  /
         \                 /
              sarvārtha
          bearing every Artha
```

The two poles do not contribute two unrelated halves. Each colors the whole
Citta according to its own form.

This is Hegel's Absolute Attribute.

## Why IV.23 follows IV.22

IV.22 said that Cit does not migrate. Citta assumes its form, and Buddhi
knows itself.

IV.23 shows what this self-cognition contains. Buddhi is not transparent to
Cit by becoming empty. It comprehends the complete relation of knowing and
objectivity within Citta.

```text
IV.22
Citta assumes the form of non-migrating Cit
                      ↓
IV.23
the whole Citta displays itself under
the Seer-form and the seen-form
```

Self-cognition is therefore already dual articulation. The Principle knows
itself through the complete difference of its Aspects.

## Attribute means the whole under one form

Hegel calls an Attribute the `relative Absolute`: the Absolute expressed in
a determinate form.

The word `relative` does not mean that the Attribute contains only a piece
of the Absolute. Hegel insists that the Attribute contains the whole content
and subsistence of the Absolute.

```text
Absolute content
       ↓ under the form of knowing
Seer-Attribute

Absolute content
       ↓ under the form of objectivity
seen-Attribute
```

Each Attribute is the whole, expressed according to a distinct
determination.

This gives `uparakta` a stronger meaning than passive staining. Each
coloring is a complete formal presentation of Citta.

## Both poles have Prajñā

The reflecting or thinking pole bears Prajñā: it is Citta colored by the
Seer. But this pole must not be called Knowing by itself. Knowing belongs to
the completed relation, not exclusively to its subjective form.
But the objective pole is not unintelligible material awaiting illumination
from outside. As an Attribute, it also bears the complete intelligible
content under the form of the seen.

```text
Seer-coloring                    seen-coloring

Prajñā as reflection            Prajñā as intelligible Being
or thinking                     of the objective determination
              \                /
               \              /
            one complete Citta
```

The two Prajñās are not two independent minds. They are two principled forms
in which one content is established as knowable.

Their combination is a third determination: Citta knows the identity and
difference of the two complete forms.

## From the two Worlds to dual coloring

Hegel recalls the two Worlds developed under Appearance:

```text
phenomenal World                 World in and for itself
outer expression                inner Law
existent immediacy              reflected immediacy
```

Each World was supposed to stand opposite the other, yet each repeatedly
displayed the same content:

- Whole and Parts contained the same totality;
- Force and Expression contained the same content;
- Inner and Outer proved identical in determination.

The two sides were not different contents. Their difference lay in the form
of immediacy through which the same whole appeared.

IV.23 compresses this result:

```text
dṛśya-coloring                  draṣṭṛ-coloring
phenomenal form                 in-and-for-itself form
              \                /
               same sarvārtha
```

## The immediacies become reflective shine

Before the Absolute, each side claimed an immediate subsistence of its own:

```text
the seen seems to exist by itself

the Seer seems to subsist by itself
```

In the Absolute, these separate immediacies are reduced to `reflective
shine`. Their distinction remains, but neither can claim self-subsistence
outside the complete relation.

```text
independent Seer               independent seen
        ↓ loses isolation     loses isolation ↓
Seer-Attribute                 seen-Attribute
        \                       /
         subsist only in the Absolute relation
```

Reflective shine does not mean illusion in the sense of mere nothing. It
means a determination whose Being consists in manifesting the whole rather
than standing independently.

## Why Citta is `sarvārtha`

Citta bears every Artha because each of its colorings contains the total
content, and their relation makes the content explicit as Knowing.

```text
not:

subject contributes awareness
object contributes content
and the halves are externally glued together


but:

the complete content appears under two total forms
whose identity and difference constitute Citta
```

`Sarvārtha` therefore names more than openness to indefinitely many objects.
It names the formal completeness of the field: any Artha can be articulated
as both determinate Being and known determination.

## Citta is not a mixture

The metaphor of coloring can suggest that Citta is a neutral substrate on
which two foreign pigments are deposited. Hegel's Attribute blocks that
interpretation.

```text
not:

neutral Citta + Seer-color + object-color


but:

Citta is actual only as the complete content
expressing itself through both determinate forms
```

There is no colorless Citta hidden underneath its Attributes. Nor are the
Attributes self-sufficient substances. Citta is their articulated totality.

## The negative as negative

An Attribute is a determination, and determination is negation. Yet because
the Attribute has the Absolute as its content, its limitation is itself
posited as limited.

Hegel calls this the negative posited as negative:

```text
first negation
the Absolute is expressed under one determinate form

second negation
that form knows itself as only one form of the whole
```

The Attribute therefore does not imprison the Absolute. By displaying its
own limitation, it becomes transparent to the complete content it expresses.

This is Prajñā as Saṃskāra at the level of Attribute: a determinate form of
Knowing whose Being includes knowledge of its own determination.

## The Attribute sublates itself

Because the finite determination receives its true subsistence from the
Absolute, it expands into an Attribute. But this very expansion sublates its
claim to be an independently existing Attribute.

```text
finite determination
        ↓ loses isolated subsistence
Attribute of the whole
        ↓ knows its form as relative
transparent expression of the Absolute
```

The Seer-form does not claim that only knowing is real. The seen-form does
not claim that only objectivity is real. Each sinks its one-sidedness into
the complete Citta relation.

## Hegel's warning: Attribute is not yet the true Absolute

Hegel does not allow the dual coloring to be the final result. Reflection
can take each Attribute and dissolve its difference into abstract identity:

```text
Seer and seen are both the Absolute
                  ↓
their difference is declared unessential
                  ↓
indeterminate identity
```

This returns to an empty Absolute. The determinate form has not penetrated
the Absolute; it has merely disappeared into it.

The true question is not only whether Seer and seen have the same content.
It is:

> How does the Absolute itself produce and sustain their determinate forms?

That question leads from Attribute to Mode in IV.24.

## Fichte: the Being of Knowing

Fichte approaches IV.23 from genesis. Giving self-genesis a Principle
produces completed and enduring Being—specifically, the Being of Knowing.

```text
self-genesis
      ↓ receives its Principle
enduring Being of Knowing
```

This Being is not an object external to Knowing. Nevertheless, both genesis
and enduring Being are mediated terms.

What is immediate is the insight into their connection.

```text
genesis                         Being of Knowing
mediated                       mediated
           \                  /
            insight into connection
                 immediate
```

Fichte therefore interprets dual coloring as a genetic relation comprehended
by Buddhi.

## Hegel and Fichte compared

Fichte and Hegel agree that neither pole can be understood independently:

```text
Fichte
genesis and enduring Being are moments
within immediate insight into their connection

Hegel
Seer and seen are Attributes carrying the whole content
within the Absolute relation
```

But their emphases differ.

| Fichte | Hegel |
|---|---|
| asks how the Principle of ordinary Knowing is generated | asks how the whole content exists under each determinate form |
| treats insight into the connection as immediate | treats each Attribute as the whole under a relative determination |
| distinguishes ordinary and transcendental Knowing | distinguishes independent immediacy from reflective shine |
| moves from factical terms toward genetic insight | moves from Attribute toward the Absolute's own modal movement |

Fichte gives the genetic act of Prajñā. Hegel gives the ontological status of
each coloring.

## Hegel's correction to Fichte

Fichte says that insight into the connection is unconditionally immediate.
Hegel forces a further question: if the connection is expressed through
determinate forms, can their difference simply disappear into immediate
insight?

Hegel's answer is no.

```text
insight into connection
must not merely absorb both terms
                 ↓
the connection must explain why it appears
as these distinct complete forms
```

Otherwise immediacy becomes another abstract Absolute. The relation is true
only when the Principle itself accounts for its dual manifestation.

## Fichte's correction to Hegel

Hegel's Attribute can appear as a formal account of two complete
expressions. Fichte insists that Buddhi must actually penetrate the genesis
of their connection.

```text
it is not enough to state:
“each side contains the whole”

the Science must reconstruct:
how the Principle produces their distinction and unity
```

Thus Fichte prevents the Attribute from becoming a static metaphysical
classification. Hegel prevents genetic insight from dissolving the
determinate forms it is meant to explain.

Together they produce a stronger Bhāṣya–Vārttika relation.

## Manas and Buddhi

The dual coloring can now be stated through our cognitive architecture:

```text
Manas
contemplates the seen and produces determinate objective cognition
                        ↑
                     Buddhi
contemplates Manas contemplating the object
and knows the complete Seer–seen relation
```

The objective cognition is not devoid of Prajñā. Its determinate Being is
already an intelligible Attribute. Buddhi's higher Prajñā comprehends why
this objective form and the reflecting or thinking form are two
presentations of one content. Knowing is their completed comprehension.

## A–U–M provisionally

The Attribute structure supports our provisional A–U–M formulation:

```text
A                              U
objective sensible form        reflecting / thinking form
               \              /
                \            /
                     M
          Knowing as Prajñā of their combination
```

A and U must not be treated as half-contents. Each is the whole under a
different form. U is reflection or thinking, not Knowing by itself. M is not
a third piece added to them; it is Knowing as comprehension of their
identity and difference.

The exact Māṇḍūkya allocation remains for later analysis. Hegel gives us the
logical rule required for that work.

## OṂ-Artha

IV.23 connects `sarvārtha` with the Attribute structure:

```text
every determinate Artha
appears under objective and reflecting forms
                         ↓
each form bears the whole content
                         ↓
their relation belongs to OṂ-Artha
```

OṂ-Artha is not a third content beyond Seer and seen. It is the complete
intelligibility in which every determinate content can appear through both
forms without falling into two separate worlds.

## Provisional result

IV.23 says:

> Citta bears every Artha because the complete content is expressed under
> both the reflecting form of the Seer and the objective form of the seen.

Hegel names each coloring an Absolute Attribute: the whole expressed through
a determinate form. Each side therefore has Prajñā, not as a separate
conscious subject, but as the intelligible Being of one complete
determination.

Fichte shows that Buddhi must penetrate the genesis of their connection.
Hegel adds that genetic insight must preserve and explain the distinct form
of each pole rather than dissolve both into abstract identity.

```text
Fichte
the insight into connection is immediate

Hegel
the connected poles each express the whole as Attribute

together
Citta is the self-comprehending field of dual complete coloration
```

IV.24 can now show that the Attribute's apparent externality is the Mode of
the Absolute—the Absolute's own movement of manifestation.
