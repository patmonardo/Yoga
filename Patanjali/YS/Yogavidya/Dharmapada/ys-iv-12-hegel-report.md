# Essence Must Appear Along the Paths of Dharma

## Yoga Sūtra IV.12 with Hegel

## The Sūtra

`atīta-anāgataṃ svarūpato 'sty adhva-bhedād dharmāṇām`

> What is past and what is yet to come exist according to their own form,
> because the Dharmas differ according to their paths.

IV.12 opens **Appearance**. Its decisive claim is not that past and future
Dharmas exist as presently available things. It is that their absence from
the present does not reduce them to sheer nothingness. Each has `svarūpa`
according to its distinct `adhvan`, its path through temporal determination.

```text
past                 present                 future
determination        manifest being          determination
reflected inward     appearing               not yet manifest
        \                |                /
         \______ one path of Dharma _____/
```

The present is therefore not a self-enclosed point. What appears carries
within it determinations that are no longer immediate and determinations that
are not yet immediate.

## Why Appearance follows IV.11

IV.11 completed the movement of Ground. Cause, result, support, and object
were shown to be the complete conditions through which a Vāsanā comes into
concrete existence. If those conditions are absent, that Vāsanā does not
come forth.

IV.12 begins when the conditions have recollected themselves into a fact.
The mediation has foundered as a separate apparatus, and the conditioned
content now stands immediately:

```text
Ground and conditions              Appearance

why the fact comes forth    ->     the fact shows what it is
```

But the fact does not lose its mediation by appearing. Its mediation is now
immanent in its form. Past and future are the first indication that present
being contains reflection within itself.

## Essence must appear

Hegel begins the section with the proposition:

> Essence must appear.

Essence is not a hidden being lodged behind Appearance. If it remained
inward and never appeared, it would be an empty abstraction. Essence has
already distinguished itself from immediate being through reflection; it
must now restore itself to being without surrendering that reflected depth.

Hegel calls the result **essential being** or **concrete existence**:

```text
immediate being
      ↓ self-negation and reflection
essence
      ↓ restoration to immediacy
essential being / concrete existence
```

The second immediacy is not a return to unreflected Being. It is Being that
has come forth from inwardness. Applied to IV.12, the presently appearing
Dharma is precisely such an essential being. Its immediacy contains the
negativity through which it has passed and through which it will pass beyond
its present form.

## `Svarūpa` is not isolated presence

The phrase `svarūpataḥ asti` must not be flattened into the claim that all
three times exist in the same manner. Their temporal distinction is essential
to their form:

- the past has being as a determination that has withdrawn from immediacy;
- the present has being as manifest concrete existence;
- the future has being as a determination whose immediacy has not yet come
  forth.

Each therefore is according to its own form precisely by not being the other
two. Their difference is not imposed by an external clock. `Adhva-bheda` is
the self-differentiation of Dharma's path.

This supplies a Hegelian meaning for the Sūtra's difficult `asti`:

> Past and future are, but their being is reflected and path-determined
> rather than immediately present.

## Reflection becomes real shine

In Reflection, the determinations of essence shine internally. Ground,
condition, identity, difference, and contradiction are moments held within
the reflective unity. With Appearance, these determinations acquire an
element of independent subsistence. Hegel calls this the completion of
reflective shine in Appearance.

IV.12 performs exactly that transition. Temporal determination is no longer
only our reconstruction of a Vāsanā's conditions. The Dharma itself appears
as having a path. Its past and future belong to what it is, although neither
stands forth with the immediacy of its present phase.

```text
internal reflection              real shine

conditioned genesis       ->     a Dharma appearing as temporal
of the determination             and internally differentiated
```

Appearance is consequently not illusion. It is essence achieving a real
showing. The error would be to isolate the showing from the reflected
movement that appears in it.

## The temporal path as negativity

Hegel's opening argument turns upon negativity. Being becomes essence because
immediate being sublates itself. Essence becomes essential being because it
again gives itself immediacy. Negativity is therefore not an external force
that attacks an otherwise static Dharma. It is the Dharma's own movement
beyond each temporal determination.

The temporal path can now be stated logically:

```text
past       = present determination sublated
present    = reflected determination made immediate
future     = present determination directed beyond itself
```

This does not make time a merely subjective arrangement. It explains why a
real Dharma cannot be exhausted by the immediate `now`: its concrete
existence is the result of reflection and therefore contains transition
within its own form.

## Hegel and Fichte

Fichte taught us to see the appearing fact genetically as a determinate
`from`. Hegel now gives that genesis objective standing.

| Fichte | Hegel |
|---|---|
| Appearance is manifestness together with a `from` | Appearance is essence restored to essential being |
| Genetic seeing comprehends how the fact comes forth | The fact itself contains reflection in its concrete existence |
| The manifold must not be accepted as merely given | Temporal difference is the Dharma's own path-determination |

Fichte disciplines the seeing. Hegel determines what is seen: not an
immediate item with a history externally attached, but essential being whose
present appearance contains the reflected paths of past and future.

## Citta-Dharma

A present Citta-determination is not a finished psychic item. It is the real
shine of a conditioned movement:

```text
past Saṃskāra
      ↓
selective manifestation of Vāsanā
      ↓
present Citta-Dharma
      ↓
prospective formation
```

The earlier and later moments are not additional Cittas stored beside the
present one. They are distinct forms of the path through which this
Citta-Dharma is what it is.

This is also the first discipline of Appearance: do not mistake what is
currently manifest for the whole Dharma. See the present as essential being,
the point at which an inwardly reflected genesis has become visible and has
already directed itself beyond its current form.

## Hegel-Vārttika

> Essence does not remain concealed behind the present Dharma; it appears as
> that Dharma's path. The past and future are according to their own forms
> because the appearing present is reflected being: it has come forth from
> inwardness and contains within its own determination the negativity by
> which it has ceased to be and is yet to become.

IV.12 therefore does more than add temporal depth to an existing fact. It
establishes the principle of the entire section:

> **Essence must appear, and its first Appearance is the path by which Dharma
> differentiates its own forms of time.**

IV.13 can now ask what the manifest and subtle Dharmas are in their common
nature as concrete existents.
