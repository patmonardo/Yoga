# The Thing Survives One Citta by Dissolving as an Isolated Thing

## Yoga Sūtra IV.16 with Hegel

## The Sūtra

`na caika-citta-tantraṃ vastu tad-apramāṇakaṃ tadā kiṃ syāt`

> The Thing does not depend upon one Citta; when it is not cognized by that
> Citta, what then would it be?

The argument is a reductio. Suppose the Thing were `eka-citta-tantra`,
governed by one Citta:

```text
this Citta cognizes the Thing      -> the Thing exists
this Citta does not cognize it     -> what could the Thing be?
```

The consequence is unacceptable. A Thing would disappear whenever one Citta
looked away, became distracted, slept, or ceased to operate.

The Sūtra's immediate result is clear:

> The absence of cognition in one Citta is not the absence of the Thing.

## Why IV.16 follows IV.15

IV.15 distinguished the paths of the same Thing and the different Cittas
that cognize it. IV.16 tests whether this distinction has objective force.

```text
IV.15    the paths of Vastu and cognition are distinct
                              ↓
IV.16    Vastu does not rise and fall with one Citta's path
```

If the Thing depended upon one Citta, it would have no distinct path. Its
supposed objectivity would be only a phase of that Citta.

But the rejection of private dependence does not yet establish a Thing
sealed outside every cognitive relation. Hegel's contribution is to show why
that opposite picture also fails.

## Vyāsa's test: the unseen back

Vyāsa asks what happens when a Citta supplies no cognition of the Thing. If
the Thing existed only through that cognition, it would become
`apramāṇaka`, unsupported by any means of knowledge, until the Citta attended
to it again.

His concrete example is decisive. If only the presented side of an object
existed, its unseen back would be nothing. But then the visible front could
not be known as the front of one complete Thing.

```text
presented face             complete Vastu
      front          !=    front + presently unseen back
```

The unseen is not sheer non-being. The object's own transformation and
relations exceed what is presently manifest in one Citta.

## Hegel's dissolution of the Thing

Hegel begins from the unstable result of IV.15. The Thing appeared to be a
collection of self-subsistent matters:

```text
matter A + matter B + matter C = this Thing
```

If the bond among them is merely an external “also,” the Thing is absolutely
alterable. Matters can enter, leave, or change proportion while the unity
that gathers them has no measure or form of its own.

Hegel calls this **absolute porosity**. The Thing does not possess a sealed
interior. Its determinations circulate through relations with other Things,
while its identity as a bare “this” supplies no content capable of stopping
that circulation.

This may initially sound opposed to Yoga. IV.16 protects the Thing; Hegel
dissolves it. But Hegel dissolves only the isolated Thing whose unity was
supposed to stand outside its relations. That dissolution is what allows the
objective content to survive the absence of one particular Citta.

## Why one Citta cannot contain the Thing

One Citta is one determinate configuration within the Thing's field of
relations. It can gather and display certain determinations, but it cannot
enclose the whole objective process.

```text
objective field of the Thing
        |
        +-- relation to Citta A
        +-- relation to Citta B
        +-- relation to other Things
        +-- presently unmanifest determinations
```

When Citta A ceases to cognize the Thing, one relation becomes inactive. The
field does not thereby collapse. The Thing's determinations continue through
other relations, other Cittas, and its own process of transformation.

The Thing survives one Citta precisely because it was never a content sealed
inside that Citta.

## Porosity is not unreality

Hegel's porous Thing is not a fictional Thing. Porosity means that its
self-subsistence is mediated through what it is not.

The matters of the Thing both:

- subsist as determinate contents;
- obtain their place through their difference from and relation to other
  contents.

Each determination is present where another is absent, yet the whole exists
only through their interpenetrating organization. The Thing is therefore a
self-contradictory mediation of subsistence through non-subsistence.

In plain language:

> The Thing is real, but it is real through relations and exclusions rather
> than through an isolated core.

This is why its reality cannot depend upon one Citta. No single cognitive
relation exhausts the network through which the Thing subsists.

## The Thing has its nothingness for substrate

Hegel gives the dissolution its sharpest formulation: the Thing subsists in
an other and therefore has its own nothingness for substrate.

This does not mean that the Thing is simply nothing. It means that the Thing
is what it is only through determinations that negate its isolated
self-sufficiency:

```text
Thing as isolated “this”        dissolves
Thing as mediated subsistence   appears
```

The abstract Thing dies; its determinate content survives as Appearance.
This is the positive result of the dissolution.

## Appearance is the higher truth

Ordinary thought contrasts Appearance with a supposedly more real immediate
Thing. Hegel reverses that valuation.

Immediate concrete existence is not yet the full truth because it conceals
the mediation through which it subsists. Appearance is higher because the
Thing's negativity and dependence upon relation have become explicit within
its concrete existence.

```text
mere concrete existence     “the Thing is simply there”

Appearance                  “the Thing is present through
                             reflected mediation”
```

Thus IV.16 does not force us to choose between private idealism and a dead
Thing-in-itself. The Thing is independent of one Citta, yet its truth is to
appear through determinate relations.

## Hegel and Fichte

Fichte examined the genesis of a new cognition. Energy and manifestness open
together: the appearance of pure Light cannot be separated from the act
through which it becomes manifest.

Hegel examines the objective counterpart. The Thing does not depend upon
this one act of manifestation, but neither does it remain an inert object
outside all manifestation.

| Fichte | Hegel |
|---|---|
| this Appearance opens with an energetic genesis | the Thing subsists through a field of mediated relations |
| Light and the act of abstraction appear together | isolated thinghood dissolves into Appearance |
| manifestness for this knowing requires this genesis | objective content is not exhausted by one cognitive relation |

Together:

> The Thing does not depend upon my cognition, but its becoming manifest for
> me is inseparable from the actual genesis of my seeing.

## Citta-mastery

IV.16 prevents a false spiritual conclusion:

```text
I withdraw attention  ->  the world has ceased
```

Withdrawal ends one Ocular presentation. It does not annihilate the Thing.
An `Ocular.spanda()` may close one dataset and open another, but the
objective field exceeds the actuality of either particular presentation.

This gives Citta-mastery a disciplined meaning:

```text
not: control the Thing by attending or refusing to attend

but: discriminate the conditions under which the Thing
     becomes manifest in this Citta
```

The unseen back of the Thing is an elementary lesson in humility for the
Oculus. What is presently visible is not the measure of what is.

## From Thing to Appearance

IV.16 completes Hegel's chapter on the Thing. The sequence is:

```text
IV.14    the Thing is one transformation
IV.15    its determinations continue across different Cittas
IV.16    its isolated unity dissolves into mediated Appearance
```

This is not the loss of objectivity. It is objectivity becoming adequate to
its own relational structure.

The next question is therefore positive. If a Thing is not known merely by
existing, what must occur for it to become manifest in a Citta? IV.17
answers: the Thing is known or unknown according to whether it colors Citta.

## Hegel-Vārttika

> The Thing does not depend upon one Citta, for one cognitive relation is
> only a moment within the mediated field of its subsistence. Yet the Thing
> is not an isolated core beyond every relation. Its immediate unity
> dissolves, and its objective content persists as the negative mediation of
> itself through others. This mediated concrete existence is Appearance.

IV.16 therefore reaches a balanced result:

> **The Thing survives the non-cognition of one Citta because its truth is
> not private presence but objective Appearance through a plurality of
> relations.**
