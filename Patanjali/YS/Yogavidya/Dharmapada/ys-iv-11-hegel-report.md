# The Conditioned Fact and Its Absence

## Yoga Sutra IV.11 with Hegel

## The Sutra

`hetu-phala-asraya-alambana-sangrhitatvad esam abhave tad-abhava`

> Because the Vāsanās are held together by cause, result, support, and object,
> when these are absent, the Vāsanās are absent.

Hegel places IV.11 at the **procession of the fact into concrete existence**.
This location determines the reading. The Sutra is not announcing an
annihilation. It states the relation between a conditioned fact and the
complete set of factors through which it exists.

## Conditions are the whole content

Hegel argues that the conditions of a fact are not materials externally used
by a separate ground. Taken together, they are the fact's whole content in
the form of immediate existence.

Applied to Vāsanā:

```text
cause
result
support       = Vāsanā's complete conditioned content
object
```

The Vāsanā is not a fifth thing hiding behind the four and waiting for them to
activate it. It is their collected unity as a determinate disposition.

This makes the logic of `abhāva` transparent:

> If the total conditions constituting this fact are absent, this fact is
> absent.

Its absence is determinate, not absolute nothingness. This Vāsanā does not
exist because the relations that constitute it are not collected.

## Ground and condition

Ground and condition initially look separate:

- the ground seems to contain why the fact is what it is;
- the conditions seem to provide the external materials allowing it to exist.

Hegel shows that each passes into the other. Ground posits the conditions, and
the conditions return into ground as one fact. When all the conditions are at
hand, the fact steps into concrete existence.

```text
scattered conditions
        |
        | recollect themselves into one ground
        v
determinate fact comes forth
```

In this coming forth, ground and condition disappear as separate mediators.
The ground does not remain underneath as a substrate. It has expressed itself
completely in the fact.

The converse belongs to the same logic. If the conditions do not recollect
themselves into this unity, the fact does not come forth. Nothing further must
be killed.

## What `abhava` removes

The fourfold apparatus sustains a Vāsanā-conditioned determination:

```text
Hetu        supplies causal genesis
Phala       supplies consequential confirmation
Āśraya      supplies a conditioned locus
Ālambana    supplies objective direction
```

Their absence removes the determinate ground-relation through which the
Vāsanā would become manifest. It does not remove reflection as such.

This distinction is essential:

```text
absence of determinate conditions -> absence of determinate Vāsanā

not

absence of determinate conditions -> absence of Eka Citta
```

Eka Citta is the reflective unity capable of being one through the many. It
is not the substrate called `āśraya` within this conditioned complex. To make
it such a substrate would reduce transcendental unity to one existent support
among others.

## The unconditioned does not sit behind conditions

“Eka Citta sits in pāramārthika” is a useful plain expression, provided
“sits” does not turn it into an inert thing behind appearance. Hegel's
absolutely unconditioned is not an entity waiting behind the conditions. It is
the complete self-mediation in which ground and conditions are moments.

Eka Citta remains pāramārthika by being the unity of the reflective movement,
not by surviving as a separate substance after the movement stops.

```text
pāramārthika Eka Citta
    = unity capable of comprehending conditioned formation and absence
    ≠ another conditioned bearer within the formation
```

The absence of Vāsanā is itself known. That knowing already demonstrates that
`abhāva` is not the annihilation of cognitive unity.

## Hegel-Varttika

> A Vāsanā is the fact constituted by the recollection of cause, result,
> support, and object into one determinate ground. When these conditions are
> absent, the fact does not come forth. Their absence negates the conditioned
> determination, not the reflective unity in which its presence or absence is
> intelligible.

IV.11 completes Reflection because ground has now disclosed the entire
conditionality of its determinate content. Citta is freed from treating
Vāsanā as a self-subsistent essence. With IV.12, this freed essence can enter
Appearance.
