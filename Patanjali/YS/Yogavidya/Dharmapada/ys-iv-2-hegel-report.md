# Nature Fills Another Form: Essence and the Unessential

## Yoga Sutra IV.2 with Hegel

`jati-antara-parinama prakrti-apurat`

> Transformation into another kind comes from nature filling it out.

Fichte explained the new form genetically: the manifold of appearance must
arise from the fullness of one living ground. Hegel now asks what makes that
ground **essence** rather than merely another being hidden behind appearance.

At first we distinguish an essential nature from its unessential form. But if
the distinction is imposed by an observer, either side can be called essential
according to the observer's interest. Nature and form then remain two
indifferent existences.

Hegel's correction is decisive:

> Essence is not selected from being by external reflection. Essence is being
> that has sublated its own immediacy and preserved it as a moment.

`Prakrti-apurat` can therefore be read as immanent fulfillment. Nature does
not stand behind the new kind and pour content into an alien container. The
new kind is nature's own previous immediacy recollected, negated, and
determined anew.

```text
not: nature + external form

but: immediate form -> self-sublation -> nature fulfilled as another form
```

The Hegelian Varttika is:

> Transformation into another kind is possible because nature is not an inert
> substrate beneath its forms. It is the negative power by which an immediate
> form becomes unessential and is preserved within a newly determined form.

The first Citta-Dharma is thus transformation from within. Citta does not
receive its forms from an external classifier; its apparent kinds are moments
of its own return from immediacy into essence.
