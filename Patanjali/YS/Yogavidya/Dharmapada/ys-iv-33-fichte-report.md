# The Sequence Is Grasped from Its Completion

## A report on Yoga Sūtra IV.33 and Fichte

## The Sūtra

`kṣaṇa-pratiyogī pariṇāmāparānta-nirgrāhyaḥ kramaḥ`

### Simple translation

> Sequence, whose moments are determined through one another, is grasped at
> the final limit of transformation.

The compact terms require careful unpacking:

- `kṣaṇa`: moment or instant;
- `pratiyogī`: correlated with, opposed to, or determined through its
  counterpart;
- `pariṇāma-aparānta`: the final limit or completion of transformation;
- `nirgrāhyaḥ`: apprehensible, determinable, or graspable;
- `kramaḥ`: sequence, ordered succession, or genetic course.

```text
moment 1 → moment 2 → moment 3 → completion
   ↑          ↑          ↑            |
   └──────────┴──────────┴────────────┘
     the complete end makes the sequence graspable
```

The end does not merely occur after the moments. It discloses what makes
them one necessary sequence.

## Absolute Necessity

IV.29–32 completed Relative Necessity. Each determination followed
necessarily once its preceding conditions had been granted.

```text
IF A, then B
IF B, then C
IF C, then D
```

But the whole chain still depended upon its first `if`: living certainty was
assumed to be principle-providing.

IV.33 asks a deeper question:

> Does the completed sequence demonstrate the necessity of the Principle
> from which it began?

```text
Relative Necessity
the Principle is assumed and its consequences are derived

Absolute Necessity
the complete derivation returns into and justifies its own Principle
```

The sequence becomes self-grounding when its completion shows why neither
its beginning nor its movement could be otherwise.

## A moment is a `pratiyogin`

The Sūtra does not treat a `kṣaṇa` as an isolated metaphysical atom. Each
moment is a `pratiyogin`: it is determinate through an opposition or
correlation.

```text
moment A
is what it is through its distinction from moment B

moment B
is what it is through preserving and transforming moment A
```

A moment taken alone gives only immediate facticity: “this happened.” Its
Artha becomes intelligible only through the transformation that relates it
to what precedes and follows.

Thus `krama` is not a pile of instants. It is the law of their determinate
relation.

## The final limit is epistemically first

Temporally, the beginning comes first and the completion last. In the
Science, however, the completion is what makes the beginning intelligible.

```text
temporal order
beginning ───────────────────► end

order of comprehension
beginning ◄─────────────────── completion
```

`Pariṇāma-aparānta` is therefore not merely the last event. It is the
standpoint from which the whole transformation can be grasped as one
completed Artha.

The final result retrospectively determines the necessity of every moment.

## Why the Guṇas had to complete their work

IV.32 said that the Guṇas' transformations end because their Artha has been
accomplished. IV.33 explains how that claim can be known.

```text
isolated Guṇa-transformation
appears contingent: it might have been otherwise

complete Guṇa-sequence
shows each transformation as a required determination
of the accomplished Artha
```

Absolute Necessity is not an external force compelling the Guṇas. It is the
complete intelligibility of their sequence.

## Fichte's problem: who authorized the Principle?

Fichte begins by reviewing the three modifications derived from primordial
Light. He then asks what authorized the Scientist of Knowing to posit Light
as immediately principle-providing.

One might argue:

```text
the Science of Knowing is I
the I can and does project
therefore Light can and does project
```

Fichte rejects this proof. It would make the factical `I` and its capacity
the ground of Light. Arbitrariness would remain at the foundation of the
Science.

For Absolute Necessity, both the acting `I` and the supposedly acting Light
must fall away as independently originating terms.

## Necessity must take place itself

Fichte states the criterion with exceptional clarity:

> The Science of Knowing must do this—or better, necessity must take place
> itself without the Science's help.

```text
not:

the scientist chooses to construct a necessary proof


but:

necessity itself determines the scientist's act
and becomes manifest through it
```

It is not sufficient to observe that attentive thinking cannot avoid
projection. Weak thinking seems able to avoid it. The Science must derive
the conditions under which projection is absolutely necessary.

## Predication requires projection

Fichte begins with an immediately evident relation:

> I cannot predicate anything of Light unless I project and objectify Light
> as the subject of the predicate.

```text
predicate something of Light
                 ↓ necessarily requires
project Light as the subject of predication
```

Here Light predicates itself: the Scientist's act is a form in which Light
projects itself through a gap and becomes intuition.

This establishes necessity, but only conditionally:

```text
IF something is to be predicated,
THEN projection must occur.
```

The consequent is necessary relative to the antecedent. But must predication
occur at all? Absolute Necessity has not yet been reached.

## Beneath the major premise

Fichte makes a striking logical observation. The predicate functions as a
minor premise; objectification of the logical subject functions as a major
premise. Each presupposes the other.

```text
major premise                     minor premise
subject is projected             predicate determines it
              \                 /
               \               /
          reciprocally presuppose
                     ↓
         a deeper ground is required
```

No proposition merely inserted as an “absolute major premise” can solve the
problem. If thinking stops at such a premise, it stops arbitrarily; if it
refuses to stop, it falls into rootless skepticism.

The ground must be deeper than either side of the inference.

## Law and primordial Fact

Fichte next identifies two reciprocally determining terms:

```text
Law
a Principle that governs factical principle-providing
but presupposes an actual self-producing Fact

Primordial Fact
an actuality possible only according to Law
```

Neither exists as Principle by itself:

```text
Law is effective only as actual Fact
Fact is possible only through Law
```

This is a higher `kṣaṇa-pratiyogī`. Each term is what it is only through the
other, and both occur in one undivided stroke.

Their reciprocity is not yet Absolute Necessity, but it reveals its form.

## The Scientist falls under the Law

The Scientist of Knowing appears to perform a free reflection about Knowing.
Yet the content of that reflection says that facticity everywhere takes up
the law of primordial projection.

The proposition must therefore apply to its own act:

```text
content of the insight
every factical act of Knowing occurs according to Law
                         ↓ applies to
form of the insight
this scientific act also occurs according to Law
```

The scientist's apparently arbitrary reflection is genetically derived from
the Law it comprehends.

Fichte calls this the first important result.

## Truth runs back into itself

The content of the proposition determines the act asserting it. Fichte says
that truth begins to run back into itself.

```text
Truth states the Law
        ↓
the Law determines the stating of Truth
        ↓
the statement demonstrates itself as an act of its own content
```

This is the logical form of `pariṇāma-aparānta-nirgrāhya`. Only after the
proposition is completed can its content be applied to the act that produced
it. The end returns upon the beginning and makes the whole sequence
intelligible.

## The Law of lawfulness

At this height, Fichte says, no particular law remains except the Law of
lawfulness itself:

> It is projected according to Law.

This Law posits itself by determining the act through which it is known.

```text
particular law
governs a determinate content

Law of lawfulness
governs the relation between Law, content,
and the act that knows the Law
```

The highest act of the Science is immediately law-conformable because the
absolute Law has real causality within it.

## No gap between Law and act

Fichte says that the absolute Law and the inwardness of the act permeate one
another without a gap.

The projection is simultaneously:

- formal: it objectifies;
- material: it expresses the essence of Knowing.

```text
absolute Law
      ↓ real causality
scientific act
      ↓ in one stroke
formal projection + material expression
```

This is no external application of a rule. The act is the actuality of the
Law, while the Law is the Principle of the act.

Here necessity is no longer imposed upon Citta. Citi-Śakti is actual as the
Law determining its own expression.

## Description and infinite renewal

One determination nevertheless remains: description. The relation describes
one term on the basis of the other.

Because description is living principle-providing, it perpetually renews
itself even while its content remains qualitatively one.

```text
unchanging content
        ↓ repeatedly reconstructed through
living description
        ↓
infinite renewal of one Artha
```

This explains why the Science repeatedly traverses the same content. Its
repetition is not failure to arrive. The living Principle must continually
reconstruct the one content as image.

This is Japa on OṂ-Artha at the level of Absolute Necessity.

## Original and reconstruction

Knowing now appears to split into two forms:

```text
primordial construction        reconstruction
original                       image or representation
               \              /
                same content
             different relational form
```

The forms are absolutely one in content and differ only through their
reciprocal relation. Object and representation provide an ordinary example.

But Fichte refuses to leave this duality unexamined. If the Law itself is
constructed by us, then the supposed original construction is also a
reconstruction—one that fails to announce itself as such.

```text
claimed original
is known only through our construction
                   ↓
it too appears as reconstruction
                   ↓
the opposition cannot be the final ground
```

## The brilliant proof collapses

Fichte openly concludes that the reasoning, despite its luster, unravels into
nothing. It still grounds itself upon a fact for which no Law has been given.

This collapse is not a return to skepticism. It is the completion of the
moment that reveals its own limit.

```text
the Science constructs the absolute Law
                    ↓ contradiction
the constructed Law cannot be primordial
                    ↓
the Law must construct itself in the Science
```

The failed proof is a `kṣaṇa-pratiyogin`: its failure is the opposed moment
through which the true form of Absolute Necessity becomes determinate.

This is liberation through failure in exact logical form.

## The remaining inner disjunction

The outer split between subject and object has already fallen away. What
remains is subtler: an inner difference between two forms of life—primordial
construction and reconstruction.

Its genetic Principle has not yet been found.

Fichte states the required resolution:

> We cannot construct the absolute Law. It constructs itself on us and in
> us. The Law itself posits us and itself in us.

```text
not:

Scientist ── constructs ──► Absolute Law


but:

Absolute Law ── posits ──► itself-in-the-scientist
                         and the scientist
```

This is the threshold of the final Sūtra. Absolute Necessity is achieved
only when the Law is not represented as our highest construction, but is
actual as the power constructing the complete Science through us.

## Citta, Krama, and Citi-Śakti

The whole development can now be stated:

```text
Citta
the articulated sequence of determinate holdings

Krama
the lawful relation through which each determination
is transformed into the next

Citi-Śakti
the Power of Knowing whose self-positing Law
makes the complete sequence intelligible
```

The Science of Citta observes and reconstructs the transformations. The
Science of Knowing reaches completion when the Law of those transformations
is seen to construct both the sequence and its scientific reconstruction.

## Absolute Necessity is not fatalism

Absolute Necessity does not mean that every empirical event was mechanically
predetermined. It means that no moment of the Science can serve as an
independent ground outside the complete self-determining relation.

```text
not:

every event is pushed by a preceding external cause


but:

every moment receives its full Artha from its place
within the self-grounding completed sequence
```

Freedom is not excluded. What falls away is arbitrariness—the claim that an
ungrounded factical act originates the Law.

## Yoga and Fichte

| Yoga IV.33 | Fichte |
|---|---|
| each moment is determined through a counterpart | Law and primordial Fact determine one another |
| sequence is grasped at transformation's completion | the proposition's content returns upon and grounds its own act |
| the end makes the beginning intelligible | truth runs back into itself |
| `krama` is more than chronological succession | the Law of lawfulness governs both content and scientific form |
| the complete sequence discloses necessity | the factical scientist falls under the Law being known |
| apparent construction fails at its limit | the Absolute Law must construct itself in and through us |

## Provisional result

IV.33 says:

> A sequence becomes intelligible as necessary only from its completed limit,
> where each moment is grasped through the opposed and correlated moments
> that make it determinate.

Fichte first derives the Scientist's act from the absolute Law by applying
the content of the insight to its own form. Truth runs back into itself. But
he then discovers that the supposedly primordial Law is still constructed
by the Scientist and therefore remains a reconstruction.

The proof must fail in order to disclose Absolute Necessity:

```text
we cannot construct the Absolute Law
                    ↓
the Law constructs itself on us and in us
                    ↓
it posits both itself and the Knowing through which it appears
```

The complete `krama` is therefore not merely narrated by the Science. Its
own Law generates the Science capable of reconstructing it.

IV.34 can now state Kaivalya as the completion of Puruṣa-Artha and,
equivalently, Citi-Śakti established in its own form.
