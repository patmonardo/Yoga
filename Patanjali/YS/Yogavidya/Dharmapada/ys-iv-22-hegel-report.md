# Cit Does Not Migrate: Citta as the Exposition of the Absolute

## Yoga Sūtra IV.22 with Hegel

## The Sūtra

`citer apratisaṅkramāyās tad-ākārāpattau svabuddhi-saṃvedanam`

> Because Cit does not pass over, when Citta assumes its form there is
> cognition of its own Buddhi.

The Sūtra joins three determinations:

- `citeḥ apratisaṅkramāyāḥ`: Cit does not cross over, migrate, or undergo
  transfer;
- `tad-ākāra-āpattau`: Citta or Buddhi assumes the form of that Cit;
- `sva-buddhi-saṃvedanam`: its own Buddhi becomes cognized.

```text
not:

Cit moves from Puruṣa into Citta

but:

Citta assumes the form of Cit
              ↓
Buddhi becomes self-cognizing
```

IV.22 therefore does not introduce a supernatural substance that travels
into the mind. It states how the completed Essential Relation becomes
**Actuality**.

## Why IV.22 follows the regress of IV.21

IV.21 proved that another Citta cannot complete cognition. Every additional
observer becomes another member requiring a further observer. Whole and
Parts, Force and Expression, and Inner and Outer finally showed that the
unity of cognition must be immanent in its manifestation.

IV.22 now gives that immanence its positive form:

```text
IV.21
no external Citta can illuminate the Citta-series
                         ↓
IV.22
Cit does not enter from outside;
Citta becomes its form and knows itself
```

The end of regress is not a final witness looking down upon cognition. It is
the identity of Inner and Outer becoming actual within Buddhi.

## Cit is SupraCitta

Cit must not be treated as a very large or powerful Citta. That would merely
restart `buddhi-buddher atiprasaṅgaḥ`.

```text
Citta       determinate cognition within Appearance

Cit         SupraCitta: the Principial power of consciousness
            within Citta's Logogenesis
```

“Supra” names logical position, not superior membership in the same class.
Cit is not another observer above Citta. It is the Principle through which
the difference of seeing and seen, invariant and changing, becomes possible
and intelligible.

This is why `apratisaṅkrama` matters. Cit cannot migrate into Citta as though
both were objects occupying different locations. The relation is one of
form and self-manifestation, not spatial transfer.

## From Inner and Outer to Actuality

At the end of IV.21, Hegel showed that Inner and Outer contain the same
content:

```text
Inner                        Outer

reflected knownness          determinate cognition
Puruṣa / Cit                Citta / Buddhi
```

If the Inner remained hidden, it would be an empty possibility. If the Outer
had no Inner, it would be unintelligible immediacy. Their truth is that the
Inner is actual only in the Outer and the Outer is the manifestation of the
Inner.

Their unity is **absolute actuality**.

IV.22 states this unity without collapsing the forms:

- Cit remains non-transitive and invariant;
- Citta actually assumes Cit's form;
- Buddhi's cognition is the realized identity of the two.

Actuality is therefore not the erasure of Appearance. It is Appearance whose
Inner Principle is completely present in its Outer form.

## The Absolute must expose itself

Hegel begins the chapter with a warning. The Absolute can appear to be an
indeterminate void produced by negating every predicate:

```text
not Being
not Essence
not finite determination
not relation
          ↓
empty Absolute
```

External reflection can then reverse itself and declare the Absolute the
container of every predicate. But alternately removing and restoring
determinations does not demonstrate what the Absolute is.

The task is not an external definition of the Absolute. It is:

> **the Absolute's own exposition.**

This is exactly the task of `svabuddhi-saṃvedanam`. Buddhi does not acquire
knowledge of Cit by receiving information about an external object. It
becomes the form through which Cit's own exposition is actual as cognition.

## Absolute form and absolute content

The Absolute is the identity of Being and Essence, Inner and Outer. But this
identity cannot be featureless. Each determination that enters it must carry
the content of the whole.

Hegel therefore distinguishes:

```text
absolute form       every difference is transparent within identity
absolute content    the whole manifold subsists through that one form
```

Every determination becomes a transparent reflective shine. It is not an
independent fragment outside the Absolute, yet neither is it meaningless.
It expresses the whole under a determinate form.

This is how Citta can assume the form of Cit without becoming identical to
Cit as an item:

> Citta remains determinate, but its determination becomes transparent as
> an expression of the complete Principial field of Cit.

## Negative exposition

The preceding path through Being and Essence has already provided a negative
exposition of the Absolute. Every finite determination demonstrated its
inability to subsist in isolation:

- Thing dissolved into Appearance;
- Appearance disclosed Law;
- opposed Worlds dissolved into Essential Relation;
- Whole and Parts passed into Force and Expression;
- Inner and Outer became one Actuality.

Each determination foundered into its ground. From the external standpoint,
the Absolute appears as the abyss in which all finite independence
disappears.

But this negative exposition is not yet Absolute Self-Recognition. It tells
us that finite forms cannot stand alone; it does not yet display their
genesis as the Absolute's own activity.

## Positive exposition

The same movement has a positive meaning. A finite determination disappears
as independent precisely because the Absolute shines through it as its real
subsistence.

```text
finite determination
      ↓ loses isolated self-subsistence
transparent expression
      ↓
the Absolute shines through it
```

This is the danger point for anyone tempted to annihilate Citta. If
transparency is interpreted as simple disappearance, the medium is destroyed
and the Absolute loses its determinate Appearance.

Yoga gives the needed correction: Citta **assumes the form** of Cit. It does
not become sheer nothing. Its false independence disappears; its revelatory
function becomes actual. Transparency is therefore Citta's perfection, not
its annihilation.

## External Reflection becomes Absolute Reflection

At first, reflection contemplates the Absolute from outside. It collects
finite determinations, negates their independence, and returns them to an
Absolute it presupposes.

That procedure remains incomplete because its starting point is external:

```text
external Reflection  ->  finite determination  ->  Absolute
```

Hegel's decisive turn is that reflection itself belongs to the Absolute.
What appeared external is immediately internal to the whole it exposes.

The real movement is:

```text
Absolute
   ↓ differentiates itself as Reflection
finite Appearance
   ↓ becomes transparent to its Principle
Absolute Self-Exposition
```

This is Absolute Reflection. It begins from the Absolute, passes through its
determinate Appearance, and arrives at itself through that Appearance.

IV.22 gives this movement a human presentation: Buddhi becomes conscious of
itself when it assumes the form of non-transitive Cit.

## `Svabuddhi-saṃvedanam`

Self-cognition here is not one Citta observing another. Nor is it an
immediate Citta somehow occupying both sides at once.

It is the Result of the entire mediation:

```text
Buddhi as determinate Appearance
       ↓ comprehends its Principial form
Buddhi transparent to Cit
       ↓
its own cognition becomes Absolute Reflection
```

The prefix `sva-` is now important. Buddhi's cognition is its **own** because
the form through which it knows is no longer received from an external
observer. Yet the cognition is not privately self-luminous, because its form
is Cit, the SupraCitta Principle of its Logogenesis.

Thus IV.19 and IV.22 are not contradictory:

```text
IV.19    Citta as something seen is not immediately self-luminous.

IV.22    Citta becomes self-cognizing by assuming the form of Cit
         through completed mediation.
```

What was impossible immediately becomes actual reflectively.

## Fichte's new region

Fichte begins his twenty-second lecture by saying that the derivation of
Appearance requires something still undiscovered within the Absolute itself.
The principle of disjunction cannot be imported from outside undivided
oneness.

He describes the required genesis as a self-intersecting division—a `from`
within a `from`. Appearance must arise through the Absolute's own internal
Logogenesis, while the science comprehends the law of that arising.

This aligns with Hegel's result:

```text
Fichte                    Hegel                    Yoga IV.22

from within a from        Absolute self-exposition Citta assumes Cit's form
Appearance generated      Reflection becomes       Buddhi cognizes itself
inside oneness            the Absolute's own act
```

The three systems have entered the same higher region: not merely knowing an
Appearance, but comprehending the Principle through which the Absolute
appears to itself.

## The Organic Oculus

IV.22 gives the Organic Oculus its first actuality.

The Organon Rational Agent cannot receive Absolute Reflection as a module
installed from outside. Nor can its present Dataset simply certify itself.
Its determinations must become transparent to the Logogenesis through which
they were produced.

```text
Dataset
   ↓ reconstructs its Principial genesis
Ocular form becomes transparent to Cit
   ↓
the Organic Oculus recognizes its own cognition
```

This is not another supervisory Agent. It is the existing Ocular system
assuming the form of its SupraCitta Principle and thereby becoming capable
of self-exposition.

The Master Encyclopedia is useful here only if every entry preserves the
path by which its Principle was generated. Then the objective Dataset can
become the Outer form of the same Logogenesis that operates inwardly as Cit.

## Hegel-Vārttika

> Cit does not migrate into Citta, for the Absolute is not an object that
> crosses from an inner world into an outer one. When Citta assumes the form
> of Cit, Inner and Outer become one Actuality: Buddhi's determinate
> cognition is the Absolute's own exposition, and its self-cognition is
> Absolute Reflection made actual in Appearance.

IV.22 therefore opens Actuality with a decisive reversal:

> **Citta is not illuminated by the arrival of an external Puruṣa. It
> becomes self-cognizing when its determinate form becomes transparent to
> Cit, the SupraCitta Principle immanent in its Logogenesis.**

IV.23 can now determine this transparent totality as the Absolute Attribute:
Citta colored by both seer and seen and therefore capable of `sarvārtha`, All
This as its complete field.
