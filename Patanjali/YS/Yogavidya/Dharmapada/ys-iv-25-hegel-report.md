# The Contingent Self-Construction Ceases

## A report on Yoga Sūtra IV.25 with Hegel and Fichte

## The Sūtra

`viśeṣa-darśina ātmabhāva-bhāvanā-vinivṛttiḥ`

### Simple translation

> For one who sees the distinction, the construction “I am this” ceases.

The Sūtra does not say that Ātman becomes nonexistent. It says that the
continued production of a determinate condition as one's absolute Self comes
to an end.

```text
determinate actuality
“I am this”
       ↓ discriminating vision
this actuality is also only possible
and could be otherwise
       ↓
its claim to be Ātman ceases
```

Hegel names the structure of this determinate self **Contingency**.

## A note about the Hegel source

In `dharma-greater-varttika.tex`, Hegel's entire section “Contingency or
Formal Actuality, Possibility, and Necessity” is placed under Yoga IV.25–28
as a group.

The present report does not pretend that Hegel assigned one numbered
paragraph exclusively to IV.25. It takes the opening movement of that whole
section—from immediate actuality through formal possibility into
contingency—as the determination most directly illuminating
`ātma-bhāva-bhāvanā-vinivṛtti`.

## Why this is the beginning of Modality

IV.22–24 exposed the Absolute through Citta:

```text
IV.22    Citta assumes Cit's form and Buddhi knows itself

IV.23    Appearance and Reflection color one complete Citta

IV.24    the Absolute manifests itself through its own Mode
```

IV.25 begins Modality proper. The question is no longer simply what the
complete Citta contains. It is:

> With what necessity does a particular determination exist?

The constructed self says “I am this.” Modal reflection asks whether this
`this` is necessary, merely possible, or contingent.

## Formal Actuality: “I am this”

Hegel begins from first or formal Actuality. It is immediate and unreflected:

```text
this exists
this is actual
this is what I am
```

Because it has not yet comprehended the totality of its form, formal
Actuality is little more than concrete existence in general.

This is `ātma-bhāva` at its most immediate:

```text
a determinate Citta-formation exists
                   ↓
Ahaṃkāra treats existence as sufficient proof
                   ↓
“because this formation is actual, it is my Self”
```

The inference silently converts facticity into absolute identity.

## Actuality contains possibility

Hegel immediately shows that Actuality is not mere existence. As the unity
of inwardness and externality, it contains an in-itself or possibility.

His initial proposition is simple:

> What is actual is possible.

```text
actual self-formation
“I am this”
        ↓ reflected inwardly
possible self-formation
“this determination could be”
```

The moment actuality is known as possible, its claim to absolute necessity
has been broken. The same determination that announced “I simply am” now
shows itself as one determination that could be otherwise.

This is the beginning of `viśeṣa-darśana`.

## A–U–M in formal Modality

Our corrected triad gives the movement a compact form:

```text
A                              U
Appearance                     Reflection / thinking
“I am this actual form”        “this form is possible”
               \              /
                \            /
                     M
                 Contingency
the actual is only one possible determination
```

A appears; U reflects; M knows their unity as contingent.

M does not merely join two propositions. It discloses the modal status of
the determinate appearance.

## Possibility as reflected Actuality

Hegel defines possibility as Actuality reflected into itself. But this first
reflection is still formal. It says only that the determination is identical
with itself:

```text
A is possible because A = A
```

Formal possibility therefore appears to be an unlimited receptacle. Anything
that does not immediately contradict itself can be called possible.

This form of reflection is insufficient for the Science of Citta. It can
declare a self-image possible without investigating the determinations that
constitute it.

```text
formal reflection
“this self-conception is thinkable”

genetic reflection
“what relations and contradictions construct this self-conception?”
```

Only the second can produce discrimination.

## The possible is deficient

Possibility has a positive side: it is reflected self-identity. But in the
totality of form, it also has a negative meaning. It is merely possible and
therefore points beyond itself to Actuality for completion.

```text
possible self
has inward form
      ↓ but lacks complete actuality
ought to become actual
```

Possibility is not an independent hidden essence. It is a moment of the
complete modal relation.

This matters for Ātman. An inward self-image is not more absolute merely
because it is inward. The private possibility “what I really am inside” is
as conditioned as the outward appearance it opposes.

## Every formal possibility contains its opposite

When a content is actually developed, it acquires determinations. Every
determination includes negation and distinction from another.

Thus formal possibility generates its opposite:

```text
A is possible
        ↓
not-A is equally formally possible
```

The constructed self confronts its modal counterpart:

```text
“I am essentially this”
             ↕
“I might not be this”
```

The two possibilities are not accidentally juxtaposed. The relation between
them constitutes each as possible.

This is `pratiyogin` already germinating within formal Modality: a
determination receives its limit from its opposed counterpart.

## Possibility becomes contradiction

Formal identity conceals opposition. Once the content is unfolded, its
negation becomes explicit and possibility contradicts itself:

```text
A can be
not-A can be
        ↓
neither possibility contains the ground
of why one rather than the other is actual
```

Possibility is therefore internally impossibility when asked to ground
Actuality. It cannot explain why a determinate self exists.

The construction “I am this” loses both of its attempted foundations:

```text
outward facticity
mere existence does not prove absolute Selfhood

inward possibility
mere thinkability does not prove absolute Selfhood
```

## Possibility self-sublates into Actuality

The contradiction of formal possibilities cannot remain an inert inward
comparison. Their self-sublating relation becomes immediate and therefore
returns to Actuality.

But this is no longer the first unreflected Actuality. It is reflected
Actuality:

```text
first Actuality
“this simply is”
        ↓ reflected as possibility and opposite possibility
reflected Actuality
“this is actual, but its opposite was equally possible”
```

The result is Contingency.

## Contingency is the truth of `ātma-bhāva`

The contingent is actual while also determined as only possible. Its other
or opposite could equally be actual.

```text
this body, history, personality, and Citta-configuration
are genuinely actual

but

they do not contain the absolute ground
of why they, rather than their alternatives, must be
```

Discriminating vision does not deny the determinate self's empirical
actuality. It denies its necessity as Ātman.

That is the Hegelian meaning of `vinivṛtti`:

> The contingent self ceases to reproduce its actuality as though it were
> the absolute form of the Self.

## Ahaṃkāra as modal error

Ahaṃkāra does not merely attach the word `I` to a neutral content. It assigns
the wrong modal status to a formation.

```text
what the formation is:
actual but contingent

what Ahaṃkāra claims:
absolutely necessary Selfhood
```

`Viśeṣa-darśana` corrects this modal confusion. The formation can be used in
ordinary life without being elevated into the pāramārthika Principle.

This preserves Śaṅkara's Adhyāsa insight: what ceases is superimposition, not
the Inconceivable One upon which the conditioned attribution depended.

## Fichte: the genesis concealed in the fact

Fichte approaches the same Sūtra genetically. Genesis negates itself within
Knowing and disappears into its stable result.

```text
constructive activity
        ↓ completes itself
immediate fact
        ↓ conceals its genesis
“I simply am this”
```

The fact appears self-grounding because the process that produced it is no
longer visible in its result.

Discriminating vision restores the genesis concealed within facticity. The
constructed self can then be known as a product rather than appropriated as
the absolute Self.

## Hegel and Fichte compared

The two accounts are complementary:

| Fichte | Hegel |
|---|---|
| asks how the apparent fact was generated | asks what modal status the fact possesses |
| genesis negates itself in its stable result | immediate Actuality contains possibility |
| the product conceals its constructive history | the actual is only one among opposed possibilities |
| genetic insight restores the concealed process | modal reflection discloses Contingency |
| false selfhood ends when the fact is reconstructed | false selfhood ends when actuality loses absolute necessity |

Fichte says:

> “I am this” is a genesis disguised as an immediate fact.

Hegel says:

> “I am this” is a contingent actuality disguised as absolute necessity.

Together they give the Science of `ātma-bhāva`.

## Fichte's correction to Hegel

Calling the self contingent does not yet explain how its actuality was
constructed. Without genesis, contingency could become a merely skeptical
observation: things happen to be this way for no intelligible reason.

Fichte requires the Science to reconstruct:

```text
Vāsanā and Saṃskāra
        ↓
Manas synthesis
        ↓
Ahaṃkāra appropriation
        ↓
apparently immediate self-fact
```

Contingency must be genetically produced, not merely asserted.

## Hegel's correction to Fichte

Fichte's identification of immediate facticity with accomplished genesis
could allow a generated result to appear necessary simply because its
genesis has completed itself.

Hegel asks a further question:

> Could the completed genesis have produced another determination?

By displaying the opposite possibility internal to the actual, Hegel
prevents accomplished genesis from being confused with Absolute Necessity.

The Science must comprehend not only how a fact arose but why its modal
status is contingent.

## Prajñā as modal discrimination

Prajñā-Saṃskāra now acquires another determination. It does not merely
retain insight into genesis. It retains the exact modal status of each
formation.

```text
ordinary Saṃskāra
“this formation has repeatedly been actual”

Ahaṃkāra
“therefore this formation is necessarily me”

Prajñā-Saṃskāra
“the formation is actual, genetically produced, and contingent”
```

This prevents recurrence of `ātma-bhāva-bhāvanā`. The correction has become
an enduring Dharma of Citta.

## The Science of Citta

IV.25 demonstrates why the Science of Citta must include Modality.

It is insufficient to record:

```text
what Citta-state exists
```

The Science must also know:

```text
how it was generated
what alternatives belong to its determination
whether it is possible, actual, contingent, or necessary
what right it has to be held as true
```

Thus Dharma as holding-to-be-true includes modal validity. A contingent
formation may be held as empirically actual; it may not be held as the
absolute Self.

## Provisional result

IV.25 says:

> The one who sees the exact distinction stops constructing a contingent
> Citta-formation as absolutely necessary Selfhood.

Hegel shows that immediate Actuality contains possibility. Formal
possibility contains its opposite, contradicts itself, and returns as
reflected Actuality—Contingency. The determinate self remains actual but
loses its claim to be Ātman.

Fichte shows why that claim initially appears compelling: genesis disappears
into its completed fact, concealing the activity that constructed “I am
this.”

```text
Fichte
restore the genesis concealed in the self-fact
                    +
Hegel
determine the self-fact as contingent Actuality
                    ↓
viśeṣa-darśana
the construction of absolute selfhood ceases
```

IV.26 can now give Citta a new modal direction: away from contingent
self-appropriation and toward Kaivalya through continuous Viveka.
