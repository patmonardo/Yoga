# Māṇḍūkya Study — OṂ and Its Artha

## Place in Organon Yoga

Yoga Sūtra I.27 calls *praṇava* the designation of Īśvara; I.28 pairs its
repetition with *tad-artha-bhāvanam*, cultivation of its meaning. The
Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad gives that second practice substantial work to do. This
study begins with its first two verses and Śaṅkara's comments, then follows
the text's own further determinations. It belongs to Yogavidya's Organon
reading, while the [paired I.28 study](../../01-prajna/YS-I.28.md) keeps the
Yoga sūtra's wording in view.

## Māṇḍūkya 1: OṂ and the whole field

> ओमित्येतदक्षरमिदं सर्वं तस्योपव्याख्यानं भूतं भवद्भविष्यदिति सर्वमोङ्कार एव । यच्चान्यत्त्रिकालातीतं तदप्योङ्कार एव ॥ १ ॥

The opening calls OṂ “all this” and explicitly includes past, present,
future, and what exceeds the three times. It announces an *upavyākhyāna*,
an exposition of that claim. Thus attending to OṂ's *artha* cannot stop at
repeating a syllable: the text asks what its universal designation means.

Śaṅkara asks how determining OṂ can serve realization of the self and
develops the relation between the expression and what it expresses. That is
an example of disciplined attention to *artha*, not merely a further
instance of vocal repetition. [Source: Māṇḍūkya 1 and Bhāṣya](../../../../Sankara/Organon/sankara/derived/Mandukya/MK_C01.json).

## Māṇḍūkya 2: From designation to what is designated

> सर्वं ह्येतद्ब्रह्मायमात्मा ब्रह्म सोऽयमात्मा चतुष्पात् ॥ २ ॥

The second verse determines “all this” as *brahman*, identifies this self
with *brahman*, and introduces the fourfold account of the self. Śaṅkara
says the first verse emphasized the designation and the second emphasizes
what is designated, in order to understand their unity. The reading now has
a definite task: follow how the four quarters clarify the meaning announced
by OṂ. [Source: Māṇḍūkya 2 and Bhāṣya](../../../../Sankara/Organon/sankara/derived/Mandukya/MK_C01.json).

## Relation to Yoga and Kośa

This is a study of the Māṇḍūkya's own argument, brought into Organon Yoga
through I.27–29. Śaṅkara's [Gītā 8.13
comment](../../../../Sankara/BG/BG-C08/gita-8.13.md) provides the concise
pair of uttering OṂ and remembering its referent. The Māṇḍūkya develops
what that referent means. The [Kośa at VAK
8.39](../../../../Vasubandhu/VAK/08-samapatti/VAK_8.39_bhasya.md)
distinguishes teaching as transmission from teaching as realization. That
comparison helps ask how *artha-bhāvanā* moves beyond possession of a sound
or text; it does not make the Kośa an OṂ commentary.

In our Buddha as Īśvara reading, the question is how a living realization
can become present in new practitioners. The Māṇḍūkya's own determination of
OṂ must be studied first; an identification with the Buddha is an Organon
proposal, not a claim made by these two Upaniṣadic verses.
