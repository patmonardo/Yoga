# YS IV.2 — Transformation into Another Kind

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

> जात्यन्तरपरिणामः प्रकृत्यापूरात्॥ ४.२॥

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

> jātyantara-pariṇāmaḥ prakṛty-āpūrāt // 4.2 //

## 3. Lexical Analysis

| Pada | Form | Force in this sūtra |
|---|---|---|
| *jāti-antara* | compound member | another kind or mode of birth |
| *pariṇāmaḥ* | nominative masculine singular | transformation into that kind |
| *prakṛti* | first member of the ablative compound | nature, the productive ground named here |
| *āpūrāt* | ablative singular of *āpūra* | from filling out or coming to fullness |

## 4. Grammar

*Jātyantara-pariṇāmaḥ* is the nominative statement; *prakṛty-āpūrāt* gives its ground in the ablative. The compound does not name a second agent that imposes a new kind upon nature. The exact metaphysics of *prakṛti* must be established through the sequence, not supplied by the word alone.

## 5. Translation

### Close translation

> Transformation into another kind comes from nature's filling out.

### Yogavidyā reading

> A new determinate kind arises as nature fills out its power of transformation.

## 6. Logical Determination

Dharmapāda begins here with **genesis**: another kind is the result of *pariṇāma*, and the ground named for that transformation is *prakṛti-āpūra*. The sūtra asks how a determinate appearance can arise from a productive ground. It has not yet stated the mechanism of karma or the constitution of a world. Those determinations will have to be shown, not inserted into *prakṛti* at the outset.

The Kośa sharpens the question without settling Yoga's answer. [VAK 4.01](../../../../Vasubandhu/Organon/04-karma/VAK_4.01.md) says the differentiated world is karma-born and defines karma through volition and what volition produces. In the [Bhāṣya to VAK 3.50](../../../../Vasubandhu/Organon/03-loka/VAK_3.50_bhasya.md), a discussion of world formation explicitly disputes the Sāṃkhya account of transformation in an enduring bearer. Yoga's *pariṇāma* must therefore be explained on its own terms; a shared word does not erase the disagreement.

## 7. Interpretive Note

The [Fichte IV.2 report](../../../Yogavidya/Dharmapada/ys-iv-2-fichte-report.md) gives the genetic task its first extended form: how can a living ground account for determinate appearances rather than leaving them as disconnected facts? That is our Yogavidyā inquiry, not a lexical translation of *prakṛti*. Kośa's Karma → Loka analysis supplies differentiated causal tests; Yoga IV.2 opens the wider question of the ground from which such differentiation becomes possible. IV.3 will ask what a *nimitta* does in that process.
