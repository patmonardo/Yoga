# YS IV.3 — Our Bhāṣya

## 1. Sūtra Anchor

> निमित्तमप्रयोजकं प्रकृतीनां वरणभेदस्तु ततः क्षेत्रिकवत्॥ ४.३॥

> *nimittam aprayojakaṃ prakṛtīnāṃ varaṇa-bhedas tu tataḥ kṣetrikavat* — The occasion does not impel the powers of nature; from it comes, rather, the opening of their barrier, as with a farmer.

## 2. Commentary

IV.2 named nature's filling out as the ground of transformation into another kind. IV.3 now asks what work remains for an occasion. *Aprayojakam* denies it the power to drive the *prakṛtis* or make their efficacy. *Varaṇa-bhedas tu tataḥ* assigns it a determinate act: the barrier is opened through it. The distinction is causal, not a dismissal of action. The farmer's cut in the bank matters, while the water's movement is not made by the farmer.

The [Kośa's VAK 4.04 Bhāṣya](../../../../Vasubandhu/Organon/04-karma/VAK_4.04_bhasya.md) poses a related question when it asks what carries the consequence of an action. In its Sautrāntika reply, the completion of the action is called an occasion (*tannimittaḥ*) for transformation of the agent's own continuum (*tasyaiva ... saṃtatipariṇāmaḥ*); the reply resists positing a further, distinct *avijñapti* dharma for that office. This is a Kośa account of karmic continuity, not the Yoga sūtra's account of *prakṛti*. It supplies a rigorous comparison: naming an occasion does not yet explain where the resulting power or continuity resides.

The [Loka Bhāṣya at VAK 3.28](../../../../Vasubandhu/Organon/03-loka/VAK_3.28_bhasya.md) distinguishes a member's cause-role from its result-role according to the relation under consideration. IV.3 likewise requires a specified causal role rather than the unqualified assertion that the farmer “causes” the water to flow. Its own specification is *varaṇa-bheda*: opening the obstruction. The Kośa's two discussions help discipline the causal question; IV.2–3 provide Yoga's answer concerning the genesis of another kind.

## 3. Project Determination

The [Fichte reading of IV.3](../../../Yogavidya/Dharmapada/ys-iv-3-fichte-report.md) brings this division into knowing. Reflection is an occasion for disclosure when it removes the projected gap between knowing and living being. It cannot stand outside being and confer on it the power to construct itself. The work of method is therefore real and precise: it opens access to a self-developing ground. Our Dharmapāda reads the farmer in that genetic sense, with the power of disclosure internal to the Dharma under study.

The three terms must remain distinct: **occasion** (*nimitta*), **barrier-opening** (*varaṇa-bheda*), and **immanent power** (*prakṛti*). Their relation is the first correction to any account that treats the completed appearance as though an external intervention had produced it from nothing.
