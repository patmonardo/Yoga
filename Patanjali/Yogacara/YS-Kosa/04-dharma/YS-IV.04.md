# YS IV.4 — Constructed Cittas and I-Am-Ness

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

> निर्माणचित्तान्यस्मितामात्रात्॥ ४.४॥

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

> nirmāṇa-cittāny asmitā-mātrāt // 4.4 //

## 3. Lexical Analysis

| Pada | Form | Force in this sūtra |
|---|---|---|
| *nirmāṇa-cittāni* | nominative neuter plural | constructed or fashioned cittas |
| *asmitā* | first member of the ablative compound | I-am-ness, the form of subjective identity |
| *mātrāt* | ablative singular of *mātra* | from the mere or bare form, without further determinations stated |

## 4. Grammar

The sandhi resolves as *nirmāṇa-cittāni asmitā-mātrāt*. The plural *cittāni* is the subject of a compressed nominal statement; arising is understood. *Asmitā-mātrāt* supplies the source in the ablative. The compound qualifies the source as mere I-am-ness; it does not say that the resulting cittas are numerically one, nor that I-am-ness is the ultimate ground of consciousness.

## 5. Translation

### Close translation

> Constructed cittas arise from mere I-am-ness.

### Yogavidyā reading

> A plurality of fashioned centers of consciousness proceeds from the bare form “I am”; their construction does not yet establish the ultimate ground of knowing.

## 6. Logical Determination

IV.2 locates transformation in nature's fullness; IV.3 assigns the occasion its work of opening a barrier. IV.4 turns to the **form in which a constructed citta appears as a center**: *asmitā-mātra*. The plurality belongs to the constructed cittas. Their shared source is stated only as the bare I-am form. The sūtra leaves their coordination for the next determination.

The [Kośa kārikā VAK 7.49](../../../../Vasubandhu/Organon/07-jnana/VAK_7.49.md) and its [Bhāṣya](../../../../Vasubandhu/Organon/07-jnana/VAK_7.49_bhasya.md) distinguish constructed manifestations (*nirmita*) from the *nirmāṇa-cittas* by which they are produced, and count fourteen such minds. This makes construction a precise causal question. Yoga IV.4, however, asks for the source of the constructed **cittas** themselves; the two texts must not exchange product and producer. The [Kośa at VAK 5.11](../../../../Vasubandhu/Organon/05-anusaya/VAK_5.11_bhasya.md) also names *asmitā*, but there it is I-am conceit nourished by a view. Yoga II.5–8 differentiates *avidyā* and *asmitā* from *rāga* and *dveṣa*, without making cognition and attachment independent systems. IV.4 asks how a knowing center arises; the relation of *citta:caitta* and *hetu:phala* in Karma must still be worked through.

## 7. Interpretive Note

The [Fichte IV.4 report](../../../Yogavidya/Dharmapada/ys-iv-4-fichte-report.md) distinguishes a living ground's self-construction from consciousness's ideal construction of it. That distinction gives the “mere” in *asmitā-mātra* philosophical force: the constructed I can present genesis to itself, yet its formal claim to be the source of genesis still requires demonstration. IV.4 poses this problem; it does not solve it by naming the I.
