# YS IV.4 — Our Bhāṣya

## 1. Sūtra Anchor

> निर्माणचित्तान्यस्मितामात्रात्॥ ४.४॥

> *nirmāṇa-cittāny asmitā-mātrāt* — Constructed cittas arise from mere I-am-ness.

## 2. Commentary

The question now moves inside the appearance of agency. IV.3 refused the external occasion the power to produce nature's transformation. IV.4 does not restore that power to an isolated subject. It says that *nirmāṇa-cittāni*, constructed cittas in the plural, proceed from *asmitā-mātra*, the bare I-am form. The ablative tells us where this plurality takes shape; *mātra* restricts what has so far been given as its source. The sūtra has not yet shown how the many acts of these cittas are ordered.

The [Kośa's VAK 7.49 kārikā](../../../../Vasubandhu/Organon/07-jnana/VAK_7.49.md) and [Bhāṣya](../../../../Vasubandhu/Organon/07-jnana/VAK_7.49_bhasya.md) bring a valuable precision to the word *nirmāṇa*. They distinguish the constructed manifestation from the construction-minds that produce it and trace those minds to results of higher knowledge; [VAK 7.50 Bhāṣya](../../../../Vasubandhu/Organon/07-jnana/VAK_7.50_bhasya.md) then locates them among determinate results of *dhyāna*. Yoga IV.4 reverses the immediate question: it asks from what the constructed cittas themselves arise. The Kośa's account prevents “construction” from becoming a vague metaphor, while Yoga's ablative directs our inquiry to a different genetic relation.

The Kośa also uses the very term *asmitā* in [VAK 5.11 and its Bhāṣya](../../../../Vasubandhu/Organon/05-anusaya/VAK_5.11_bhasya.md). There it is I-am conceit, nourished by *satkāya-dṛṣṭi*, whose manifestation is disabled when that view is broken. That is a determinate analysis of an afflicted formation. Yoga II.3–8 differentiates *avidyā* and *asmitā* from *rāga* and *dveṣa*: II.5 concerns mistaken cognition, II.6 the identification of seeing power with its instrument, while II.7–8 concern attachment to pleasure and aversion to pain. These are distinct determinations within one developing life, not a clean partition of theoretical and practical faculties. IV.4's *asmitā-mātra* raises the theoretical question of how the I-form enters a knowing citta; its arising and effects must also be examined through *citta:caitta* and *hetu:phala* in Karma.

## 3. Project Determination

The [Fichte reading of IV.4](../../../Yogavidya/Dharmapada/ys-iv-4-fichte-report.md) distinguishes real self-construction from its ideal reconstruction within consciousness. The constructed citta may say “I construct,” but that saying alone cannot establish that its I-form is the power by which being constructs itself. Yet a mistaken claim about the knowing subject does not erase the citta's capacity for determinate knowledge. Theoretical Reason can know through the very construction whose ground it must still examine. The bare I is the form in which genesis becomes present to a center of knowing; its validity has to be derived from the living ground. Thus IV.4 continues the genetic argument of IV.2–3 instead of adding an external creator under the name of a subject.

This first pass keeps the precise question open: how can one source-form yield many constructed cittas, and what unifies their differing activities? The [Kośa's Karma opening](../../../../Vasubandhu/Organon/04-karma/VAK_4.01_bhasya.md) already turns from differentiated Loka to the *cetanā* and action that generate it. That suggests a further inquiry into the arising of citta and its factors through cause and result, beyond simply classifying the world's dependent arising. IV.5 is positioned to determine the relation among the constructed cittas; its answer is not assumed here.
