# Editorial Method for the Yoga–Kośa Commentary

This is the working procedure for the sūtra studies, our Bhāṣya, and the essays. **Yoga is the Science of Consciousness** is the governing determination of the opening. **Nirodha is sublation** in our Yoga reading. Yogavidyā is the research that sets the standard for this commentary; the Bhāṣya presents what can be taught clearly at its own level. The Kośa supplies exact analyses and tests, and its meaning remains under investigation. A received Yoga commentary may be compared when useful, but it does not decide our reading.

## Work through a sūtra or a block

1. **State the Yoga problem.** Read the Sanskrit and its immediate sequence. Record morphology, syntax, and a grammatical gloss where one is needed. Then state the project's leading determination plainly. A grammatical gloss does not displace the reading the commentary is teaching.
2. **Locate the Yogavidyā commitment.** Identify which established distinctions govern the passage: for example consciousness and its movements, Form : Essence : Principle, or sublation. If the research has not settled an issue, mark the claim as a working determination and keep the Bhāṣya within what has been shown.
3. **Read the Kośa passage in its own argument.** Record the exact passage, the term's function there, and what the passage actually establishes. Test how it illuminates the Yoga problem. Its categories can develop our analysis, but a shared word does not make the two arguments identical.
4. **Write the paired Bhāṣya.** Begin with the sūtra's work in the sequence. Use Kośa evidence where it bears on that work. State the project determination positively and at a readable level. Put the longer derivation, competing possibilities, and difficult Kośa questions in an essay.
5. **Check the block.** Read the adjacent studies, Bhāṣyas, and essays as one argument. A change to a governing term must be carried through its translations, cross references, and summaries. Look for an inherited premise that still directs the prose after its leading sentence has been corrected.

## The test for an inadequate principle

A *doṣa-bīja* is our provisional name for a seed of error in the developing account: a principle or assumption whose consequences conflict with the Science being taught. When one appears, trace its consequences through the block. State the contradiction, determine which claim must lose its authority, and retain whatever the earlier formulation genuinely established. That is the editorial analogue of **sublation**; merely replacing a word is insufficient.

I.2 gives the immediate example. “Restraint of the movements” had become the leading translation. It then governed I.3–12 and the essays, even though the established project determination was “Yoga is the Science of Consciousness.” Revision required a new opening and a check of the entire block's reasoning. The Sanskrit syntax remains visible as a gloss; the Science now leads the argument. [I.2 study](01-prajna/YS-I.02.md) · [I.1–12 review](essays/ys-i-01-12-kosa-review.md)

The Kośa's own account of *nirodha* is still to be worked through. Its technical distinctions are evidence to examine, not a completed definition of Yoga's sublation. The proposed application of *nirodha* to inadequate principles or *doṣa-bīja* belongs in that inquiry; it should not be attributed to a Kośa passage before the passage has been shown to support it.

## Completion check

For a revised block, verify the Sanskrit anchors and paired structure; read the prose for the governing determination; check essay links and source attributions; run the YS audit and focused tests. The audit confirms structure, not philosophical coherence. The authorial review decides whether the block now teaches the Science without contradiction.
