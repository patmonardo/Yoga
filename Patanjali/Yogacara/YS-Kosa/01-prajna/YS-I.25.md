# YS I.25 — The Unsurpassed Seed of Omniscience

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

> तत्र निरतिशयं सर्वज्ञबीजम्॥ १.२५॥

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

> tatra niratiśayaṃ sarvajña-bījam // 1.25 //

## 3. Lexical Analysis

| Pada | Form | Force |
|---|---|---|
| *tatra* | locative adverb | in that one, referring to Īśvara of I.24 |
| *niratiśayam* | neuter nominative singular | unsurpassed; admitting no superior |
| *sarvajña-bījam* | neuter nominative singular compound | seed of omniscience or of an omniscient one |

## 4. Grammar

*Tatra* takes up Īśvara. The two neuter forms agree: the seed of omniscience
is unsurpassed there. The transmitted source also notes the variant
*sarvajñatva-bījam* (“seed of the state of omniscience”); the main reading is
*sarvajña-bījam*. The sūtra does not itself explain how *bījam* functions.

## 5. Translation

### Literal translation

> In that one, the seed of omniscience is unsurpassed.

## 6. Logical Determination

I.24 distinguished Īśvara by freedom from affliction and its conditioned
sequence. I.25 adds a determination of knowing. *Niratiśayam* marks an
unsurpassed degree; the word *bījam* leaves the relation between seed and
fully manifest knowledge to be worked out.

## 7. Interpretive Note

Śaṅkara calls the Lord *sarvajña* in his [Gītā 15.17
commentary](../../../Sankara/BG/BG-C15/gita-15.17.md), where the verse names
an imperishable *puruṣa* and *īśvara*. That is a close comparison in
vocabulary, while the Gītā's account of sustaining the three worlds is its
own claim. The [Kośa Bhāṣya](../../../Vasubandhu/Sources/kosabhasya.txt)
(467|14–467|21) calls the Buddha omniscient by the capacity for accurate
knowledge wherever directed, rather than by simultaneously attending to
everything. These are distinct accounts of unsurpassed knowing; neither
settles the meaning of Yoga's *bīja* on its own.
