# YS I.29 — Inward Consciousness and the Absence of Obstacles

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

> ततः प्रत्यक्चेतनाधिगमोऽप्यन्तरायाभावश्च ॥ १.२९॥

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

> tataḥ pratyak-cetana-adhigamo 'py antarāya-abhāvaś ca // 1.29 //

## 3. Lexical Analysis

| Pada | Form | Force |
|---|---|---|
| *tataḥ* | ablative adverb | from that practice of I.28 |
| *pratyak-cetana-adhigamaḥ* | nominative singular compound | realization of inward consciousness |
| *api* | particle | also |
| *antarāya-abhāvaḥ* | nominative singular compound | absence of obstacles |
| *ca* | conjunction | and |

## 4. Grammar

*Tataḥ* gives the source of two coordinated results. *Adhigamaḥ* and
*abhāvaḥ* are the two nominative heads; *api* and *ca* mark their addition.
The sūtra does not yet enumerate the obstacles; I.30 does that.

## 5. Translation

### Literal translation

> From that, realization of inward consciousness also, and the absence of
> obstacles.

## 6. Logical Determination

The practice of I.28 bears on both knowing and its impediments. Repetition
joined to cultivation of meaning leads toward inward realization and a
clearing of obstacles. I.29 names the results without claiming that this is
their only possible route.

## 7. Interpretive Note

Śaṅkara uses *pratyak-cetana* in his [Gītā 15.17
commentary](../../../Sankara/BG/BG-C15/gita-15.17.md) for the inner
consciousness of all beings. This is a lexical contact, not an identity of
doctrines. The [Kośa at VAK 8.39](../../../Vasubandhu/VAK/08-samapatti/VAK_8.39_bhasya.md)
distinguishes transmitted teaching from its realization (*adhigama*),
making a closer comparison for the move from I.28's expression and meaning
to I.29's realization. [Sāṃkhya Kārikā
45](../../../Reference/Samkhya.txt) also says *aiśvaryād avighātaḥ*:
non-obstruction follows from *aiśvarya*. That distinct route prevents us
from treating the absence of obstacles as a unique sign of the Īśvara
practice. Neither comparison supplies I.30's obstacle list.
