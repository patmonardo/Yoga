# YS I.18 — Our Bhāṣya

## 1. Sūtra Anchor

> विरामप्रत्ययाभ्यासपूर्वः संस्कारशेषोऽन्यः ॥ १.१८॥

> *virāma-pratyaya-abhyāsa-pūrvaḥ saṃskāra-śeṣo 'nyaḥ* — The other [samādhi] is preceded by practice of a presentation of cessation, with latent impressions remaining.

## 2. Commentary

I.17's four forms were all *sālambana*, with object-support. The received Bhāṣya to I.18 calls the other samādhi *asaṃprajñāta*: all the movements of consciousness have subsided, yet *saṃskāras* remain. Higher dispassion is its means. The Bhāṣya says that object-supported practice cannot, as such, accomplish this unsupported condition; it describes practice of the objectless *virāma-pratyaya* as preceding consciousness becoming *nirālambana*, as though it had reached absence. Thus “other” marks a change in the relation to support, and *saṃskāra-śeṣa* specifies what survives that change.

The Kośa distinguishes two kinds of *nirodha*. Through special discriminative *prajñā* one attains *pratisaṃkhyānirodha*, disjunction from outflowing dharmas. *Apratisaṃkhyānirodha* is the non-arising of future dharmas when their conditions are lacking. That pair provides a sharp question for I.17–18: what does knowing release, and what ceases to have the conditions for appearance? Yet I.18 explicitly has practice and residual impressions, whereas the Kośa classifies its two cessations as unconditioned. The [companion essay](../essays/01-prajna/virama-and-the-two-kosa-nirodhas.md) develops the relation and its limits.

## 3. Project Determination

The useful Kośa-first distinction is between a cessation attained by discriminative insight and a non-arising through absent conditions. I.17–18 invite that two-sided analysis, especially because the second changes the status of object-support. The Yoga Bhāṣya, however, does not name either Kośa cessation, and its *asaṃprajñāta* is described through an antecedent practice and a remaining trace. The mapping remains a question for the next joint review of I.17–18.
