# YS I.14 — Practice Firmly Grounded

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

> स तु दीर्घकालनैरन्तर्यसत्कारासेवितो दृढभूमिः ॥ १.१४॥

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

> sa tu dīrghakālanairantaryasatkārāsevito dṛḍhabhūmiḥ // 1.14 //

## 3. Lexical Analysis

| Pada | Form | Force |
|---|---|---|
| *saḥ* | nominative masculine singular | that practice of I.13 |
| *tu* | indeclinable | but, moreover |
| *dīrghakāla* | compound member | a long time |
| *nairantarya* | compound member | uninterrupted continuity |
| *satkāra* | compound member | respect, earnest regard |
| *āsevitaḥ* | masculine nominative singular, past participle | cultivated, attended to |
| *dṛḍhabhūmiḥ* | masculine nominative singular | having firm ground |

## 4. Grammar

*Saḥ* refers to *abhyāsaḥ* in I.13. The long compound qualifies that practice through the manner of its cultivation: over a long time, without interruption, and with *satkāra*. The final predicate *dṛḍhabhūmiḥ* says that such practice has a firm ground. The sūtra does not specify a fixed number of days or make duration alone sufficient.

## 5. Translation

### Literal translation

> But that practice, cultivated for a long time, without interruption, and with earnest regard, becomes firmly grounded.

## 6. Logical Determination

I.13 defines practice as effort toward *sthiti*; I.14 asks when that effort has become stable. The received Bhāṣya repeats the three conditions and explains *satkāra* through austerity, celibate discipline, knowledge, and confidence. It then specifies the result: the practice is not quickly overcome by *vyutthāna-saṃskāra*, the formations of emergence from collectedness. Firm ground is thus tested by resistance to a contrary formation, not by elapsed time alone.

## 7. Interpretive Note

The [Kośa companion essay](../essays/01-prajna/firm-grounded-practice-according-to-kosa.md) compares this durability with the Kośa's analysis of *vīrya*, cultivation, and a transformed basis that no longer lets certain afflictions sprout. The Kośa passage concerns abandonment on its own path; it does not define Yoga's *dṛḍhabhūmi*.
