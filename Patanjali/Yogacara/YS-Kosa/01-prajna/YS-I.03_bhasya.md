# YS I.3 — Our Bhāṣya

## 1. Sūtra Anchor

> तदा द्रष्टुः स्वरूपेऽवस्थानम्॥ १.३॥

> *tadā draṣṭuḥ svarūpe 'vasthānam* — Then there is the seer's abiding in its own form.

## 2. Commentary

*Tadā* ties the seer's abiding to I.2's sublation. *Draṣṭuḥ* gives whose abiding is at issue, and *svarūpe* gives its locus. A movement's claim to stand for the seer has been overcome; the seer can be known in its own form. This is the first positive result named by the Science, not the manufacture of a new seer.

The Kośa lets us state the problem more sharply. VAK 1.16 defines differentiated cognition by apprehension of its object. In the discussion of sight at source p. 31, the Kośa tests several ways of assigning “seeing” to eye or cognition, including a warning against treating conventional agent-phrases as independent entities. That analysis describes the conditioned work of seeing. I.3's *draṣṭṛ* must be read from Yoga's sūtra, not quietly substituted with Kośa *vijñāna*. The Kośa comparison marks the boundary of an object-cognition account. [Kośabhāṣya, VAK 1.16 and source p. 31](../../../Vasubandhu/Sources/kosabhasya.txt).

## 3. Project Determination

The Science of Consciousness must distinguish the seer from a conditioned cognition without separating seeing from the work of consciousness. I.3 names the seer's own-form abiding; I.4 names its other relation to the movements. Their opposition supplies the first problem the Science will work through.
