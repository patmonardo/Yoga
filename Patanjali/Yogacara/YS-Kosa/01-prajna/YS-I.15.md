# YS I.15 — Vairāgya and Mastery

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

> दृष्टानुश्रविकविषयवितृष्णस्य वशीकारसंज्ञा वैराग्यम्॥ १.१५॥

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

> dṛṣṭānuśravikaviṣayavitṛṣṇasya vaśīkārasaṃjñā vairāgyam // 1.15 //

## 3. Lexical Analysis

| Pada | Form | Force |
|---|---|---|
| *dṛṣṭa* | compound member | seen, experienced |
| *ānuśravika* | compound member | known by received teaching |
| *viṣaya* | compound member | object or sphere of experience |
| *vitṛṣṇasya* | genitive masculine singular | of one without thirst |
| *vaśīkāra* | compound member | mastery, bringing under control |
| *saṃjñā* | nominative feminine singular | designation, recognition |
| *vairāgyam* | nominative neuter singular | dispassion |

## 4. Grammar

The genitive *vitṛṣṇasya* refers to a person free of thirst for both classes of *viṣaya*: those experienced and those known from teaching. *Vaśīkārasaṃjñā* is the feminine nominative designation applied to the neuter predicate *vairāgyam*; the gender difference does not make two states. The sūtra defines this grade of dispassion through freedom from thirst and the name “mastery.”

## 5. Translation

### Literal translation

> Dispassion is the state called mastery in one free from thirst for experienced and traditionally taught objects.

## 6. Logical Determination

I.12 gave practice and dispassion as two means; I.13–14 specified practice; I.15 specifies dispassion. Its range includes ordinary objects and promised attainments known through teaching. The received Bhāṣya attributes this mastery to seeing defects in objects and the force of discriminative examination (*prasaṃkhyāna*), even amid contact with divine or nondivine objects. Thus its account is not merely physical withdrawal from objects. I.16 will name a further, higher dispassion; this verse defines the present grade.

## 7. Interpretive Note

The [Kośa companion essay](../essays/01-prajna/vairagya-and-viraga-according-to-kosa.md) examines *rāga* within refined meditative states, worldly and supramundane dispassion, and *virāgadhātu*. It also records the precise Sāṃkhya textual tension: *virāga* is a sattvic form of Buddhi in Kārikā 23, while Kārikā 45 connects *vairāgya* with *prakṛtilaya*. Neither statement is silently substituted for Yoga I.15's definition.
