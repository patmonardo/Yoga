# YS I.28 — Repetition and Cultivation of Meaning

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

> तज्जपस्तदर्थभावनम्॥ १.२८॥

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

> taj-japas tad-artha-bhāvanam // 1.28 //

## 3. Lexical Analysis

| Pada | Form | Force |
|---|---|---|
| *tat* in *taj-japaḥ* | demonstrative compound member | of that, the *praṇava* named in I.27 |
| *japaḥ* | nominative singular | repetition, recitation |
| *tad-artha* | compound | the *artha* of that designation |
| *bhāvanam* | nominative singular | cultivation, sustained bringing to mind |

## 4. Grammar

The sūtra places two nominal expressions together: repetition of that
designation and cultivation of its *artha*. The two occurrences of *tat*
refer back through I.27; *artha* keeps the practice directed beyond sound.

## 5. Translation

### Literal translation

> Repetition of that, and cultivation of its *artha*.

## 6. Logical Determination

I.27 gave *praṇava* as Īśvara's designation. I.28 states a practice with two
inseparable moments: repeated expression and attention to its *artha*.
This is the first explicit *artha* in Prajñāpāda. Its place in the
Īśvara sequence does not make it the impure Objective Artha later
examined in *savitarkā samāpatti*.
I.29 will state what follows from this practice.

## 7. Interpretive Note

Śaṅkara's [Gītā 8.13 commentary](../../../Sankara/BG/BG-C08/gita-8.13.md)
joins uttering OṂ to remembering the Lord as its meaning. The
[Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad 1–2](../../../Sankara/Organon/sankara/derived/Mandukya/MK_C01.json)
expounds OṂ as the whole of what is, including what exceeds the three times;
Śaṅkara discusses the relation of designation and designated. Attending to
those determinations is a concrete example of cultivating OṂ's *artha*.
Our question of Subjective Artha and Artha as Absolute Attribute is
introduced here, then developed through the [samāpatti
essays](../essays/01-prajna/savitarka-nirvitarka-and-the-three-bases.md)
and the [Yogavidyā Māṇḍūkya study](../Yogavidya/Prajnapada/mandukya-om-artha-study.md).
The [Kośa at VAK 8.39](../../../Vasubandhu/VAK/08-samapatti/VAK_8.39_bhasya.md)
distinguishes transmitted teaching from realized teaching, a useful
comparison for how practice takes up meaning. It makes no claim about OṂ.
