# YS I.42 — Our Bhāṣya

## 1. Sūtra Anchor

> तत्र शब्दार्थज्ञानविकल्पैः सङ्कीर्णा सवितर्का समापत्तिः ॥ १.४२॥

> *tatra śabdārthajñānavikalpaiḥ saṅkīrṇā savitarkā samāpattiḥ* — There, samāpatti intermixed with distinctions of word, *artha*, and knowledge is savitarkā.

## 2. Commentary

I.41 made consciousness a clear jewel capable of resting in grasper,
grasping, or grasped. I.42 places a definite content in that clarity.
Word, impure *artha*, and knowledge are present together but are not yet
discriminated in their complete relation. The sūtra calls this
*saṅkīrṇā*, intermixed, and still calls it *samāpatti*. Mixture here
marks work to be done within a genuine attainment.

At the basic Kośa level, [VAK 1.32](../../../Vasubandhu/VAK/01-dhatu/VAK_1.32_bhasya.md)
shows that *vitarka* and *vicāra* may be associated with cognition and
that their occurrence differs by domain and meditative level. This
does not supply Yoga's three terms for I.42. Yoga gives those terms
itself: *śabda*, *artha*, and *jñāna*. The Kośa helps us examine the
associated operations by which such a field can be held.

## 3. Project Determination

We read *savitarkā* as the Dharma-skandha process of an impure Dharma.
Here *artha* is the impure Objective Artha of *saṃvṛti* and implicates an
object; calling it simply “object” would conceal the determination at
issue.
Form Base supplies a determinate presentation. Essence Base discloses
it through naming, relation, and knowing, while the Principle governing
their unity has not yet been made explicit. *Saṃvṛti* here is a working
field of opposed Form and Essence determinations, not an empty illusion.
The [companion essay](../essays/01-prajna/savitarka-nirvitarka-and-the-three-bases.md)
works through this structure and its transition to I.43.
