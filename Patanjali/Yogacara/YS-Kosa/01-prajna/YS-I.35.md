# YS I.35 — Object-Bearing Activity and Mental Steadiness

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

> विषयवती वा प्रवृत्तिरुत्पन्ना मनसः स्थितिनिबन्धिनी ॥ १.३५॥

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

> viṣayavatī vā pravṛttir utpannā manasaḥ sthiti-nibandhinī // 1.35 //

## 3. Lexical Analysis

| Pada | Form | Force |
|---|---|---|
| *viṣayavatī* | feminine nominative singular | possessing an object-field |
| *vā* | alternative particle | or |
| *pravṛttiḥ* | feminine nominative singular | activity, arising operation |
| *utpannā* | feminine nominative singular participle | having arisen |
| *manasaḥ* | genitive singular | of the mind |
| *sthiti-nibandhinī* | feminine nominative singular | binding or securing steadiness |

## 4. Grammar

*Viṣayavatī*, *utpannā*, and *sthiti-nibandhinī* all qualify
*pravṛttiḥ*. The sūtra says that an arisen, object-bearing activity
secures the mind's steadiness. It does not identify the object-field or
specify an extraordinary sensory experience.

## 5. Translation

### Literal translation

> Or, an arisen activity possessing an object-field secures steadiness of
> the mind.

## 6. Logical Determination

I.35 adds another *vā* to the sequence of practices that answer dispersion.
The emphasis moves from the operations on breath in I.34 to an activity
with determinate content. Its value here lies in establishing *sthiti*;
the text does not yet call it *samāpatti*.

## 7. Interpretive Note

The [Kośa Bhāṣya at VAK 8.01](../../../Vasubandhu/VAK/08-samapatti/VAK_8.01_bhasya.md)
describes *ekāgratā* as one-object-supportedness. This offers a precise
comparison for how content and steadiness can belong together, while
Yoga's *viṣayavatī pravṛtti* remains its own expression. The [I.41
report](../../../Docs/Prajnapada/ys-i-41-fichte-report.md) will take up the
more developed relation of consciousness to grasper, grasping, and grasped
in *samāpatti*.
