# YS I.10 — Nidrā and the Support of Absence

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

> अभावप्रत्ययालम्बना वृत्तिर्निद्रा ॥ १.१०॥

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

> abhāvapratyayālambanā vṛttir nidrā // 1.10 //

## 3. Lexical Analysis

| Pada | Form | Force |
|---|---|---|
| *abhāva* | compound member | absence, nonappearance |
| *pratyaya* | compound member | presentation or cognitive condition |
| *ālambanā* | nominative feminine singular, qualifying *vṛttiḥ* | having as support |
| *vṛttiḥ* | nominative feminine singular | movement of consciousness |
| *nidrā* | nominative feminine singular | sleep |

## 4. Grammar

The compound *abhāva-pratyaya-ālambanā* qualifies *vṛttiḥ*: the movement has an absence-presentation as its support. *Nidrā* is the predicate noun. The sūtra therefore names a definite movement through its support rather than defining sleep by a simple lack of all movement.

## 5. Translation

### Literal translation

> The movement supported by a presentation of absence is sleep.

## 6. Logical Determination

I.10 gives the fourth *vṛtti* from I.6 its own *ālambana*. The movement is supported by *abhāva-pratyaya*, a presentation of absence. The Science must distinguish this movement from the ordinary presentation of a thing, from error, and from word-led construction. Its support makes sleep intelligible as a movement; it does not make the absence a final knowledge of consciousness.

## 7. Interpretive Note

The [Kośa essay](../essays/01-prajna/nidra-and-absorption-according-to-kosa.md) distinguishes dull contraction, wholesome gathering around one *ālambana*, and deliberate cessation. This is useful for our Bhavacakra reading: a daily release from the current object-configuration can become an occasion for renewed determination. The sūtra calls sleep an absence-supported *vṛtti*, so ordinary sleep itself is not thereby a Kośa *samāpatti* or the achievement of samādhi.
