# YS I.33 — Four Cultivations and Clarity of Consciousness

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

> मैत्रीकरुणामुदितोपेक्षाणां सुखदुःखपुण्यापुण्यविषयाणां भावनातश्चित्तप्रसादनम्॥ १.३३॥

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

> maitrī-karuṇā-muditā-upekṣāṇāṃ sukha-duḥkha-puṇya-apuṇya-viṣayāṇāṃ bhāvanātaś citta-prasādanam // 1.33 //

## 3. Lexical Analysis

| Pada | Force |
|---|---|
| *maitrī* | friendliness |
| *karuṇā* | compassion |
| *muditā* | appreciative or sympathetic joy |
| *upekṣā* | equanimity |
| *sukha-duḥkha-puṇya-apuṇya-viṣayāṇām* | taking happiness, suffering, virtue, and non-virtue as their respective fields |
| *bhāvanātaḥ* | through cultivation |
| *citta-prasādanam* | clarification or serene lucidity of consciousness |

## 4. Grammar

The two genitive plurals set out four cultivations and four fields. Their
parallel order supports pairing friendliness with happiness, compassion
with suffering, joy with virtue, and equanimity with non-virtue.
*Bhāvanātaḥ* gives the means; *citta-prasādanam* names its result. The
verse itself does not use the term *apramāṇa*.

## 5. Translation

### Literal translation

> Through cultivating friendliness, compassion, joy, and equanimity with
> regard to happiness, suffering, virtue, and non-virtue, respectively,
> consciousness becomes clear.

## 6. Logical Determination

I.32 proposed practice of one principle against dispersion. I.33 develops
a fourfold practice in the relational field. Its stated result is
*citta-prasādanam*: clarity of consciousness. The later *samāpatti*
sequence at I.41 will specify a further condition of consciousness; this
verse does not yet define it.

## 7. Interpretive Note

The [Kośa Bhāṣya at VAK 8.29–30](../../../Vasubandhu/VAK/08-samapatti/VAK_8.29_bhasya.md)
names the same four cultivations as *apramāṇas*. It makes an immeasurable
multitude of beings their object-support and describes their antidotal
functions. Yoga I.33 instead gives four paired fields and the result of
clarity. [VAK 8.31](../../../Vasubandhu/VAK/08-samapatti/VAK_8.31_bhasya.md)
also says these practices do not themselves sever afflictions, a useful
limit when comparing their effects across the two texts.
