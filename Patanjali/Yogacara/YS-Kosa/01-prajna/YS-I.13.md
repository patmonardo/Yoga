# YS I.13 — Practice for Steadiness

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

> तत्र स्थितौ यत्नोऽभ्यासः ॥ १.१३॥

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

> tatra sthitau yatno 'bhyāsaḥ // 1.13 //

## 3. Lexical Analysis

| Pada | Form | Force |
|---|---|---|
| *tatra* | indeclinable | there, in that matter |
| *sthitau* | locative singular of *sthiti* | in or toward steadiness, stable abiding |
| *yatnaḥ* | nominative masculine singular | effort, exertion |
| *abhyāsaḥ* | nominative masculine singular | practice |

## 4. Grammar

External sandhi gives *yatnaḥ abhyāsaḥ* → *yatno 'bhyāsaḥ*. The two nominatives identify effort as practice, while *sthitau* specifies the state toward which effort is directed. *Tatra* connects this definition to I.12. The sūtra does not say that effort is itself an already achieved steadiness.

## 5. Translation

### Literal translation

> There, practice is effort toward steadiness.

## 6. Logical Determination

I.12 named *abhyāsa* as one means of restraint; I.13 defines it by its aim, *sthiti*. The received Bhāṣya specifies this as the tranquil flow of consciousness free of movements (*cittasyāvṛttikasya praśāntavāhitā sthitiḥ*). Effort (*yatna*), vigor (*vīrya*), and enthusiasm (*utsāha*) are directed to it, and practice is the undertaking of means with the intention to establish it. This is more determinate than repetition alone.

## 7. Interpretive Note

The [Kośa companion essay](../essays/01-prajna/sthiti-and-abhyasa-according-to-kosa.md) tests *sthiti* against the Kośa's debate about continuance of a conditioned stream and *vīrya* against its account of wholesome mental factors. Neither comparison makes Yoga's meditative steadiness a separate Kośa dharma.
