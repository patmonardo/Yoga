# Saṃprajñāta and Kośa Dhyāna

## A Kośa companion essay for Yoga Sūtra I.17

The sūtra names *vitarka*, *vicāra*, *ānanda*, and *asmitā* as forms accompanying *saṃprajñāta* samādhi. The received Yoga Bhāṣya makes their relation exact. *Vitarka* is gross engagement of consciousness with an object-support (*cittasyālambane sthūla ābhogaḥ*); *vicāra* is subtle engagement; *ānanda* is delight; and *asmitā* is unitary awareness (*ekātmikā saṃvit*). The first samādhi has all four; the second lacks *vitarka*; the third also lacks *vicāra*; the fourth is *asmitā* alone. All are *sālambana*, with object-support. The list thus becomes a sequence through which an object-supported samādhi is progressively specified. [Yoga I.17 with Bhāṣya](https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/yoga-sutra-with-bhasya-sanskrit/d/doc1216814.html).

## The Kośa's gross and subtle mental engagement

The Kośabhāṣya asks what distinguishes *vitarka* from *vicāra*. VAK 2.33 answers with *vaudārya* and *sūkṣmatā*, grossness and subtlety, and the explanation says *cittaudārikatā vitarkaḥ; cittasūkṣmatā vicāraḥ*. This is a strong point of contact with the Yoga Bhāṣya's gross and subtle *ābhoga*. Yet their formulations answer different questions. Yoga describes how consciousness engages its support within this samādhi sequence; the Kośa classifies distinct mental factors and then considers how both can occur with one mind. Gross and subtle are also relative to a level, as the Kośa discussion itself notes. Their common vocabulary therefore sharpens the comparison without proving that the two analyses name the same operation in every respect. [Kośabhāṣya, source pp. 60–61](../../../../Vasubandhu/Sources/kosabhasya.txt).

## One object-support, several factors

VAK 8.1 defines attained *dhyāna* as wholesome one-pointedness of mind (*kuśalacittaikāgratā*); its Bhāṣya explains one-pointedness as having a single object-support (*ekālambanatā*). With its entourage, this attainment comprises the five aggregates. The point is more precise than simply calling meditation “focus”: samādhi is a factor by which minds are one-pointed, and its surrounding factors matter. Yoga I.17 likewise closes its fourfold explanation by saying that all four samādhis have object-support. The Kośa's *ekālambanatā* and Yoga's *sālambanatā* are related descriptions, though the former specifies one support and the latter states that support is present. [Kośabhāṣya, VAK 8.1, source pp. 432–33](../../../../Vasubandhu/Sources/kosabhasya.txt); [Yoga I.17 with Bhāṣya](https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/yoga-sutra-with-bhasya-sanskrit/d/doc1216814.html).

## A sequence with its own logic

The Kośa also enumerates factors of four *dhyānas*. The first has *vitarka*, *vicāra*, *prīti*, *sukha*, and one-pointedness or samādhi. The second has inward clarity, *prīti*, *sukha*, and one-pointedness. The third has equanimity, mindfulness, clear comprehension, *sukha*, and samādhi; the fourth has neutral feeling, purified equanimity and mindfulness, and samādhi. In the third list, the Kośa explicitly says that *sthiti* is a synonym of samādhi. Across these levels, factors fall away and others appear while samādhi persists. This makes the Kośa sequence a useful analytic companion to Yoga I.17's successive removals. [Kośabhāṣya, VAK 8.7–8, source pp. 437–38](../../../../Vasubandhu/Sources/kosabhasya.txt).

The resemblance has a limit exactly where it is most tempting. Kośa first *dhyāna* contains *vitarka* and *vicāra*, and second *dhyāna* lacks them both; Yoga's second form, *savicāra*, still retains *vicāra*. Kośa *prīti* and *sukha* are separate factors, whereas Yoga I.17 names *ānanda*. Yoga's fourth is *asmitāmātra*, not the Kośa's fourth-factor list. Four levels in each source do not yield a one-to-one alignment. The jewel here is the method of specification: an attained state becomes intelligible by its object-support, its factors, and the exact transition between their configurations. That reading belongs to both texts as a comparison; their distinct lists remain intact.
