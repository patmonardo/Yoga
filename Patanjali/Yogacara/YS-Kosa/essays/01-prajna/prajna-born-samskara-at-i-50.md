# The Prajñā-Born Saṃskāra

## A Samāpatti Essay on Yoga Sūtra I.50

*Tajjaḥ saṃskāro 'nya-saṃskāra-pratibandhī*: the formation born from **that** Prajñā inhibits other formations. The demonstrative joins I.50 to the truth-bearing, distinct Prajñā of I.48–49. Its insight changes the conditions of future knowing. The sūtra assigns a causal efficacy to the result of practice without making the resulting formation identical with Truth. [YS I.50](../../01-prajna/YS-I.50.md)

Our Saṃskāra Base contains learned dispositions and capacities for further determination. It is not a warehouse of completed objects or finished Principles. When Prajñā sees a Principle, a new formation can support the ability to see accordingly again. Its *pratibandha* checks formations that would reassert a contrary, less discriminating way of taking Form and Essence. This is how the Science of Principles has practical continuity within consciousness.

The [Kośa at VAK 1.02](../../../../Vasubandhu/VAK/01-dhatu/VAK_1.02_bhasya.md) distinguishes cultivated prajñā directed toward attainment from stainless prajñā. It helps frame the question of how cultivation bears on achieved knowing. Yoga's answer here is more specific: a Prajñā-born *saṃskāra* inhibits other *saṃskāras*. We should not substitute a generic theory of memory for that explicit operation.

The power of this formation also sets its limit. If the supporting *saṃskāra* were treated as the Principle itself, the Science would depend forever on a product of its own exercise. I.51's *tasyāpi*—“even of that”—will apply *nirodha* to this privileged formation. Within our reading, that is the sublation of its independent authority. The Truth disclosed through it is not thereby erased.
