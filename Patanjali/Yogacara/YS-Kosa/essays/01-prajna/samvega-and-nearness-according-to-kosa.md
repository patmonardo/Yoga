# Saṃvega and Nearness According to the Kośa

## A Kośa companion essay for Yoga Sūtra I.21

I.21 says *tīvra-saṃvegānām āsannaḥ*: for those of intense urgency, [attainment] is near. The received Bhāṣya supplies the subject: attainment of samādhi and its fruit. It first differentiates practitioners by the strength of their means and of their *saṃvega*. Nearness is therefore not assigned to a feeling in isolation; it belongs to an intensively pursued path. [Yoga I.21 with Bhāṣya](https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/yoga-sutra-with-bhasya-sanskrit/d/doc1216818.html).

The Kośa offers an unexpectedly close phrase in its discussion of the preparatory factors leading to penetration of the truths (*nirvedhabhāgīya*). Its Bhāṣya says that one who has developed these factors at a principal dhyāna level will necessarily see the truths in that very life, *tīvra-saṃvegatvāt*—because of intense urgency. This is not a general equation of excitement with realization. The sentence specifies a developed set of factors, a level of meditative attainment, and a result. Urgency makes the already configured path pressing and effective. [Kośabhāṣya, VAK 6.21, source p. 347](../../../../Vasubandhu/Sources/kosabhasya.txt).

Yoga I.20 had just named the five means culminating in *prajñā*; I.21 adds the degree of their pursuit. Kośa VAK 6.21 places *saṃvega* beside preparatory cultivation and the seeing of truths. The parallel is strong at the level of path logic: intensity and prepared capacity together affect nearness. The endpoints differ: Yoga's Bhāṣya names samādhi and its fruit; the Kośa names seeing the noble truths. I.22 will refine Yoga's gradations further, so the present comparison remains deliberately one verse long.
