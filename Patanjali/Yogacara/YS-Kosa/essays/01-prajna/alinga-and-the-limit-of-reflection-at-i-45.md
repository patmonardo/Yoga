# Aliṅga: The Limit of Reflective Marks

## A Samāpatti Essay on Yoga Sūtra I.45

I.44 moved from the Form work of *vitarka* to *vicāra*, Reflection on subtle *viṣaya*. I.45 gives that movement a terminus: subtle objectivity ends at *aliṅga*. The word matters because a *liṅga* is a mark by which one determination points beyond itself. Reflection can follow such marks toward increasingly subtle conditions; it cannot simply presume that a final condition will be another marked item. [YS I.45](../../01-prajna/YS-I.45.md) · [Vicāra essay](vicara-reflection-and-the-mark-system-at-i-44.md)

Yoga II.19 names four divisions of the guṇas: *viśeṣa*, *aviśeṣa*, *liṅgamātra*, and *aliṅga*. That later sūtra helps locate the terminus within the seen. *Aliṅga* is not a convenient name for the whole of Truth. It is the limit of the marked series being investigated. To mistake it for an already completed Principle would end inquiry precisely where the mark system becomes most revealing. [Yoga source](../../../Sources/Yoga.txt)

Our Central Mark System asks of each mark what it discloses, how its reference is secured, and what makes the transition to its condition legitimate. **Essence Base** is this reflective labor. At *aliṅga*, Essence reaches a boundary in the mode of objectivity it has used. The boundary is philosophically productive: a Principle of reflection must account for the series and its limit. The unmarked endpoint alone cannot do that work by being given a grander name.

The [Kośa on *vitarka* and *vicāra*](../../../../Vasubandhu/VAK/01-dhatu/VAK_1.32_bhasya.md) distinguishes their association with cognition across meditative levels. It gives us reason to keep their operations apart. Yoga supplies the marked and unmarked structure here; the Kośa does not state its four guṇa divisions. In the present project, the Kośa distinction supports the question of how Reflection functions, while I.45 determines the boundary that Reflection must comprehend.

This boundary leads directly to I.46. Even the attainment that reaches *aliṅga* is still among the samāpattis called **seeded**. Its subtle terminus remains a support for knowing. The discovery of that limit is real; its interpretation is still to be completed.
