# Bhava-Pratyaya and Attainment in the Kośa

## A Kośa companion essay for Yoga Sūtra I.19

The Yoga Bhāṣya distinguishes two conditions for the other samādhi of I.18: *bhava-pratyaya*, conditioned by a mode of existence, and *upāya-pratyaya*, conditioned by means. I.19 names the first for *videhas* and *prakṛtilayas*. The commentator says the bodiless gods subsist through their own impressions and pass through a result that feels *like* *kaivalya*. Those absorbed in Prakṛti likewise experience a seeming *kaivalya* until their still-operative consciousness returns. The repeated *iva* matters. The passage marks the difference between resemblance to release and a completed release. [Yoga I.19 with Bhāṣya](https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/yoga-sutra-with-bhasya-sanskrit/d/doc1216816.html).

The Kośa offers a particularly strong comparator in its account of non-percipient attainment (*asaṃjñisamāpatti*) and the non-percipient beings. One enters this attainment through a wish for escape (*niḥsṛtīcchā*), taking it to be a way out. It produces a specified rebirth-result among non-percipient beings. Elsewhere the Bhāṣya says that when the old attainment-impressions are exhausted and no new ones have grown, those beings fall away, like arrows whose momentum is spent. Neither the absence of active cognition nor the intention to escape establishes irreversible liberation. [Kośabhāṣya, VAK 2.41–42, source pp. 68–69](../../../../Vasubandhu/Sources/kosabhasya.txt).

The contrast inside the Kośa is equally important. Its *nirodhasamāpatti* is an attainment of an Ārya, entered with the idea of peaceful abiding and gained through application (*prayoga*). The Kośa explicitly says it is not obtained merely through dispassion. This is a different doctrinal classification from the Yoga Bhāṣya's *bhava-pratyaya* and *upāya-pratyaya*, but it makes the source of attainment a decisive question. [Kośabhāṣya, VAK 2.43, source p. 70](../../../../Vasubandhu/Sources/kosabhasya.txt).

Here is the jewel: the phenomenology of apparent release is insufficient. Ask what produced the state, what impressions sustain it, whether they ripen or expire, and whether its attainment belongs to a liberating path. Yoga answers about *videhas* and *prakṛtilayas* in its own metaphysics; the Kośa answers about non-percipient and cessation attainments through its own path distinctions. The resemblance between the questions sharpens both without making their groups identical.
