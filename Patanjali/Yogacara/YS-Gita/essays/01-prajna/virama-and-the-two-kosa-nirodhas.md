# Virāma and the Kośa's Two Nirodhas

## A Kośa companion essay for Yoga Sūtra I.18

I.17 and I.18 form a compelling pair for reading with the Kośa. The first names *saṃprajñāta* samādhi; its Bhāṣya distinguishes four forms and says each has an object-support (*sālambana*). The second names “the other,” preceded by practice of a presentation of cessation (*virāma-pratyaya*) and leaving *saṃskāras* as residue. Its Bhāṣya calls this *asaṃprajñāta* and says consciousness becomes without support (*nirālambana*). The pair asks what becomes of determinate cognition and its support at cessation. [Yoga I.17 with Bhāṣya](https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/yoga-sutra-with-bhasya-sanskrit/d/doc1216814.html); [Yoga I.18 with Bhāṣya](https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/yoga-sutra-with-bhasya-sanskrit/d/doc1216815.html).

## The Kośa's decisive distinction

VAK 1.5–6 names two *nirodhas* among unconditioned dharmas. *Pratisaṃkhyānirodha* is disjunction (*visaṃyoga*) from outflowing dharmas, attained through *pratisaṃkhyā*: a special discriminative *prajñā* concerning the noble truths. The Bhāṣya insists on distinct disjunctions for distinct bonds. Seeing one affliction cease does not automatically make all further counteractive cultivation unnecessary. [Kośabhāṣya, VAK 1.5–6, source pp. 3–4](../../../../Vasubandhu/Sources/kosabhasya.txt).

*Apratisaṃkhyānirodha* is different: a future dharma is permanently prevented from arising because the necessary conditions are missing (*pratyayavaikalya*), without that discriminative attainment. The Kośa's example is perceptual. When eye and mind are absorbed in one visible form, other forms and other sense-objects pass by; the corresponding cognitions cannot later arise with those past objects as their support. The distinction concerns **how non-arising is secured**, not simply whether an experience currently appears. [Kośabhāṣya, VAK 1.6, source p. 4](../../../../Vasubandhu/Sources/kosabhasya.txt).

The Kośa later reports a dispute over this very causal boundary. One account says insight prevents further arising of afflictions, while subsequent suffering fails to arise because its afflictive cause is absent. An objection replies that even the latter non-arising depends on discrimination; the placement of that effect under *apratisaṃkhyānirodha* is contested. This matters for our reading: the Kośa itself does not offer a simple rule that every later absence is independent of knowledge. [Kośabhāṣya, source p. 92](../../../../Vasubandhu/Sources/kosabhasya.txt).

## The Gītā's two paths

The proposed pairing has a practical analogue in the Gītā. At 5.2 Kṛṣṇa names *saṃnyāsa* and *karma-yoga* together as two ways leading to the highest good, and there prefers *karma-yoga* to the mere renunciation of action. At 3.3, however, his formal “twofold steadfastness” is *jñāna-yoga* for the Sāṃkhyas and *karma-yoga* for the Yogins. These are two formulations of the relation between knowing, doing, and relinquishing, not a Gītā statement about the Kośa's two unconditioned *nirodhas*. [Gītā 5.2](https://www.gitasupersite.iitk.ac.in/srimad?choose=1&etgb=1&etsiva=1&etssa=1&field_chapter_value=5&field_nsutra_value=2&language=dv&setgb=1); [Gītā 3.3](https://www.gitasupersite.iitk.ac.in/srimad?ecsiva=1&etgb=1&etsiva=1&etssa=1&field_chapter_value=3&field_nsutra_value=3&language=dv&scsh=1&setgb=1).

This matters because “the other” in Yoga I.18 need not mean sheer inactivity. Its own wording requires *abhyāsa*, practice; the Gītā likewise calls one who acts without depending on the fruit both a *saṃnyāsin* and a Yogin. Thus action and relinquishment can determine each other within a path. Whether the I.17–18 relation can be described through that Gītā pair must be argued from the Yoga wording, while the Kośa still asks the separate causal question of discrimination versus absent conditions. [Gītā 6.1](https://www.gitasupersite.iitk.ac.in/srimad?choose=1&ecsiva=1&etgb=1&etpurohit=1&etsiva=1&field_chapter_value=6&field_nsutra_value=1&language=dv&scsh=1).

## Testing the proposed I.17–18 correspondence

One can read I.17 as the side of determinate cognition and I.18 as the side on which object-supported presentations no longer arise. That gives the proposed pairing of I.17 with *pratisaṃkhyānirodha* and I.18 with *apratisaṃkhyānirodha* real analytic force. It directs attention to the transition from a supported configuration to the cessation of its movements, and to the role of conditions in any non-arising. This is a **Kośa-guided interpretation** of the Yoga pair, not a pair of technical terms used by Patañjali or the Yoga Bhāṣya.

The first identification requires care. I.17 defines forms of *saṃprajñāta* samādhi; it does not say that their object-supported cognition is disjunction from outflowing dharmas through insight into the noble truths. The latter is the Kośa's particular definition of *pratisaṃkhyānirodha*. Even if the Yoga sequence makes discernment increasingly refined, its four forms are not thereby four instances of that Kośa unconditioned cessation.

The second identification is more demanding. I.18 says *abhyāsa-pūrvaḥ*, preceded by practice, and *saṃskāra-śeṣaḥ*, with latent impressions remaining. Its Bhāṣya explicitly names higher dispassion as the means and describes practice of the objectless *virāma-pratyaya*. Kośa *apratisaṃkhyānirodha*, by definition, is gained through deficient conditions rather than discriminative attainment, and belongs to its class of unconditioned dharmas. The Yoga passage can be compared with the logic of non-arising when support is no longer operative; it cannot be rendered as “cessation without practice” or as the Kośa's unconditioned entity. [Yoga I.18 with Bhāṣya](https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/yoga-sutra-with-bhasya-sanskrit/d/doc1216815.html); [Kośabhāṣya, VAK 1.5–6, source pp. 3–4](../../../../Vasubandhu/Sources/kosabhasya.txt).

## The jewel for the next review

The two texts together give a more exact question than “is there cognition or not?” Yoga asks how practice and higher dispassion lead from the four supported forms to a condition without such support, while latent traces remain. The Kośa asks whether a specific non-arising is attained through discriminative release or through the absence of conditions for a future dharma. Reading I.17–18 again with both questions in hand may reveal a deeper relation between discernment, support, and non-arising. The present essay establishes the textual pressure points; it leaves the proposed identification open for that joint review.
