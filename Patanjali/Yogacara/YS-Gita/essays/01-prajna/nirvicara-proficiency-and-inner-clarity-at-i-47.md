# Nirvicāra Vaiśāradya: Reflection Becomes Clear

## A Samāpatti Essay on Yoga Sūtra I.47

I.46 named the preceding samāpattis *sabīja*. I.47 says that **in proficiency at nirvicārā**, there is *adhyātma-prasāda*, inner clarity. The locative *vaiśāradye* makes proficiency the condition, and I.48 will specify what appears *there*: truth-bearing Prajñā. The verse does not need to name that result in advance. [YS I.47](../../01-prajna/YS-I.47.md)

The skill in question is learned within **Essence Base**. Reflection must follow subtle relations, test its own marks, and recognize their limit at *aliṅga*. A single striking insight is insufficient if the mark cannot be distinguished from what it marks. Proficiency means that this distinction can be sustained in the act itself. The result is an inner field clear enough to receive a Principle without replacing it with Reflection's favored image.

The [Kośa's treatment of prajñā](../../../../Vasubandhu/VAK/01-dhatu/VAK_1.02_bhasya.md) distinguishes cultivation directed toward stainless prajñā from the stainless attainment with its attendants. It supplies a useful path question: how does exercised knowing acquire a different status? Yoga I.47 states its immediate condition in the vocabulary of *nirvicārā* and *prasāda*. We need not identify the two texts' attainment schemes to use that question.

Our claim is that the Principle found in Reflection can now remain visible *as the Principle of reflection*. It is no longer confused with one of the marks used to reach it. This is why clarity follows the labor of I.44–45 rather than bypassing it. The Form Principle discovered at I.43 has not vanished; its relation to the Essence Principle still awaits a determinate Concept.

The [I.48 essay](rtambhara-prajna-at-i-48.md) takes up the next word. *Tatra* will tie Prajñā to this attained clarity and ask what it means for knowing to bear Truth.
