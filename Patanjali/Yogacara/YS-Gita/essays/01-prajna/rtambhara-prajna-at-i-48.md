# Ṛtambharā Prajñā: The Science Bears Truth

## A Samāpatti Essay on Yoga Sūtra I.48

*Ṛtambharā tatra prajñā*: there, in the clarity achieved through *nirvicārā-vaiśāradya*, Prajñā bears truth. The short sūtra makes an exact transition. I.47 gave the condition; I.48 names the knowing that becomes possible under it. I.49 will test its distinct *viṣaya* and *artha*. [YS I.48](../../01-prajna/YS-I.48.md)

For this project, **Prajñā is the Science of Principles**. In the earlier samāpattis a Principle was found in Form and one in Essence. Here their truth has to become explicit in a knowing able to distinguish them and comprehend their relation. Truth-bearing does not mean possession of a finished inventory. It means that the Principle governs the act's determination, so that the act can show why its Form and reflective mediation belong together.

This is why Prajñā cannot be reduced to a fortunate answer. An answer may happen to be correct while the knower remains unable to show the Principle that makes it intelligible. The jewel of I.41 has been trained to take the coloring of grasper, grasping, and grasped; I.48 asks whether this one relation is now clear enough to bear its truth. The discovery remains accountable to the detailed Form and Essence work that prepared it.

The [Kośa at VAK 1.02](../../../../Vasubandhu/VAK/01-dhatu/VAK_1.02_bhasya.md) calls ultimate Abhidharma stainless prajñā together with its attendants. That is a strong comparison for wisdom as an attained complex rather than a solitary abstract thought. Our identification of Yoga's *ṛtambharā prajñā* with a Science of Principles is the Organon determination, not a quotation of Vasubandhu's definition.

The decisive question now becomes **what this Prajñā knows in its own way**. I.49 answers by distinguishing its field from prajñā based on hearing and inference. The distinction must be shown in a determinate *artha*, not in a claim of greater intensity.
