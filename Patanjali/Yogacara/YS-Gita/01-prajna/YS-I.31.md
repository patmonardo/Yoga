# YS I.31 — What Accompanies Dispersion

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

> दुःखदौर्मनस्याङ्गमेजयत्वश्वासप्रश्वासा विक्षेपसहभुवः ॥ १.३१॥

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

> duḥkha-daurmanasya-aṅgamejayatva-śvāsa-praśvāsā vikṣepa-sahabhuvaḥ // 1.31 //

## 3. Lexical Analysis

| Pada | Force |
|---|---|
| *duḥkha* | pain or suffering |
| *daurmanasya* | mental distress or dejection |
| *aṅgamejayatva* | trembling or restlessness of the limbs |
| *śvāsa-praśvāsāḥ* | inhalation and exhalation, here noticed as disturbed breathing |
| *vikṣepa-sahabhuvaḥ* | arising together with dispersion |

## 4. Grammar

The first compound gives four accompanying phenomena. *Vikṣepa-sahabhuvaḥ*
predicates their co-occurrence with *vikṣepa*. The sūtra does not give them
as a second list of obstacles or say that every symptom appears in every
case.

## 5. Translation

### Literal translation

> Pain, mental distress, trembling of the limbs, and inhalation and
> exhalation arise together with dispersion.

## 6. Logical Determination

I.30 named the obstacles as dispersions of consciousness. I.31 describes
what accompanies that condition across feeling, body, and breath. It
therefore deepens the diagnosis before I.32 states a counterpractice. The
immeasurables at I.33 and the *samāpatti* sequence beginning at I.41 are
still ahead.

## 7. Interpretive Note

The [Kośa treatment of feeling at VAK 2.09](../../../Vasubandhu/VAK/02-indriya/VAK_2.09_bhasya.md)
keeps *duḥkha* and *daurmanasya* distinct, which helps preserve the two
terms here. Śaṅkara's [Gītā 6.2
comment](../../../Sankara/BG/BG-C06/gita-6.02.md) calls attachment to
action's fruit a cause of *citta-vikṣepa*. It offers a comparison for
dispersion, not an explanation of all four accompaniments in I.31.
