# YS I.51 — Seedless Samādhi

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

> तस्यापि निरोधे सर्वनिरोधान्निर्बीजः समाधिः ॥ १.५१॥

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

> tasyāpi nirodhe sarva-nirodhān nirbījaḥ samādhiḥ // 1.51 //

## 3. Lexical Analysis

| Pada | Form | Force |
|---|---|---|
| *tasya api* | genitive singular with inclusion | even of that prajñā-born saṃskāra |
| *nirodhe* | locative singular | upon its nirodha; in our reading, sublation |
| *sarva-nirodhāt* | ablative singular | because of the nirodha of all |
| *nirbījaḥ samādhiḥ* | masculine nominative singular | samādhi without seed |

## 4. Grammar

The genitive points to the saṃskāra of I.50. The locative gives
the condition, and the ablative gives the ground for the predicate
*nirbījaḥ samādhiḥ*. The sūtra includes even the privileged
truth-born saṃskāra in the scope of nirodha.

## 5. Translation

### Literal translation

> Upon the nirodha even of that, because of the nirodha of all,
> there is seedless samādhi.

## 6. Logical Determination

I.50's prajñā-born saṃskāra inhibited other formations. I.51
subordinates even that formation to *nirodha*. The chapter ends
with *nirbīja samādhi*: knowing is no longer dependent on a seed
to hold its achieved truth.

## 7. Interpretive Note

In our Science of Principles, *nirodha* is sublation: the result
of the prajñā-born formation is preserved without treating that
formation as the final Principle. The [Kośa VAK 8.01 Bhāṣya](../../../Vasubandhu/VAK/08-samapatti/VAK_8.01_bhasya.md)
analyzes samāpatti through its object-support and factors; Yoga's
I.51 names the limit of dependence upon such seed. The
[Fichte report](../../../Docs/Prajnapada/ys-i-51-fichte-report.md)
develops the larger absolute-knowledge reading.
