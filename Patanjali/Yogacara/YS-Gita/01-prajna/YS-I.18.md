# YS I.18 — The Other Samādhi

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

> विरामप्रत्ययाभ्यासपूर्वः संस्कारशेषोऽन्यः ॥ १.१८॥

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

> virāma-pratyaya-abhyāsa-pūrvaḥ saṃskāra-śeṣo 'nyaḥ // 1.18 //

## 3. Lexical Analysis

| Pada | Form | Force |
|---|---|---|
| *virāma* | compound member | cessation, stopping |
| *pratyaya* | compound member | cognition or presentation; here a presentation of cessation |
| *abhyāsa* | compound member | repeated practice |
| *pūrvaḥ* | masculine nominative singular | having that practice as antecedent |
| *saṃskāra-śeṣaḥ* | masculine nominative singular | with latent impressions remaining |
| *anyaḥ* | masculine nominative singular | the other [samādhi] |

## 4. Grammar

*Virāma-pratyaya-abhyāsa* forms the antecedent specified by *-pūrvaḥ*. Both *pūrvaḥ* and *saṃskāra-śeṣaḥ* qualify *anyaḥ*, the other samādhi relative to I.17's *saṃprajñātaḥ*. The sūtra does not itself say *asaṃprajñāta* or *nirbīja*; the received Bhāṣya supplies those names.

## 5. Translation

### Literal translation

> The other [samādhi] is preceded by practice of a presentation of cessation and has only latent impressions remaining.

## 6. Logical Determination

The Bhāṣya calls this *asaṃprajñāta samādhi*: when all the movements of consciousness have subsided, restraint remains with *saṃskāras* as residue. It names higher dispassion as its means and explains that an object-supported practice cannot simply produce the unsupported condition. Practice of the *virāma-pratyaya*, lacking an object, precedes consciousness becoming, as it were, without support. The residue prevents “cessation” from meaning that every causal trace has already vanished.

## 7. Interpretive Note

The [Kośa companion essay](../essays/01-prajna/virama-and-the-two-kosa-nirodhas.md) tests I.17–18 against *pratisaṃkhyānirodha* and *apratisaṃkhyānirodha*. The comparison turns on how cessation is reached and what cannot arise; it does not treat Yoga's meditative state as one of the Kośa's unconditioned dharmas.
