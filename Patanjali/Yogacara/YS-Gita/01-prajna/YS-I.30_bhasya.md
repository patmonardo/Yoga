# YS I.30 — Our Bhāṣya

## 1. Sūtra Anchor

> व्याधिस्त्यानसंशयप्रमादालस्याविरतिभ्रान्तिदर्शनालब्धभूमिकत्वानवस्थितत्वानि चित्तविक्षेपास्तेऽन्तरायाः ॥ १.३०॥

> *vyādhi-styāna-saṃśaya-pramāda-ālasya-avirati-bhrānti-darśana-alabdha-bhūmikatva-anavasthitatvāni citta-vikṣepās te 'ntarāyāḥ* — These dispersions of consciousness are the obstacles.

## 2. Commentary

I.29 promised both inward realization and the absence of obstacles. I.30
specifies the latter by naming nine ways consciousness can be dispersed.
Illness can disrupt practice through the body; stagnation, heedlessness, and
laziness weaken its exercise; doubt and mistaken seeing disturb its
determination; non-withdrawal pulls it toward other objects. Even progress
has two distinct vulnerabilities: a ground may not be attained, or it may
be attained without stable establishment.

The [Kośa Bhāṣya at VAK 5.44](../../../Vasubandhu/VAK/05-anusaya/VAK_5.44_bhasya.md)
calls unwillingness to go, taking a wrong path, and doubt about the path
obstacles to liberation, using the image of travel. This gives a useful
comparison for Yoga's doubt, mistaken seeing, and failure of progress. It
does not identify those three Yoga terms with the Kośa's three. [VAK
5.47](../../../Vasubandhu/VAK/05-anusaya/VAK_5.47.md) also names *styāna*
as an enveloping affliction, making the shared term more precise than a
general parallel about distraction.

[Gītā 4.40](../../../Sankara/BG/BG-C04/gita-4.40.md) describes a person
whose mind is given over to doubt; Śaṅkara relates that doubt to failure
of trust in teaching and teacher. The comparison makes *saṃśaya* a serious
obstacle to undertaking knowledge, while Yoga's list gives it a specific
place among the nine dispersions.

I.28's *artha-bhāvanā* thus faces determinate impediments, rather than a
single vague distraction. In the [Māṇḍūkya
study](../Yogavidya/Prajnapada/mandukya-om-artha-study.md), sustained
attention to OṂ's meaning provides one concrete instance of the work that
these impediments can interrupt. I.31 will describe their accompanying
symptoms; I.32 will state a practice directed against them.

## 3. Project Determination

The nine obstacles mark points at which the movement from transmitted
expression to realized meaning can break down. Our Buddha as Īśvara reading
does not erase their differences. It asks how the teaching becomes active in
consciousness despite each way it can be dispersed.
