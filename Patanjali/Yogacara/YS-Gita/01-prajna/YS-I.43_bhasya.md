# YS I.43 — Our Bhāṣya

## 1. Sūtra Anchor

> स्मृतिपरिशुद्धौ स्वरूपशून्येवार्थमात्रनिर्भासा निर्वितर्का ॥ १.४३॥

> *smṛti-pariśuddhau svarūpa-śūnyevārtha-mātra-nirbhāsā nirvitarkā* — When memory is purified, samāpatti seems empty of its own form and *artha* alone shines: nirvitarkā.

## 2. Commentary

I.42 named the mixture of word, *artha*, and knowledge. I.43 gives
its first clarification: memory is purified. The inherited name and
recognized form no longer decide in advance what the impure *artha*
can disclose.
The sūtra says the attainment is *as though* empty of its own form;
the comparison matters because the activity of knowing has not
vanished. What shines is *artha-mātra*: the impure Objective Artha now freed
from domination by its remembered name. The object implicated in it
has not thereby become a pure object.

The [Kośa's VAK 1.31](../../../Vasubandhu/VAK/01-dhatu/VAK_1.31_bhasya.md)
distinguishes the final three mental and dharma domains as capable
of being with or without outflows. That distinction helps us ask
what sort of cognition is operating here. It does not turn every
wordless presentation into ultimate truth. Yoga gives a disciplined
criterion in *smṛti-pariśuddhi* and *artha-mātra-nirbhāsa*.

## 3. Project Determination

In our account, *nirvitarkā* lets *vijñāna* find the Principle of the
Form Base determination. Purified memory lets that determination be
investigated without the retained name dictating its result. This is
the first Principle found in the seeded samāpatti movement; it does
not yet complete the Essence Base reflection opened by I.44. The
[companion essay](../essays/01-prajna/savitarka-nirvitarka-and-the-three-bases.md)
develops the transition.
