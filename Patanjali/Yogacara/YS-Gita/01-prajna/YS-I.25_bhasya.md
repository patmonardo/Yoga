# YS I.25 — Our Bhāṣya

## 1. Sūtra Anchor

> तत्र निरतिशयं सर्वज्ञबीजम्॥ १.२५॥

> *tatra niratiśayaṃ sarvajña-bījam* — In that one, the seed of omniscience is unsurpassed.

## 2. Commentary

*Tatra* keeps I.25 with the Īśvara of I.24. The previous sūtra determined
freedom from *kleśa*, *karma*, *vipāka*, and *āśaya*; this one determines a
relation to knowledge. It calls the *sarvajña-bījam* unsurpassed. It does not
yet say that knowledge is a stock of facts, nor does it spell out how the
seed becomes manifest.

In [Gītā 15.17](../../../Sankara/BG/BG-C15/gita-15.17.md), the verse names
the imperishable supreme *puruṣa* as *īśvara*. Śaṅkara explicitly calls him
*sarvajña*. This is a strong verbal and conceptual contact with I.24–25.
Śaṅkara also reads this Lord as sustaining the three worlds; that further
claim belongs to the Gītā passage and need not be inserted into I.25.

The [Kośa Bhāṣya, 467|14–467|21](../../../Vasubandhu/Sources/kosabhasya.txt),
answers an objection that no single moment of mind knows everything. It says
the Buddha is called *sarvajña* because his continuum has the capacity for
undistorted knowledge of whatever he turns to. This shows that omniscience
need not mean one simultaneous inventory of all objects. It also gives a
different account of its basis from the sūtra's *puruṣa-viśeṣa*. We can put
the accounts in conversation without erasing that difference.

Read as Prajñāpāda, the verse gives dedication an orientation toward
unsurpassed knowing. The seed is named here; the following sūtra will name
Īśvara as teacher even of the earlier teachers. That order matters: the
claim about teaching follows the claim about knowledge.

## 3. Project Determination

The sūtra gives *sarvajña-bījam* as a principle of knowing associated with
Īśvara. Its power for our Science of Principles is a question for continued
reading, not an already completed equation with the Gītā or Kośa accounts.
