# YS I.40 — Mastery from the Smallest to the Greatest

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

> परमाणु परममहत्त्वान्तोऽस्य वशीकारः ॥ १.४०॥

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

> paramāṇu parama-mahattvānto 'sya vaśīkāraḥ // 1.40 //

## 3. Lexical Analysis

| Pada | Form | Force |
|---|---|---|
| *paramāṇu* | compound member | smallest atom; lower limit of scale |
| *parama-mahattva-antaḥ* | masculine nominative singular | reaching the limit of greatest magnitude |
| *asya* | genitive singular | of this practitioner or consciousness in practice |
| *vaśīkāraḥ* | masculine nominative singular | mastery, command |

## 4. Grammar

*Vaśīkāraḥ* is the subject; *paramāṇu parama-mahattvāntaḥ* describes
its range. *Asya* marks whose mastery is at issue. The sūtra gives the
limits of a capacity, without naming a new method or the *samāpatti* that
follows in I.41.

## 5. Translation

### Literal translation

> This practitioner's mastery extends from the smallest atom to the
> greatest magnitude.

## 6. Logical Determination

The *vā* alternatives end at I.39. I.40 states their achieved range as
*vaśīkāra*. I.41 then describes *samāpatti* through the jewel and the
grasper, grasping, and grasped. The change from alternative practices to
mastery makes I.40 the threshold of that sequence.

## 7. Interpretive Note

The [Kośa Bhāṣya at VAK 8.01](../../../Vasubandhu/VAK/08-samapatti/VAK_8.01_bhasya.md)
defines attained *samāpatti* as wholesome one-pointedness, explains
one-pointedness through one object-support, and includes the accompanying
five aggregates in the fuller account. This clarifies why mastery cannot
be reduced to bare fixation. The Kośa does not itself translate Yoga's
range from *paramāṇu* to *parama-mahattva*. A fuller comparison belongs to
the [Yogavidya essay](../Yogavidya/Prajnapada/ys-i-40-range-and-samapatti.md).
