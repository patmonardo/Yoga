# YS I.2 — Our Bhāṣya

## 1. Sūtra Anchor

> योगश्चित्तवृत्तिनिरोधः ॥ १.२॥

> *yogaś cittavṛttinirodhaḥ* — Yoga is the Science of Consciousness.

## 2. Commentary

The Science takes consciousness in its determinate movements. Its first task is to know those movements and their *nirodha*: their sublation within the whole relation of knowing. This is why I.5–11 must distinguish the movements before I.12 gives the means by which their sublation is cultivated. I.3–4 first show what is at stake for the seer.

The Kośa gives a powerful way to inspect the target. VAK 2.23 says *citta* and its associated factors cannot arise apart; VAK 2.34 specifies common support, object, and aspect in their association. A movement can therefore be examined as a determinate configuration of cognition and factors rather than a free-standing item in a list. This is an analytic comparison, not a claim that Yoga has adopted the Kośa's inventory. [Kośabhāṣya, VAK 2.23, 2.34](../../../Vasubandhu/Sources/kosabhasya.txt).

Kośa VAK 1.6 distinguishes a disjunction attained through discriminative *prajñā* from non-arising when conditions are missing. This is a useful comparison of two specific Kośa operations. It does not determine what Yoga means by the sublation of *citta-vṛttis*. [Kośabhāṣya, VAK 1.6](../../../Vasubandhu/Sources/kosabhasya.txt).

## 3. Project Determination

“Yoga is the Science of Consciousness” is our leading translation and the governing determination of this Bhāṣya. The Sanskrit compound can be glossed as “the sublation of the movements of consciousness.” The Science must show what a movement is, how it claims authority, and how that claim is sublated. Kośa analysis assists the first question; Yoga's sequence must answer the whole.

## 4. Review Status

Revised opening determination. Read I.3–4 as the first contrast and I.5–12 as the analysis and cultivation of sublation.
