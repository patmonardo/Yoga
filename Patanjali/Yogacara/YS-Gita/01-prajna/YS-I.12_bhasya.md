# YS I.12 — Our Bhāṣya

## 1. Sūtra Anchor

> अभ्यासवैराग्याभ्यां तन्निरोधः ॥ १.१२॥

> *abhyāsavairāgyābhyāṃ tannirodhaḥ* — Their sublation is through practice and dispassion.

## 2. Commentary

The five movements have been distinguished; I.12 asks how their *nirodha* is cultivated. Practice and dispassion are given in the instrumental dual. Practice makes the investigation of each movement steady; dispassion releases the claim by which a movement would rule the whole field. The two means work together in the Science of Consciousness.

The Kośa distinguishes *pratisaṃkhyānirodha*, disjunction attained through discriminative *prajñā*, from *apratisaṃkhyānirodha*, non-arising when conditions are missing. These are two unconditioned dharmas in the Kośa; Yoga I.12 gives two means for the sublation of *citta-vṛttis*. The [companion essay](../essays/01-prajna/nirodha-according-to-kosa.md) compares the passages without identifying their terms.

## 3. Project Determination

The Kośa's discriminative disjunction sharpens the question of what knowing achieves through practice; its condition-dependent non-arising sharpens the question of what no longer sustains a movement. Yoga answers in its own sequence: the five operations have been distinguished, and *abhyāsa* with *vairāgya* is now named as their means of sublation. The relation of those means must be further developed at I.13–16.
