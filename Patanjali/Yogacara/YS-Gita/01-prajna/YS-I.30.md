# YS I.30 — The Obstacles as Dispersions of Consciousness

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

> व्याधिस्त्यानसंशयप्रमादालस्याविरतिभ्रान्तिदर्शनालब्धभूमिकत्वानवस्थितत्वानि चित्तविक्षेपास्तेऽन्तरायाः ॥ १.३०॥

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

> vyādhi-styāna-saṃśaya-pramāda-ālasya-avirati-bhrānti-darśana-alabdha-bhūmikatva-anavasthitatvāni citta-vikṣepās te 'ntarāyāḥ // 1.30 //

## 3. Lexical Analysis

| Pada | Force |
|---|---|
| *vyādhi* | illness |
| *styāna* | mental stagnation or torpor |
| *saṃśaya* | doubt |
| *pramāda* | heedlessness |
| *ālasya* | laziness |
| *avirati* | failure to withdraw from attachment |
| *bhrānti-darśana* | mistaken seeing |
| *alabdha-bhūmikatva* | not having attained a ground |
| *anavasthitatva* | inability to remain established |
| *citta-vikṣepāḥ* | dispersions of consciousness |
| *te 'ntarāyāḥ* | those are obstacles |

## 4. Grammar

The nine terms form the opening list, ending in the neuter plural
*-tvāni*. *Citta-vikṣepāḥ* and *antarāyāḥ* predicate what this list amounts
to: dispersions of consciousness, hence obstacles. The last two items
distinguish failing to reach a ground from failing to remain there.

## 5. Translation

### Literal translation

> Illness, mental stagnation, doubt, heedlessness, laziness, failure to
> withdraw, mistaken seeing, failure to attain a ground, and failure to
> remain established: these dispersions of consciousness are the obstacles.

## 6. Logical Determination

I.29 announced the absence of obstacles as a result of the practice of
meaning. I.30 now names the obstacles. The list covers bodily condition,
energy and attention, judgment, attachment, and stability on the path. The
common predicate is *citta-vikṣepa*, not a claim that all nine have one cause.

## 7. Interpretive Note

The [Kośa Bhāṣya on VAK 5.44](../../../Vasubandhu/VAK/05-anusaya/VAK_5.44_bhasya.md)
analyzes unwillingness, deviation, and doubt as obstacles to traversing the
path; [VAK 5.47](../../../Vasubandhu/VAK/05-anusaya/VAK_5.47.md) includes
*styāna* among enveloping afflictions. These are useful comparisons, not a
second enumeration of Yoga's nine. Śaṅkara's [Gītā 4.40
comment](../../../Sankara/BG/BG-C04/gita-4.40.md) treats doubt as an
obstacle to knowledge. Each source gives *saṃśaya* a distinct setting.
