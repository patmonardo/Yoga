# YS I.36 — Our Bhāṣya

## 1. Sūtra Anchor

> विशोका वा ज्योतिष्मती ॥ १.३६॥

> *viśokā vā jyotiṣmatī* — Or, [an activity] free from sorrow and luminous.

## 2. Commentary

The feminine forms can carry forward *pravṛttiḥ* from I.35. There an
arisen activity with an object-field steadied the mind; here the offered
activity is characterized as *viśokā* and *jyotiṣmatī*. The two
determinations belong together. It is free from sorrow and luminous, not
merely bright in an unspecified sense.

The [Kośa Bhāṣya at VAK 5.59](../../../Vasubandhu/VAK/05-anusaya/VAK_5.59_bhasya.md)
calls *ālokasaṃjñā*, a conception of light, a countercondition to the
sinking of mind associated with *styāna* and *middha*. This is a useful
practical comparison after Yoga's *styāna* at I.30. It does not settle
whether the luminosity of I.36 is a visual appearance or a mode of
knowing.

[Gītā 13.17](../../../Sankara/BG/BG-C13/gita-13.17.md) speaks of a
“light of lights” beyond darkness. Śaṅkara explains this through the light
of consciousness by which other lights appear. That is a stronger
metaphysical determination than I.36 states. It can guide our Organon
question about intelligibility, while Yoga's brief alternative retains
its own wording.

The sequence continues toward *samāpatti*, where I.41 will describe the
relation of grasper, grasping, and grasped. Our Principle Base, Essence
Base, and Form Base reading treats them as moments of one rational
process. I.36 may contribute the theme of luminosity to that reading, but
it has not yet supplied the threefold account.

## 3. Project Determination

Luminosity here can be studied as the clarity in which a determinate
activity becomes accessible without sorrow's disturbance. This is a
provisional Organon reading of the two adjectives, to be tested against
the later *samāpatti* sequence.
