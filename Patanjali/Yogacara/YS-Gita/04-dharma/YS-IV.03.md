# YS IV.3 — The Occasion and the Barrier

## 1. Sanskrit (Devanāgarī)

> निमित्तमप्रयोजकं प्रकृतीनां वरणभेदस्तु ततः क्षेत्रिकवत्॥ ४.३॥

## 2. Sanskrit (IAST)

> nimittam aprayojakaṃ prakṛtīnāṃ varaṇa-bhedas tu tataḥ kṣetrikavat // 4.3 //

## 3. Lexical Analysis

| Pada | Form | Force in this sūtra |
|---|---|---|
| *nimittam* | nominative neuter singular | occasion or contributing condition |
| *aprayojakam* | nominative neuter singular | not an impeller of the transformation |
| *prakṛtīnām* | genitive plural | of the powers of nature |
| *varaṇa-bhedaḥ* | nominative masculine singular | opening or breaking of a barrier |
| *tu* | contrastive particle | marks the occasion's actual office |
| *tataḥ* | ablative adverb | from that occasion |
| *kṣetrikavat* | indeclinable comparison | like a farmer tending a field |

## 4. Grammar

*Nimittam* is the subject of the first nominal clause; *aprayojakam* is its predicate, with *prakṛtīnām* specifying what it does not impel. The second nominal clause gives the positive determination: *varaṇa-bhedaḥ tu tataḥ*, yet the opening of the barrier is from that occasion. The farmer comparison explains the restricted causal role. The plural *prakṛtīnām* must not be lost in a singular paraphrase of nature.

## 5. Translation

### Close translation

> The occasion does not impel the powers of nature; from it comes, rather, the opening of their barrier, as with a farmer.

### Yogavidyā reading

> The farmer opens a channel, but the water flows by its own power. Likewise, the occasion removes an obstruction to transformation; it does not supply nature's power to transform.

## 6. Logical Determination

IV.2 locates transformation into another kind in *prakṛty-āpūra*. IV.3 determines what an apparent external cause contributes to that genesis. The occasion has an effective, limited office: it breaks a barrier. The power thereby released belongs to the *prakṛtis*. Denying that the occasion impels them does not deny that an opening was required.

The [Kośa Bhāṣya at VAK 4.04](../../../../Vasubandhu/Organon/04-karma/VAK_4.04_bhasya.md) offers a useful causal distinction within its Sautrāntika reply: the completion of an action can be the *nimitta* for transformation of the agent's continuum, without requiring a separate new dharma to carry that result. Yoga's farmer is not describing karmic completion, but both analyses require us to locate the transformation in the relevant continuum or power rather than crediting the occasion with the whole result.

## 7. Interpretive Note

The [Fichte IV.3 report](../../../Yogavidya/Dharmapada/ys-iv-3-fichte-report.md) extends the farmer's distinction into the Science of Knowing. Philosophical reflection can remove an obstruction to insight; it cannot produce the living ground whose self-disclosure is known. This is the genetic question opened by IV.2, now made exact by IV.3's division between occasion, barrier, and power.
