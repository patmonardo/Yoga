# YS IV.2 — Our Bhāṣya

## 1. Sūtra Anchor

> जात्यन्तरपरिणामः प्रकृत्यापूरात्॥ ४.२॥

> *jātyantara-pariṇāmaḥ prakṛty-āpūrāt* — Transformation into another kind comes from nature's filling out.

## 2. Commentary

The sūtra states a genesis, not a catalogue of kinds. *Jātyantara* is the determinate difference achieved; *pariṇāma* is its becoming; *prakṛty-āpūra* names the fullness from which that becoming proceeds. The new kind cannot be explained by merely pointing to its completed appearance. We must show what makes the transformation possible.

The [Kośa kārikā VAK 4.01](../../../../Vasubandhu/Organon/04-karma/VAK_4.01.md) gives a precise comparison: *karmajaṃ lokavaicitryam*, the world's differentiation is born from karma. Its [Bhāṣya](../../../../Vasubandhu/Organon/04-karma/VAK_4.01_bhasya.md) specifies both the world of beings and the receptacle-world, then distinguishes volition itself from bodily and verbal action produced through it. Thus the Kośa does more than place a formed world before us: it asks how differentiated existence is generated. Yoga IV.2 also asks for genesis, while naming *prakṛti's* filling out rather than *cetanā* as the immediate ground of its claim.

The difference is sharpest in the [Loka Bhāṣya at VAK 3.50](../../../../Vasubandhu/Organon/03-loka/VAK_3.50_bhasya.md). Vasubandhu permits *pariṇāma* for conditioned factors producing another kind of effect, yet challenges a Sāṃkhya account in which an enduring substance bears successive properties. We cannot use his objection as if it were Yoga's explanation, or assume that Yoga's *prakṛti* is the inert bearer he attacks. The two accounts meet over a real problem: what persists, what changes, and what warrants saying that a different kind has arisen?

## 3. Project Determination

Fichte's [Science of Knowing reading of IV.2](../../../Yogavidya/Dharmapada/ys-iv-2-fichte-report.md) leads our Yogavidyā Bhāṣya. If the ground is living and complete, its determinate appearances must be comprehended genetically from that ground. The sūtra gives the first concise formula for that inquiry: a new kind through the filling out of nature. Kośa's analysis of karma-born world differentiation and its critique of a merely abiding bearer give the inquiry necessary tests. They do not set the final limits of Yoga's Absolute Reflection.

This begins Dharmapāda as the Science of an impure Dharma's genesis and disclosure. The relation to pure Prajñā and Absolute Dharma is the horizon of the chapter, not a result already established by IV.2. IV.3 will distinguish a contributing condition from the productive fullness named here.
