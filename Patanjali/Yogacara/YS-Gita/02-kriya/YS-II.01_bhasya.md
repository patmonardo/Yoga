# YS II.1 — Our Bhāṣya, first working draft

This is the first pass of **our** commentary on the sūtra. The chapter is **Kriyā-pāda** in our study. We will build this reading directly from the sūtra sequence and the latest `Vasubandhu/Organon` studies, then develop a separate Organon formulation. This draft does not attribute our two-path thesis to the wording of II.1.

## 1. Sūtra Anchor

> तपःस्वाध्यायेश्वरप्रणिधानानि क्रियायोगः ॥ २.१॥

> *tapaḥsvādhyāyeśvarapraṇidhānāni kriyāyogaḥ*

> Discipline, self-study, and dedication to Īśvara are yoga of action.

## 2. Commentary

The question is how yoga is undertaken where consciousness is still affected by the movements that yoga seeks to govern. The answer begins with *kriyā*: an ordered practice. The three terms specify that practice through discipline, study, and dedication. They must be held together because the sūtra gives them one name, *kriyāyogaḥ*.

*Tapas* names the exertion and restraint by which practice gains force. *Svādhyāya* gives practice a relation to teaching and examination; effort alone cannot decide what it ought to cultivate. *Īśvara-praṇidhāna* directs practice beyond the practitioner's attachment to its own performance. This account of their relation is our interpretive proposal. The sūtra itself names the three members and their unity, without spelling out their mutual operation.

II.2 will give the first explicit test of this proposal: this yoga is for cultivating samādhi and attenuating the kleśas. If our reading of any member cannot explain its role in those two purposes, we should revise it.

## 3. Project Determination

I.17 explicitly names *saṃprajñāta*; I.18 introduces an *anyaḥ*, an “other” mode. Our working name for the latter is *asaṃprajñāta*. Kriyā-pāda must be able to explain the work of practice with respect to both, rather than treating cessation as the sole scientific end and articulated knowing as a disposable preliminary. This is our two-path thesis to test through the sūtras, beginning with the purpose stated in II.2.

The Kośa provides a methodological provocation, not a direct equation between Buddhist paths and these two modes of samādhi. The latest Organon study at `Vasubandhu/Organon/06-marga/VAK_6.23.md` distinguishes Śrāvaka, Pratyekabuddha, and Buddha/Bodhisattva trajectories; `VAK_6.67.md` distinguishes their modes of awakening while retaining a common Path-system. Those findings warn us against making one trajectory exhaust the science. Our criticism of an Arhat-only reading of Yoga, including the reading we associate with Vyāsa, is our interpretive position; it is not stated by II.1 or proved merely by the Kośa's classifications.

For this first effort, the operative question is precise: what does each member of *kriyāyoga* do for the cultivation of knowing, for cessation, and for their relation? The later Organon account should express the determinations won by that reading, not supply answers before the sūtras have been studied.
