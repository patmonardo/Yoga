# Beginningless Vasana and the Absolutely Unconditioned

## Yoga Sutra IV.10 with Hegel

`tasam anaditvam ca-asisa nityatvat`

> These Vāsanās are beginningless because the wish to continue is permanent.

Fichte identified the persistent wish with the should that demands a new
genesis of seeing. Hegel asks whether this beginningless circle is therefore
absolute.

Ground and condition initially presuppose one another:

```text
Vāsanā supplies the ground of manifest desire
manifest desire supplies the condition for renewed Vāsanā
```

No first member can be found inside the circle. Every condition refers to a
ground, and every determinate ground presupposes conditions. This explains
`anaditva` without requiring an eternal mental substance.

But Hegel's **absolutely unconditioned** is not an infinite first cause placed
before the series. It is the whole movement in which ground and condition lose
their apparent independence and are recognized as moments of one fact.

The wish to continue is `nitya` so long as each conditioned identity treats
its own continuation as the ground of another conditioned identity. Its
beginninglessness describes the closure of the reproductive circle, not the
ultimate truth of Citta.

Hegel therefore adds a decisive correction:

> What has no beginning within a conditioned circle need not be unconditioned.

The absolutely unconditioned is reached when the complete unity of ground and
conditions comes into view. Then the circle can proceed into existence as one
fact—or, if the factors collecting a Vāsanā are absent, that Vāsanā need not
arise.

This prepares IV.11 without deciding it. Eka Citta is not threatened. The
question is whether the supposedly permanent Vāsanā-series has any standing
apart from the conditions through which it is collected.
