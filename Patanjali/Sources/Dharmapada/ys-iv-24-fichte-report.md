# Citta Works for a Higher Artha

## A report on Yoga Sūtra IV.24 and Fichte

## The Sūtra

`tad asaṃkhyeya-vāsanābhiś citram api parārthaṃ saṃhatya-kāritvāt`

### Simple translation

> Although Citta is variegated by countless Vāsanās, it works for a higher
> Artha, because it acts only through combination.

The important terms are:

- `asaṃkhyeya-vāsanābhiḥ`: by countless Vāsanās;
- `citram api`: although manifold, variegated, or patterned;
- `parārtham`: for another, for a purpose beyond itself, or for a higher
  Artha;
- `saṃhatya-kāritvāt`: because it acts by combination, assembly, or the
  cooperation of parts.

The Sūtra does not deny the reality of the manifold Citta. It denies that
this manifold construction is its own final Principle.

```text
countless Vāsanās
        ↓
variegated Citta
        ↓ works through combination
cannot be its own final ground
        ↓
works for a higher Artha
```

## Why this follows IV.23

IV.23 established that Citta, colored by the Seer and the seen, bears every
Artha. IV.24 now asks whether this universally receptive Citta is itself the
ultimate knower.

Its answer is no. Citta bears every Artha, but it does so through a manifold
construction shaped by innumerable Vāsanās. What operates only through such
a combination operates for an end that cannot be reduced to any one of its
constituents.

```text
IV.23                            IV.24

Citta bears every Artha         Citta is not the final Artha
through the complete            because its activity is
Seer–seeing–seen relation       composite and purposive
```

## Vāsanā and the constructed Cittas

Vāsanās run through the constructed Cittas and give their activity its
particular pattern. They are not bizarre additional creatures living inside
one mind. They are the tendencies through which the cognitive manifold is
repeatedly organized.

```text
Vāsanā 1 ─┐
Vāsanā 2 ─┼─► constructed pattern of Manas and Citta
Vāsanā 3 ─┤
   ...    ─┘
```

This explains `citram`: Citta is colored not only by its present object but
by an incalculable history of formative tendencies. When Manas encounters an
object, these formations condition what is selected, connected, remembered,
and pursued.

Yet the pattern is not the Principle that knows the pattern.

## `Saṃhatya-kāritva`: working through combination

The composite character of Citta is not a defect. It is the condition under
which determinate cognition becomes possible.

```text
sense presentation
      +
Manas synthesis
      +
memory and Vāsanā
      +
the Seer–seen relation
      ↓
determinate cognition
```

But a combination does not explain the Principle of its combination. Its
parts can perform their assigned functions without knowing the Artha of the
whole. IV.24 therefore passes from the assembled cognitive mechanism to the
Principle for which it operates.

This is the strong meaning of `parārtha`.

## `Parārtha` without an external spectator

The conventional reading makes the composite Citta exist for an entirely
separate Puruṣa, as a bed exists for the person who uses it. That argument
correctly sees that the composite is not self-grounding. It becomes
misleading when its purpose is converted into an external substance that
stands outside cognition and merely watches it.

Here `para` names transcendence of the composite level:

```text
not:

an external Puruṣa watches the Citta-machine


but:

constructed Citta ── operates for ──► Buddhi's higher Artha
```

Buddhi is pāramārthika: it is the Principle of the Manas–object relation.
Manas and the constructed Cittas are its determinate, transactional
operation. Their purpose is fulfilled when Buddhi knows the law of their
construction.

Puruṣa can therefore remain a Spectral Tattva: the invariant Seer-Aspect of
the relation. It need not become a second mind receiving products from
Citta.

## Fichte's Bhāṣya: the return of the `should`

Fichte now observes that a hypothetical `should` has returned. It joins a
conditioning term to a conditioned term:

> Given a Principle for self-genesis, knowing's enduring Being should
> follow.

Earlier this `should` belonged to merely apparent or ordinary knowing. It
connected terms while silently assuming its premise. Fichte had therefore
seemed to leave it behind.

It now returns at the transcendental level, but with a different task:

```text
ordinary `should`                 transcendental `should`

joins given terms                derives their connection
from a tacit premise             from a genetically grounded premise
```

The first is an operation within the constructed Citta. The second is
Buddhi's comprehension of the Principle of that construction.

## The middle ground

Fichte distinguishes two extremes:

```text
transcendental knowing       actually existent knowing
genetic insight              ordinary cognition
             \              /
              \            /
                 premise
          unites and separates
```

The premise is their middle ground. Ordinary knowing lives from it without
knowing its genesis. Transcendental knowing makes its genesis intelligible.

This is Fichte's exact contribution to `saṃhatya-kāritva`. The assembled
cognitive process is not discarded. Its elements are comprehended from the
middle Principle that both distinguishes and connects them.

## From factical combination to genetic unity

Previously, the two terms—knowing's self-genesis and its enduring
Being—were encountered factically and separately. Fichte says that they are
now united genetically in the insight into their connection.

```text
factical knowing

self-genesis        enduring Being
found separately    found separately

                 ↓

genetic knowing

one insight contains both terms a priori
as moments of their necessary connection
```

Once the connection is seen genetically, its terms no longer have to be
reassembled empirically each time. They reside a priori within the insight.

This does not abolish the empirical Citta. It reveals the higher Artha for
which its combinations have been working.

## Vāsanā, Saṃskāra, and Prajñā

Our distinction can now be stated precisely.

Ordinary Vāsanā and Saṃskāra preserve the results of Manas's activity. They
condition later cognition, often without disclosing the Principle through
which they operate.

Prajñā is also Saṃskāra, but in a higher sense:

> Prajñā-Saṃskāra is the enduring Being of Knowing established as Sattva.

```text
ordinary Saṃskāra
Manas's activity retained as a conditioning formation

                    ↓ mastered by Buddhi

Prajñā-Saṃskāra
the Principle of knowing retained as an intelligible formation
```

Prajñā in itself is pāramārthika. Prajñā as an attained Saṃskāra is that
universal Principle specified within a determinate cognitive history. It is
a posteriori in its attainment and embodiment, not in the truth of its
Principle.

This is how the countless Vāsanās can be mastered without simply erasing
Citta. Their composite activity becomes transparent to the Being of
Knowing.

## Sattva, Rajas, and Tamas

The opening of the eyes gives a small model of IV.24:

```text
Sattva    the field appears luminously and immediately
Rajas     Manas rapidly selects, relates, and determines
Tamas     the moving synthesis settles into stable Things
```

Manas moves so quickly that its combining activity usually disappears into
its result. The composite appears immediate. Vāsanā supplies countless
already operative paths through that activity.

Prajñā reverses the concealment. Buddhi sees the apparently immediate result
as the product of a principled combination. Sattva then ceases to mean only
the first luminosity of appearance; it becomes the durable transparency of
the process to its own Principle.

## The inward hypotheticalness

Fichte goes still further. Even transcendental science initially appears to
produce the connection through rules that *we* freely adopted. Its insight
therefore still seems hypothetical:

```text
we choose a maxim
        ↓
we construct the connection
        ↓
the result seems dependent on our arbitrary procedure
```

This is the deepest remaining Vāsanā of the Science of Knowing: the belief
that its necessity rests upon the activity of a factical `We`.

Fichte therefore demands that this inward hypotheticalness be negated. The
connection must show itself as categorical—true and necessary through its
own content, not because we happened to construct it.

```text
hypothetical insight               categorical insight

the free We seems to               the content necessitates
support the construction     ──►    its own construction
```

Science must justify its own procedure before it has truly begun. It must
be liberated from arbitrariness and accident.

## Yoga and Fichte

| Yoga IV.24 | Fichte |
|---|---|
| Citta is patterned by countless Vāsanās | ordinary knowing operates from tacitly assumed premises |
| Citta acts through combination | conditioning and conditioned terms are joined by a `should` |
| the composite works for a higher Artha | factical knowing points beyond itself to genetic insight |
| the combination is not self-grounding | the premise must be grounded through its genesis |
| Buddhi masters the Citta construction | transcendental knowing comprehends the law of ordinary knowing |
| `parārtha` is categorical purpose | science must negate arbitrariness and justify itself |

Fichte lets us read the Sūtra without destroying the Citta:

> The manifold cognitive construction is preserved, but its truth lies in
> the self-grounding insight that comprehends why its moments belong
> together.

## OṂ-Artha

IV.23 gave Citta the capacity for `sarvārtha`, every determinate meaning.
IV.24 orders that capacity toward `parārtha`, the Artha beyond the assembled
mechanism.

```text
sarvārtha                         parārtha

every meaning that can            the higher Artha governing
appear in Citta                    the complete construction
                 \               /
                  \             /
                      OṂ-Artha
```

OṂ-Artha is not one more object contemplated by Manas. It is the complete
intelligibility in which every determinate Artha and the Principle of their
connection belong together.

Japa on OṂ-Artha is therefore not repetition imposed upon an inert Citta.
It is the progressive transformation of tacit combinations into genetic
insight, until the construction speaks from its own categorical Principle.

## Provisional result

IV.24 says:

> Although Citta is diversified by innumerable Vāsanās, its composite
> activity is for a higher Artha.

The Sūtra does not kill Citta. It discriminates the constructed cognitive
field from the Principle for which that field operates.

Fichte supplies the movement from the ordinary `should`, which connects
terms through tacit premises, to transcendental knowing, which grounds the
premise genetically. Yet even this insight must overcome its apparent
dependence upon an arbitrary factical `We` and establish its categorical
necessity.

Actuality has therefore advanced:

```text
IV.22    Buddhi knows itself by attaining Citi's form

IV.23    Citta bears every Artha through the Seer–seen relation

IV.24    Citta's countless formations work for the higher Artha
         disclosed by categorical self-knowing
```

IV.25 can now ask what happens to the construction of selfhood when this
discriminating vision has become actual.
