# Only the Congruent Disposition Appears

## A report on Yoga Sutra IV.8 and Fichte

## The Sutra

`tatas tad-vipaka-anugunanam eva-abhivyakti vasananam`

### Plain translation

> From that, only those Vāsanās congruent with the resulting maturation become
> manifest.

IV.7 distinguished the yogin's uncolored action from ordinary karma. IV.8 now
explains how colored karma ordinarily determines appearance. A karmic result
does not manifest every latent tendency at once. The present maturation
selects only the Vāsanās suited to it.

```text
field of latent Vāsanās
       |
       | selected by congruence with present Vipāka
       v
manifest disposition
```

`Anuguna` is the hinge: corresponding to, conforming with, or congruent with.
`Abhivyakti` is manifestation, not creation. What becomes visible was latent,
but only a suitable field allows it to appear.

## Fichte's Bhasya

Fichte is now trying to derive appearance from pure being. If a construction
really participates in being, it cannot be grounded in the vain I that merely
asserts it. It must be grounded in being itself. Under that condition,
appearance is not arbitrary: the one whole being necessarily manifests in the
construction appropriate to it.

This supplies a logical reading of congruence:

| Yoga IV.8 | Fichte |
|---|---|
| latent Vāsanās | the multiplicity of possible appearance |
| determinate Vipāka | the present condition of manifestation |
| `anuguna` | conformity of appearance with its condition |
| `abhivyakti` | being actually appearing in construction |

The present appearance is therefore neither freely invented by an empirical
I nor mechanically copied from an external object. It is the determinate
Vāsanā whose manifestation agrees with the actual causal field.

## Citta-Dharma

IV.8 defines the selectivity of Citta. Citta does not display the whole latent
field indiscriminately. It manifests the determination that its present
conditions make capable of appearing.

> Citta is a conditioned power of manifestation: among many latent
> dispositions, it exhibits only the one congruent with the present result.

Fichte adds that true appearance must finally be grounded in being rather than
in the bare assertion of consciousness. The unresolved question is how a
conditioned manifestation can express necessary being. IV.9 begins to answer
by identifying the continuity of form through separated appearances.
