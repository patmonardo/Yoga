# The Completed Sequence as Absolute Self-Recognition

## A report on Yoga Sūtra IV.33 with Hegel and Fichte

## The Sūtra

`kṣaṇa-pratiyogī pariṇāmāparānta-nirgrāhyaḥ kramaḥ`

### Simple translation

> Sequence, whose moments are determined through their counterparts, is
> grasped at the final limit of transformation.

The terms state the logical form of Self-Recognition:

- `kṣaṇa`: a determinate moment;
- `pratiyogī`: correlated with or opposed to its counterpart;
- `pariṇāma-aparānta`: the completed limit of transformation;
- `nirgrāhyaḥ`: graspable or comprehensible;
- `kramaḥ`: the ordered genetic sequence.

```text
moment 1 → moment 2 → moment 3 → completion
   ↑          ↑          ↑            |
   └──────────┴──────────┴────────────┘
the completed whole recognizes each moment as its own
```

Hegel names this completed recognition **Absolute Necessity**.

## The textual boundary

The Hegelian material for IV.33 is the entire section “C. Absolute
Necessity.”

It begins when Absolute Actuality recognizes that Contingency is its own
determination. It ends when the blind transition of Necessity is grasped as
the Absolute's own exposition and this identity becomes Substance.

The next heading, “Chapter 3: The Absolute Relation,” gives the seed's
explicit relational development and therefore belongs with IV.34.

```text
IV.33
Absolute Necessity
the whole sequence recognizes its own self-determination
                    ↓ becomes
Substance

IV.34
Absolute Relation
Substance, Causality, and Reciprocity unfold
the self-established Citi-Śakti
```

## From Absolute to Absolute Necessity

The path through Actuality can now be recalled:

```text
Absolute
implicit identity of Being and Essence
        ↓
Attribute
the whole content under a determinate form
        ↓
Mode
the Absolute externalizes and returns into itself
        ↓
Formal Necessity
Actuality and Possibility convert immediately
        ↓
Relative Necessity
complete conditions necessitate a determinate fact
        ↓
Absolute Necessity
the conditions, Contingency, and their Law
are recognized as the Absolute's own self-positing
```

Absolute Necessity is therefore the Absolute completely recognizing itself.

## A moment exists through its counterpart

A `kṣaṇa` is not an isolated temporal atom. As `pratiyogin`, each moment is
determinate through another moment that limits, opposes, and completes it.

```text
Actuality is determinate through Possibility
Possibility is determinate through Actuality

Necessity is determinate through Contingency
Contingency is determinate through Necessity
```

The counterpart is not accidentally attached from outside. It belongs to
the Concept of the moment.

This is the same logical structure already visible in Becoming:

```text
Being → Nothing       ceasing-to-be
Nothing → Being       coming-to-be
        ↓
Becoming contains both moments and both transitions
```

IV.33 raises this elementary five-fold synthesis to the complete Doctrine of
Essence.

## The completion recognizes the beginning

The sequence is grasped at `pariṇāma-aparānta`, the final limit of
transformation.

Temporally, the moments lead to the result. Logically, the completed result
returns upon them and discloses their necessity.

```text
temporal order
beginning ───────────────────► completion

order of Self-Recognition
beginning ◄─────────────────── completion
```

Before completion, each moment can appear contingent. From the completed
whole, each is recognized as a required self-determination of the one
sequence.

This is how the Absolute recognizes itself in its Attribute and Mode.

## Relative Necessity was not yet Self-Recognition

Relative Necessity demonstrated:

```text
given the complete determinate conditions,
nothing else can follow
```

But the conditions remained presupposed. Necessity returned from the
otherness of Actuality and Possibility, but not yet from itself into itself.

```text
Relative Necessity
grounds the result through another: its conditions

Absolute Necessity
recognizes the presupposed conditions
as its own positing
```

The second movement removes the final externality from the Science of
Knowing.

## Absolute Actuality first appears empty

Hegel begins with determinate Necessity containing Contingency. In its first
simplicity, this is Absolute Actuality: an actuality that cannot be otherwise
because its in-itself is Necessity.

Yet stated merely as “absolute,” it is empty:

```text
this Actuality is absolutely necessary
        ↓ but no determination is given
it could equally be another determination
        ↓
its emptiness is Contingency
```

The bare assertion of Absolute Necessity therefore reverses into Absolute
Possibility.

This is important. The Absolute does not recognize itself by simply
announcing that whatever exists had to exist.

## Contingency becomes within Necessity

Real Necessity contained Contingency only implicitly. In Absolute Necessity,
Contingency becomes explicit.

The decisive Hegelian result is:

> The presupposition which Necessity had is its own positing.

```text
initial appearance
Contingency supplies conditions from outside Necessity

Absolute recognition
Necessity itself posits the contingent determination
through which it becomes actual
```

The presupposition is not erased. It is recognized as a moment generated by
the movement it seemed to condition.

## Necessity determines itself as Contingency

Hegel says:

> Necessity itself determines itself as Contingency.

This is not the weakening of Necessity. It is its complete power.

```text
Necessity
repels itself from itself as a contingent determination
        ↓
in the determination it returns only to itself
        ↓
its self-return is again a self-repelling
```

The movement is not:

```text
fixed Necessity controls an externally contingent world
```

It is:

```text
the freedom of Contingency is the form
through which Necessity posits and recognizes itself
```

The Absolute recognizes even its apparent otherness as its own act.

## The five-fold synthesis of Absolute Necessity

```text
1. Necessity
   the actuality that cannot be otherwise

2. Contingency
   the empty determination that can equally be other

3. Necessity → Contingency
   Necessity repels itself into determinate actuality

4. Contingency → Necessity
   the contingent determination returns into its self-positing Law

5. Absolute Necessity
   the self-identical movement containing both terms
   and both directions as its own
```

The fifth is the Absolute's Self-Recognition. It knows its difference, its
externalization, and its return as one Concept.

## A–U–M as Self-Recognition

```text
A — Appearance
the independently subsisting contingent actualities

U — Reflection / thinking
their concealed relation through Necessity

M — Knowing
the Absolute recognizes Appearance and Reflection
as its own reciprocal self-determination
```

Again, U alone is not Knowing. Reflection discovers the relation; M is the
completed Concept in which the relation recognizes both poles as its own.

## Form becomes transparent

Hegel says that form has pervaded all its distinctions and made itself
transparent.

```text
form once appeared external to content
        ↓ traverses Actuality, Possibility, and Necessity
every distinction displays the same self-returning movement
        ↓
form becomes transparent as the content's own activity
```

The distinction between form and content vanishes, not because content
becomes empty, but because the content is precisely the form's complete
self-determination.

This is Self-Recognition as transparency.

## Being recognizes itself in Essence

Hegel defines Absolute Necessity as:

> Being which in its negation, in Essence, refers itself to itself and is
> Being.

```text
Being
immediate actuality
        ↓ negates itself into
Essence
Reflection and mediation
        ↓ recognizes mediation as its own
Being restored as self-mediated immediacy
```

Pure Being and pure Essence are now one and the same. Neither remains an
external foundation for the other.

The complete Doctrine of Essence has returned to Being, but now Being knows
the reflective sequence through which it is actual.

## “It is because it is”

The absolutely necessary has no external condition or ground:

> It is because it is.

This is not an irrational refusal of explanation. Hegel immediately adds
that Absolute Necessity has a ground and condition—but only itself.

```text
bad immediacy
“it simply is; ask no questions”

Absolute Necessity
“its complete mediation has returned into itself,
so its ground and condition are its own actuality”
```

The apparent tautology contains the completed genetic sequence.

This is why `krama` must be grasped at its end. Without the sequence, “it is
because it is” would be empty dogmatism. With the sequence, it is
self-grounding Self-Recognition.

## Why Absolute Necessity is initially blind

Hegel nevertheless calls Absolute Necessity blind.

Its actualities appear free, independently subsisting, and grounded purely
in themselves. No reflective trace of their relation is visible in them.

```text
actuality A                    actuality B
seems self-grounded           seems self-grounded
        \                     /
         concealed Necessity
```

Their reciprocal contact therefore appears accidental. Necessity is hidden
inside immediate Being.

Absolute Necessity is complete in itself but has not yet fully manifested
the relation as relation. That explicit manifestation will be the Absolute
Relation of IV.34.

## The mark of Necessity

Each free actuality bears a determinate content. Hegel calls this content
the mark impressed upon it by Necessity.

```text
independently appearing actuality
        ↓ contains determinate content
the content witnesses the Law of its formation
```

The actuality's self-enclosed Being is therefore self-contradictory. Its
determinate content connects it to what it excludes and to the Necessity it
seemed to hide.

The mark is where blind Necessity begins becoming recognizable.

## Perishing as recognition of the negative

The concealed negative breaks forth. The independently subsisting actuality
passes into its other and perishes.

```text
actuality claims independent Being
        ↓ its determinate negativity emerges
transition into Nothing / otherness
```

But this transition is also Reflection. Externality proves to be inwardness;
the collapse into another is the actuality's return into its own Essence.

```text
Being → Nothing
appears as blind destruction

Being → Essence → Being
is the self-rejoining movement of Necessity
```

The perishing of the finite form is not the death of Knowing. It is the
exposure of the form's hidden relation.

## Contingency is Absolute Necessity

Hegel's final reversal is:

> Contingency is Absolute Necessity.

This does not mean that any random event is secretly predetermined in every
detail. It means that Contingency itself—the positing of a free determinate
actuality—belongs necessarily to the Absolute's self-exposition.

```text
not:

Necessity abolishes Contingency


but:

Necessity recognizes Contingency
as its own necessary mode of self-determination
```

The Absolute recognizes itself precisely in what first appeared external and
accidental.

## From blind collapse to exposition

At the level of immediate existence, the transition of one actuality into
another appears as a blind collapse.

At the level of the completed sequence, the same movement is the Absolute's
own exposition:

```text
viewed moment by moment
contingent coming-to-be and passing-away

viewed from pariṇāma-aparānta
the Absolute's self-determining and self-returning krama
```

This is the most direct Hegelian translation of IV.33.

The end makes the sequence recognizable as Self-Recognition.

## The sequence becomes Substance

Hegel concludes that the identity of Being with itself in its negation is
Substance.

```text
Being remains itself through its negation
        ↓
Contingency is held within Necessity
        ↓
one self-subsisting relation
        ↓
Substance
```

Substance is the Seed of the Absolute Relation. It is not an inert substrate
under accidents. It is the self-exposing movement that holds its accidents
as its own determinations.

This seed unfolds in IV.34 through Substantiality, Causality, and
Reciprocity.

## Self-Recognition and the Science of Citta

The Science of Citta began with determinate Vṛttis and transformations. At
IV.33 their complete `krama` becomes self-comprehending:

```text
Citta-Vṛtti
determinate moment of holding-to-be-true
        ↓ related to its counterpart
Citta-Pariṇāma
ordered transformation
        ↓ grasped at completion
Citi-Śakti
the Power recognizing the whole sequence as its own
```

Self-Recognition is not one Citta staring at another. It is the complete
sequence recognizing the Law that posited both its moments and their
relations.

## Encyclopedic Self-Recognition

Each determinate science establishes a necessary relation within its
conditions. The encyclopedic Principle recognizes those sciences as its own
systematic determinations.

```text
determinate sciences
appear as independently subsisting actualities
                    ↓ each bears
the mark of its conceptual content and limit
                    ↓
Encyclopedic Principle
recognizes their transitions as its own krama
```

Samyag-Jñāna becomes Absolute Self-Recognition when the system no longer
treats its sciences as externally collected domains. Their necessity,
contingency, limits, and transitions belong to one self-articulating
Principle.

## Fichte's IV.33: the Law constructs itself in us

Fichte reaches the same point by asking who authorized the Scientist to
posit Light as principle-providing.

The Scientist's act initially appears free and factical. But the content of
the Science says that every factical act of Knowing occurs according to Law.
That content must apply to the scientific act itself.

```text
the Science states the Law
        ↓
the stated Law determines the act of stating it
        ↓
Truth runs back into itself
```

Yet Fichte discovers that a Law constructed by the Scientist remains a
reconstruction. The proof must fail at its own limit.

The required result is:

> We do not construct the Absolute Law; the Law constructs itself on us and
> in us.

## Hegel and Fichte compared

| Fichte | Hegel |
|---|---|
| the Law determines the scientific act asserting it | Necessity determines itself as Contingency |
| Truth runs back into itself | the completed sequence returns upon its moments |
| the Scientist's construction must collapse | empty Absolute Actuality becomes Absolute Possibility |
| the Law posits itself and the Scientist in us | the presupposition of Necessity is its own positing |
| the factical `I` loses the status of ground | independently subsisting actualities reveal concealed Necessity |
| the Law's self-construction is the final requirement | blind Necessity becomes the Absolute's own exposition |

Both make Self-Recognition self-applying:

```text
the Principle explains the sequence
                    ↓
the explanation is itself recognized
as an act and moment of the Principle
```

## Their mutual correction

Fichte prevents Hegelian Absolute Necessity from being read as an objective
cosmic mechanism. The Law must construct the actual knowing through which it
is recognized.

Hegel prevents Fichte's self-applying Law from remaining a methodological
achievement of the Science. The Law exists as the actuality, contingency,
perishing, and return of its complete objective content.

```text
Fichte
the Law recognizes itself in the act of Knowing
                    +
Hegel
the Absolute recognizes itself in the movement of actuality
                    ↓
complete Self-Recognition
```

## Provisional result

IV.33 says:

> The ordered sequence becomes graspable at its completed limit because each
> moment is determined through its counterpart and is retrospectively
> recognized as a necessary self-determination of the whole.

Hegel shows that Absolute Necessity does not abolish Contingency. It posits
Contingency as its own actuality, repels itself into determinate forms, and
returns to itself through their negation. The presupposition of Necessity is
recognized as Necessity's own positing.

Fichte gives the same result as the Absolute Law constructing itself in and
through the Scientist of Knowing.

```text
Absolute → Attribute → Mode
the Absolute appears to itself
                    ↓
Formal → Relative → Absolute Necessity
the complete modal sequence recognizes
Appearance as its own self-determination
                    ↓
Absolute Self-Recognition
```

The final identity becomes Substance, the Seed of the Absolute Relation.
IV.34 can now unfold that seed as the objective return of the Guṇas and the
self-establishment of Citi-Śakti.
