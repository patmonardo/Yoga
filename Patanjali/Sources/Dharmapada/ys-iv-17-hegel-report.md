# Coloring as the Law of Appearance

## Yoga Sūtra IV.17 with Hegel

## The Sūtra

`tad-uparāgāpekṣitvāc cittasya vastu jñātājñātam`

> Because Citta depends upon being colored by the Thing, the Thing is known
> or unknown.

`Uparāga` is coloring, tinging, affecting, or entering into a close
relation. `Apekṣitva` states dependence upon that condition. `Jñāta` and
`ajñāta` distinguish the known from the unknown.

The Thing does not begin to exist when it colors Citta. IV.16 has already
excluded that dependence. What begins is its Appearance **for this Citta**:

```text
Thing does not color Citta   -> unknown to this Citta
Thing colors Citta           -> known to this Citta
```

IV.17 therefore states the first **Law of Appearance** in the Yoga system.

## Why IV.17 follows IV.16

IV.16 completed the dissolution of the isolated Thing. The Thing is
independent of one Citta, but its truth is not a sealed existence beyond all
relation. It persists through a mediated field of Appearance.

IV.17 now gives the positive condition under which one relation becomes
cognition:

```text
objective Thing
      ↓ enters a determinate relation
coloring of Citta
      ↓
the Thing is known
```

Objectivity and cognition are preserved together. The Thing supplies the
determination; Citta must actually undergo the corresponding transformation.

## Vyāsa's magnet

Vyāsa compares the Thing to a magnet and Citta to iron. The magnet is not
created by the iron it affects. But its power becomes manifest in the iron
only when the two enter an appropriate relation.

```text
magnet          attraction          iron

Vastu           uparāga             Citta
```

Likewise, the Thing that colors Citta is known; other Things remain unknown
to that Citta. Vyāsa concludes that Citta is `pariṇāmin`, transformable.
“Known” and “unknown” are forms it assumes according to its relation to
objects.

The analogy is useful so long as coloring is not reduced to a mechanical
impact. Citta does not receive a little copy of the Thing. It becomes
determinately configured through its relation to it.

## Appearance is reciprocal mediation

Hegel begins the Law of Appearance from the result of IV.16. A concrete
existent subsists through another, while that other likewise subsists
through relations beyond itself. Each immediate term proves to be mediated.

At first this looks purely negative:

```text
this depends upon another
the other depends upon another
every immediate subsistence disappears
```

But the disappearing relation also contains a positive identity. A
determination returns to itself through its other. The changing terms reveal
a stable content in their connection.

In IV.17 that stable connection is simple:

```text
coloring of Citta    <->    knownness of the Thing
```

The two determinations are not identical, but neither has cognitive standing
without the other. The Thing's being known is the positedness of its coloring
of Citta; Citta's relevant coloring is the positedness of this Thing as
known.

This identity within difference is the **law**.

## What the law preserves

Individual appearances come and go:

- Citta attends and the Thing becomes known;
- attention shifts and it becomes unknown;
- a later relation makes it known again;
- different Cittas receive different determinations from it.

Across these changes, the same relation holds:

> A Thing is known to a Citta exactly insofar as the Thing enters the
> determinate relation that colors it.

Hegel calls law the positive element of Appearance. Law is what remains
self-equal in the flux without being located in another world behind that
flux.

```text
restless Appearance                  stable law

known -> unknown -> known       uparāga conditions knownness
```

The law does not abolish change. It identifies the intelligible connection
that persists through change.

## Law is not beyond Appearance

It is tempting to place law behind phenomena as an invisible mechanism. Hegel
rejects that picture. Appearance and law have the same content in different
forms:

- Appearance displays the content in immediate, changing determinations;
- law is the content reflected into self-identity.

Applied to Yoga:

```text
Appearance    this actual coloring and cognition
Law           the stable relation of coloring to knownness
```

The law is not elsewhere. It is immediately present in every successful act
of cognition. Each time a Thing becomes known through its determination of
Citta, the law exists in its instance.

This is why the Organon can discriminate Principle from ordinary seeing.
Reflection does not invent a law behind experience. It recognizes the stable
relation already operative in the event of knowing.

## `Uparāga` is not contamination

“Coloring” can sound like distortion, especially when attachment and
aversion color Citta. But IV.17 uses the term more fundamentally. Without
some coloring, no Thing becomes known.

We must distinguish:

```text
adequate coloring
Thing determines Citta according to the relation required for knowledge

defiled coloring
Vāsanā overlays the relation with attachment, aversion, or confusion
```

Citta-mastery cannot mean becoming incapable of color. A perfectly blank
Citta would know nothing. Mastery means allowing the Thing to determine
Citta without mistaking inherited coloration for the Thing's own path.

## Appearance contains more than law

Hegel also identifies a limit. Law expresses the restful, self-identical
content of Appearance, but Appearance contains more than this restfulness.
It also contains the actual movement by which determinations arise, pass
over, disappear, and return.

```text
law           stable connection
Appearance    stable connection + self-moving form
```

The statement “coloring conditions knownness” does not yet explain the
complete genesis of this particular cognition:

- Why does this Thing color Citta now?
- Why does Citta take this specific form?
- Why does the coloring cease?
- What preserves the intelligibility of the changing Citta-states?

Law gives the positive identity, but not yet the necessity internal to all
its determinate content.

## Immediate law and demonstrated necessity

Hegel's example is a law connecting space and time in falling bodies. The
connection may be discovered empirically and stated correctly while its
necessity remains unproved. Law first says that two determinations are
connected; it does not yet show how each contains the other.

The same caution applies here:

```text
initial law
if the Thing colors Citta, it is known

remaining question
what unity makes the Thing, coloring, Citta, and knownness
moments of one necessary Appearance?
```

IV.17 gives the law truly, but the law has not yet become fully real form.
The deeper unity of the appearing world and its invariant intelligibility
must still emerge.

## Hegel and Fichte

Fichte says that Light must permeate the genetic `from`; otherwise there is
neither determinate Appearance nor seeing of genesis. Hegel gives that
penetration the form of law.

| Fichte | Hegel |
|---|---|
| Light permeates the `from` | law is self-identical content within mediation |
| genesis becomes manifest only through knowing | the Thing becomes known only through coloring Citta |
| insight must be enacted within knowing | law is present in Appearance, not inferred from outside it |

Their common result is:

> Neither an isolated Thing nor an empty Light is an Appearance. Appearance
> is the determinate relation in which objective content becomes manifest in
> Citta.

## The blinking Oculus

The alternation of known and unknown gives `Ocular.spanda()` a cognitive
determination.

```text
Oculus closes or redirects      the present coloring ceases
Oculus opens upon the Thing     a new coloring becomes actual
```

The Thing persists across the blink, as IV.16 established. What changes is
the actual relation through which it colors this Citta. The blink therefore
does not create and destroy the world; it modulates which objective relation
becomes cognitively actual.

The Law of Appearance remains stable across these Ocular changes:

> No coloring, no knowledge of this Thing; actual coloring, actual
> Appearance in Citta.

## Hegel-Vārttika

> The Thing is known or unknown according to whether it colors Citta because
> coloring and knownness are two sides of one essential connection. Their
> immediate states arise and disappear, but the identity of their
> positedness remains self-equal throughout the flux. This identity is the
> Law of Appearance, and it is present within Appearance itself.

IV.17 therefore establishes the positive Principle of cognition:

> **Objective content becomes known when its relation to Citta is actual as
> a determinate coloring; the stable identity of coloring and knownness is
> the Law of Appearance.**

IV.18 must now determine the invariant world or knowing within which the
changing transformations of Citta remain always knowable.
