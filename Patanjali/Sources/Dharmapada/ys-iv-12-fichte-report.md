# The Temporal Paths of Appearance

## A report on Yoga Sūtra IV.12 and Fichte

## The Sūtra

`atīta-anāgataṃ svarūpato 'sty adhva-bhedād dharmāṇām`

### Simple translation

> What is past and what is yet to come exist in their own form, because the
> Dharmas differ according to their temporal paths.

This translation keeps four claims together:

- `atīta` is what has passed;
- `anāgata` is what has not yet come;
- `svarūpataḥ asti` says that each is, according to its own form;
- `adhva-bheda` distinguishes the paths or courses of the Dharmas.

The Sūtra does not say that the past and future exist just as the present
does. If they did, there would be no temporal difference. It says that they
are not sheer non-being. A Dharma is past, present, or future according to
the distinct path by which its determination appears.

```text
past                  present                 future
has appeared          is manifest             is yet to appear
     \                    |                    /
      \________ distinct paths of Dharma _____/
```

The simplest meaning is:

> The present appearance is not the whole determination of the fact.

## Why this follows IV.11

IV.11 explained the absence of a Vāsanā. It is collected by cause, result,
support, and object; when that conditioned collection is absent, the Vāsanā
is absent.

IV.12 begins from the converse situation. The conditions are not absent. A
determinate fact has come into existence and must now be considered as an
appearance.

But coming into existence does not reduce the fact to an isolated present.
The present Dharma has a path: it has become from a prior determination and
is directed toward a determination not yet manifest.

```text
Reflection                           Appearance

conditions of presence/absence  ->  the conditioned fact stands forth
                                      along a temporal path
```

Thus Appearance follows Reflection exactly. Reflection accounts for how the
fact is conditioned. Appearance asks how the conditioned fact shows what it
is.

## Fichte's Bhāṣya

Fichte begins with the fact that ordinary consciousness encounters a
manifold. We find many appearances and many determinate states. The task is
not merely to accept this manifold as given, but to understand how it appears
within knowing.

He therefore distinguishes two ways of knowing.

### Reconstructive knowing

Reconstructive knowing begins from an assumed fact and draws a conditional
relation:

```text
if this is so  ->  then that must follow
```

The relation may be evident while its starting point remains hypothetical.
Such knowing can arrange appearances, but it cannot yet demonstrate their
genesis. It knows how one assumed determination is connected with another,
not why the appearing complex is there at all.

### Genetic knowing

Genetic knowing does not manufacture an appearance from outside. It attends
to the appearing act itself. Fichte's decisive point is that we do not freely
invent the manifestness that arises in insight. When we actually see, the
manifestness makes itself evident and carries our reflection with it.

Knowing consequently has two inseparable aspects:

```text
it is                 the fact of manifestness
it is a "from"        the fact appears through a genesis
```

The appearance is not merely found; it is found as having come forth. To
explain appearance is to comprehend this `from` without placing an external
maker behind it.

## Yoga and Fichte

| Yoga IV.12 | Fichte |
|---|---|
| past and future are according to their own form | appearance cannot be reduced to the isolated present fact |
| Dharmas differ by their paths | the manifold must be understood through its genesis |
| the present is one mode of appearance | ordinary consciousness encounters the result as given |
| temporal difference belongs to the fact | genetic knowing comprehends the fact as a `from` |

Fichte helps us avoid two bad interpretations.

First, the Sūtra is not proposing three simultaneously present objects named
past, present, and future. Their difference is a difference in the path of
appearance.

Second, it is not asking us merely to infer an invisible past and future from
a visible present. Such inference would remain reconstructive and
hypothetical. The aim is to see the present determination genetically: as a
fact whose very form shows that it has come forth and can pass into another
form.

## Appearance without illusion

`Appearance` here does not mean a false image concealing an inaccessible
reality. The fact really appears. The error is to take the fact only as it is
immediately present and detach it from the movement that determines it.

For a simple example, consider a seed. Its past is not visibly beside it, and
its future plant is not a miniature object hidden inside it. Nevertheless,
what has formed this seed and what it is capable of becoming belong to its
determinate path. Without that path we do not yet know what presently
appears.

To see through appearance therefore means:

> See the appearance so completely that its genesis and direction become
> intelligible within it.

It does not mean looking away from the appearance toward another world.

## Citta-Dharma

At the end of Reflection, Citta mastered the conditions by which a Vāsanā is
present or absent. It can now examine an existing cognition without mistaking
its present content for a self-contained reality.

Every constructed Citta has a temporal path:

```text
Saṃskāra from prior cognition
              ↓
Vāsanā selectively manifests
              ↓
present Citta determination
              ↓
prospective action and formation
```

Past and future are not extra Cittas mysteriously existing alongside the
present one. They name different forms of determination within the connected
process by which Citta appears.

This also begins to clarify the Kośa maxim of seeing things as they actually
are. A merely present item is an abstraction. To know the Dharma is to know
its conditioned arising, its present manifestation, and its real direction
of transformation.

## Provisional result

IV.12 opens Appearance by giving the existing fact temporal depth.

Reflection showed why the fact can be present or absent. Now that the fact
has come into existence, Appearance shows that its truth is not exhausted by
the current instant. Its past and future are according to their own forms,
distinguished as paths of the Dharma.

Fichte gives the corresponding discipline of knowledge:

> Do not merely reconstruct relations among assumed appearances. Learn to
> see the appearing fact genetically, as a determinate `from`.

This is the first step toward Actuality. Before we can see how things
actually are, we must cease treating their present appearance as a finished,
self-explanatory fact.
