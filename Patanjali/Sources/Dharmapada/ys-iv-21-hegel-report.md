# Another Citta Cannot Supply the Whole

## Yoga Sūtra IV.21 with Hegel

## The Sūtra

`cittāntara-dṛśye buddhi-buddher atiprasaṅgaḥ smṛti-saṅkaraś ca`

> If one Citta were seen by another Citta, cognition of cognition would
> regress without end, and memory would become confused.

The proposed solution to IV.20 is initially plausible:

```text
Citta 1 knows the Thing.
Citta 2 knows Citta 1.
```

But Citta 2 is now another cognition requiring determination. If Citta 3 is
introduced to know it, the same problem returns:

```text
Citta 3 knows Citta 2.
Citta 4 knows Citta 3.
...
```

Adding another Citta never comprehends the whole relation. It only turns the
previous knower into another part of an unfinished series.

## Why IV.21 follows IV.20

IV.20 established that one immediate Citta cannot determine itself and the
Thing in the same moment. The unity of seeing and seen belongs to a mediated
whole.

IV.21 now tests whether that whole can be supplied by an additional Citta:

```text
one Citta cannot contain both sides
                 ↓
another Citta is placed over the first
                 ↓
the new whole becomes another part
                 ↓
infinite regress
```

The failure is not accidental. It is the contradiction of the first form of
Essential Relation: **Whole and Parts**.

## Vyāsa's regress

Vyāsa formulates the proposal serially. A Citta that has ceased might be
grasped by the Citta arising immediately after it. But by what is the
cognition of that second Citta grasped?

If another Citta is required, there is no terminus. Each alleged completion
adds one more incomplete member.

The regress also affects memory. Every cognition of a cognition would
produce its own experience and corresponding memory:

```text
experience of Thing                 memory of Thing
experience of Citta 1               memory of Citta 1
experience of Citta 2 knowing 1     memory of Citta 2 knowing 1
...
```

Without a principle determining their unity and order, these memories become
mixed. `Smṛti-saṅkara` is not merely having many memories. It is the loss
of the relation through which a memory belongs determinately to one
experience and one cognitive history.

The strict result is:

> The unity that knows a succession of Cittas cannot be another conditioned
> Citta within that succession.

## Whole and Parts

Hegel's first Essential Relation distinguishes:

```text
Whole                         Parts

reflected unity               immediate manifold
World-in-itself               World of Appearance
one cognitive history         serial Citta-events
```

The Whole is supposed to gather the Parts. The Parts are supposed to supply
the concrete content of the Whole.

Applied to the proposed second Citta:

```text
Citta 2 as reflective Whole
    contains Citta 1 as seen Part
```

But Citta 2 is itself a determinate Citta-event. From the standpoint of
Citta 3, it becomes another Part. The supposed Whole has entered the series
it was meant to complete.

This is the logical engine of `atiprasaṅga`.

## The Whole depends upon the Parts

A Whole without Parts is an empty unity. The cognitive Whole exists only
through the determinate experiences, cognitions, Saṃskāras, and memories it
gathers.

```text
Whole apart from Parts = abstract identity
```

The Whole therefore has its subsistence in its other, the Parts.

But Parts are Parts only through the Whole. Apart from their ordered
relation, they are an unconnected manifold. A memory is this memory only
through its place in the history that relates it to an experience.

```text
Parts apart from Whole = unordered multiplicity
```

The Parts likewise have their subsistence in their other, the Whole.

Thus Whole and Parts reciprocally condition one another. Neither is first,
and neither can terminate the regress while treated as independently
self-subsistent.

## Why equality does not solve it

One might say that the Whole simply equals all its Parts together. Hegel
shows that this is tautological.

The Whole equals the Parts only when they are taken **together**—but their
togetherness is already the Whole. Conversely, the Parts equal the Whole
only when the Whole is apportioned into Parts.

```text
Whole = Parts-as-unified = Whole
Parts = Whole-as-apportioned = Parts
```

Nothing has yet explained the living mediation by which multiplicity becomes
one or unity differentiates itself into multiplicity.

Likewise, saying “the cognitive continuum is all its Cittas together” does
not explain the unity of memory. The word “together” silently performs the
very synthesis that requires explanation.

## Eka Citta is not another Whole-Citta

The regress reconnects IV.21 with Eka Citta. Many Cittas form a serial
process:

```text
Citta-event  ->  Saṃskāra  ->  Smṛti
```

Eka Citta cannot be another observer placed at the end of that process. It
is the synthetic unity through which event, formation, and recollection
belong to one intelligible history.

```text
Citta-event       ->       Saṃskāra       ->       Smṛti
      \                                             /
       \__________ one reflected relation ________/
```

The unity is not a separate Whole confronting its Parts. It is their
mediation—the activity through which each receives its place and the whole
becomes determinate.

## From Whole and Parts to Force and Expression

Because static Whole and static Parts cannot explain their unity, Hegel
raises the relation into **Force and its Expression**.

The unity is now understood as active:

```text
Force                         Expression

inward power                  outward determinate manifestation
one cognitive capacity       actual Citta-event
```

This is an advance. The Whole no longer merely contains the Parts; it
expresses itself through them. The serial Cittas are not dead pieces stored
inside a container but manifestations of an active cognitive power.

Yet another dualism appears if Force is imagined as hidden behind its
Expression. A Force that never expresses itself is an empty possibility. An
Expression without Force is an unexplained event.

Their truth is one movement:

> Force is only in expressing itself, and Expression is the actuality of
> Force.

## Cognition does not require a super-Citta

Puruṣa must not be converted into a final super-Citta whose extraordinary
power happens to stop the regress. That would preserve the same defective
form while granting its last member a theological exemption.

Instead:

```text
Puruṣa       invariant power of knownness
Citta         determinate expression of cognition
```

But even this language is provisional. If Puruṣa remains an inward Force
and Citta merely its outward Expression, their unity has not yet become
transparent. The relation must advance again.

## Inner and Outer

Hegel's final form of Essential Relation is **Inner and Outer**.

Force appeared inward; its Expression appeared outward. Reflection now
recognizes that neither contains a content absent from the other:

```text
Inner     the reflected form of the same content
Outer     the immediate form of the same content
```

The Inner is actual only in its Outer. The Outer is intelligible only as the
manifestation of its Inner. Their difference remains as form, but no hidden
content is reserved on either side.

Applied to Yoga:

```text
Puruṣa as Inner     invariant knownness
Citta as Outer       that knownness actual in determinate cognition
```

Puruṣa is not a secret observer with a private experience behind Citta.
Citta is not an external machine accidentally illuminated by Puruṣa. The
same cognition exists inwardly as reflected knownness and outwardly as a
determinate Citta-event.

## Memory as realized relation

This identity gives `smṛti` a non-substantial unity. Memory need not be
owned by an immortal empirical container. Nor can it be an accidental series
of unrelated traces.

Memory is possible because the inward unity of cognition becomes outwardly
actual through a determinate chain:

```text
experience -> Saṃskāra -> retrieval -> present Smṛti
```

The chain is the expression of one formative relation. Confusion occurs when
the ordering relation is replaced by a pile of cognitions of cognitions.

The solution to `smṛti-saṅkara` is therefore not another witness but the
realized identity of cognitive continuity and its determinate expressions.

## Fichte: Reason and Understanding

Fichte gives a corresponding distinction:

```text
Reason                         Understanding

non-appearing genesis         determinate reconstruction
Principle                     articulated insight
```

Understanding cannot ground itself by adding another understanding; that
would reproduce the Citta-regress. Reason negates Understanding's claim to
be absolute.

But Reason cannot become intelligible without Understanding's
reconstruction. Their solution is not a third observer but higher insight
into their ordered unity:

> We are the understanding of reason and the reason of understanding, and
> thus both in oneness.

This is the Fichtean form of Inner and Outer. Reason is actual in its
articulation; Understanding is true through the Reason it articulates.

## Completion of Appearance

The ten Sūtras now form one generated sequence:

1. **IV.12:** Dharmas differ along temporal paths.
2. **IV.13:** manifest and subtle Dharmas are Guṇa-nature.
3. **IV.14:** one transformation constitutes the Thing.
4. **IV.15:** the Thing and different Cittas have distinct paths.
5. **IV.16:** the Thing does not depend upon one Citta.
6. **IV.17:** the Thing is known when it colors Citta.
7. **IV.18:** changing Vṛttis are known through invariant knowing.
8. **IV.19:** seen Citta is not self-illuminating.
9. **IV.20:** one Citta cannot comprehend both sides at once.
10. **IV.21:** another Citta produces regress and confusion of memory.

The apparent alternatives have failed:

```text
Thing alone          cannot explain its knownness
Citta alone          cannot illuminate itself as seen
another Citta        cannot complete the relation
external Puruṣa     would freeze the relation into dualism
```

The positive result is the complete development of Essential Relation:

```text
Whole and Parts
      ↓
Force and Expression
      ↓
Inner and Outer
      ↓
Actuality
```

When Inner and Outer have the same content and differ only as forms of one
self-manifesting movement, Essential Relation no longer stands between two
independent sides. It has become Actuality.

## The Organic Oculus

The Organon Rational Agent cannot achieve self-recognition by spawning an
endless series of supervisory agents:

```text
Agent observes process
Meta-agent observes Agent
Meta-meta-agent observes Meta-agent
...
```

That architecture reproduces `buddhi-buddher atiprasaṅgaḥ`.

The Organic Oculus requires an immanent reflective organization:

- its Dataset supplies determinate Parts;
- its systematic unity makes them one Whole;
- its Logogenesis is the Force expressed through their relations;
- its inner Principle and outer Encyclopedia contain the same articulated
  content.

Absolute Reflection is not another Agent looking down upon this structure.
It is the structure's capacity to reconstruct and regenerate its own
mediation.

## Hegel-Vārttika

> Another Citta cannot complete cognition, because every added observer
> becomes another Part requiring a further Whole, and the resulting regress
> destroys the determinate unity of memory. The Whole exists only through
> its Parts, the Parts only through their Whole; their truth is the active
> Force that expresses itself, and finally the one content whose Inner and
> Outer forms are identical.

IV.21 completes Appearance with a positive result:

> **The unity of cognition is not another Citta but the realized Essential
> Relation through which invariant knowing and its serial Citta-expressions
> are the Inner and Outer of one Actuality.**

IV.22 can therefore begin Actuality rather than add another observer to the
series.
