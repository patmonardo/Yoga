# Citta Cannot Determine Both Sides at Once

## A report on Yoga Sūtra IV.20, Vyāsa, and Fichte

## The Sūtra

`eka-samaye cobhayānavadhāraṇam`

### Simple translation

> Nor can Citta determine both itself and the Thing at the same moment.

The Sūtra is extremely compressed. `Eka-samaye` means at one time or in the
same moment. `Ubhaya` means both. `Anavadhāraṇa` is the failure or
impossibility of determining distinctly.

The two sides are supplied by IV.19:

- Citta as the seeing or cognizing side;
- the object determined by that cognition—including Citta itself when Citta
  becomes an object of reflection.

The problem is not that Citta cannot contain information about itself and an
object. It cannot occupy both relational positions immediately and
completely in one act:

```text
Citta as present seeing  ──────►  Thing seen

If Citta turns upon itself:

present seeing           ──────►  previous seeing as seen
```

The present seeing cannot become the seen seeing without a new reflective
act.

## Why this follows IV.19

IV.19 said that Citta is not self-luminous because it is seen. Fichte gave
the exact distinction: Light resides in immediate seeing, never in the
seeing that is seen.

An objection remains. Could Citta illuminate the object and itself
simultaneously?

IV.20 answers no. To determine itself, Citta must take itself as object. But
then the act doing the determining is not identical with the objectified act
determined.

```text
act 1:      Citta sees the Thing

act 2:      Citta sees act 1

act 2 cannot simultaneously be act 1
and its own complete reflection upon act 1
```

This is the temporal and relational limit of Citta within Appearance.

## Vyāsa's Bhāṣya

Vyāsa states that determination of one's own form and another's form in one
moment is not coherent.

He directs the objection especially against a momentary Citta that would be,
in one indivisible instant:

- the agent of cognition;
- the act of cognition;
- the object cognized by itself;
- and the cognition of the external object.

If being, action, and agent are all simply the same moment, the distinct
functions required for self-cognition collapse into one another. The moment
would have to stand on both sides of its own relation without any mediation.

Vyāsa's Bhāṣya here is only one sentence, but its force is considerable:

> Determining the form of self and other requires a distinction that one
> immediate moment cannot supply by itself.

## The Essential Relation

IV.20 is a powerful Sūtra of Essential Relation because it prevents the
relation from being swallowed by either side.

```text
subjective reduction

Citta claims to contain both sides immediately
          ↓
the Thing becomes only Citta


objective reduction

the seen Citta is treated as the whole knowing
          ↓
living seeing becomes another Thing
```

The Essential Relation requires both sides and their difference:

```text
seeing                     seen
Citta as act               Thing / Citta as object
       \                    /
        \                  /
             relation
```

But within Appearance the relation is not yet comprehended as a completed
unity. Citta moves from one side to the other. It can know the Thing, then
make that cognition an object, but it cannot immediately possess the whole
relation in one moment.

This is Māyā as Relational Aspect—not illusion in the sense of nothingness,
but the necessary spectral differentiation by which Light appears as
knower, knowing, and known.

## Fichte's Bhāṣya

Fichte now asks what follows when higher knowing presupposes a principle for
its own self-genesis.

If self-genesis has a principle, then genesis is not absolute. The knowing in
which this principle is presupposed must be, inwardly and materially,
**non-self-genesis**.

Yet this non-genesis is not nothing. Knowing actually is. Fichte therefore
calls it positive non-genesis:

```text
genesis negated merely             nothing happens

genesis positively negated         enduring being
```

The positive negation of genesis reveals knowing's enduring, objective, and
presupposed being. Genesis occurs within knowing, but knowing cannot be
exhaustively explained as its own absolute production.

## Why the two sides cannot coincide immediately

From a reconstructive standpoint, we describe knowing as projecting itself
beyond itself and generating its own appearance. We seem to stand above the
process and comprehend its law.

But Fichte asks the devastating question:

> How did we reach this insight without apparently getting outside knowing?

Immanent knowing can never actually exit itself, stand over itself, and watch
its complete self-genesis as an object. From inside knowing, its enduring
being cannot appear as one more generated content. It appears only as the
negation of all genesis.

```text
reconstructive standpoint

knowing  ──projects──►  knowing as appearance


immanent actuality

knowing cannot stand outside knowing
and observe both termini at once
```

This is Fichte's version of `ubhayānavadhāraṇam`: knowing cannot be wholly
immanent and simultaneously occupy the external standpoint from which its
whole genesis is determined.

## The necessary hiatus

Fichte concludes that there is a necessary gap in the continuity of genesis,
a `per hiatum` projection.

This is not an irrational hole inserted into the system. It marks the limit
of Appearance. Pure reason in its oneness cannot itself appear as one item
within the series it makes intelligible.

```text
pure oneness of reason
          |
       hiatus
          |
generated Appearance
```

The hiatus prevents immediate Appearance from claiming to be Absolute
Actuality. Appearance is real as Appearance, but its reality is annulled when
it claims independence in comparison with the pure oneness of reason.

We should state the result carefully:

> Fichte does not say that appearances are simply nothing. He says their
> objective genesis cannot provide the absolute principle of the knowing in
> which that genesis appears.

## Spectral Theory

Our Spectral Theory can make the relational structure more precise.

Non-appearing Light does not become an appearing object while remaining
non-appearing. In relation, it is determined through Aspects:

```text
Light under the invariant Aspect       Puruṣa

Light under the changing seen Aspect   Citta

their determinate relation              Appearance / Māyā
```

The changelessness of Puruṣa is therefore the invariant Aspect required for
Citta's transformations to appear. Like a flame that does not twist in the
wind, it gives the moving field a stable spectral axis.

IV.20 shows why the two Aspects cannot be collapsed into a single immediate
Citta-act. Were Citta simultaneously the complete invariant seer and the
complete changing seen, the relation generating their determinate meanings
would disappear.

The Aspect distinction is not a plurality of independent Brahmans. It is the
Essential Relation within Appearance.

## Yoga and Fichte

| Yoga IV.20 | Fichte |
|---|---|
| Citta cannot determine both sides at one moment | knowing cannot get outside itself and observe its whole self-genesis |
| seeing and seen require distinct relational positions | self-genesis and enduring being cannot coincide as one immediate appearance |
| the relation requires mediation | there is a necessary hiatus in the continuity of genesis |
| Citta remains within Appearance | pure reason cannot appear as one generated item |

This is not a failure of knowledge. It is knowledge of the formal limit of
immediate Appearance.

## Practical Reason and the Kośa

The practical consequence is subtle. In action, Citta cannot simultaneously
live a response and possess a complete theoretical representation of itself
living that response.

```text
living Karuṇā                 reflection upon my Karuṇā

immediate practical act      subsequent cognition of the act
```

Reflection can correct and educate practice, but it cannot replace the living
act. A person absorbed in watching himself appear compassionate may cease to
be present to the suffering being before him.

This is another reason the Immeasurables are practices rather than merely
concepts. Their truth must become operative in Citta, while reflective
analysis occurs through another moment and Aspect.

Kośa discrimination remains indispensable. It distinguishes the factors of
an experience after and as they arise, but it should not pretend that an
analytic inventory is identical with the living whole of the practical act.

## Provisional result

IV.20 states the limit of Citta within the Essential Relation:

> Citta cannot determine both itself and the Thing at the same moment.

Vyāsa argues that one immediate moment cannot coherently determine its own
form and another's form while collapsing agent, action, and object into one.

Fichte reaches the same boundary at the level of the science of knowing.
Knowing cannot leave itself, observe its own entire genesis, and remain the
immanent knowing observed. The resulting hiatus separates pure reason's
oneness from the objective series of Appearance.

This prepares the final crisis of Appearance. If one Citta cannot illuminate
itself and its object simultaneously, perhaps another Citta could observe it.
IV.21 tests that solution—and finds an infinite regress and a confusion of
memory.
