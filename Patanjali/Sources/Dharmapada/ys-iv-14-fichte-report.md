# The Thing Is One Transformation

## A report on Yoga Sūtra IV.14 and Fichte

## The Sūtra

`pariṇāmaikatvād vastu-tattvam`

### Simple translation

> The Thing is actual because its transformation is one.

Or, keeping the compact Sanskrit structure:

> The reality of the Thing consists in the unity of its transformation.

`Pariṇāma` is transformation: a determinate becoming from one form into
another. `Ekatva` is oneness or unity. `Vastu` is the Thing or object, and
`tattva` is its truth, actuality, or what-it-is.

The Sūtra does not say that the Thing remains one because an unchanging core
stands behind its transformations. It makes the stronger claim that the
Thing's unity consists in the transformation itself.

> The Thing is not first given and then altered. It is the one transformation
> of its determinations.

## Why the Thing did not yet exist in IV.13

IV.13 said:

> The Dharmas, whether manifest or subtle, are Guṇa-formed.

This establishes a common qualitative nature across manifest and subtle
determinations. But it does not yet establish that these determinations make
one Thing.

The three Guṇas could still be taken as merely different factors:

```text
sattva       rajas       tamas
```

IV.14 adds the decisive determination. Their differentiation is one
`pariṇāma`:

```text
            sattva
              /\
             /  \
          rajas--tamas
              |
              |
       one transformation
              |
              v
            VASTU
```

The triangle is not a collection of three independent substances. Each Guṇa
has its determination only in conjunction with the other two. Their
`saṃyoga`, their triadic conjunction, is active as one transformation. This
unity is the `tattva` of the Thing.

## Fichte's Bhāṣya

Fichte now animates the result reached in IV.13. Light immediately posits a
`from`:

```text
                 L
                 |
              a — b
```

The diagram contains three inseparable determinations:

1. **Light** — the one qualitative actuality of knowing.
2. **The `from`** — its living genesis or transition.
3. **The termini `a–b`** — the difference that arises through that genesis.

The `from` does not connect an independently finished `a` with an
independently finished `b`. The termini arise as termini only in the `from`.
Nor does light observe the transition from outside. Because light is
identical with the `from`, its unchanged qualitative oneness permeates and
comprehends both terms.

This is Fichte's triadic structure:

```text
one light
    ↓
one genetic “from”
   ↙             ↘
term a          term b
   ↘             ↙
 one comprehended transformation
```

The unity is neither an additional fourth item nor a substrate concealed
beneath `a` and `b`. It is the one living transition through which they are
different and together.

## Transformation within transformation

Fichte then places another `from` within the first. The original disjunction
can divide into subordinate disjunctions:

```text
original “from”
      a — b
      |
      └── subordinate a — b
```

Because the same light is identical with the original `from`, it accompanies
all these subordinate divisions “at one stroke.” However complex the
articulation becomes, the transformations remain comprehended within one
qualitative actuality.

This gives a powerful sense to `pariṇāmaikatva`:

> A transformation remains one not by excluding its internal differences,
> but by comprehending every subordinate difference as a moment of the same
> genetic movement.

## Yoga and Fichte

| Yoga IV.14 | Fichte |
|---|---|
| `pariṇāma`, transformation | the living `from` |
| differentiated Guṇas in `saṃyoga` | the termini `a–b` produced through disjunction |
| `ekatva`, unity of transformation | one light permeates every primary and subordinate `from` |
| `vastu-tattva`, the truth of the Thing | the manifold is comprehended as one genetic act |

Fichte studies this structure specifically as Light. Yoga gives it greater
objective power through `guṇātman`: the same logic defines the natural
constitution of the Thing.

Fichte shows how Light remains one across a generated disjunction. Yoga says
that the differentiated Guṇas, through the unity of their transformation,
constitute an actual `vastu`.

## The first creation and its reconstruction

Fichte distinguishes two levels.

First is the pure, immediate `from`: Light's original self-positing. Here the
genesis is one living act.

Second is the division reconstructed in intuition. Intuition separates the
living transformation into terms and views them as beings:

```text
living genesis                 reconstructed intuition

one self-positing “from”  ->   a thing here, another thing there,
                               followed by an external relation
```

In Fichte's severe language, true life perishes in this second construction
and is reduced to the intuition of dead being. The science of knowing must
therefore resolve the finished object back into the living genesis through
which it is one.

This does not destroy the Thing. It reveals its `tattva`:

> What appears as a finished object is actually the unity of a living
> transformation.

## Triadic Saṃyoga

The Thing is neither pure identity nor mere multiplicity.

```text
pure identity alone       no determinate Thing

three isolated Guṇas      an aggregate, not one Thing

triadic Saṃyoga           differentiated factors whose
                          transformation is one
```

Sattva, rajas, and tamas should therefore not be imagined as ingredients
mixed in different quantities inside an otherwise independent object. They
are mutually determining functions of one transformation:

- sattva makes manifestation and intelligibility possible;
- rajas differentiates and moves the determination;
- tamas gives resistance, persistence, and determinate limitation.

No one of these is the Thing. No pair is sufficient. Their active conjunction
constitutes the transformation whose unity is the Thing.

## Citta and the Thing

This point is essential before Yoga introduces the plurality of Cittas in
IV.15.

The Thing cannot be reduced to one Citta's present representation. Its unity
has already been established as the unity of its Guṇa-transformation. A Citta
may encounter one manifestation of that transformation, while another Citta
encounters it differently.

Thus IV.14 secures the object before IV.15 differentiates cognitive paths:

```text
IV.14                         IV.15

one transformation           different Cittas encounter
constitutes the Thing   ->    the same Thing differently
```

This prevents transcendental idealism from degenerating into private
idealism. The Thing is known in Citta, but its `tattva` is not manufactured
by the empirical perspective of any one Citta.

## Provisional result

IV.13 established the common Guṇa-nature of manifest and subtle Dharmas.
IV.14 brings the Thing into existence:

> The triadic Guṇa-differentiation is one transformation, and that unity is
> the actuality of the Thing.

Fichte gives the specialized science of this structure in Light. The one
Light is the living `from` that produces its disjoined terms, permeates them,
and accompanies every subordinate division as one genesis.

Yoga states the more comprehensive ontological result with astonishing
economy:

> `pariṇāmaikatvād vastu-tattvam` — because the transformation is one, there
> is the actual Thing.

The Thing is therefore neither a dead substrate behind change nor a bundle
of appearances. It is **triadic Saṃyoga actual as one transformation**.
