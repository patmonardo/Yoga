# Savitarka Samāpatti: Word, Artha, and Knowledge in Mixture

## A report on Yoga Sūtra I.42 and Fichte

## The Sūtra

`tatra śabda-artha-jñāna-vikalpaiḥ saṅkīrṇā savitarkā samāpattiḥ`

### Relatively necessary ordinary translation

> There, the Samāpatti intermixed with the conceptual differentiations of
> Word, Artha, and Knowledge is Savitarka Samāpatti.

This is not a criticism of thought. It specifies the first determinate form
of Samāpatti. Citta has become transparent enough to hold the complete act,
but three distinguishable moments still arrive mixed together:

```text
Śabda                    Artha                    Jñāna
Word / sign              meaning / thing         Knowledge
expression               what is meant           its being-known
       \                    |                    /
        \                   |                   /
             Savitarka Samāpatti
             unity still in mixture
```

Fichte supplies the exact scientific problem:

> The living Principle is originally one, but ordinary representation and
> language allow its distinct moments to collapse unnoticed into one
> another. The Science of Knowing must make those differences visible
> without destroying their organic unity.

## Why I.42 follows the transparent jewel

I.41 established transparent Citta as the midpoint of grasper, grasping, and
grasped. I.42 asks what first appears within that transparency.

It is not yet absolutely pure Artha. It is Artha as already named,
conceptually distinguished, and known:

```text
transparent Citta
        ↓ takes a determinate coloring
Word — Artha — Knowledge
        ↓ still mutually entangled
Savitarka Samāpatti
```

The jewel is clear; the coloring is nevertheless composite.

## `Saṅkīrṇa`: mixture, not error

`Saṅkīrṇa` means mixed, intermingled, or blended. The mixture need not be
false. Its limitation is that the moments have not yet been discriminated
according to their Principles.

When a word is heard, ordinary cognition moves almost instantaneously:

```text
sound or sign
    → intended Artha
        → cognition of that Artha
```

The three occur as one factical event. We know what is meant, but we have
not yet comprehended the genetic distinction between:

- the expression,
- the determination expressed,
- and the Knowledge in which it is expressed and held true.

Savitarka is therefore genuine Samāpatti, but Samāpatti in which the
construction has not become completely transparent to its own genesis.

## `Vikalpa`: the work of differentiation

Here `vikalpa` should not be reduced to fantasy or arbitrary fiction. It is
the differentiating act through which one living unity is articulated as
Word, Artha, and Knowledge.

The problem is two-sided:

```text
without differentiation       without unity
the moments collapse          the moments fall apart
into an obscure immediacy      into unrelated fragments

             Science of Knowing
      exact distinction within living unity
```

This is why Fichte defends apparently minute distinctions. If even one
constructible distinction remains concealed, the Science has not traced
multiplicity completely to its Principle.

## Fichte: the Science stands between Oneness and multiplicity

Fichte says that the Science of Knowing has two reciprocal tasks:

```text
trace multiplicity back to absolute Oneness
                         ↕
deduce multiplicity from absolute Oneness
```

It therefore stands neither in undifferentiated Oneness nor in empirical
multiplicity. It persists between them, viewing distinctions from the
standpoint of their origin.

This is precisely the standpoint of Savitarka:

```text
not mere empirical words, things, and cognitions

not an empty Oneness in which every distinction disappears

but Word–Artha–Knowledge held together
as distinguishable moments of one cognitive act
```

Savitarka begins the scientific ascent because it refuses both confused
identity and disconnected difference.

## The true Oneness is not a simple term

Fichte gives a crucial warning: whatever appears before us as a simple
Oneness cannot, merely for that reason, be the true Oneness.

The true Oneness is the living Principle of both:

```text
apparent unity                    apparent multiplicity
       \                            /
        \                          /
          inwardly organic Principle
```

It does not first exist as an inert One and subsequently manufacture
difference from outside. It is intrinsically the Principle of unification
and differentiation at once.

Applied to I.42:

```text
Śabda, Artha, and Jñāna are not three alien substances
later glued together by Citta.

Their distinction is generated within one living act of Knowing.
```

Their immediate mixture is therefore intelligible. What Savitarka has not
yet attained is not Oneness, but the precise comprehension of how this
Oneness differentiates itself.

## Word is not Artha

The Word presents or marks a determination. It is not identical with the
Artha it presents.

```text
Śabda
the sensible or intelligible expression

Artha
the determination, intent, or matter expressed
```

Yet their relation is not arbitrary in the completed act. A Word functions
as Word only by bearing an Artha, while an articulated Artha appears through
some determinate form.

Savitarka knows the Artha through the Word but has not yet fully released
the Artha from the factical conditions of its expression.

## Artha is not Knowledge

Artha is what is determined; Jñāna is its being known. These too must not be
collapsed.

```text
Artha                         Jñāna
what is meant                 the manifestation and holding
                              of what is meant as known
```

But neither can stand scientifically without the other. An Artha wholly
outside Knowledge would not be manifest as Artha. Knowledge without any
Artha would be empty assertion.

The point is not to choose one pole. It is to comprehend their Essential
Relation.

## Jñāna is not merely another representation

If Jñāna were simply a third object added beside Word and Artha, the Sūtra
would give only a list:

```text
Word + Artha + a mental picture called Knowledge
```

Fichte’s analysis prevents this mistake. Knowing is the living activity in
which Word and Artha are distinguished and united. Yet in Savitarka this
activity is also taken factically as one distinguishable moment of the
mixture.

Thus Jñāna has a double position:

```text
within the mixture
Jñāna appears as a distinguishable cognition

as Principle
Knowing is the living field in which the whole distinction occurs
```

This difference between cognition and the Principle of Knowing is one of
the precise distinctions that Savitarka prepares us to see.

## Factical Savitarka and genetic comprehension

At first, the three moments are simply found together:

```text
I hear or think the Word
I understand its Artha
I know what it means

“it is so”
```

This is factical manifestness. The unity occurs, but its necessity remains
unexplained.

The Science then asks genetically:

```text
Why must expression, meaning, and Knowledge arise together?
What single Principle differentiates itself into all three?
How does each moment condition and illuminate the others?
```

Savitarka is the disciplined construction of this problem. It does not yet
claim the completely purified solution.

## Two inseparable grounds of distinction

Fichte describes two grounds of distinction that occur in one stroke:

```text
Being | Thinking
Sensible | Supersensible
```

Factically, the divisions appear inseparable. Genetic Science cannot merely
report this fact; it must comprehend the higher Principle in which their
grounds are one.

For I.42, the correspondence is illuminating:

```text
Śabda                     Artha                    Jñāna
sensible/intelligible     determination of Being  manifestation in Thinking
sign
```

This is not a rigid equation. It shows why the Savitarka mixture is so
powerful: it contains the entire problem of Being and Thinking in the
apparently simple event of understanding a Word.

## The principle is neither a dead One nor a heap

Fichte’s general schema may be expressed as:

```text
                 living Principle
                 /      |       \
                x       y        z
```

The Principle unifies x, y, and z because it differentiates itself into
them. Conversely, the terms lead back to their Principle only when their
reciprocal relations are grasped.

For Savitarka:

```text
              Science of Citta / Knowing
                  /       |       \
               Śabda    Artha    Jñāna
```

The first task is not to erase the three. It is to discover whether the
unity currently holding them is the highest Principle or only a provisional
one. If it is provisional, the Science must rise again until it reaches the
Oneness that is absolute differentiation itself.

## “Away with all words and signs”

Fichte eventually declares that words and signs must be surrendered. This
does not make I.42 dispensable. It states the limit that Savitarka must
recognize from within itself.

The Word can guide construction, but it cannot substitute for living
insight:

```text
Word indicates
Concept differentiates
Artha is constructed
Insight must see for itself
```

If we cling to the sign as though it possessed the insight, Śabda conceals
Artha. If we discard the sign before completing the construction, the
distinction remains vague. Savitarka uses the Word knowingly and discovers
the point at which the Word must become transparent.

## Light and the objectification of Light

In immediate insight, Fichte says, neither of the opposed terms contains
the truth. Absolute insight rests in their living Oneness:

```text
pure Light
from nothing, out of nothing, going nowhere
```

But the moment we consider Light as an object and distinguish its Life from
it, the Concept both posits and negates itself. The living Light has been
objectified.

This supplies the deepest interpretation of `saṅkīrṇa`:

```text
pure living insight
        ↓ reflected and expressed
Word — Artha — Knowledge
        ↓
appearance of the living insight in conceptual mixture
```

The mixture is not external contamination. It is the necessary appearance
of Insight when Insight is reflected, articulated, and made scientifically
available.

## Savitarka as impure Unconditioned Knowing

I.41 distinguished the pure and impure Unconditioned:

- Pure: the self-sufficient Light itself.
- Impure: that same Light genuinely present in a determinate representative
  insight.

I.42 now gives the first specification of the impure Unconditioned:

```text
the Unconditioned is truly present
        ↓
but present through Śabda, Artha, and Jñāna
        ↓
and therefore mixed with its determinate appearance
```

“Impure” does not mean false or sinful. It means that the Principle appears
through conditions that have not yet become entirely transparent.

## The A–U–M form of Savitarka

The three moments can be read through our working specification:

```text
A — Appearance
Śabda and the articulated objective determination

U — Reflection / Thinking
the differentiation and return through which Artha is meant

M — Knowing / Concept
the comprehension that holds expression, meaning,
and reflection within one act
```

But in Savitarka, M is not yet perfectly free of A and U. Knowing appears
inside the mixture whose Principle it must comprehend.

This is the Principle of the Science of Citta in its first explicit form:

```text
Citta does not merely receive a Word,
possess an Artha,
and then add Knowledge.

Citta is the living differentiation and synthesis
through which Word bears Artha and Artha becomes known.
```

## A discipline for Scientists of Knowing

For a first-time trainee, I.42 gives a precise exercise:

1. State the Word exactly.
2. Determine the Artha it is intended to bear.
3. Distinguish that Artha from the cognition of it.
4. Observe how all three arise together in one act.
5. Do not mistake their immediate mixture for their genetic Principle.
6. Hold the mixture steady until its construction becomes transparent.

The aim is not terminological mastery. It is direct discrimination of the
NLP pipeline of Artha without losing the living Oneness that makes the
pipeline possible.

## Compact synthesis

Yoga gives the structure:

```text
Śabda + Artha + Jñāna + Vikalpa
        ↓ intermixed
Savitarka Samāpatti
```

Fichte gives its scientific meaning:

```text
ordinary language collapses precise distinctions
        ↓
Science constructs the distinctions explicitly
        ↓
each difference is traced to the living Principle
that differentiates and unifies it
        ↓
Word, Artha, and Knowledge become transparent
as one organic act of Knowing
```

Savitarka is therefore not inferior because it thinks. It is preliminary
because its Thinking still bears Word, Artha, and Knowledge in mixture.

The achievement of I.42 is immense: the transparent Citta of I.41 now begins
to articulate its own Principle. The Science of Citta appears as the Science
of how Artha enters Word, how Word enters Reflection, and how both are held
as Knowledge.

The next movement must not annihilate this articulation. It must purify the
mixture so that Artha can shine without the inherited confusions of Word,
memory, and conceptual construction.
