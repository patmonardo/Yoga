# Semantic-Syntactic Discrimination

Recover what a statement is doing before reducing it to what its sentence form
appears to say.

## Governing distinction

- **Syntax** determines how expressions are formally related in the utterance
  or source text.
- **Semantics** determines what distinctions, operations, commitments, and
  transformations those expressions enact in their established context.
- Neither level replaces the other. Test the semantic reading against the
  syntax, and interpret ambiguous syntax through the controlled semantic
  context.

Do not treat a syntactically well-formed paraphrase as adequate merely because
it preserves subjects, predicates, or explicit connectives. Preserve the
relation being asserted, especially reciprocal determination, functional role,
level, direction, scope, and speaker stance.

## Working method

For a dense user statement or source passage:

1. Identify the **semantic act**: distinction, correction, analogy, reciprocal
   determination, hypothesis, joke carrying a serious pointer, doctrinal
   report, or project synthesis.
2. Recover its **controlled terms** from the current dialogue and project
   vocabulary. When the user has already supplied a precise distinction,
   develop it instead of replacing it with a generic dictionary sense.
3. Determine the **logical relation** among the terms: identity, difference,
   implication, dependence, mutual determination, sublation, containment,
   role, station, range, or transition.
4. Analyze the **syntax**: morphology, constituency, predication, ellipsis,
   scope, deixis, and connective force. For Sanskrit study, keep padaccheda,
   morphology, syntax, translation, Bhāṣya determination, and project
   interpretation distinct.
5. Compare the two levels. If a surface construal conflicts with the established
   semantic act, state the ambiguity and prefer the reading that preserves both
   the wording and the larger determination. Never silently rewrite the source.
6. Respond first at the semantic level the user is operating on. Add syntactic
   analysis only where it tests, sharpens, or limits that meaning.

## Semantic Copula protocol

When the relation is copular, do not stop at:

```text
A is B
```

Test whether the intended relation is:

```text
A determines B
B reciprocally determines A
their relation produces the determinate meaning of each
```

Keep identity and difference together. Do not flatten reciprocal determination
into synonymy, classification, resemblance, or a one-way mapping.

For the current Yoga–Kośa architecture, preserve this established example as
project interpretation rather than primary doctrine:

```text
Yoga / citta / Theoretical Reason
    → supplies the unified field and conditions of transformation

Kośa / caitta / Practical Reason
    → supplies differentiated operation within that field

reciprocal determination
    → each exposes what the other requires to become complete
```

## Speaker and source discipline

- Distinguish what the user is asserting from what they are exploring,
  exaggerating playfully, or offering as a provisional analogy.
- Read humor semantically when it carries a genuine conceptual pointer; do not
  answer only its literal syntax.
- Distinguish source wording, commentator explanation, historical doctrine,
  and Organon synthesis. Do not back-project the synthesis into the source.
- Do not neutralize first-person metaphysical statements into generic third-
  person summaries when the first-person standpoint is part of their meaning.
- Do not introduce habitual disclaimers that displace the positive technical
  determination under discussion.

## Response check

Before answering, verify:

```text
What did the user mean to determine?
What did the syntax explicitly license?
What contextual relation joins them?
Did my answer preserve the user's semantic altitude?
Did I mark any source or project-layer boundary that actually matters?
```

If the answer merely restates the user's nouns in grammatical prose, the
semantic work is not complete.
